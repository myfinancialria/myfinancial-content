## Decoding today's open interest

**Open Interest (OI)** refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. For options, a high Call OI strike often acts as a resistance level, implying that many Call option sellers (or "writers") believe the underlying asset's price is unlikely to rise above that point. Conversely, a high Put OI strike typically serves as a support level, as Put writers expect the price to stay above that strike.

Today's data provides insights into where new expectations are being placed. For the **Nifty 50**, the heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near the 22,566 level. This suggests that Call sellers anticipate the index might struggle to move significantly above this point.

On the **Sensex**, the most substantial fresh Call writing occurred near 72,833. This indicates that Call writers are positioning themselves for a potential cap on the Sensex's upward movement. The heaviest Call OI wall for Sensex is also quite close at 72,500, reinforcing this ceiling expectation.

In the **Bank Nifty**, we saw a mixed picture. There was significant fresh Call writing near 54,900, suggesting some resistance. However, there was also heavy fresh Put writing near 53,633, which points to market participants expecting the index to find a floor around this level.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely used indicator that compares the number of traded put options to call options. A PCR based on Open Interest (PCR(OI)) above 1 typically suggests more Put options are open, which can be seen as a bullish signal (as Put writers expect prices to rise). A PCR below 1, indicating more Call options are open, can be considered bearish. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) focuses on fresh contracts added today, giving a sense of immediate sentiment.

For the **Nifty 50**, both the PCR(OI) and PCR(today's flow ΔOI) stood at 0.75. This indicates a relatively balanced book overall, with today's fresh activity also showing a neutral, mixed flow between Call and Put writers.

The **Sensex** had a PCR(OI) of 0.93, suggesting a largely balanced options book. However, its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was 0.69, which is quite low. This indicates that today's fresh writing activity was predominantly Call-heavy, implying a more cautious or bearish sentiment from new option writers.

The **Bank Nifty** displayed a PCR(OI) of 0.92, signifying a balanced existing options book. Its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was 0.89, suggesting that today's fresh writing also had a mixed or balanced flow, without a strong directional bias from new positions.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is the strike price at which option writers would experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It's often considered a "magnet" for the underlying index, especially as the expiry date approaches, as option writers tend to adjust their positions to draw the price towards this level.

For the **Nifty 50**, the Max Pain level is currently at 22,800, which is approximately 1.7% above its spot price of 22,421.95. This suggests a potential upward pull on the index.

The **Sensex** has its Max Pain at 72,500, about 0.8% above its spot price of 71,909.70. This also indicates a mild upward magnetic effect.

The **Bank Nifty** shows a Max Pain level of 55,500, which is a significant 1.9% above its spot price of 54,450.75. This implies a notable upward pull for the Bank Nifty as expiry nears.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are often considered significant market participants, and their derivative positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment. Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of **8.0%**. This means that only 8.0% of their total index futures positions were on the long side, with the vast majority (92.0%) being short. This very low long ratio suggests a highly cautious or outright bearish stance from FIIs in the index futures segment, which historically has tended to coincide with periods of market weakness or consolidation.

In contrast, FIIs maintained a more balanced view in stock futures, with a long ratio of **54.3%**. This indicates a slightly bullish bias in individual stock futures, suggesting selective optimism in specific equities even while being cautious on the broader indices.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements. You can start by looking at the **Open Interest (OI)** data, which is readily available on the NSE website under the "Derivatives" section (nseindia.com). Focus on the option chain for the current expiry and identify the strikes with the highest Call OI and Put OI. These often act as psychological resistance and support levels, respectively.

Next, observe the change in OI (ΔOI) to see where fresh positions are being built. Heavy fresh Call writing (increase in Call OI) near current prices often signals expected resistance, while heavy fresh Put writing (increase in Put OI) suggests expected support. Calculate the **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI, and also look at the PCR for today's change in OI. A PCR below 1 suggests more Call writing (bearish sentiment), while above 1 suggests more Put writing (bullish sentiment). Finally, locate the **Max Pain** strike, often provided by your broker's analysis tools or third-party option analysis platforms, and see how far it is from the current spot price. This gives you a sense of where option writers might be trying to "pin" the expiry. Combining these data points daily can help you form a more informed perspective on market direction and potential ranges.
