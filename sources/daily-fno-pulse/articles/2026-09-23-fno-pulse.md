## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been settled. It provides a valuable insight into where market participants, particularly option writers (sellers), are positioning themselves.

The highest Call Open Interest (CE OI) strike typically represents a significant resistance level, meaning many call options have been sold at that price, with writers expecting the market to stay below it. Conversely, the highest Put Open Interest (PE OI) strike often acts as a strong support level, as put writers anticipate the market remaining above this point.

For the **Nifty 50**, today's fresh Call writing was heaviest near the 23,850 strike. This suggests that call sellers anticipate the index might find a cap around this level. On the Put side, the heaviest fresh writing was observed near 23,283, indicating that put sellers are establishing a potential floor for the index around this point.

In the **Sensex**, fresh Call writing was most concentrated around 75,433, implying that call writers expect the index to face resistance there. Simultaneously, significant fresh Put writing was seen near 73,933, suggesting that option sellers believe this level could provide robust support.

The **Bank Nifty** witnessed its heaviest fresh Call writing near 57,066, with call sellers potentially seeing this as a ceiling. On the other hand, substantial fresh Put writing near 56,366 indicates that put sellers are positioning for a floor at this level.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that helps gauge overall market sentiment in the options segment. It is calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 generally suggests a bullish bias, as it indicates more put options have been written (sellers expect prices to rise or stay stable, thus protecting against a fall). Conversely, a value below 1 often points to a bearish sentiment, with more call options written (sellers expect prices to fall or stay stable, thus capping upside).

Today's PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) for the **Nifty 50** stood at 0.99, suggesting a largely balanced book of outstanding Call and Put options. However, the PCR based on today's fresh flow of Open Interest (ΔPCR) was 1.42. This indicates that a significant portion of today's new option writing was in Puts, leaning towards a bullish sentiment from fresh positions.

For the **Sensex**, the PCR(OI) was 1.32, which is above 1 and suggests that put options significantly dominate, potentially indicating robust downside protection. The ΔPCR for Sensex was even higher at 2.37, highlighting a very strong preference for fresh put writing today, reinforcing a bullish stance.

The **Bank Nifty** recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.96, similar to Nifty, indicating a relatively balanced outstanding options book. However, its ΔPCR for today's flow was 1.34, again showing a clear inclination towards fresh put writing, which typically suggests a bullish undertone for the session.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is a concept in options theory that refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum cumulative financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that specific price. As expiry approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this "Max Pain" strike, acting like a magnetic pull.

For the **Nifty 50**, the Max Pain strike is currently at 23,500, which is very close to its spot closing price of 23,446.80. This proximity suggests that option writers are positioned for the index to potentially hover around this level.

The **Sensex** has its Max Pain strike at 74,500. This is approximately 0.4% below its spot closing price of 74,828.25. This slight downward pull from Max Pain could imply a minor consolidation or a gentle drift towards this level as expiry nears.

In the case of the **Bank Nifty**, the Max Pain strike is at 57,000. This is about 0.8% above its spot closing price of 56,548.90. This upward pull from Max Pain suggests that option writers are positioned for the index to potentially move higher towards this level by expiry.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, the FII Index futures long ratio stands at 10.7%. This indicates that only a small percentage of their total index futures positions are on the 'long' side, with a substantial majority being 'short'. Historically, such a low long ratio in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or net bearish stance from these institutional players in the broader market.

In contrast, the FII Stock futures long ratio is 53.9%. This suggests a slightly more balanced, yet mildly bullish, positioning in individual stock futures, implying that while they may be cautious on the broader index, they are selectively positive on specific stocks.

Synthesising the various signals from today's options and futures data, a generally positive, albeit somewhat nuanced, sentiment appears to be emerging for the immediate future. The individual bias checks for Nifty 50 and Sensex lean mildly bullish, while Bank Nifty shows a more pronounced bullish inclination. Historically, such a divergence between retail/HNI-driven options writing (often reflected in PCR) and institutional futures positioning has sometimes led to periods of range-bound movement as these differing views contend. When these signals align in this direction, the next session has historically tended to see the market attempting to hold onto gains, with strong support levels being defended by put writers, even as institutional caution in index futures remains a factor to observe.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to form your own informed perspective. You can access much of this data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website or through your broker's platform.

To start, look for the "Option Chain" on the NSE website for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty. Here, you can identify the strikes with the highest Open Interest for both Calls and Puts, which indicate key resistance and support levels. Next, check the "F&O Bhavcopy" for daily changes in Open Interest (ΔOI) to see where fresh writing activity is concentrated. You can then calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) for both total OI and today's ΔOI. For FII positioning, the "Participant-wise Open Interest" file, also available on the NSE archives, provides crucial data on how institutional players are positioned in futures and options. Combining these data points – observing where fresh money is being put to work, understanding the Max Pain dynamics, and noting institutional sentiment – allows you to build a comprehensive picture of the market's underlying structure each day.
