## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. In options, the strike price with the highest Call OI typically acts as a potential resistance level, meaning many call options have been sold at that strike, with writers expecting the price not to rise above it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI often signals a potential support level, as many put options have been sold, with writers anticipating the price will not fall below it.

For the **Nifty 50**, today's fresh writing activity showed a clear preference for puts. The heaviest fresh Call (CE) writing was observed near 24,283, indicating that call sellers believe this level could act as a ceiling. However, significantly heavier fresh Put (PE) writing near 23,533 suggests that put sellers are actively building a floor, expecting the index to find support around these levels. The aggregate flow saw a net build of 28.8 million in Put OI versus 7.4 million in Call OI, reinforcing the put-heavy sentiment.

On the **Sensex**, fresh Call writing was most prominent near 76,833, suggesting that call writers anticipate this level to cap upward moves. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was concentrated around 76,700, indicating put sellers are establishing a floor close to the current spot. This dual activity points to a potentially tight range.

For **Bank Nifty**, fresh Call writing was heaviest near 57,200, suggesting that call sellers expect the index to struggle above this point. In contrast, fresh Put writing was concentrated around 57,333, with put sellers expecting support to hold at or above this strike.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the number of put options traded to the number of call options traded. A PCR based on Open Interest (PCR(OI)) above 1 generally suggests that more put options are open than call options, which can be interpreted as a bullish signal by some, as put writers profit when prices rise. Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more call options are open, potentially signaling a bearish sentiment. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) looks at fresh writing activity for the day.

For **Nifty 50**, the PCR(OI) stands at 0.82, indicating a relatively balanced book with slightly more call options open overall. However, today's flow ΔPCR is a robust 3.91, which is a strong bullish signal. This means that a significant amount of fresh put options were written today compared to call options, suggesting that option sellers are actively betting against a fall in the index.

The **Sensex** has a PCR(OI) of 0.92, also reflecting a largely balanced options book. Its today's flow ΔPCR is 1.7, which leans mildly bullish, indicating more fresh put writing than call writing during the session.

**Bank Nifty** recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.83, suggesting a balanced to slightly call-heavy overall open interest. The today's flow ΔPCR, however, was -24.26, which is a very low number and suggests a mixed or balanced fresh flow, not clearly skewed towards puts or calls despite the overall positive sentiment indicated by other factors.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option buyers (both calls and puts) would collectively experience the maximum financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that specific price. Option writers, conversely, would experience the least loss or maximum profit at this level. As expiry approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this "max pain" point, acting like a magnet.

For **Nifty 50**, the max pain point is at 24,250, which is approximately 1.2% above its current spot of 23,962.80. This suggests a potential upward pull towards the max pain level as we approach expiry.

The **Sensex** has its max pain point at 76,800, which is very close to its current spot of 76,741.82. When max pain is near the spot price, it often indicates that the market expects the index to consolidate around current levels into expiry.

**Bank Nifty**'s max pain is significantly higher at 58,500, approximately 2.2% above its current spot of 57,252.45. This substantial difference suggests a strong potential for the index to be pulled higher towards this level as expiry nears.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are often considered significant players in the Indian derivatives market. Their positioning in index futures can offer insights into their broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of **9.6%**, with 31,455 long contracts against 297,706 short contracts. This is a very low long ratio, indicating a predominantly short position in index futures. Historically, such a low long ratio in FII index futures has tended to coincide with periods of caution or a bearish outlook from this segment of the market. It suggests that FIIs are largely hedged or positioned for potential downside in the broader indices.

In stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of **54.2%**, with 3,815,377 long contracts versus 3,223,806 short contracts, indicating a slightly bullish stance on individual stocks.

## How to read this yourself daily

To interpret the market's pulse daily, you can look at a few key data points. Start by checking the Open Interest (OI) data for Call and Put options on the NSE website (archives.nseindia.com/products/content/derivatives/equities/fo_underlying_openinterest.htm). Identify the strike prices with the highest Call OI and Put OI; these often act as immediate resistance and support levels. Also, observe the change in OI (ΔOI) for the day, particularly for strikes near the current spot price. A significant increase in Put OI suggests fresh put writing and potential support, while a rise in Call OI indicates fresh call writing and potential resistance.

Next, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI. A PCR above 1 is generally considered bullish, and below 1, bearish. Also, look at the PCR based on today's ΔOI for a sense of fresh sentiment. Finally, track the FII derivative positioning data, also available on the NSE website (archives.nseindia.com/content/nsccl/fao_participant_oi_DDMMYYYY.csv), specifically their index futures long and short positions. A very low long ratio, as seen today, often signals caution from institutional players. By combining these insights, you can form a more holistic understanding of the market's underlying positioning.
