## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been closed or exercised. When we talk about "Call walls" or "Put bases," we are referring to strike prices where the Open Interest is highest. A **Call wall** (highest Call OI) typically indicates a level where option sellers (Call writers) believe the underlying index will not rise beyond, thus acting as a potential resistance. Conversely, a **Put base** (highest Put OI) signifies a level where Put writers expect the index to find support and not fall below.

For **NIFTY 50**, the heaviest Call Open Interest is positioned at 24,300, which is approximately 0.5% above the spot price. This suggests that option sellers anticipate NIFTY 50 might find it challenging to move significantly past this level. On the Put side, the heaviest Open Interest is at 24,000, about 0.7% below spot, indicating a strong support zone. Today, fresh Call writing was most prominent near 24,516, implying some option sellers expect the upside to be capped around that level. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was concentrated near 24,083, suggesting conviction among Put sellers that this level will act as a floor.

For **SENSEX**, the most significant Call Open Interest is at 78,000, which is 1.0% above the current spot. This strike could serve as a notable resistance point. While the data for Put walls wasn't explicitly provided, the broad range suggests a more diffused distribution of Put OI. Today's fresh Put writing was heaviest near 76,366, indicating that option sellers are establishing support around this strike.

In **BANK NIFTY**, both the heaviest Call OI and Put OI are concentrated at 57,500, right at the day's closing spot. This tight convergence suggests a strong battleground for option writers, with both bulls and bears seeing this level as crucial. ## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely watched indicator in the options market. It is calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest (PCR(OI)), or by dividing the volume of Puts traded by Calls traded (PCR(volume)). A PCR(OI) value above 1 generally suggests a bullish sentiment, as there are more Puts outstanding than Calls, implying more participants are selling Puts (expecting prices to rise). A value below 1 often indicates a bearish sentiment, with more Calls outstanding. The "today's flow ΔPCR" looks at the change in Open Interest for Puts versus Calls during the day, giving insight into fresh positioning.

For **NIFTY 50**, the PCR(OI) stands at 0.72, which suggests a slightly bearish bias from the overall outstanding positions, indicating more Call Open Interest compared to Put Open Interest. However, today's flow ΔPCR was -51.43, which indicates a mixed or balanced fresh writing activity, without a clear directional bias from today's new positions.

**SENSEX** has a PCR(OI) of 0.98, which is very close to 1, signaling a largely balanced book among option writers. Today's flow ΔPCR was 1.15, also suggesting a mixed to slightly put-heavy fresh writing, but overall balanced.

**BANK NIFTY** shows a PCR(OI) of 1.05, indicating a slightly bullish stance from the overall outstanding positions, with slightly more Put Open Interest. More notably, today's flow ΔPCR was 1.54, which points to a clear put-heavy fresh writing, suggesting that new option sellers are more inclined to sell Puts, expecting the index to hold its ground or move higher.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is the strike price at which option writers, collectively, would experience the minimum financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that particular strike. It's often considered a magnetic point because, as expiry approaches, the underlying index has historically shown a tendency to gravitate towards this level.

For **NIFTY 50**, the Max Pain strike is at 24,400, which is approximately 0.9% above the current spot price of 24,175.65. This suggests a potential upward pull for the index as expiry draws nearer.

**SENSEX** has its Max Pain strike at 77,400, which is very close to the current spot price of 77,264.51. This indicates that option writers are positioned for the index to expire around its current levels, implying a sideways movement.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the Max Pain strike is at 57,600, also very near the current spot price of 57,496.30. Similar to SENSEX, this suggests a potential for the index to consolidate around its current levels as expiry approaches.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market dynamics doesn't require complex tools; you can gather much of this information yourself. To start, visit the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website (nseindia.com) and navigate to the "Derivatives" section. Here, you can find the "Option Chain" for NIFTY, BANK NIFTY, and SENSEX. This chain will show you the Open Interest (OI) for various Call and Put strikes. Look for the strikes with the highest OI on both the Call and Put sides – these are your potential resistance and support levels.

Additionally, to calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR), you can sum up the total Put OI and total Call OI from the option chain and divide the former by the latter. For FII positioning, the NSE also publishes daily participant-wise Open Interest data under the "Archives" section, usually found under "Market Data" or "Reports." By tracking these numbers daily and observing how the highest OI strikes shift, how the PCR evolves, and what FIIs are doing, you can begin to form your own informed perspective on the market's underlying sentiment.
