# India F&O Pulse — 04 Sep 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Indian Indices Show Mixed Signals, With Bank Nifty Leaning Mildly Bearish

**TL;DR**
*   Nifty 50's option data suggests a range-bound outlook, with significant Call writing indicating potential resistance above current levels.
*   Sensex also appears positioned for consolidation, though today's fresh Put writing offered some underlying support.
*   Bank Nifty shows a mildly bearish tilt, with Call writers actively capping upside potential and fresh flows favouring Call selling.

## What the indices did today

The Nifty 50 closed the day at 23,897.70, registering a marginal gain of 0.10%. The index finished just below its heaviest Call Open Interest (OI) wall at 24,000, suggesting this level could act as a near-term ceiling.

The Sensex advanced by 0.48% to settle at 76,515.43. While showing a stronger positive move, it also closed beneath its significant Call OI wall at 77,000, indicating potential resistance ahead.

In contrast, the Bank Nifty saw a slight dip of 0.02%, closing at 57,369.65. The index finished close to both its heaviest Call and Put OI walls at 57,500, suggesting a tight range for the immediate future, with a slight lean towards the Call side in terms of fresh activity.

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding Call or Put contracts that have not yet been settled. High Call OI at a particular strike price often acts as a resistance level, as many option sellers (writers) believe the price will not rise above that point. Conversely, high Put OI at a strike price typically acts as a support level, as Put writers expect the price to stay above that level.

For the Nifty 50, the heaviest Call OI is observed at 24,000, which is approximately 0.4% above the spot price, indicating a potential ceiling for the index. On the Put side, the data suggests a strong base around 23,000. Today's fresh Call writing was most prominent near 24,166, implying that Call sellers anticipate the Nifty 50 may struggle to move significantly higher than this level. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was concentrated around 23,883, suggesting Put sellers expect the index to find support close to current levels.

Sensex saw its heaviest Call OI at 77,000, a level about 0.6% above its current spot, which could serve as a resistance point. Fresh Call writing today was notable near 77,633, with writers positioning for a cap around that strike. On the Put side, fresh writing was heaviest near 76,100, suggesting a floor is being built by Put sellers around this mark.

Bank Nifty's option chain shows significant Call OI at 57,500, a mere 0.2% above spot, indicating a close resistance. Interestingly, the heaviest Put OI is also at 57,500, just 0.2% below spot, suggesting a very tight range around this level. Today's aggregate flow in Bank Nifty showed a net build-up in Call OI (0.1 million contracts) significantly outweighing Put OI (0.0 million contracts), which suggests a more cautious stance from option writers on the upside.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total Put Open Interest to the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests a bullish sentiment, as more Put options are open, implying more support. A value below 1 often indicates a bearish sentiment, with more Call options open, suggesting resistance.

For the Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.87, which suggests a relatively balanced book with a slight lean towards Call options, indicating a neutral to mildly cautious sentiment. Today's flow, measured by the change in Open Interest (ΔPCR), was -3.22, suggesting a mixed to balanced flow of fresh writing activity.

Sensex recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.05, indicating a balanced book with a slight edge towards Put options, which can be interpreted as neutral to mildly supportive. The ΔPCR for today's flow was 1.34, meaning fresh writing leaned towards Puts, suggesting some underlying confidence from Put sellers.

Bank Nifty's PCR(OI) is exactly 1.0, signifying a perfectly balanced book in terms of total outstanding contracts. However, today's flow ΔPCR was 0.27, which points to fresh writing being predominantly on the Call side, suggesting a cautious or mildly bearish sentiment emerging from today's activity.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is a concept in options trading that refers to the strike price at which option buyers (both Calls and Puts) would collectively experience the maximum financial loss at expiry. It is often seen as a gravitational pull for the underlying index, especially as expiry approaches, as option writers (who are typically larger players) tend to profit if the price closes near this level.

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain strike is at 24,050, which is about 0.6% above its current spot price. This suggests a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry nears.

Sensex's Max Pain is identified at 76,700, which is very close to its current spot price. This implies that the index might gravitate towards its current levels by expiry.

Bank Nifty's Max Pain is at 57,500, also very close to its spot price. This suggests a strong magnetic effect around current levels, with option writers positioned for the index to settle near this strike.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment. Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 11.1%. This means that out of their total Index futures positions, only 11.1% were long contracts, while a substantial 88.9% were short. Historically, such a low long ratio in FII Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish sentiment among these large participants, often preceding periods of consolidation or downside pressure, or indicating potential for short covering if sentiment shifts.

In stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of 55.0%, suggesting a more balanced to mildly bullish stance on individual stocks compared to their index-level positioning.

## Stocks where money moved today

Among the F&O watchlist, Reliance Industries (RELIANCE) was a top gainer, rising 1.50% to ₹1322.00 with a volume of over 1.3 crore shares. Adani Enterprises (ADANIENT) also performed well, up 1.28% to ₹2938.00. Bajaj Finance (BAJFINANCE), Kotak Mahindra Bank (KOTAKBANK), and HDFC Bank (HDFCBANK) also saw positive moves.

On the losing side, Bharti Airtel (BHARTIARTL) saw a decline of 1.55% to ₹1840.00. Maruti Suzuki (MARUTI) was down 1.27% to ₹12694.00. State Bank of India (SBIN), TCS, and ICICI Bank (ICICIBANK) also ended the day in the red. These movements are purely observational and reflect where trading activity was concentrated today.

## What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When the option chain data presents a mixed picture like today, with Nifty 50 and Sensex showing range-bound biases and Bank Nifty leaning mildly bearish, it historically tends to coincide with a period of consolidation in the market. The presence of strong Call walls just above current spot prices for all major indices, coupled with fresh Call writing activity, suggests that option writers are positioned for limited upside in the immediate term.

The low FII Index futures long ratio further reinforces a cautious outlook from institutional players. While Max Pain for Nifty 50 is slightly above spot, the proximity of Max Pain to current levels for Sensex and Bank Nifty, combined with significant OI at these strikes, indicates a potential for the indices to remain anchored around these points. Historically, such a confluence of signals has tended to precede sessions where indices might trade within defined ranges, with any significant moves potentially facing resistance or finding support at the levels indicated by high Open Interest.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements. You can access the raw data from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, specifically the "Option Chain" section for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty. Your brokerage platform may also provide this data in a user-friendly format.

Look for the "Open Interest" column for both Call and Put options across different strike prices. The strikes with the highest OI often indicate key resistance (for Calls) and support (for Puts) levels. Also, pay attention to the "Change in Open Interest" column, which tells you where fresh buying or selling (writing) activity occurred today. A significant increase in Call OI at a strike suggests fresh Call writing, implying resistance, while an increase in Put OI suggests fresh Put writing, implying support. By observing these patterns daily, you can develop a better understanding of how market participants are positioning themselves.
