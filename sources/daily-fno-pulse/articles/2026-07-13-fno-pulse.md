## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, like Calls and Puts, that have not yet been settled. When we talk about the "highest Call OI strike," it indicates the strike price where the maximum number of Call options are currently open. This strike often acts as a significant resistance level, as Call option writers (sellers) profit if the price stays below this level. Conversely, the "highest Put OI strike" signifies the strike where the maximum Put options are open, typically acting as a strong support level because Put option writers profit if the price stays above it.

For the **NIFTY 50**, today's fresh Call option writing was most concentrated near 24,433. This suggests that Call sellers anticipate the index might find a cap around these levels. On the Put side, the heaviest fresh writing was observed near 24,050, indicating that Put sellers expect the index to find a floor around this mark. The aggregate flow for Nifty showed a substantial net Put OI build of 55.4 million contracts, significantly outweighing the Call OI build of 0.9 million, reinforcing the put-heavy positioning.

In the **SENSEX**, fresh Call writing was most prominent near 78,233, implying that Call writers foresee potential resistance at higher levels. For Puts, the heaviest fresh writing was seen near 76,333, suggesting that Put writers are establishing support at these lower strikes.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the most significant fresh Put writing occurred near 58,400. This indicates that Put sellers are actively building positions, expecting the index to hold above this level. While there was some fresh Call writing, the aggregate flow showed a net Put OI build of 0.1 million contracts, compared to a negligible Call OI build, further highlighting the bullish tilt from option writers.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely watched indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR(OI) value above 1 typically suggests more Put options are outstanding than Call options, often interpreted as a sign of underlying support or a potentially bullish sentiment. Conversely, a value below 1 might indicate more Call options, suggesting potential resistance or a bearish lean. The "PCR(today flow ΔOI)" looks specifically at the change in open interest for the day, giving insight into fresh positioning.

For **NIFTY 50**, the PCR(OI) stands at 1.53, which is significantly above 1. This indicates a strong dominance of Put options in the overall open interest, suggesting that option writers are collectively positioned for downside protection. Today's flow ΔPCR, at a very high 63.32, further reinforces this, showing that fresh writing was overwhelmingly put-heavy, with many new Put options being written today. This combination typically suggests strong conviction among option writers that the downside is well-protected.

**SENSEX** has a PCR(OI) of 1.21, indicating a relatively balanced but slightly put-heavy book, suggesting a moderate level of support. Its PCR(today flow ΔOI) of 1.44 shows that today's fresh option writing also leaned towards Puts, contributing to the overall support structure.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the PCR(OI) is 0.81, which is below 1, suggesting a slightly higher concentration of Call options in the overall open interest. However, its PCR(today flow ΔOI) is a robust 2.75. This significant fresh put writing today, despite the lower overall PCR(OI), indicates that option writers are actively building support and expecting the index to move higher or consolidate.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying asset expires at that specific price. It's often considered a "magnet" because, as an expiry date approaches, the underlying index or stock tends to gravitate towards this level, as option writers, who collectively hold significant positions, might try to manage their risk to minimise losses.

For **NIFTY 50**, the Max Pain level is at 23,950, which is approximately 1.1% below its current spot price of 24,211. This suggests a potential downward pull towards this level as expiry nears.

The **SENSEX** Max Pain is at 77,400, which is very close to its spot price of 77,616.40. When Max Pain is near the spot, it often suggests a consolidation phase or that the market is already hovering around a level where option writers are comfortable.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the Max Pain is at 58,500, which is about 0.6% above its current spot of 58,131.45. This indicates a potential upward pull, suggesting that option writers might be positioned for the index to move higher towards this level by expiry.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can be a valuable exercise for any market participant. You can access the necessary data points daily from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website or through your broker's trading platform.

To start, look for the "Option Chain" data for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty. Identify the strike prices with the highest Open Interest for both Calls and Puts – these are your key resistance and support levels. Next, observe the "Change in Open Interest" column to see where fresh writing (new positions) occurred today. Significant increases in Put OI at lower strikes or Call OI at higher strikes can give clues about where option writers expect support and resistance. Finally, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI. You can also calculate the PCR for the day's change in OI to see the immediate sentiment. Combining these observations, along with the Max Pain level (often provided by analytical tools or can be calculated), helps paint a comprehensive picture of market positioning. Remember, no single indicator tells the whole story; it's the confluence of multiple signals that offers a more robust perspective.
