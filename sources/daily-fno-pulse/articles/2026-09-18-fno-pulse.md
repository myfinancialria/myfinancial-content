## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been settled. For Call options, the strike with the highest OI often acts as a **resistance level** or a "ceiling," because many option writers (sellers) believe the market will not rise above this point. Conversely, for Put options, the strike with the highest OI typically serves as a **support level** or a "floor," as Put writers are betting the market will not fall below it.

Today, for the Nifty 50, the heaviest Put OI was observed at 23,300, just slightly below the spot price, indicating a robust support base. The largest Call OI stood significantly higher at 24,000, suggesting a considerable upside resistance. In terms of fresh activity, today's heaviest fresh Put writing was seen near 23,150, reinforcing the expectation of a floor. However, fresh Call writing near 23,733 suggests some call sellers anticipate a cap around that level.

For Sensex, both the heaviest Call OI and Put OI were concentrated around 74,500. This implies a potential consolidation zone, with option writers expecting the index to hover around this level. The heaviest fresh Put writing was noted near 74,400, indicating confidence in a floor close to the current spot.

Bank Nifty saw its heaviest Put OI at 57,500, a fair distance below the spot, suggesting a strong foundational support. The heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near 57,066, pointing to a potential resistance. Interestingly, fresh Put writing was also significant at 55,500, further solidifying lower support levels.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely watched indicator that compares the volume or open interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests a more bullish sentiment from option buyers or Put writers, as more Puts are being bought/sold than Calls. A PCR below 1, conversely, indicates a more bearish sentiment.

For the Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stood at 1.06, which suggests a largely balanced book with a slight lean towards bullishness. However, the PCR based on today's fresh flow of Open Interest (ΔPCR) was significantly higher at 2.51. This strong surge in fresh Put writing compared to Call writing today indicates that option writers are aggressively building support, expecting the Nifty to either hold or move higher.

The Sensex recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.22, also pointing to a relatively balanced to mildly bullish positioning. Its ΔPCR for today was 1.29, suggesting that fresh option writing was also slightly skewed towards Puts, contributing to a mildly bullish flow.

Bank Nifty's PCR(OI) was 1.03, indicating a balanced options book. However, its ΔPCR for today was -0.8, which, despite the negative number, suggests a mixed or balanced fresh flow rather than a strong directional bias from new writing.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is a concept in options trading that refers to the strike price at which the maximum number of options (both Calls and Puts) would expire worthless, causing the least collective financial loss for option writers (sellers). As expiry approaches, the underlying asset's price often tends to gravitate towards this "Max Pain" strike, acting like a magnetic pull.

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain strike is at 23,300, which is very close to the current spot price of 23,346.40. This suggests that option writers are positioned for the Nifty to potentially settle around this level.

The Sensex's Max Pain is at 74,500, also quite close to its spot of 74,294.96. This indicates a similar gravitational pull towards 74,500 as expiry draws near.

For the Bank Nifty, the Max Pain strike is at 57,000. This is approximately 1.1% above its current spot of 56,358.70, suggesting that option writers might be anticipating an upward movement towards this level as the expiry date approaches.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals daily can empower you to interpret market sentiment. You can begin by visiting the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, specifically the "Derivatives" section. Look for "Option Chain" data for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex. Here, you'll find the Open Interest (OI) for various Call and Put strikes. Identify the strikes with the highest OI for both Calls and Puts to understand the immediate resistance and support levels.

To gauge the day's fresh activity, look at the "Change in Open Interest" column. Significant increases in Call OI at a particular strike suggest new call writing (bearish for that level), while increases in Put OI suggest new put writing (bullish for that level). You can also calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI. For FII positioning, the NSE publishes a daily "Participant-wise Open Interest" report under the "Reports" section, which details FII activity in futures and options. Combining these data points can help you form a more informed perspective on market positioning and potential tendencies.
