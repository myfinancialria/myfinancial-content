## Decoding today's open interest
Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts that have not yet been settled. The strike price with the highest Call OI typically acts as a significant resistance level, as Call option writers (sellers) expect the market to stay below it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI often serves as a strong support level, as Put option writers anticipate the market will remain above that point.

Today, Nifty saw the heaviest fresh Call writing (new Call options sold) near 22,550, above the spot price, suggesting potential resistance. Significant fresh Put writing (new Put options sold) was observed near 22,050, below the spot, indicating a potential floor. For Sensex, notable fresh Call writing occurred around 73,266, positioning this as a potential upside cap. Bank Nifty had fresh Call writing near 55,833, suggesting resistance, alongside fresh Put writing near 54,300, which could form a support base.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering
The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is an indicator that compares the number of outstanding Put options to Call options. A PCR(OI) value below 1, with more Call options open, is often seen as bearish (Call writers expect prices to fall). A value above 1, with more Put options open, can be bullish (Put writers expect prices to rise). The PCR(today flow ΔOI) reflects the sentiment from new positions added during the day.

Nifty 50's PCR(OI) stood at 0.59, indicating Call options dominate the open interest, which historically suggests capped upside. Its PCR(today flow ΔOI) was -0.4, showing a mixed flow of new positions. Sensex recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.74 and a PCR(today flow ΔOI) of -0.02, both pointing to a relatively balanced book and flow for the day. Bank Nifty's PCR(OI) was 0.93 and its PCR(today flow ΔOI) was -0.19, also indicating balanced overall open interest and fresh positioning.

## Max pain and the magnet effect
"Max Pain" is the strike price where option writers (sellers) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that level. It often acts as a 'magnet' for the index, especially as expiry approaches, as option writers collectively aim to minimise their losses or maximise profits.

Nifty 50's Max Pain was at 22,700, approximately 1.5% above its current spot price, suggesting a potential upward pull from option writers. Sensex's Max Pain was 72,500, about 0.8% above its spot, also indicating a possible upward bias. Bank Nifty's Max Pain at 55,500, about 1.1% above its spot, further aligns with a potential upward drift. This "magnet effect" strengthens closer to expiry.

## How to read this yourself daily
To interpret market sentiment daily, you can regularly review key data points. Start by visiting the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, specifically the "Live Equity Market" section, to access the option chain data for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty. Identify the strike prices with the highest "Open Interest" for both Call and Put options; these represent significant resistance and support levels, respectively.

Next, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR below 1 often indicates more Call writing (bearish), while above 1 suggests more Put writing (bullish). For "Max Pain," some financial websites and broker platforms provide this calculation directly, showing the strike where option writers would incur the least loss. Finally, review FII derivative statistics from NSE archives ("Participant-wise Open Interest" CSV) for institutional positioning. Consistent observation of these metrics can help you understand collective market sentiment.
