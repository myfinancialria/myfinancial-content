# India F&O Pulse — 09 Sep 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Option data today points to a cautious undertone for Nifty and Sensex, despite some underlying support for Bank Nifty.

**TL;DR**
*   Nifty 50's option data suggests a mildly bearish outlook, with fresh Call writing indicating potential resistance above current levels.
*   Sensex also appears mildly bearish, as significant Call writing and a low Put-Call Ratio point to capped upside.
*   Bank Nifty, however, shows a mildly bullish bias, with Max Pain and Put option support suggesting potential for upward movement.

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) provides a snapshot of the total number of outstanding Call and Put option contracts that have not yet been settled. The highest Call Open Interest at a particular strike price suggests that many Call option sellers (also known as 'writers') believe the index is unlikely to move above that level, effectively acting as a resistance. Conversely, the highest Put OI indicates a level where many Put option writers expect the index to find support.

For Nifty 50, today's heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near the 23,600 strike. This suggests that Call sellers anticipate the index might find it challenging to move significantly above this level in the near term, acting as a potential ceiling. On the Put side, fresh writing was concentrated near 23,200, indicating that Put sellers view this as a potential floor for the index. The aggregate flow for Nifty showed a net Call OI build of 56.2 million contracts, significantly higher than the 26.1 million Put OI build, reinforcing the cautious sentiment from Call writers.

Sensex saw its heaviest fresh Call writing near the 75,200 strike, implying that option writers believe the upside for Sensex could be capped around this level. Fresh Put writing was notable near 73,333, suggesting a base of support. The overall flow for Sensex also showed a dominance of Call writing, with a net Call OI build of 23.6 million contracts against 7.9 million Put OI build.

Bank Nifty's fresh Call writing was most prominent near 56,700, potentially marking a resistance level. However, a significant Put base was observed at 57,500, which is currently above the spot price, indicating strong support expectations from Put writers at that level.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio, or PCR, is a popular indicator that helps gauge overall market sentiment by comparing the total Open Interest (OI) of Put options to Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests a bullish bias (more Put writing than Call writing, implying support), while a PCR below 1 often indicates a bearish bias (more Call writing than Put writing, implying resistance).

For Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stood at 0.71, which is considered a balanced book, not leaning strongly in either direction. However, the PCR based on today's fresh flow (ΔPCR) was 0.46, indicating that today's new option writing was significantly skewed towards Calls. This combination suggests that while the overall book might be balanced, the fresh activity today pointed towards a more cautious or bearish sentiment.

Sensex presented a more bearish picture with a PCR(OI) of 0.6, suggesting that Call options dominated the outstanding contracts, potentially capping upside movement. The ΔPCR for Sensex was even lower at 0.34, reinforcing that today's fresh writing was heavily concentrated in Calls, indicating a strong bearish sentiment from option writers.

Bank Nifty had a PCR(OI) of 0.97, which is close to 1, suggesting a relatively balanced book. The ΔPCR for Bank Nifty was -0.13, indicating a mixed or slightly Put-heavy fresh flow, which aligns with a neutral to mildly bullish undertone for the day.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is a concept that suggests the index tends to gravitate towards the strike price where the highest number of options (both Calls and Puts) would expire worthless, causing maximum financial pain to option writers. As expiry approaches, this level can act like a magnet, pulling the spot price towards it.

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain strike is at 23,800, which is approximately 1.6% above its current spot price of 23,431.50. This suggests that the "pin pull" from Max Pain is currently pointing upwards.

Sensex's Max Pain is located at 75,900, about 1.5% above its spot price of 74,764.23. This also indicates an upward pull from the Max Pain perspective.

Bank Nifty's Max Pain is at 57,500, a significant 2.1% above its current spot of 56,295.55. This suggests a strong upward pull from the Max Pain level. It's important to remember that the magnetic effect of Max Pain strengthens as the weekly or monthly expiry draws closer.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment. Today, FIIs held a relatively low long ratio in Index futures, standing at 11.0% (39,027 long contracts versus 315,277 short contracts). Historically, such a low long ratio in index futures has tended to coincide with periods of market caution or consolidation, as FIIs are net short on the index.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a more balanced position, with a long ratio of 54.4% (3,454,240 long contracts versus 2,889,772 short contracts). This suggests a more neutral to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks compared to their bearish bias on the broader indices.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market dynamics. You can start by looking at the Open Interest data, which is freely available on the NSE website under the "Derivatives" section. Specifically, look for the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex. Here, you can identify the strike prices with the highest Call OI and Put OI, which represent potential resistance and support levels, respectively.

Next, track the daily change in Open Interest (ΔOI) for both Calls and Puts. A significant increase in Call OI at a particular strike, especially above the current spot price, suggests fresh Call writing and potential resistance. Conversely, a rise in Put OI below the spot price indicates fresh Put writing and potential support. Calculating the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) from total OI and from today's ΔOI can give you a quick sentiment check. Finally, observing FII derivative positioning, also available on the NSE website, provides insight into how large institutional players are positioning themselves. Combining these data points can help you form a more informed perspective on the market's likely direction.
