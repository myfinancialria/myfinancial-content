# India F&O Pulse — 24 Sep 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options contracts provides crucial insights into market sentiment and potential support and resistance levels. The strike price with the highest Call OI typically acts as a resistance level, as many participants have sold Call options at that price, expecting the market to not rise above it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI often serves as a support level, indicating where many participants have sold Put options, anticipating the market will not fall below that point.

For the NIFTY 50, the heaviest Put OI is currently observed at the 23,000 strike, which is very close to today's closing spot price. This suggests a strong foundational support is being built around this level. On the Call side, the 24,000 strike holds the highest Call OI, indicating a significant resistance zone further up. Today's trading saw fresh Call writing, meaning new Call options were sold, particularly around the 23,250 level. This activity suggests that some market participants expect the index to face a cap around this region. Simultaneously, fresh Put writing was notable near 22,716, implying that some Put sellers anticipate the index to find a floor around this lower level.

In the BANK NIFTY, the highest Put OI is currently at 57,500, which is considerably above the current spot price, suggesting that option writers are positioned for a potential rebound or have adjusted their positions. The highest Call OI is also seen at 57,500, indicating a strong resistance zone at that level. Today, the heaviest fresh Call writing occurred near 56,166, suggesting that Call sellers believe the index might struggle to move past this point in the near term.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator derived from options open interest. It is calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1.0 generally suggests that more Put options have been written than Call options, which can be interpreted as a potentially bullish signal as Put writers expect the market to rise or remain stable. Conversely, a PCR below 1.0 indicates more Call writing, often seen as a potentially bearish signal.

For the NIFTY 50, the PCR based on Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.72. This value, being below 1.0, suggests a slightly higher inclination towards Call writing compared to Put writing in the overall options book, indicating a somewhat balanced to mildly cautious sentiment. The change in PCR from today's flow (ΔPCR) was -0.02, which is a minor adjustment, suggesting the day's option activity was relatively balanced in terms of new Call and Put writing.

The BANK NIFTY's PCR(OI) is at 0.86, also below 1.0, indicating a similar sentiment of slightly more Call writing in the overall open interest. The ΔPCR for Bank Nifty was -0.31, a more significant negative shift, implying that today's option activity saw a relatively stronger emphasis on fresh Call writing compared to Put writing, potentially reflecting some immediate bearish sentiment from option writers.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the least financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that specific price. As an options expiry date approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this "max pain" strike, much like a magnet, as option writers try to manage their positions to minimise potential losses.

For the NIFTY 50, the max pain strike is currently at 23,500. This is approximately 1.9% above the current spot price of 23,063.10. The fact that max pain is significantly higher than the spot price suggests that options writers, on balance, are positioned for a potential upward movement or consolidation above current levels by expiry.

The BANK NIFTY's max pain is located at 56,600, which is about 2.1% above its spot price of 55,438.50. Similar to Nifty, this positioning indicates that option writers might be expecting the Bank Nifty to move higher towards this level as expiry draws closer.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 11.0%. This means that for every 100 index futures contracts held by FIIs, only 11 were long (buy positions) while 89 were short (sell positions). A low long ratio like this indicates a significant net short position in index futures by FIIs. Historically, a consistently low FII index futures long ratio has tended to coincide with periods of caution or consolidation in the broader market.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of 53.7%, with 3,470,181 long contracts against 2,988,101 short contracts. This ratio suggests a more balanced to slightly bullish stance by FIIs in individual stock futures, indicating selective buying interest in specific companies.

## What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we synthesise the various signals from today's options and futures data, a nuanced picture emerges. Despite the market correction seen today, the options data for both Nifty and Bank Nifty lean towards a mildly bullish outlook. However, the FIIs' substantial net short positioning in index futures presents a counter-signal, as large institutional selling in futures has historically tended to coincide with periods of market weakness or consolidation. The fresh Call writing observed today also indicates some immediate resistance being built. Therefore, while the options structure suggests an underlying mild bullish bias, the FII positioning and fresh Call writing indicate that any upward movement might face resistance, and the market could exhibit volatility.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to form your own informed perspective. You can access options data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" for Nifty and Bank Nifty. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in a user-friendly format.

When analysing, look for the strike prices with the highest Open Interest for both Calls and Puts – these are your key support and resistance levels. Also, observe the "Change in Open Interest" column to identify where fresh buying or selling (writing) activity is concentrated for the day. Calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI. Track the "Max Pain" level, which is often available on analytical platforms or can be calculated by summing the intrinsic value losses for all options at various strike prices. By combining these data points, you can build a comprehensive view of how market participants are positioned and what the collective sentiment might be for the upcoming sessions.
