## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides insights into the market's sentiment and potential support and resistance levels.

When we look at the option chain, the strike price with the **highest Call OI** is often considered a significant resistance level. This is because a large number of Call options at that strike means many traders have sold (written) these calls, expecting the underlying index not to rise above that level. For Nifty, the heaviest Call OI is at 24600, which is just below the current spot. For Sensex, it's at 79000, slightly above spot. Bank Nifty shows the heaviest Call OI at 58000, which is below its current spot, indicating that call writers were caught off guard by today's upward move or are positioning for a pullback.

Conversely, the strike price with the **highest Put OI** is typically seen as a strong support level. A high Put OI indicates that many traders have sold (written) these puts, believing the index will not fall below that level. Nifty's heaviest Put OI is at 24200, Sensex's at 78000, and Bank Nifty's at 58000.

Today's fresh writing, which refers to the new contracts added during the session, gives us a real-time view of where option sellers are placing their bets.
For **Nifty**, the heaviest fresh Call (CE) writing was observed near 24683, suggesting that call sellers expect the index to face a ceiling around this level. On the other hand, the heaviest fresh Put (PE) writing was near 24550, indicating that put sellers anticipate a floor forming around this point. The aggregate flow for Nifty showed a significantly larger build-up in Put OI (36.1M) compared to Call OI (3.1M) today, reinforcing the idea of put writers actively defending lower levels.

For **SENSEX**, fresh Call writing was concentrated near 79233, implying a potential cap above the current spot. Fresh Put writing was notable near 78066, suggesting a floor below the current spot. The overall flow saw more Put OI (3.7M) added than Call OI (1.7M), leaning towards a bullish bias from fresh positioning.

In **BANK NIFTY**, fresh Call writing was heaviest near 58166, close to the spot, while fresh Put writing was seen near 57766, below the spot. The aggregate flow also indicated more Put OI (0.9M) addition than Call OI (0.2M), aligning with the other indices in terms of fresh put-heavy activity.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a popular indicator that compares the number of traded put options to call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests a more bullish sentiment, as more puts are being bought or written, often implying expectations of support. A PCR below 1 typically indicates a more bearish sentiment, with calls dominating.

For **Nifty 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 1.33. This value, being well above 1, suggests that option writers have a significant number of put options open compared to calls, often interpreted as a robust support base. The PCR based on today's fresh flow (ΔPCR) is a very high 11.76, indicating that today's new option writing was overwhelmingly put-heavy, further strengthening the perception of downside protection.

The **SENSEX** recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.04, which is slightly above 1, pointing to a relatively balanced book with a mild bullish tilt. Its ΔPCR for today was 2.19, showing that fresh writing also leaned towards puts, suggesting confidence in current or higher levels.

**BANK NIFTY** has a PCR(OI) of 0.69, which is below 1. This suggests that call options dominate the overall open interest, often interpreted as a potential cap on the upside. However, its ΔPCR for today was 4.53, indicating that fresh writing was significantly put-heavy, similar to Nifty and Sensex. This divergence between the overall PCR(OI) and today's flow suggests that while the existing structure might be call-heavy, new positions are being built with a put-heavy bias, possibly anticipating a bounce or defending current levels.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max pain" is a concept in options trading that refers to the strike price at which the maximum number of options will expire worthless, causing the greatest financial loss for option buyers and the greatest profit for option sellers (writers). As expiry approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this "max pain" strike, acting like a magnet.

For **Nifty 50**, the max pain strike is at 24400, which is about 1.5% below its current spot of 24,774.30. This suggests that option writers, on aggregate, would benefit most if Nifty were to close closer to 24400 at expiry.

**SENSEX** has its max pain strike at 78500, which is very close to its current spot of 78,639.03. When max pain is near the spot price, it often indicates that the index might consolidate around these levels as expiry draws closer.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the max pain strike is 58000, which is about 0.4% below its current spot of 58,247.95. Similar to Nifty, this suggests a potential downward pull towards 58000 as expiry approaches, aligning with the heavy Call OI at that level.

It's important to remember that the "magnet effect" of max pain becomes stronger as the weekly or monthly expiry date draws nearer.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides clues about broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held a **13.0% long ratio in Index futures**. This means that out of their total open positions in index futures, only 13.0% were long contracts, while the remaining 87.0% were short. Historically, a relatively low long ratio in index futures from FIIs has tended to coincide with a cautious or even bearish outlook from this segment of the market. It suggests that the "big money" players are predominantly positioned on the short side in index futures.

In **Stock futures**, FIIs maintained a long ratio of **55.1%**. This indicates a slightly bullish stance in individual stock futures, with more long positions than short.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding the pulse of the market through derivatives data can be a powerful skill. You can access much of this information directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section. Look for the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex. Here, you'll find the Open Interest (OI) for various Call and Put strikes.

To calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR), you'll need to sum up the total Put OI and total Call OI for all strikes and then divide the former by the latter. For today's flow, you'd look at the change in OI (ΔOI) for puts and calls. Many brokers also provide this data in a more digestible format through their trading platforms or analytical tools. By regularly tracking these key data points – total OI, fresh OI changes, and the PCR – you can start to identify patterns in how option writers are positioning themselves and what that might imply for potential support and resistance levels in the market.
