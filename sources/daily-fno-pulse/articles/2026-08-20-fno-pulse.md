# India F&O Pulse — 20 Aug 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Indian Indices Close with a Mildly Bullish Tone as Option Writers Build Support

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides insight into the total number of positions held by market participants.

When we look at **Call Open Interest (Call OI)**, the strike with the highest OI often acts as a resistance level. This is where call option writers (sellers) have taken positions, expecting the underlying asset's price not to rise above this point. Conversely, the strike with the highest **Put Open Interest (Put OI)** typically serves as a support level, indicating where put option writers expect the price not to fall below.

For the **NIFTY 50**, the heaviest Call OI wall stands at 24,500, while the strongest Put OI base is at 24,000. Today's fresh Call writing was most concentrated near 24,550, suggesting that some call sellers anticipate the index might find a cap around these higher levels. On the Put side, the heaviest fresh Put writing was observed near 24,116, indicating that put sellers are building a floor close to the current spot price.

The **SENSEX** saw its heaviest Call OI wall at 77,600 and its strongest Put OI base at 77,500. Fresh Call writing today was most active near 77,700, implying some market participants expect the upward movement to be capped around that level. Simultaneously, significant fresh Put writing occurred near 77,400, reinforcing the idea of a floor being established below the current spot.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the most substantial Call OI wall is at 58,000, with the heaviest Put OI base at 57,000. Today's fresh Call writing was most prominent near 57,733, suggesting that call sellers expect a ceiling around this strike. However, notable fresh Put writing was seen near 57,400, indicating that put sellers are establishing support around the current levels.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator in the options market. It is calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1.0 generally suggests that more puts have been written than calls, which is often interpreted as a bullish signal by option writers who expect prices to rise or remain stable. Conversely, a PCR below 1.0 indicates more calls have been written, potentially suggesting a bearish sentiment.

For the **NIFTY 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 1.03, suggesting a relatively balanced options book with a slight tilt towards put writing. However, the PCR based on today's change in Open Interest (PCR(today flow ΔOI)) was -1.11, indicating a mixed or balanced flow of fresh writing during the session.

The **SENSEX** recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.08, also pointing to a balanced book with a slight put-heavy bias. More significantly, its PCR(today flow ΔOI) was 3.63, which means today's fresh option writing was heavily skewed towards puts, often interpreted as a bullish signal as put sellers expect prices to hold or rise.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the PCR(OI) is 0.79, suggesting a slightly call-heavy options book. Its PCR(today flow ΔOI) was -0.99, indicating a mixed or balanced flow of new positions taken today.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the options were to expire at that particular price. This strike often acts like a "magnet" for the underlying asset's price, particularly as the expiry date draws closer. Option writers, who typically hold larger positions, may try to manage their exposures such that the price converges towards this point.

For the **NIFTY 50**, the Max Pain strike is at 24,250, which is very close to its spot close of 24,231.85. This suggests that option writers are currently positioned for the index to hover around these levels.

The **SENSEX** also has its Max Pain at 77,500, nearly identical to its spot close of 77,537.72. This indicates a similar positioning by option writers for the Sensex to expire close to its current price.

Interestingly, for **BANK NIFTY**, the Max Pain strike is at 58,000, which is approximately +0.9% above its spot close of 57,495.90. This positioning suggests a potential upward pull for the Bank Nifty towards the 58,000 level as expiry approaches.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment. Looking at the FII derivative positioning data from the NSE, their stance in Index futures continues to be cautious.

FIIs held a long ratio of **9.3%** in Index futures today, meaning that for every 100 contracts, only about 9 were long positions, while the remaining were short. Specifically, they held 24,335 long contracts against 236,448 short contracts. Historically, such a low long ratio in Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or slightly bearish outlook from this segment of large investors.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a more balanced to mildly positive stance, with a long ratio of **55.1%**. This translates to 3,630,708 long contracts versus 2,964,410 short contracts. This suggests a more optimistic view on individual stocks compared to the broader index.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can empower you to interpret market sentiment daily. You can access options Open Interest data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the 'Derivatives' section, specifically the 'Option Chain' for NIFTY, BANK NIFTY, and FINNIFTY. Most brokerage platforms also provide this information in an easily digestible format.

When reviewing the option chain, focus on the strikes with the highest Call OI and Put OI. These indicate significant resistance and support levels, respectively. Also, pay attention to the 'Change in Open Interest' column to identify where fresh positions are being built, which can signal new conviction from option writers. Calculating the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI gives you a quick snapshot of overall market sentiment. Remember, these are tools for understanding market positioning, not for making direct trading decisions.
