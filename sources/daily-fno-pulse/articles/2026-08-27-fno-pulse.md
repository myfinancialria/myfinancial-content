# India F&O Pulse — 27 Aug 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

**Nifty and Bank Nifty show signs of potential resistance, while Sensex appears to have a more balanced to mildly bullish setup for the upcoming session.**

**TL;DR**
*   **NIFTY 50:** The options data suggests a mildly bearish outlook, with significant call writing indicating potential resistance around current levels and a cap on upside movement.
*   **SENSEX:** The positioning in Sensex appears more balanced to mildly bullish, with a higher Max Pain point and fresh put writing suggesting some underlying support.
*   **BANK NIFTY:** Similar to Nifty, Bank Nifty's options data points to a mildly bearish sentiment, with strong call writing at or near the current spot price suggesting immediate resistance.

## What the indices did today

The **NIFTY 50** closed the day at 24,090.85, down 0.48%. It ended just below its heaviest Call Open Interest (OI) wall at 24,300 and slightly above its strongest Put OI base at 24,000. This positioning suggests the index found some support at the lower end of its implied range but faced resistance as it tried to move higher.

The **SENSEX** saw a decline of 0.70%, settling at 76,933.59. The index closed within its option-chain implied range of 75,000 to 78,000, suggesting that while there was a downward move, the broader range held. The overall positioning indicates a relatively contained trading environment for the day.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the index finished at 57,509.95, a decrease of 0.47%. Interestingly, both the heaviest Call OI and Put OI walls are clustered right at the 57,500 strike. This indicates that the index closed precisely at a level where both buyers and sellers of options are strongly positioned, suggesting a tight battle for direction.

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been settled. On an option chain, the strike price with the highest Call OI is often considered a significant resistance level, as many participants have sold Call options at that strike, expecting the price to not rise above it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI is typically seen as a strong support level, as many have sold Put options there, anticipating the price will not fall below it. These "walls" represent areas where option writers, or sellers, have taken significant positions.

Today, for the NIFTY 50, the heaviest Call OI wall stands at 24,300, which is about 0.9% above the spot price. This suggests that option writers are collectively positioning for the Nifty to face considerable resistance if it attempts to move towards this level. The strongest Put OI base is at 24,000, just 0.4% below spot, indicating a potential floor. Today's fresh writing in Nifty was heavily skewed towards Calls, with a net Call OI build of 56.4 million contracts significantly outweighing the 14.8 million Put OI build. This fresh selling of Calls further reinforces the idea of a potential ceiling.

In SENSEX, the implied range is wider, between 75,000 and 78,000. Today's heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near 78,166, suggesting that option sellers believe the upside might be capped around this level. On the Put side, the heaviest fresh writing was near 76,000, indicating that put sellers are expecting this level to act as a floor. This balanced fresh writing suggests a tug-of-war between bullish and bearish sentiments within a defined range.

For BANK NIFTY, the most significant Call OI and Put OI are both concentrated at the 57,500 strike, right at the day's closing price. This suggests a very tight range with strong opinions on both sides. Fresh Call writing today was heaviest near 57,800, indicating that option sellers are positioning for a cap around this slightly higher level. The aggregate flow also showed a net Call OI build of 0.9 million contracts, more than double the Put OI build of 0.4 million, pointing towards a cautious stance among option writers.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that helps gauge market sentiment. It's calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest (PCR-OI), or by dividing the volume of Puts traded by Calls traded (PCR-Volume). A PCR value below 1.0 generally suggests that more Call options are being written or traded than Put options, which can be interpreted as a bearish signal, as Call writers expect prices to not rise. Conversely, a PCR above 1.0 often indicates more Put writing, which can be seen as a bullish signal, as Put writers expect prices to not fall.

For **NIFTY 50**, the PCR(OI) stands at 0.56, which is significantly below 0.7. This low ratio indicates a dominance of Call options in the open interest, suggesting that option writers are collectively positioned for the upside to be capped. The PCR(today's flow ΔOI) is even lower at 0.26, meaning that today's fresh option writing was heavily skewed towards Calls. This combination strongly suggests a bearish undertone, with fresh positions reinforcing the existing resistance.

The **SENSEX** shows a PCR(OI) of 1.01, which is very close to 1.0. This indicates a relatively balanced book between Put and Call open interest, suggesting a neutral to slightly bullish sentiment from a broader perspective. The PCR(today's flow ΔOI) is 0.8, which is also relatively balanced, leaning slightly towards Call writing but not overwhelmingly so. This suggests a mixed or balanced flow of fresh positions for the day.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the PCR(OI) is 1.03, indicating a balanced book of outstanding Put and Call options, similar to Sensex. However, the PCR(today's flow ΔOI) is 0.45, which is quite low. This suggests that today's fresh option writing was predominantly in Call options, indicating a more cautious or bearish sentiment from new positions taken during the day.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum cumulative loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It's often referred to as a "magnet effect" because, as an expiry date approaches, the underlying index or stock tends to gravitate towards this Max Pain strike. This is because option writers, who typically hold larger positions, would benefit most if the price closes near this level, and they often try to manage their positions to achieve this outcome.

Today, the Max Pain for **NIFTY 50** is at 24,500, which is about 1.7% above the current spot price of 24,090.85. This suggests that if the "magnet effect" were to play out, the index might see an upward pull towards this level by expiry.

For **SENSEX**, the Max Pain is at 77,500, approximately 0.7% above its spot price of 76,933.59. This also indicates a potential upward pull towards the Max Pain strike.

**BANK NIFTY** has its Max Pain at 57,700, which is about 0.3% above its spot price of 57,509.95. This relatively close proximity suggests a mild upward pull, but also indicates that the index is already quite near the level where option writers would be most comfortable.

It's important to remember that the Max Pain theory becomes more influential as the expiry date draws nearer, as option writers' positions become more sensitive to the closing price.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, the FII Index futures long ratio stands at a low 9.8%. This means that out of their total open positions in index futures, only 9.8% are long contracts, while a substantial 90.2% are short contracts (24,190 long contracts versus 221,982 short contracts). Historically, such a heavily net short positioning by FIIs in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from these large institutional players on the broader market.

In Stock futures, the FII long ratio is 54.3%, with 3,420,712 long contracts against 2,879,342 short contracts. This indicates a slight net long position in individual stock futures, suggesting a more balanced or marginally positive view on specific stocks, even as their index futures positioning remains heavily bearish.

## Stocks where money moved today

In the F&O segment, some stocks saw notable price and volume movements today.

Among the top gainers were **ADANIENT**, which rose by 1.83% to ₹3169.00, and **KOTAKBANK**, which gained 1.80% to ₹424.20, with a significant volume of over 3.29 crore shares. **ICICIBANK** also moved up by 0.91% to ₹1443.00, while **TITAN** increased by 0.77% to ₹5139.30. **AXISBANK** saw a marginal gain of 0.08% to ₹1256.00.

On the losing side, **HDFCBANK** experienced a notable decline of 2.23% to ₹711.00, accompanied by a very high volume of over 4.65 crore shares. **BHARTIARTL** was down 1.25% at ₹1878.30, and **RELIANCE** fell by 1.22% to ₹1282.20. **TCS** decreased by 0.95% to ₹2248.40, and **SBIN** closed lower by 0.87% at ₹1042.90. These movements highlight specific pockets of strength and weakness within the broader market.

## What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we look at the combined signals from today's options and futures data, a nuanced picture emerges for the upcoming session. The Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty both show a mildly bearish bias, primarily driven by low PCR values and significant fresh call writing, suggesting that option writers are positioning for potential resistance and limited upside. Historically, such a setup has tended to coincide with days where these indices consolidate or face downward pressure.

In contrast, the Sensex displays a more balanced to mildly bullish outlook, with its PCR closer to neutral and Max Pain indicating an upward pull. This suggests that while there might be broader market caution, the Sensex could show relative resilience or even attempt to move higher within its defined range.

The FII positioning, with a heavily net short stance in index futures, historically tends to coincide with a cautious sentiment from institutional investors. When FIIs are net short to this extent, the broader market has often experienced periods of consolidation or downward drift.

Overall, the data suggests that the market might face headwinds, particularly for Nifty and Bank Nifty, with strong resistance levels being established by option writers. However, the Sensex might offer some pockets of stability. It is important to remember that these are interpretations of positioning data and not predictions of future price movements.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals daily can empower you to better comprehend market dynamics. You can access the raw data from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, specifically the "Option Chain" section for index options and the "FII Derivatives" report for institutional positioning. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in an easily digestible format.

To interpret it yourself, start by looking at the Option Chain for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty. Identify the strike prices with the highest Call Open Interest (resistance) and Put Open Interest (support). Then, check the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) for overall sentiment. A low PCR (below 1, especially below 0.7) often indicates bearishness, while a high PCR (above 1, especially above 1.3) can suggest bullishness. Also, note the Max Pain strike and its distance from the current spot price. Finally, review the FII derivative data to see how large institutional players are positioned in index futures; a high net short position often suggests caution. Combining these data points can provide a holistic view of market participants' collective expectations and positioning for the near term.
