# India F&O Pulse — 09 Nov 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

*   **Nifty 50:** The options data suggests the index is likely to remain range-bound, with open interest indicating strong walls on both sides.
*   **Sensex:** Fresh option writing points towards a mildly bearish sentiment, with Call writers expecting a cap on upward movement.
*   **Bank Nifty:** Significant fresh Put writing and overall Put Open Interest build-up indicate a mildly bullish bias, suggesting market participants anticipate support at lower levels.

The **Bank Nifty** was the strongest performer among the major indices, surging 1.51% to close at 59,741.25. This strong move placed it above its ATM strike of 59700, though it still sits below the heaviest Call Open Interest wall at 58000, suggesting potential resistance ahead.

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides insights into market sentiment and potential support and resistance levels. The strike with the highest Call OI often acts as a significant resistance level, meaning many market participants expect the price to struggle to move above it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI typically serves as a strong support level, where many expect the price to find a floor.

For **Nifty 50**, the option chain indicates substantial Call Open Interest around 26000 and significant Put Open Interest around 24000. These strikes effectively form the upper and lower boundaries that option writers are currently positioned for, suggesting a broad range for the index.

In **Sensex**, the heaviest fresh Call writing today was observed near the 82666 strike. This suggests that Call sellers, often referred to as 'writers', are actively taking positions, implying they expect the index's upward movement to be capped around these levels. Conversely, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 79500, indicating that Put sellers anticipate the index will find support there.

For **Bank Nifty**, the aggregate flow for the day showed a significant net build-up in Put Open Interest (1.1 million contracts) compared to Call Open Interest (0.2 million contracts). This suggests that more participants are selling Puts, expecting the index to either rise or stay above these levels. Specifically, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 59666, which aligns closely with the day's closing price, indicating strong immediate support expectations.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests a bullish sentiment as there are more Puts outstanding than Calls, implying more participants are betting on a rise or hedging against a fall. A value below 1 generally indicates a bearish sentiment. We also look at the PCR based on today's change in Open Interest (ΔOI) to gauge fresh money flow.

For **Nifty 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.84, which is considered a balanced book, indicating neither a strong bullish nor bearish bias from the overall outstanding positions. Today's flow, as reflected by the ΔPCR of 1.02, also suggests a mixed or balanced fresh activity in the options market.

The **Sensex** has a PCR(OI) of 0.78, again suggesting a relatively balanced book. However, today's flow ΔPCR is 0.68, which is lower than 1, indicating that fresh writing activity was predominantly in Calls. This implies that more new participants are selling Calls, expecting the index to not rise significantly, contributing to a mildly bearish flow.

**Bank Nifty** shows a PCR(OI) of 0.85, which is also in the balanced territory. Crucially, its ΔPCR for today's flow is a high 5.59. This significantly elevated value suggests that today's fresh writing was heavily skewed towards Puts, with many participants selling Puts, anticipating an upward move or strong support for the index.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It's often considered a "magnet" for the index, especially as expiry approaches, as option writers tend to steer the market towards this point to maximise their profits or minimise losses.

For **Nifty 50**, the Max Pain strike is at 25000, which is very close to its closing spot price of 25,044.65. This suggests that option writers are currently positioned for the index to hover around these levels by expiry.

The **Sensex** also has its Max Pain strike at 81100, almost exactly where it closed today at 81,106.65. This alignment reinforces the idea of a potential expiry around current levels for Sensex.

However, for **Bank Nifty**, the Max Pain strike is at 58900, which is about 1.4% below its current spot price of 59,741.25. While Max Pain can act as a magnet, especially closer to expiry, its position below the current spot suggests that if the "pin pull" effect were to play out, it would imply a downward drift towards this level.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer clues about broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 26.8%, meaning only 26.8% of their index futures positions were long, while a larger portion (73.2%) were short. Historically, a lower FII Index futures long ratio has tended to coincide with a cautious or slightly bearish outlook from these large institutional players.

In contrast, their Stock futures long ratio stood at 58.4%, indicating a more balanced to mildly bullish stance on individual stocks in the futures segment. This suggests FIIs might be more selective in their stock-specific bets while maintaining a cautious approach on the broader index.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily F&O data. To start, you can visit the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website (nseindia.com) and look for the "Derivatives" section. Here, you'll find the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and other indices.

On the option chain, pay attention to the "Open Interest" column for both Calls and Puts. The strike prices with the highest OI on the Call side indicate potential resistance, while those with the highest OI on the Put side suggest potential support. You can also track the "Change in OI" to see where fresh money is flowing. To calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR), sum up the total Put OI and divide it by the total Call OI. For FII data, look for the "Participant-wise Open Interest" reports on the NSE website, typically found under the "Historical Data" or "Daily Reports" section. By observing these data points daily and understanding how they shift, you can begin to form your own informed perspective on market positioning.
