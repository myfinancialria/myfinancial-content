## Decoding today's open interest

**Open Interest (OI)** refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as Call or Put options, that have not yet been settled. It provides a measure of market participation and conviction at various price levels. The strike price with the highest Call OI typically acts as a significant resistance level, as many participants have sold (written) Call options at that strike, expecting the price not to rise above it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI usually serves as a strong support level, as many have written Put options there, anticipating the price will not fall below it.

Today, for the Nifty 50, the aggregate flow showed a net build-up of Call Open Interest (OI) at 47.8 million contracts, significantly higher than the 19.7 million in Put OI. This indicates that more participants were writing fresh Call options than Put options. The heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near the 24,350 strike, suggesting that Call sellers are positioning for a potential cap on upward movement around this level. On the Put side, the heaviest fresh writing was near 24,250, implying that Put sellers anticipate this level could act as a floor.

For the Sensex, today's heaviest fresh Call writing was near 78,000, indicating that option writers expect this level to act as a ceiling. Interestingly, the heaviest fresh Put writing was also observed at 76,333, suggesting that market participants are also establishing a floor at lower levels.

In the Bank Nifty, the heaviest fresh Call writing was seen near the 58,400 strike, where Call sellers are positioning for a potential resistance point.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely used indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR(OI) value above 1 typically suggests a bullish sentiment as there are more Put options open than Call options, implying that more market participants are selling Puts (expecting prices to rise). A value below 1, conversely, often indicates a bearish bias. The PCR(today's flow ΔOI) looks specifically at the change in Open Interest for the day, giving insight into fresh positioning.

For the Nifty 50, the PCR(OI) stood at 0.96, indicating a largely balanced book of existing positions. However, the PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was 0.41, a notably low figure. This suggests that today's fresh option writing was heavily skewed towards Call options, with significantly more Calls being written than Puts. Historically, such a low daily PCR has tended to coincide with market participants anticipating resistance or a potential downward bias.

The Sensex had a PCR(OI) of 0.87, also indicating a relatively balanced existing book. Its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was 0.84, suggesting a more mixed or balanced flow of fresh writing for the day compared to Nifty.

Bank Nifty's PCR(OI) was 0.82, showing a balanced existing options book. Its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) of 0.66 indicates that today's fresh writing was predominantly Call-heavy, similar to the Nifty, suggesting that option writers are establishing potential caps on upward movement.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is the strike price at which option writers would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It's often considered a magnetic point because, as expiry approaches, the underlying asset's price has historically tended to gravitate towards this level.

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain point is currently at 24,250, which is very close to the spot price of 24,270.85. When Max Pain is at or near the spot price, it often suggests a potential for the index to consolidate around current levels.

The Sensex's Max Pain is at 78,000, which is approximately +0.3% above its spot price of 77,763.91. This positioning of Max Pain above the current spot has historically tended to coincide with a slight upward pull on the index as expiry draws nearer.

Bank Nifty's Max Pain is at 58,500, which is about +1.0% above its spot price of 57,938.50. A Max Pain point significantly above the current spot can historically tend to suggest an upward bias or a potential for the index to move higher towards this level as expiry approaches.

A low FII Index futures long ratio, such as today's 9.6%, has historically tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish sentiment from these institutional players regarding the broader market indices.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market dynamics daily can empower you to interpret market sentiment. You can start by looking at the Open Interest data, which is freely available on the NSE website under the "Derivatives" section. Specifically, look for the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty. Identify the strike prices with the highest Call OI and Put OI; these act as your immediate resistance and support levels.

Next, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR). You can do this by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI for the entire option chain. Also, observe the change in OI for the day (ΔOI) to understand fresh positioning and calculate the daily PCR. Finally, track the FII derivative positioning, also available on the NSE website under "Participant-wise Open Interest." By combining these data points, you can form your own daily understanding of where option writers and large institutions are positioning themselves, offering a glimpse into potential market tendencies for the upcoming session.
