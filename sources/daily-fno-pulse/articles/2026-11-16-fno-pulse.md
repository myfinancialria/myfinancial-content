## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options contracts provides valuable insights into market participants' expectations. The strike with the highest Call OI typically represents a significant resistance level, as many call options have been sold at that price, indicating that writers believe the price is unlikely to rise above it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI often acts as a strong support level, as put options sold at this strike suggest writers expect the price to hold above it. These "walls" indicate where option writers are positioned, defining potential boundaries for the index.

For the **NIFTY 50**, today's heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near the 25516 strike. This suggests that call sellers anticipate the index might find resistance around this level, potentially capping further upward movement. On the Put side, the heaviest fresh Put writing was seen near 25150, indicating that put sellers are comfortable providing support around this strike, expecting the index to hold above it. The aggregate flow showed a significantly larger build in Put OI (29.5 million) compared to Call OI (4.4 million) today, reinforcing the idea of stronger support being built.

In the **SENSEX** options, the heaviest fresh Call writing occurred near 82766, implying that call writers expect the index to face a cap around this mark. Meanwhile, substantial fresh Put writing was noted near 80833, suggesting that put writers are establishing a floor at this level.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the heaviest fresh Call writing was concentrated near 62033, indicating that call sellers believe the index may struggle to move significantly higher than this point. On the other hand, fresh Put writing was most prominent near 60633, suggesting put writers are building a base of support at this level.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR(OI) value above 1 typically suggests more Put writing (often seen as bullish by option writers who sell puts expecting prices to rise or stay stable), while a value below 1 indicates more Call writing (often seen as bearish by option writers who sell calls expecting prices to fall or stay stable). PCR(today flow ΔOI) specifically looks at the change in OI for the current day, giving a snapshot of fresh activity.

The **NIFTY 50** had a PCR(OI) of 0.85, indicating a relatively balanced book with a slight leaning towards Call Open Interest. However, today's flow ΔPCR was significantly high at 6.7, suggesting that fresh writing activity was heavily dominated by Put options. This combination of a balanced overall book with strong fresh put writing leans towards a mildly bullish sentiment for the day.

For the **SENSEX**, the PCR(OI) stood at 0.88, also indicating a balanced options book. Today's flow ΔPCR was 1.5, suggesting that fresh writing saw more put options being written than call options. This points to a mildly bullish sentiment from today's option writers.

The **BANK NIFTY** recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.82, which is a balanced book. However, today's flow ΔPCR was negative at -372.55, which suggests a mixed or balanced fresh flow, not strongly favouring either puts or calls on the day.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is the strike price at which option writers would experience the minimum cumulative loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It's often considered a magnetic point, especially as expiry approaches, as the underlying index tends to gravitate towards this level.

For the **NIFTY 50**, the max pain point is at 25350, which is very close to today's spot close of 25,291.10. This proximity suggests that option writers are currently positioned for the index to expire around current levels.

The **SENSEX** also has its max pain point at 82100, almost precisely at its spot close of 82,104.79. This indicates that option writers are comfortable with the index expiring near its current price.

The **BANK NIFTY**'s max pain is at 61200, which is approximately 0.6% below its spot close of 61,575.90. When max pain is below the current spot, it can sometimes suggest a slight downward pull towards that level as expiry nears.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can empower you to form your own perspective on market movements. You can begin by visiting the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, specifically the 'Derivatives' section. Here, look for the 'Option Chain' for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty for the upcoming expiry.

From the option chain, you can identify the highest Call Open Interest and highest Put Open Interest strikes, which act as immediate resistance and support levels, respectively. To gauge fresh activity, compare the 'Change in Open Interest' column for Calls and Puts across different strikes. A significant increase in Call OI at a particular strike suggests fresh selling and potential resistance, while a rise in Put OI indicates fresh selling and potential support. You can also calculate the PCR(OI) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI, and similarly, the PCR(today flow ΔOI) by dividing the total change in Put OI by the total change in Call OI. Max pain data is often available on various financial portals or can be calculated by summing the losses for option writers at each strike. Combining these observations daily can help you understand the prevailing sentiment among option writers.
