# India F&O Pulse — 08 Oct 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Nifty shows bearish leanings today, while Sensex appears range-bound and Bank Nifty exhibits a mildly bullish undertone, with FIIs maintaining a cautious stance in index futures.

**TL;DR**
*   **Nifty 50:** The options data suggests a bearish bias, with significant Call writing indicating potential resistance levels.
*   **SENSEX:** Positioning appears mixed to range-bound, as both Call and Put writers established significant levels.
*   **BANK NIFTY:** The data indicates a mildly bullish sentiment, with Put writers establishing a strong base close to the current spot.

## What the indices did today

The **NIFTY 50** closed the day at 22,231.80, down 1.64%. This move placed it well below the immediate resistance suggested by Call Open Interest walls, and closer to the support levels indicated by Put Open Interest. The overall options positioning points towards a bearish sentiment for the index.

The **SENSEX** ended at 71,593.24, marking a 1.44% decline today. It settled within its broader option-chain implied range of 70,000 to 74,000. The day's action, combined with the options data, suggests a more range-bound or mixed outlook for the Sensex.

The **BANK NIFTY** saw a relatively milder decline of 0.98%, closing at 54,515.05. It remained above its closest heavy Put Open Interest wall at 55,000, which could act as a floor. The options data for Bank Nifty hints at a mildly bullish bias, despite the day's fall.

## Decoding today's open interest

**Open Interest (OI)** refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides insights into the total money flowing into the market for a particular contract. A high Call OI at a certain strike price typically indicates that many traders have *sold* or *written* Call options at that level, expecting the price to stay below it. Conversely, a high Put OI at a strike suggests many traders have *sold* or *written* Put options, expecting the price to stay above that level. These levels often act as significant resistance (for Calls) and support (for Puts).

For the Nifty 50, today's heaviest fresh Call (CE) writing was observed near the 22,400 strike. This suggests that Call sellers, often referred to as 'writers', believe the index might struggle to move significantly above this level in the near term, effectively capping potential upside. In contrast, fresh Put (PE) writing was comparatively lower, reinforcing the bearish sentiment.

On the SENSEX, significant fresh Call writing was seen around the 72,500 strike, indicating expectations of resistance there. However, there was also notable fresh Put writing near 70,166, suggesting that Put sellers anticipate this level to act as a floor. This dual action contributes to the mixed or range-bound outlook.

For the BANK NIFTY, while there was some fresh Call writing near 55,233, the heaviest Put Open Interest at 55,000, which is just below the spot price, suggests a strong immediate support base. This positioning by Put writers indicates confidence in the index holding above this level.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely used sentiment indicator calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value below 1.0 typically suggests more Call writing than Put writing, often interpreted as a bearish signal. Conversely, a PCR above 1.0 indicates more Put writing, which can be seen as a bullish sign.

For the Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stood at 0.64, which is below 0.7, indicating a dominance of Call writing and suggesting potential upside resistance. The PCR based on today's fresh flow of Open Interest (PCR(today flow ΔOI)) was even lower at 0.31, reinforcing the observation that today's fresh writing was heavily skewed towards Calls. This combination strongly suggests that options writers are positioned for a capped upside.

The SENSEX had a PCR(OI) of 0.87, which is closer to a balanced book, not strongly bullish or bearish. However, its PCR(today flow ΔOI) was 0.6, indicating that new positions added today were more Call-heavy. This suggests a cautious sentiment emerging from today's fresh activity, despite the overall Open Interest being more balanced.

For the BANK NIFTY, the PCR(OI) was 0.95, also indicating a relatively balanced book of overall Open Interest. The PCR(today flow ΔOI) was -0.45, which implies a mixed or balanced flow of fresh writing today, not leaning heavily towards either Calls or Puts. This balanced fresh flow, coupled with a near-neutral overall PCR, contributes to the mildly bullish bias primarily driven by the proximity of strong Put support.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that specific price. It's often considered a "magnet" for the underlying index as expiry approaches, as option writers, who typically hold larger positions, may try to steer the market towards this level to maximise their profits or minimise losses.

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain point is at 22,700, which is approximately 2.1% above its current spot of 22,231.80. This suggests that if the index were to gravitate towards this level by expiry, Call writers at lower strikes and Put writers at higher strikes would face the least collective loss.

The SENSEX's Max Pain is at 72,500, about 1.3% above its spot of 71,593.24. Similar to Nifty, this indicates a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry nears.

For the BANK NIFTY, the Max Pain stands at 55,400, which is roughly 1.6% above its current spot of 54,515.05. This also suggests an upward inclination for the index towards this "least pain" point for option writers.

It is important to remember that while Max Pain can act as a magnet, its influence typically strengthens as the weekly or monthly expiry date draws closer.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of **8.5%**. This means that only 8.5% of their total Index futures positions were on the 'long' side (expecting prices to rise), while the vast majority were 'short' (expecting prices to fall). Historically, such a low long ratio in Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from these large institutional players on the broader market.

In contrast, their Stock futures long ratio was more balanced at **54.8%**, indicating a relatively neutral to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks within the F&O segment. This suggests that while FIIs are broadly cautious on the index level, they might be selectively bullish on specific counters.

## Stocks where money moved today

Among the F&O watchlist, **INFY** and **AXISBANK** were the top gainers, rising 0.50% and 0.20% respectively. INFY saw a volume of nearly 1.5 crore shares, while AXISBANK traded over 73 lakh shares.

On the losing side, **ADANIENT** saw a significant decline of 5.36%, with over 44 lakh shares traded. **ITC** also fell sharply by 4.03%, recording a very high volume of over 5.3 crore shares. **RELIANCE** was down 2.46% with nearly 1.4 crore shares changing hands. Other notable losers included **LT** (-2.06%) and **MARUTI** (-1.94%). The high volumes in ITC and RELIANCE suggest considerable participant activity in these stocks today.

## What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When Nifty's Put-Call Ratio (both overall and fresh flow) is significantly below 0.7, combined with heavy fresh Call writing at nearby strikes, and FIIs maintain a very low long ratio in index futures, the next session has historically tended to coincide with either continued downward pressure or a struggle for the index to move significantly higher. The bearish signals from Nifty's options data, coupled with FIIs' cautious index futures positioning, suggest that overall market sentiment may remain subdued.

However, the mixed to mildly bullish signals from Sensex and Bank Nifty, particularly their Max Pain points being above spot and Bank Nifty's strong Put base, suggest that any broad market weakness might not be uniform across all indices. Historically, such a divergence can lead to selective stock-specific action or a rotational shift in market leadership, rather than a uniform market-wide move. The elevated India VIX, indicating increased market volatility, also suggests that sharp moves in either direction are possible, and caution is warranted.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily movements better. You can start by looking at the Open Interest data, which is readily available on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in an easily digestible format.

Focus on the strike prices with the highest Call OI and Put OI. These represent the key resistance and support levels identified by option writers. Next, observe the change in Open Interest (ΔOI) for the day to see where fresh positions are being built. A significant increase in Call OI at a strike above the current spot, coupled with a low Put-Call Ratio, often points to potential resistance. Conversely, strong Put OI build-up below the spot price, especially with a higher PCR, can indicate support. Finally, keep an eye on the FII derivative statistics, also available on the NSE website, to gauge the positioning of institutional players. Combining these data points can help you form a more comprehensive view of the market's underlying sentiment.
