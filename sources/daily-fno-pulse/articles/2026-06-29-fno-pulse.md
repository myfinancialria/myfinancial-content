## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been settled. It provides a snapshot of where market participants, especially option writers (sellers), have placed their bets. The strike with the **highest Call OI** typically acts as a significant resistance level, as many participants expect the price to stay below it. Conversely, the strike with the **highest Put OI** often serves as a strong support level, as many expect the price to remain above it.

For the **NIFTY 50**, the heaviest Put OI is observed at 24,000, which is just 0.2% below the spot price. This suggests that option writers are comfortable defending this level as a potential floor. On the Call side, today's heaviest fresh Call writing was seen near 24,366, indicating that new Call sellers anticipate the index might struggle to move significantly above this point. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was concentrated near 23,816, suggesting some confidence among Put sellers in this level holding as support. The aggregate flow for Nifty today showed a net Call OI build of 66.3 million contracts, significantly higher than the 13.8 million Put OI build, which points to more participants expecting a cap on upside movement.

In **SENSEX**, the heaviest Call OI is at 77,000, which is merely 0.4% above the spot price. This level appears to be a strong immediate ceiling for the index. Today's heaviest fresh Put writing was near 75,233, suggesting that Put sellers are establishing positions expecting the index to stay above this level.

For **BANK NIFTY**, fresh Call writing was most prominent near 58,500, indicating that Call sellers believe the index may not easily cross this mark. On the Put side, fresh writing was heaviest near 57,666, suggesting that Put sellers are looking at this level as a potential immediate floor. The aggregate flow for Bank Nifty saw a net Call OI build of 3.6 million contracts, substantially outweighing the 0.7 million Put OI build, reinforcing the view that more participants are betting on limited upside.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a key indicator derived by dividing the total Open Interest of Put options by the total Open Interest of Call options. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests a bullish sentiment among option writers, as more Puts are being written (betting on prices rising). Conversely, a PCR below 1 often indicates a bearish sentiment, with more Calls being written (betting on prices falling).

For **NIFTY 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.72, which suggests a relatively balanced book but with a slight leaning towards more Call writing overall. However, today's fresh flow, represented by the change in Open Interest (ΔOI), shows a PCR of 0.21. This significantly lower figure indicates that today's new option writing was heavily skewed towards Calls, suggesting a fresh bearish sentiment from option writers for the immediate future.

The **SENSEX** PCR(OI) is 0.78, also indicating a somewhat balanced but Call-heavy overall positioning. Today's flow ΔPCR for SENSEX is 0.61, which, while higher than Nifty's, still points to a greater proportion of fresh Call writing compared to Put writing during the day.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the PCR(OI) is 0.84, suggesting a slightly more balanced book than Nifty and Sensex, but still below 1.0. However, the PCR based on today's flow ΔOI is a very low 0.2, signalling that fresh option writing in Bank Nifty was overwhelmingly dominated by Call selling. This indicates a strong immediate bearish sentiment among option writers for the banking index.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is a concept in options theory that refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum cumulative loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It's often viewed as a "magnet" because as expiry approaches, the underlying index or stock price has historically tended to gravitate towards this level, as option writers may try to manage their positions to minimise losses.

For **NIFTY 50**, the Max Pain strike is at 24,200, which is approximately 1.1% above its current spot price of 23,946.25. This suggests that if the index were to gravitate towards the Max Pain point by expiry, it would imply an upward movement from current levels.

The **SENSEX** Max Pain is positioned at 77,200, about 0.6% above its spot price of 76,728.37. Similar to Nifty, this indicates a potential upward pull towards the Max Pain level as expiry nears.

**BANK NIFTY** has its Max Pain strike at 58,000, which is approximately 0.5% above its current spot price of 57,727.35. This also suggests a slight upward pull towards the Max Pain level. It's important to remember that the "magnet effect" of Max Pain typically strengthens as the expiry date draws closer.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held an **Index futures long ratio of 17.0%**. This ratio is derived from their total long positions (60,489 contracts) versus their total short positions (294,940 contracts) in index futures. A long ratio this low, significantly below 50%, historically tends to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from FIIs on the broader market indices. It suggests that a substantial portion of FIIs are positioned for potential downside or consolidation in the indices.

In **Stock futures**, FIIs maintained a long ratio of **54.6%** (long 4,035,844 contracts, short 3,359,553 contracts). This ratio, slightly above 50%, indicates a more balanced to mildly bullish stance on individual stocks within the F&O segment, contrasting with their more bearish positioning in index futures.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding the nuances of F&O data can empower you to form your own informed perspectives on market movements. You can access much of this data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically looking for "Option Chain" data for indices like Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty. Many brokerage platforms also offer intuitive interfaces to view this information.

When you look at the option chain, pay close attention to the "Open Interest" (OI) column for both Calls and Puts at various strike prices. The strikes with the highest OI for Calls often indicate where resistance might be, while the highest Put OI suggests potential support. Also, observe the "Change in OI" (ΔOI) for the day; this tells you where fresh positions are being built. A significant increase in Call ΔOI at higher strikes, combined with a low Put-Call Ratio (PCR) for the day's flow, could suggest that option writers are betting on a cap to upside movement. Conversely, strong Put ΔOI at lower strikes, especially with a higher PCR, might indicate confidence in those levels holding as support. Combining these observations with the overall PCR (OI) and the Max Pain level can help you gauge the market's likely range and potential direction for the upcoming sessions.
