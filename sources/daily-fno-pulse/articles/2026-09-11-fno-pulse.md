# India F&O Pulse — 11 Sep 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options contracts provides valuable insights into where market participants, particularly option writers, are positioning themselves. A high **Call Open Interest** at a particular strike typically acts as a resistance level, indicating that many participants believe the underlying asset will not rise above that price. Conversely, a high **Put Open Interest** at a strike often serves as a support level, suggesting expectations that the asset will not fall below that price.

For the **NIFTY 50**, the heaviest Put OI is observed at the 23,300 strike, suggesting this level could act as a strong support base. On the Call side, the 24,000 strike holds the highest OI, indicating a significant resistance point. Today's fresh Call writing activity was notably concentrated near 23,483, suggesting that option sellers anticipate the upside to be capped around these levels in the near term.

The **SENSEX** saw its heaviest Call OI at 75,000, which is very close to the current spot price, potentially acting as an immediate ceiling. The strongest Put OI base is further down at 72,000. Fresh Call writing today was significant near 75,666, reinforcing the expectation of resistance at higher levels. Interestingly, there was also fresh Put writing near 73,500, which could indicate some participants are expecting this level to hold as a floor.

In the **BANK NIFTY**, a unique situation emerged with both the heaviest Put OI and Call OI concentrated at the 57,500 strike. This suggests that 57,500 is perceived as a critical pivot or battleground level by option writers. Despite this, the heaviest Put OI at 57,500, which is above the current spot, indicates that option sellers are anticipating strong support at these higher levels. Fresh Call writing was observed near 56,566, suggesting some immediate resistance around the current spot.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely watched indicator that helps gauge market sentiment. It is calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1.0 generally suggests a bullish bias (more Puts written than Calls, indicating support expectations), while a value below 1.0 often indicates a bearish bias (more Calls written than Puts, suggesting resistance expectations). A PCR close to 1.0 typically points to a balanced or neutral sentiment.

For the **NIFTY 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 1.11, indicating a largely balanced options book. The PCR based on today's fresh flow of Open Interest (ΔPCR) was -5.02, suggesting that today's fresh writing activity was also quite mixed, not leaning strongly towards either bullish or bearish sentiment.

The **SENSEX** recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.19, which also suggests a balanced overall positioning. However, its ΔPCR for today was 1.43, indicating that fresh option writing today was predominantly in Puts. Historically, a higher ΔPCR from fresh writing tends to coincide with an expectation of support or upward movement by Put sellers.

The **BANK NIFTY** had a PCR(OI) of 0.94, which is slightly below 1.0, hinting at a marginally bearish overall book. However, the ΔPCR for today was a significant 10.61. This exceptionally high figure suggests that today's fresh option writing was overwhelmingly dominated by Put sellers, which historically tends to coincide with a strong expectation of an upward move or robust support from these participants.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is a concept in options theory that refers to the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It often acts as a psychological magnet, with the underlying index historically tending to gravitate towards this level as expiry approaches.

For the **NIFTY 50**, the Max Pain level is at 23,400, which is precisely where the index closed today. This suggests a neutral pull, with the market potentially consolidating around current levels.

The **SENSEX** has its Max Pain at 74,500, which is approximately 0.4% below its current spot price. This indicates a potential downward pull towards this level as expiry nears.

The **BANK NIFTY** shows its Max Pain at 57,500, a significant 1.6% above its current spot price. This suggests a strong upward pull, with option writers potentially anticipating the index to move higher towards this level by expiry. The closer we get to the expiry date, the stronger this "magnet effect" of Max Pain tends to become.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held a **long ratio of 11.2% in Index futures**. This means that for every 100 contracts, only about 11 were long, while the vast majority were short (41,252 long contracts versus 326,134 short contracts). Historically, such a low long ratio in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or even bearish outlook from FIIs on the broader market indices.

In **Stock futures**, FIIs maintained a long ratio of **54.1%** (3,454,581 long contracts against 2,934,979 short contracts). This indicates a relatively balanced to slightly positive stance on individual stocks, suggesting selective buying interest in specific counters.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these F&O data points can empower you to form your own market perspective. You can access the raw options data from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Equity Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" for NIFTY, BANK NIFTY, and FINNIFTY. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in a user-friendly format.

When you look at the option chain, focus on the strikes with the highest Open Interest on both the Call and Put sides. The strike with the highest Call OI often represents a strong resistance level, while the strike with the highest Put OI suggests a significant support level. These are your "OI walls." To calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR), simply divide the total Put OI by the total Call OI. A rising PCR can suggest increasing bullish sentiment, while a falling PCR might indicate growing bearishness. For Max Pain, look for the strike where the total outstanding premium for both calls and puts is at its lowest; this strike often acts as a magnet. By observing these data points daily and understanding how fresh writing activity (changes in OI) impacts them, you can develop a more informed view of market sentiment and potential price movements.
