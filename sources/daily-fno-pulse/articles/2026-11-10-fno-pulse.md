# India F&O Pulse — 10 Nov 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Call Writers Maintain Control as Indices Show a Bearish Lean

**TL;DR**
*   Nifty 50's options data, despite a slight positive close, suggests a mildly bearish undertone with significant call writing activity.
*   Sensex also closed marginally higher, but its options structure, particularly fresh call writing, indicates a similar mildly bearish sentiment.
*   Bank Nifty, despite a strong positive day, exhibits a bearish setup from its options data, with max pain significantly below spot and heavy call writing.

## What the indices did today

The **Nifty 50** managed a modest gain of 0.23% today, closing at 25,044.65. The index finished near its ATM (At The Money) strike of 25050, but the option chain walls, indicating significant open interest, suggest a broader range between 24000 and 26000. The day's action saw the index navigate within this implied range, with call writers positioning themselves for a potential cap.

The **Sensex** also saw a slight uptick, rising 0.13% to close at 81,106.65. Similar to Nifty, it ended very close to its ATM strike of 81100. The broader option-chain implied range for Sensex is noted between 78500 and 83000, suggesting that today's movement was well within the established boundaries of significant option activity.

Meanwhile, **Bank Nifty** showed considerable strength, advancing 1.51% to settle at 59,741.25. Despite this strong upward move, its ATM strike was 59700. The option chain data, however, points to a strong resistance at 58000, which is notably below today's closing price, suggesting a potential ceiling that option writers are defending.

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been settled. It gives us a sense of where market participants, especially option writers (sellers), are building their positions. A **Call Option's highest Open Interest strike** often acts as a significant resistance level, meaning many sellers believe the index will not rise above this point. Conversely, the **highest Put Option Open Interest strike** typically serves as a strong support level, where sellers expect the index to not fall below.

Today's data reveals a clear picture of fresh writing activity. For **Nifty 50**, the heaviest fresh Call (CE) writing was observed near the 23600 strike. This indicates that call sellers are actively positioning themselves, expecting the index to potentially face a cap around or below this level. In **Sensex**, similar sentiment was seen with the heaviest fresh CE writing near 78200, suggesting call writers anticipate the index struggling to move significantly above this mark. For **Bank Nifty**, the heaviest fresh CE writing was even lower, near 56800. This is a particularly noteworthy signal, as it suggests option writers are quite confident in capping any further upside, despite the index's strong positive move today.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely watched sentiment indicator derived from options data. It is calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest (or volume) by the total Call Open Interest (or volume). A PCR value below 1 generally suggests that more Call options are open or being traded compared to Put options, which is often interpreted as a bearish signal, as Call writers anticipate a cap on upside. Conversely, a PCR above 1 can be seen as bullish.

Today, the PCR based on Open Interest (PCR(OI)) for **Nifty 50** stood at 0.85, indicating a relatively balanced book with a slight lean towards Call Open Interest. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR) was 0, suggesting a mixed or balanced flow of new positions being added.

For **Sensex**, the PCR(OI) was 0.78, again pointing to a balanced book with a slightly higher concentration of Call Open Interest. The ΔPCR was also 0, reflecting a balanced addition of new Put and Call positions today.

**Bank Nifty** recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.84, similar to Nifty, suggesting a balanced options book with a slight edge to Call Open Interest. Its ΔPCR was also 0, indicating a balanced flow of fresh positions for the day. While the PCR(OI) values are not extremely low, the combination with heavy fresh Call writing suggests that the market participants are not aggressively betting on significant upside from current levels.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is a concept in options trading that refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying index or stock were to expire at that specific price. As expiry approaches, the underlying tends to gravitate towards this "max pain" level, as option writers, who hold the majority of open interest, have an incentive to see the index expire there. It acts like a magnetic pull.

For **Nifty 50**, the max pain level is currently at 25000, which is very close to its spot closing price of 25,044.65. When max pain is near the spot, it often suggests that the market is fairly priced for expiry, and there isn't a strong directional pull from this indicator alone.

The **Sensex** also sees its max pain at 81100, almost precisely at its spot close of 81,106.65. This alignment, similar to Nifty, implies a neutral pull from the max pain perspective for Sensex.

However, **Bank Nifty** presents a different picture. Its max pain is at 58900, which is approximately 1.4% below its spot closing price of 59,741.25. This significant difference suggests that option writers would benefit if Bank Nifty were to decline towards 58900 by expiry. This "pin pull" being downwards is a notable bearish signal, especially as we get closer to the weekly or monthly expiry.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are often considered "smart money" due to their significant capital and research capabilities. Their positioning in futures and options can offer insights into their market outlook.

As of yesterday's data (November 9, 2026), the FII Index futures long ratio stood at **26.8%**. This means that out of their total open positions in index futures, only 26.8% were long contracts, while the remaining 73.2% were short. Historically, a lower FII long ratio in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish sentiment from these large institutional players. Their **Stock futures long ratio** was 58.4%, indicating a more balanced, slightly bullish stance on individual stocks, but the index futures positioning often carries more weight for overall market direction. Today's FII data from NSE is not yet published, so we are observing yesterday's positioning.

## Stocks where money moved today

Observing individual stock movements within the F&O segment can highlight where significant participant interest lies.

Among the top gainers today were **SBIN**, up 2.56% with a volume of over 2.4 crore shares, **LT** which gained 1.82%, and **HDFCBANK**, rising 1.79% with a substantial volume of over 6.4 crore shares. **ICICIBANK** and **MARUTI** also saw positive movement. These banking and auto sector stocks seem to have attracted buying interest today.

On the losing side, **ITC** declined 1.64%, followed by **BAJFINANCE** down 1.56%. **INFY** dropped 1.49%, and **TCS** fell 0.94%. **ADANIENT** also closed in the red, down 0.90%. The selling pressure in these stocks, particularly in IT and some large-cap names, suggests some profit booking or cautious sentiment in these counters.

## What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we synthesise today's various signals, a consistent theme emerges. The overall options data, particularly the heavy fresh Call writing across Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty, suggests that market participants are actively building positions to cap potential upside. The Put-Call Ratios, while not extremely low, lean towards Call Open Interest, reinforcing this view. Bank Nifty's max pain being significantly below its spot price adds a bearish pull for that index as expiry draws closer. The FIIs' low long ratio in index futures, as per yesterday's data, also historically tends to coincide with a cautious outlook from institutional players.

Historically, when these signals align in this direction – with significant call writing, max pain below spot for a key index, and a cautious FII stance – the next session has tended to witness challenges for sustained upward momentum. It often suggests that any rallies might be met with selling pressure, as option writers defend their positions, and the market may consolidate or see a downward bias.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to form your own informed perspective. You can access the raw data from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" and "Participant-wise Open Interest" reports. Many brokerage platforms also provide user-friendly interfaces to view option chain data.

To interpret the data, first look at the Option Chain for the highest Call and Put Open Interest strikes, as these indicate potential resistance and support zones. Then, observe where fresh Call and Put writing (change in OI) is happening relative to the current spot price. Heavy fresh Call writing above the spot suggests a potential ceiling, while heavy fresh Put writing below the spot suggests a potential floor. Calculate the PCR(OI) and PCR(Volume) to gauge overall market sentiment. Finally, keep an eye on the FII positioning data, which is usually updated by NSE after market hours, to understand how large institutions are positioning themselves. Combining these data points daily can help you identify prevailing market biases.
