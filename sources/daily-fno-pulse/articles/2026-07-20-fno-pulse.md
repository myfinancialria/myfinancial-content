# India F&O Pulse — 20 Jul 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Index futures data points to caution, while options suggest a mixed picture with Nifty and Bank Nifty showing mild bullish undertones, and Sensex leaning mildly bearish.

**TL;DR**
*   **Nifty 50:** Options data suggests a mildly bullish bias, with strong put writing providing support, despite a slight downward pull from max pain.
*   **SENSEX:** The overall options structure for Sensex points to a mildly bearish outlook, largely due to call-heavy fresh writing today.
*   **BANK NIFTY:** Despite a significant fall today, Bank Nifty's options data indicates a mildly bullish bias, with max pain suggesting an upward pull.

## What the indices did today

The **Nifty 50** closed the day at 24,238.50, registering a modest decline of 0.39%. It finished just above its heaviest Put Open Interest (OI) wall at 24,200, suggesting this level could act as immediate support.
The **Sensex** saw a larger dip, closing at 77,708.52, down 0.57% for the session. It ended the day right around its Max Pain level of 77,700, indicating a balanced tug-of-war between call and put writers.
Meanwhile, the **Bank Nifty** experienced a more significant fall, closing at 57,945.00, a drop of 0.98%. Despite the day's decline, it closed just below its heaviest Put OI base at 58,000, which might offer a psychological floor.

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been settled. When we talk about the highest Call OI strike, it typically indicates a level where a significant number of call options have been sold (written). Call writers generally profit if the underlying asset stays below this strike price, so this level often acts as a **resistance**. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike signifies a level where many put options have been written. Put writers profit if the underlying asset stays above this strike, making this level a potential **support**.

Today's fresh writing activity gives us a glimpse into where new sellers are positioning themselves. For the Nifty 50, we observed the heaviest fresh Call writing near 24,350, suggesting that call sellers are expecting the index to remain capped around this level. On the Put side, significant fresh writing was seen near 24,000, indicating that put sellers anticipate the index to find a floor there.

For the Sensex, the heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near 79,033, implying a potential ceiling around this mark. Fresh Put writing, on the other hand, was concentrated near 76,866, suggesting a possible support level.

In Bank Nifty, fresh Call writing was most prominent near 58,566, indicating that call writers might view this as an upper bound. Fresh Put writing was heaviest near 58,000, reinforcing this as a potential support.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator calculated by dividing the total Open Interest of Puts by the total Open Interest of Calls. A PCR greater than 1 typically suggests a bullish sentiment, as more puts are written (expecting prices to rise), while a PCR less than 1 often indicates a bearish sentiment, with more calls written (expecting prices to fall). We also look at the PCR based on today's change in Open Interest (ΔOI) to gauge the immediate sentiment from fresh positions.

For the Nifty 50, the PCR (OI) stood at 1.31, which is above 1, suggesting that put writers dominate, historically indicating that the downside might be well-protected. However, today's flow ΔPCR was -0.25, pointing to a mixed or balanced flow of fresh positions for the day.

The Sensex had a PCR (OI) of 1.02, which is close to 1, indicating a relatively balanced book of existing positions. The ΔPCR for today's flow was 0.01, suggesting that fresh writing for the day was slightly call-heavy, which can be interpreted as a mildly bearish signal for the immediate term.

Bank Nifty's PCR (OI) was 0.85, below 1, implying a slight dominance of call writing in existing positions. The ΔPCR for today's flow was -0.19, suggesting a mixed or balanced flow of fresh positions, despite the existing call-heavy bias.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the least amount of financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that specific price. It's often considered a magnet, especially as the expiry date approaches, as the underlying index tends to gravitate towards this level.

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain point is at 24,100, which is about 0.6% below today's spot price. This suggests a potential downward pull towards this level as expiry nears.
The Sensex's Max Pain is precisely at 77,700, aligning closely with its spot price. This indicates that option writers are positioned for the index to potentially expire around its current levels.
Bank Nifty's Max Pain is at 58,400, which is approximately 0.8% above its current spot price. This could imply an upward pull for the index towards this level.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are often seen as significant players in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment. Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 9.4%. This ratio represents the percentage of long positions relative to total open positions in index futures. Historically, a very low FII index futures long ratio has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish stance from these large participants.

In stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of 54.0%, indicating a slight preference for long positions in individual stocks.

## Stocks where money moved today

In the F&O watchlist, several stocks saw notable movements today. Among the top gainers were Bharti Airtel, which rose 1.64% to ₹1940.10, and State Bank of India (SBIN), up 1.50% to ₹1060.00. ICICI Bank also performed well, gaining 1.10% to ₹1460.20, with a significant volume of over 2.22 crore shares.

On the losing side, Axis Bank saw a sharp decline of 5.46% to ₹1256.00, accompanied by high volume of over 2.53 crore shares. HDFC Bank also fell significantly by 5.12% to ₹777.60, with an exceptionally high volume of over 5.6 crore shares. Maruti Suzuki India (MARUTI) was down 2.09% to ₹13514.00, and Kotak Mahindra Bank (KOTAKBANK) dropped 2.03% to ₹382.05, also with substantial volume.

## What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we synthesise the various signals from today's F&O data, we observe a nuanced picture. The Nifty 50's options data, particularly the high PCR(OI) and strong put base, historically tends to suggest underlying support, even with a slight downward pull from max pain. For the Sensex, the balanced PCR(OI) but call-heavy fresh writing and Max Pain at spot historically tends to coincide with a more range-bound to mildly bearish outlook. Bank Nifty, despite today's fall, shows a mildly bullish bias from its Max Pain and put base proximity. The overall FII index futures positioning, with a low long ratio, has historically tended to coincide with a cautious sentiment from institutional players. When these signals align in this manner, the next session has historically tended to exhibit volatility within defined ranges, with indices potentially testing both support and resistance levels.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can empower you to form your own perspectives on market movements. To start, you can access Open Interest data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the 'Derivatives' section, specifically the 'Option Chain' for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in an easily digestible format.

Look for the total Open Interest for Calls and Puts at various strike prices. Identify the strikes with the highest Call OI and Put OI to understand potential resistance and support levels. Calculate the PCR by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI. Also, pay attention to the change in Open Interest (ΔOI) for the day to see where fresh positions are being built. For FII positioning, the NSE archives publish a daily 'Fao Participant Oi' CSV file which you can download and analyse. By consistently tracking these metrics, you can begin to identify patterns and understand the collective positioning of market participants.
