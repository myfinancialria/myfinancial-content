## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options, that have not yet been settled. For options, the strike with the highest Call OI is often seen as a significant resistance level or a "ceiling" because many Call option sellers (writers) believe the index will not rise beyond that point. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI is typically considered a strong support level or a "floor," as Put option sellers expect the index to stay above it.

Today's data provides some interesting insights into these "walls." For **Nifty 50**, the heaviest Call OI sits at 24,200, which is very close to the spot price, suggesting a strong immediate ceiling. The heaviest Put OI, acting as a potential floor, is at 24,150, also quite close to the spot. The fresh Call writing today was heaviest near 24,200, indicating that new Call sellers expect the Nifty to be capped around this level. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was concentrated near 24,150, suggesting Put sellers anticipate a floor there. The aggregate flow shows a significantly larger build-up in Call OI (89.9M) compared to Put OI (8.1M), which can be interpreted as a more bearish sentiment from option writers.

For **Sensex**, both the heaviest Call OI and Put OI are clustered around 77,500, right at the spot price. This indicates a very tightly contested zone. Today's heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near 78,333, suggesting Call sellers are looking for a cap further up. Conversely, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 76,000, implying Put sellers are comfortable establishing a floor at a lower level.

In **Bank Nifty**, the heaviest Call OI is at 59,000, while the heaviest Put OI is at 58,000. Fresh Call writing today was most significant near 58,233, suggesting Call sellers anticipate resistance around that level. However, fresh Put writing was concentrated near 56,833, indicating Put sellers are building a base lower down.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely used sentiment indicator calculated by dividing the total Open Interest of Put options by the total Open Interest of Call options. A PCR generally above 1.0 suggests a more bullish sentiment (more Put writing than Call writing), while a ratio below 1.0 indicates a more bearish sentiment (more Call writing than Put writing).

For **Nifty 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) is 0.81, which suggests a relatively balanced book, leaning slightly towards more Call writing. However, the PCR based on today's fresh flow (ΔPCR) is a low 0.09. This significantly low ΔPCR indicates that today's new option writing was heavily skewed towards Calls, suggesting a fresh bearish sentiment from option writers.

**Sensex** shows a PCR(OI) of 0.78, similar to Nifty, implying a balanced to slightly Call-heavy book. Its ΔPCR for today's flow stands at 0.52, which, while higher than Nifty's, still indicates that fresh writing activity today saw more Calls being written than Puts.

**Bank Nifty** has a PCR(OI) of 0.84, also suggesting a balanced options book with a slight inclination towards Call writing. Its ΔPCR for today's flow is 0.61, indicating that fresh writing activity was still more Call-heavy than Put-heavy for the day.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is a concept in options trading that refers to the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that particular strike. It's often thought of as a "magnet" because as expiry approaches, the underlying index tends to gravitate towards this level, as option writers try to minimise their losses.

For **Nifty 50**, the Max Pain strike is at 24,200, which is very close to today's spot price of 24,187.70. This suggests that option writers are positioned for the index to potentially expire around current levels.

**Sensex** has its Max Pain at 77,700, which is slightly above its current spot of 77,470.11. This indicates a potential upward pull towards that level as expiry nears.

**Bank Nifty's** Max Pain is at 58,300, notably higher than its spot price of 57,835.35. This suggests that option writers, on aggregate, would prefer the Bank Nifty to move upwards towards 58,300 by expiry. This upward bias from Max Pain is considered a mildly bullish signal for Bank Nifty.

It's important to remember that the "magnet effect" of Max Pain typically becomes stronger as the weekly or monthly expiry date draws closer.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can empower you to form your own perspectives on market dynamics. You can typically find Open Interest data, including the option chain, on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in an easily digestible format.

To interpret it, look for the strikes with the highest Call OI and Put OI to identify potential resistance and support levels. Track the change in OI (ΔOI) throughout the day to see where fresh positions are being built, particularly for Calls and Puts. Calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) for both total OI and daily ΔOI to gauge overall and immediate sentiment. Finally, locate the Max Pain strike to understand where option writers are collectively positioned for expiry. Combining these insights can help you develop a more informed view of the market's underlying structure.
