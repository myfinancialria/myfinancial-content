## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides a measure of market activity and liquidity.

When we look at an option chain, the strike price with the **highest Call OI** often acts as a significant resistance level. Call option writers (sellers) profit if the price stays below their strike, so a large accumulation here suggests many participants believe the underlying index will not rise beyond this point. Similarly, the strike price with the **highest Put OI** typically indicates a strong support level. Put option writers profit if the price stays above their strike, implying a consensus that the index is unlikely to fall below this level.

Today's fresh writing, which refers to newly added Open Interest, gives us clues about where new positions are being taken. For the **NIFTY 50**, the heaviest fresh Call (CE) writing was observed near 23,533. This suggests that call sellers are expecting the index to find a cap around this level. Conversely, the heaviest fresh Put (PE) writing was near 23,250, indicating that put sellers anticipate this level to act as a floor. The aggregate flow for NIFTY 50 showed a significant net Put OI build of 46.5 million contracts, far outweighing the 4.8 million in Call OI, reinforcing the idea of strong downside protection being built.

For the **SENSEX**, fresh Call writing was most concentrated near 77,166, implying that call sellers expect resistance here. On the Put side, the heaviest fresh writing was around 74,500, suggesting that put sellers are establishing support at this level.

In **BANK NIFTY**, fresh Call writing was notable near 55,200, which call sellers might see as a potential ceiling. Meanwhile, the heaviest fresh Put writing was directly at 55,000, suggesting that put sellers are actively defending this level as a base.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that compares the total Open Interest of put options to call options. A PCR(OI) value above 1 typically suggests that more put options are outstanding than call options, often interpreted as a potentially bullish signal because put writers expect prices to rise or stay stable, providing downside protection. Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more call options, which can be seen as a bearish or cautious signal.

For **NIFTY 50**, the PCR(OI) stands at 1.39, which is well above 1. This indicates a dominance of put options in the overall open interest, suggesting that option writers have built substantial downside protection. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) is even higher at 9.62, meaning that the fresh writing activity today was overwhelmingly dominated by put options. This combination strongly suggests that option writers are collectively positioned for the index to hold its ground or move higher, with significant confidence in the current support levels.

The **SENSEX** PCR(OI) is 0.97, which is very close to 1. This suggests a relatively balanced book between put and call options in the overall open interest, indicating a neutral or range-bound sentiment from option writers. However, today's flow ΔPCR is 1.39, showing that fresh writing activity leaned towards puts, providing some fresh support.

**BANK NIFTY** has a PCR(OI) of 0.89, which is slightly below 1, indicating a slight dominance of call options in the overall open interest. Despite this, today's flow ΔPCR is 1.34, meaning that fresh writing activity today was more concentrated in put options. This fresh put writing suggests that new positions are being built to support the index.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max pain" is a concept in options theory that refers to the strike price at which option buyers (both calls and puts) would collectively experience the maximum financial loss at expiration. Conversely, it's the strike price where option sellers (writers) would collectively make the most profit. As expiry approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this "max pain" strike, as option writers, who typically hold larger positions, try to steer the market towards their profitable zone. It's often described as a "magnet effect."

For **NIFTY 50**, the max pain level is 23,000, which is -1.9% below the current spot of 23,440.80. This suggests that if the index were to converge towards max pain, it would imply a downward pull from the current levels.

The **SENSEX** max pain is at 75,200, sitting -0.7% below its spot of 75,692.78. Similar to NIFTY 50, this indicates a slight downward pull from the current spot if the max pain theory holds true.

In contrast, **BANK NIFTY** has its max pain at 55,600, which is +1.1% above its spot of 55,002.20. This suggests a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry nears.

It's important to remember that the magnet effect of max pain becomes stronger as the weekly or monthly expiry date draws closer.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are often considered significant players in the Indian markets due to their substantial capital. Their derivative positioning can offer insights into how "big money" is viewing the market.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of **10.6%**. This means that out of their total open index futures positions, only 10.6% were long (bets on rising prices), while the vast majority (89.4%) were short (bets on falling prices). Historically, such a low long ratio in index futures from FIIs has often tended to coincide with a cautious or even bearish outlook from this participant group on the broader market indices.

In Stock futures, FIIs had a long ratio of **57.9%**, indicating a more balanced, slightly bullish stance on individual stocks compared to their index futures positioning.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market dynamics can be a valuable skill for any informed investor. You can access much of this data yourself daily. The **Open Interest (OI)** data for options chains is readily available on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" for NIFTY, BANK NIFTY, and SENSEX. Here, you can see the total OI for each strike price for both Calls and Puts. By observing where the highest OI is concentrated, you can identify potential support and resistance levels.

To calculate the **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)**, you simply divide the total Put OI by the total Call OI. For today's flow (ΔOI), you would look at the change in OI for puts and calls. Many brokerage platforms also provide these calculations directly. The FII derivative positioning data is published daily by the NSE under the "Participant-wise Open Interest" section in the archives. By regularly tracking these data points, you can develop your own understanding of how market participants are positioning themselves and what that might historically tend to imply for market movements.
