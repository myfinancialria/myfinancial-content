## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, like options or futures, that have not yet been closed, exercised, or expired. It essentially tells us where market participants have committed their capital. A high OI at a particular strike price suggests significant interest and often acts as a psychological barrier or magnet for the underlying asset.

For today's expiry, the highest Call OI for NIFTY 50 was observed at the 22,000 strike. This level acts as a "Call Wall" or resistance, meaning a large number of participants have sold Call options here, expecting the NIFTY to stay below this level. Conversely, the highest Put OI was concentrated at the 21,800 strike, forming a "Put Wall" or support. Here, many participants have sold Put options, anticipating the NIFTY to remain above this mark.

In terms of fresh writing, which indicates new positions being added, we saw significant Call writing at the 22,000 and 22,100 strikes for NIFTY 50. This suggests that option sellers are actively building positions, reinforcing these levels as potential resistance. For BANK NIFTY, fresh Call writing was prominent at 46,500, while fresh Put writing was seen at 46,000, outlining a potential trading range.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator that compares the number of put options traded to the number of call options traded. It helps gauge whether investors are predominantly buying puts (bearish sentiment) or calls (bullish sentiment).

For NIFTY 50, today's PCR based on Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stood at 0.98. A PCR(OI) close to 1.0 suggests a relatively balanced sentiment, with neither put writers nor call writers showing overwhelming dominance. The PCR based on today's trading volume (PCR(flow)) was 1.05, indicating a slight increase in Put option trading relative to Call options during the session. This combination suggests that while overall open positions are balanced, there was a minor tilt towards bearish hedging or speculation during the day.

For BANK NIFTY, the PCR(OI) was 0.95, also indicating a balanced to slightly cautious stance. The PCR(flow) for BANK NIFTY was 1.10, showing a stronger inclination towards Put buying or writing during the day compared to Call options.

SENSEX recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.02, hinting at a marginally bullish bias from the perspective of overall open positions. Its PCR(flow) was 0.90, suggesting more Call option activity during the day.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is the strike price at which the total intrinsic value paid out to option holders at expiration is smallest, meaning the largest dollar amount of options expires worthless. It is often considered a "gravitational centre" for the underlying asset, especially as the expiry date draws closer. The theory suggests that market makers and institutions, who are often option sellers, have an incentive to see the price close near this level to minimise their payouts.

For NIFTY 50, the Max Pain strike for the current expiry is at 21,900. With the NIFTY closing at 21,950.20, it suggests a potential pull towards 21,900 as expiry nears.

BANK NIFTY's Max Pain is identified at 46,200. Given its close at 46,280.75, the index is currently trading slightly above this "magnet" level.

SENSEX's Max Pain strike is at 73,000. Its closing price of 73,150.50 places it just above this level, indicating that 73,000 could act as a significant pivot point. The closer we get to expiry, the stronger this "magnet effect" tends to be.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are major participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning offers insights into institutional sentiment.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 37.0%. This means that out of their total open index futures positions, 37.0% were long (expecting prices to rise) and the remaining 63.0% were short (expecting prices to fall). A ratio below 1.0, and particularly this low, historically tends to coincide with a cautious or bearish tilt in their index outlook.

In Stock futures, FIIs had a long ratio of 58.4%. This indicates a more balanced to slightly bullish positioning in individual stocks compared to their index futures stance. When FIIs are net short in index futures but show some long positioning in stock futures, it can sometimes suggest hedging activity against their cash market holdings, rather than a purely bearish view across the board.

## What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we observe a combination of a low FII Index futures long ratio, a balanced to slightly cautious PCR, and indices trading near their Max Pain levels, the market has historically tended to exhibit range-bound behaviour with potential for volatility around key option strikes. The significant Call writing at higher levels suggests that option sellers are comfortable defending those ceilings, while Put writing at lower strikes indicates support. This alignment of signals often points towards a session where indices may consolidate or trade within defined boundaries, with any strong directional moves potentially facing resistance or finding support at the levels indicated by Open Interest. It suggests a market where options writers are actively managing their positions, aiming for expiry near the Max Pain strike.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements. You can access options Open Interest data, including Call and Put OI, and their changes, directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the 'Derivatives' section. Look for the 'Option Chain' for NIFTY, BANK NIFTY, and other indices. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in an easily digestible format.

To calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR), simply divide the total Put OI (or volume) by the total Call OI (or volume) for an index. For FII positioning, the NSE publishes daily participant-wise Open Interest data in a CSV format, which you can download and analyse. By consistently tracking these data points – Open Interest, PCR, Max Pain, and FII positioning – and observing how they evolve relative to the index's price action, you can develop a more informed perspective on potential market dynamics for the upcoming sessions. Remember, these are tools for understanding market positioning, not guarantees of future price movements.
