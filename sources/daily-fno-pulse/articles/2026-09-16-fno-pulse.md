## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been settled. When we talk about the highest Call OI strike, it generally signifies a level where a large number of call option sellers (or "writers") believe the market is unlikely to rise above, thus acting as a potential resistance. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike indicates a level where many put option sellers expect the market to find support and not fall below, effectively acting as a floor.

For **Nifty 50**, the heaviest Put OI is currently situated at 23,000, suggesting a strong support zone. Today's fresh put writing was most concentrated near 23,150, indicating that put sellers are actively establishing positions, anticipating that the index will hold above this level.

In the **Sensex** options, the most substantial Put OI is at 74,000, forming a robust support. While there was significant fresh put writing near 73,266, suggesting confidence in a floor, there was also notable fresh call writing near 74,933, which might imply some resistance expectations at higher levels.

**Bank Nifty** shows its heaviest Put OI at 57,500, which is also its implied upper range and Max Pain point. This indicates a strong confluence of resistance and potential magnet for the index.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total Open Interest of put options to call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests a bullish sentiment, as more puts are being written (implying expectations of prices not falling), while a PCR below 1 can indicate a bearish sentiment.

For **Nifty 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 1.03, indicating a largely balanced book. However, the PCR based on today's fresh flow (ΔPCR) is a significant 1.87. This suggests that today's option activity saw a substantial preference for writing put options over call options, often interpreted as a bullish signal where writers are confident the market will not fall below certain levels.

The **Sensex** also presents a balanced PCR(OI) at 1.08. More notably, its ΔPCR for today's flow is a very strong 2.62, pointing towards an overwhelming amount of fresh put writing. This indicates that option writers are aggressively selling puts, suggesting a strong expectation of support and an overall bullish sentiment for the index.

**Bank Nifty** has a PCR(OI) of 0.89, which is slightly below 1, suggesting a cautious but still largely balanced stance. Its ΔPCR for today's flow is -0.58, which indicates a mixed or relatively balanced fresh writing activity today, not leaning strongly in either direction.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the underlying asset expires at that specific price. As expiry approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this "Max Pain" strike, acting like a magnetic pull.

For **Nifty 50**, the Max Pain level is at 23,300, which is approximately 0.4% above its current spot price. This suggests a potential upward pull for the index as expiry nears.

The **Sensex** has its Max Pain exactly at its current spot price of 74,300. This implies that option writers are currently positioned for the index to expire around its current levels, indicating a neutral short-term expectation from this metric.

**Bank Nifty** shows its Max Pain at 57,500, a significant 2.1% above its current spot. This indicates a strong potential upward pull, as option writers would incur the least loss if the index were to expire at this higher level.

## What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When the option data, particularly fresh put writing and Max Pain, aligns to suggest upward momentum or strong support, the market has historically tended to exhibit a positive bias in the subsequent session. The dominant fresh put writing for Nifty and Sensex today, combined with Max Pain levels at or above current spot, indicates that option writers are largely positioned for stability or an upward drift. While FIIs show a cautious stance in index futures, the strong domestic option writing activity often provides a counter-signal for immediate market direction. Therefore, based on the confluence of these signals, the next session has historically tended to open with a mild to moderate positive sentiment, with key support levels likely to be defended.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements. To start, you can access the daily option chain data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section. Look for "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex. Here, you'll find the Open Interest (OI) for various Call and Put strikes.

To calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR), simply divide the total Put OI by the total Call OI for a given expiry. For "today's flow" (ΔPCR), you would look at the change in OI for puts and calls. Identifying the strikes with the highest OI for both calls and puts will show you the immediate resistance and support levels. Max Pain calculators are available on many financial portals and broker platforms (like Fyers, as mentioned in the disclaimer) and can give you a quick read on where option writers are positioned. Combining these data points with FII derivative statistics (also available on the NSE website under "Participant-wise Open Interest") can provide a comprehensive picture of market sentiment and positioning.
