## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts that have not yet been settled. In options, the strike price with the highest Call OI often acts as a significant resistance level, as many traders have sold Calls at that strike, expecting the price not to rise above it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI typically acts as a strong support level, as many traders have sold Puts there, anticipating the price will not fall below it.

For **Nifty 50**, today saw the heaviest fresh Call writing near the 23,066 level. This suggests that Call sellers, often referred to as 'writers', believe the index might find a cap around this level. On the Put side, fresh writing was concentrated near 22,566, indicating that Put sellers anticipate this level could act as a floor.

In **Sensex**, the aggregate flow showed a significant build in Put OI (5.0 million contracts) compared to Call OI (1.1 million contracts) today. The heaviest fresh Put writing was observed near 72,333, implying that Put writers expect this level to hold as support.

For **Bank Nifty**, fresh Call writing was most prominent near 54,200, suggesting potential resistance. Meanwhile, strong fresh Put writing was seen around 53,966, indicating that option writers might be building a floor around this area.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total Put Open Interest to the total Call Open Interest. A PCR(OI) value above 1 generally suggests that more Puts are outstanding than Calls, which can be interpreted as a bullish sentiment (as Put writers expect prices to rise or stay stable). A value below 1, conversely, might suggest a bearish bias. We also look at PCR(today's flow ΔOI), which considers only the change in OI for the day, giving insight into fresh positioning.

For **Nifty 50**, the PCR(OI) stands at 1.11, indicating a relatively balanced options book overall. However, today's flow ΔPCR was -2.16, which suggests that fresh Call writing outpaced fresh Put writing today, or Puts were unwound more aggressively, leading to a mixed or balanced flow for the day.

**Sensex** recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.17, also showing a balanced options book. Crucially, its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was a robust 4.38. This significant positive shift indicates that today's fresh writing was heavily skewed towards Puts, suggesting a bullish sentiment from option writers for the immediate term.

**Bank Nifty** has a PCR(OI) of 0.82, which is still considered relatively balanced. Its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was -2.63, pointing to a mixed or balanced flow for the day, similar to Nifty, where fresh Call writing or Put unwinding was more dominant.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers would suffer the least aggregate financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. As expiry approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this Max Pain strike, as option writers, who hold significant positions, might try to manage their risk. This is often referred to as the "magnet effect."

For **Nifty 50**, the Max Pain strike is at 22,650, which is approximately 0.5% below its current spot price of 22,753.20. This suggests a potential downward pull towards this level as expiry nears.

**Sensex** has its Max Pain at 73,200, about 0.6% below its spot price of 73,606.75. This also indicates a potential magnetic pull towards a slightly lower level.

In contrast, **Bank Nifty**'s Max Pain is at 55,000, which is approximately 1.2% above its current spot price of 54,366.70. This positioning of Max Pain above the spot suggests a potential upward pull for the index.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Historically, such a low long ratio in Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from FIIs on the broader market.

In Stock futures, their long ratio was more balanced at **56.8%** (3,690,619 long vs. 2,809,523 short), suggesting a relatively neutral to mildly bullish stance on individual stocks.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can be a powerful tool for your daily financial awareness. You can start by looking at the Open Interest (OI) data, which is freely available on the NSE website under the "Derivatives" section. Look for the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex. Identify the strikes with the highest Call OI and Put OI; these are your immediate resistance and support levels.

Next, track the daily change in OI (ΔOI) for Calls and Puts at various strikes. A significant build-up in Call ΔOI at a particular strike might suggest fresh resistance, while a build-up in Put ΔOI could indicate new support. You can also calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI. For FII positioning, the NSE publishes a daily "Participant-wise Open Interest" CSV file, which provides the breakdown of FII, DII, Client, and Pro activity in futures and options, allowing you to track their long and short positions. Many brokerage platforms also provide these data points in a more user-friendly format.
