## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. When we look at Call Option (CE) Open Interest, it indicates the total number of Call options that are still "open" or active. High Call OI at a particular strike price often suggests that many option writers (sellers) believe the underlying index will not rise above that level, effectively acting as a potential resistance or 'ceiling'. Conversely, high Put Option (PE) Open Interest at a strike price suggests that many option writers believe the index will not fall below that level, often acting as a potential support or 'floor'.

For the Nifty 50, today's heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near the 25,433 strike. This indicates that option sellers are expecting the index to face resistance around this level, suggesting a potential cap on upward movement. On the Put side, the heaviest fresh Put writing was seen near 24,750, implying that option sellers anticipate this level to act as a floor. The aggregate flow for Nifty 50 showed a significant build in Call OI (32.1 million contracts) compared to Put OI (14.8 million contracts), reinforcing a cautious sentiment from option writers.

In the Sensex, the heaviest Call OI was seen at 81,500, very close to the spot price, indicating a nearby ceiling. Similarly, the heaviest Put OI was also at 81,500, suggesting a strong floor around the same level. Today's fresh Call writing was concentrated near 82,200, while fresh Put writing was near 80,666, pointing to a potential trading band.

For the Bank Nifty, the heaviest Call OI was at 61,000, just above the closing price, acting as a close ceiling. The heaviest Put OI was at 60,500, suggesting a strong support base. Today, fresh Call writing was notable near 61,566, while fresh Put writing was concentrated near 60,800.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that helps gauge market sentiment by comparing the volume or open interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR value below 1 generally suggests that more Call options are being written (sold) than Put options, which can be interpreted as a bearish signal, as Call writers profit if the market stays below their strike price. Conversely, a PCR above 1 indicates more Put writing, often seen as a bullish signal.

For the Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.76, indicating a balanced book with a slight leaning towards more Call writing. However, today's flow, reflected in the change in Open Interest (ΔOI), shows a PCR(today flow ΔOI) of 0.46. This significantly lower value suggests that fresh writing activity today was heavily skewed towards Calls, reinforcing a bearish sentiment from option writers for the immediate future.

The Sensex recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.84, also indicating a relatively balanced options book. Its PCR(today flow ΔOI) was 0.81, suggesting that today's fresh writing activity was quite mixed and balanced between Calls and Puts, aligning with a range-bound view.

For the Bank Nifty, the PCR(OI) was 0.9, again indicating a fairly balanced options landscape. However, its PCR(today flow ΔOI) was 1.33. This higher value suggests that today's fresh writing saw more Put options being written than Calls, which historically tends to coincide with a belief that the downside is limited, offering some short-term bullish support.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the least financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that specific level. It's often considered a 'magnet' because, as expiry approaches, the index sometimes tends to gravitate towards this point, as option writers, who hold the majority of the open interest, might try to manage their positions to minimise losses.

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain level is at 25,400, which is approximately 1.0% above its current spot price of 25,144.30. This suggests that option writers, on aggregate, would prefer the Nifty to expire higher than its current level.

The Sensex has its Max Pain at 81,700, which is very close to its spot price of 81,592.45. This proximity indicates that option writers are largely neutral on the Sensex's expiry point, expecting it to hover around current levels.

The Bank Nifty's Max Pain is set at 60,500, which is about 0.9% below its current spot of 61,034.10. This suggests that option writers would collectively incur the least loss if the Bank Nifty were to expire lower than its current position. As we approach expiry, the influence of this Max Pain level tends to strengthen.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment. Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 34.6%. This means that out of their total index futures positions, only 34.6% were long (bets on prices going up), while the remaining 65.4% were short (bets on prices going down). Historically, a lower FII Index futures long ratio, especially below 50%, has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish stance from these large institutional players on the broader market.

In stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of 58.1%, indicating a slightly bullish bias in individual stock bets compared to their index futures positioning.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to form your own informed perspectives. To start, you can regularly check the "Option Chain" data available on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website or through your broker's trading platform. Look for the Open Interest (OI) column for both Call and Put options across various strike prices. The strikes with the highest OI often indicate key support and resistance levels. You can also track the "Change in OI" to see where fresh positions are being built, which helps understand the day's dominant sentiment.

Additionally, keep an eye on the Put-Call Ratio (PCR), which some brokers or financial websites calculate for you. Comparing the PCR based on total OI with the PCR based on today's change in OI can reveal if the recent market action is reinforcing or diverging from the longer-term positioning. Finally, monitoring the FII derivative statistics, also available on the NSE website, provides insight into how large institutional players are positioning themselves in futures and options, offering another layer to your daily market analysis.
