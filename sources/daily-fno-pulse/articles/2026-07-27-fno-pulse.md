## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been settled. When we look at the highest Call OI strike, it generally indicates a level where option writers (sellers) have taken significant positions, expecting the underlying index to not rise above that strike. This often acts as a resistance level. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike typically represents a level where Put writers expect the index to not fall below, thus acting as a support level.

For the **Nifty 50**, the heaviest Call Open Interest is currently observed at the 24200 strike, suggesting that option writers are positioned for the index to potentially face resistance around this level. On the Put side, the heaviest Open Interest is at 23000, indicating a strong support base. Today's fresh writing activity saw the heaviest new Call writing near 24200, reinforcing the idea that call sellers expect a cap there. Simultaneously, fresh Put writing was concentrated near 23950, suggesting some immediate support building close to the current spot.

In the **Sensex**, while the overall option-chain implied range is 75000-78500, today's fresh Call writing was heaviest near 78100. This indicates that option sellers are expecting the index to potentially cap its upside around this mark. On the Put side, significant fresh writing was observed near 76700, suggesting that Put sellers believe this level could act as a floor.

For the **Bank Nifty**, the heaviest Put Open Interest is at 57000, which is very close to the current spot price, indicating a strong immediate support level. Today's fresh Call writing was most prominent near 57400, suggesting that call sellers anticipate resistance there. However, fresh Put writing also occurred heavily near 57000, reinforcing the potential for a floor. Notably, the aggregate flow for the day showed a net Call Open Interest build of 0.6 million contracts, significantly higher than the 0.0 million contracts added to Put Open Interest, which suggests a relatively more cautious sentiment from option writers.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a valuable indicator derived from option Open Interest. It is calculated by dividing the total Open Interest of Put options by the total Open Interest of Call options for a given expiry. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests that more Put options have been written than Call options, which can sometimes be interpreted as a bullish bias by option writers, as they expect the market to stay above those Put strikes. Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more Call writing, often suggesting a relatively bearish sentiment.

For the **Nifty 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 1.08, which suggests a largely balanced book overall. However, the PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR) is -1.32, indicating a mixed to balanced flow of fresh writing activity for the day.

The **Sensex** shows a PCR(OI) of 1.12, also pointing to a generally balanced options book. Interestingly, its PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR) is 1.74, which suggests that today's fresh writing was predominantly in Put options, potentially indicating a belief in a floor being established by option writers.

In the **Bank Nifty**, the PCR(OI) is 0.73, which, while still considered balanced, leans slightly towards more Call writing compared to Put writing. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR) is 0.03, suggesting that today's fresh writing was significantly more concentrated in Call options, indicating a cautious stance from option writers for the banking index.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** refers to the strike price at which option writers, or sellers, would collectively face the least amount of financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that specific level. It's often considered a potential "magnet" for the expiry price, as large institutional option writers tend to manage their positions to minimise their collective losses, especially as expiry approaches.

For the **Nifty 50**, the Max Pain strike is at 24000, which is currently very close to the spot price. This suggests that option writers are positioned for the index to potentially gravitate towards this level by expiry.

The **Sensex** has its Max Pain strike at 76600, which is approximately 0.3% below the current spot price. This indicates a slight downward pull from the Max Pain perspective for the Sensex.

In the **Bank Nifty**, the Max Pain strike is at 57800, positioned about 1.2% above the current spot price. This suggests a potential upward pull towards this level by expiry, from the perspective of option writers' positioning.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Historically, such a low long ratio in FII Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or relatively bearish outlook from these large participants on the broader market indices.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily F&O data. You can start by visiting the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, specifically the "Derivatives" section, and look for the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, or Sensex. Here, you'll find the Open Interest (OI) data for various Call and Put strikes. Look for the strikes with the highest total OI for both Calls and Puts – these often indicate major resistance and support levels, respectively.

Next, pay attention to the "Change in OI" column. This tells you where fresh buying or selling (writing) activity occurred today. A significant increase in Call OI at a particular strike suggests fresh Call writing, implying sellers expect the price to stay below that level. Similarly, a rise in Put OI indicates fresh Put writing, suggesting sellers anticipate the price to stay above that level. You can also calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI, and track its change daily. Finally, while more complex to calculate manually, understanding the concept of "Max Pain" as a potential expiry magnet can add another layer to your analysis. By consistently observing these data points and how they evolve each day, you can develop a more informed perspective on market sentiment and potential price behaviour.
