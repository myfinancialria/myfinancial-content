# India F&O Pulse — 13 Aug 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Nifty and Sensex exhibit mild bullish undertones as Bank Nifty signals caution.

**TL;DR**
*   **NIFTY 50:** The day's data suggests a mildly bullish bias, with Max Pain indicating a potential upward pull.
*   **SENSEX:** A mildly bullish sentiment appears for Sensex, primarily driven by fresh Put writing today.
*   **BANK NIFTY:** Bank Nifty data leans towards a mildly bearish outlook, with significant fresh Call writing observed.

## What the indices did today

The **NIFTY 50** closed the session at 24,395.85, registering a slight dip of 0.16% today. The index settled below its highest Call Open Interest (OI) wall at 25,000 and above its Put OI base at 24,000, indicating a potential trading range for the near term.

The **SENSEX** ended the day marginally higher at 78,079.96, gaining 0.15%. It concluded the session very close to its heaviest Call OI at 78,100, suggesting this level might act as immediate resistance, while a strong Put base sits at 77,800.

Meanwhile, the **BANK NIFTY** saw a decline of 0.43%, closing at 57,635.25. The index finished well below its primary Call OI wall at 58,000, which also coincided with its Put OI base, pointing to a potential pivot around this level.

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. When we look at the highest Call OI strike, it indicates the price level where the maximum number of Call options have been sold (or "written"). Option writers typically sell Calls when they believe the underlying asset's price will not rise above that strike, making it a potential resistance level. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike signifies the price level where the maximum number of Put options have been written. Put writers generally sell Puts when they expect the price to stay above that strike, suggesting it could act as a support level.

For **NIFTY 50**, the heaviest Call OI is observed at 25,000, suggesting that option sellers believe the index might struggle to move significantly above this level. The strongest Put OI base is at 24,000, implying a potential floor for the index. Today's fresh Call writing was most prominent near 24,800, indicating new sellers are positioning for a potential cap around this level. On the Put side, fresh writing was heaviest near 24,216, suggesting new Put sellers anticipate a floor forming around this mark.

In **SENSEX**, the Call wall is quite close at 78,100, which had the heaviest Call OI, suggesting immediate resistance. The Put base is at 77,800, indicating a potential support. Today, fresh Call writing was concentrated near 78,200, with sellers expecting a ceiling. Fresh Put writing, on the other hand, was heaviest near 77,900, with Put sellers anticipating support.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the heaviest Call OI and Put OI both converge at 58,000, suggesting this strike could be a crucial pivot point. Today's fresh Call writing was most significant near 58,733, indicating that option sellers are expecting the index to remain below this level.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that helps gauge the overall sentiment in the options market. It's calculated by dividing the total Open Interest of Put options by the total Open Interest of Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests a more bullish sentiment (more Puts written than Calls), while a PCR below 1 often indicates a bearish bias (more Calls written than Puts). We also look at the PCR based on today's change in Open Interest (ΔOI) to understand the sentiment of fresh positions taken during the day.

For **NIFTY 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.76, which suggests a relatively balanced book with a slight lean towards Call writing. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR) is 0.92, indicating that fresh positions taken today were also somewhat balanced, leaning slightly towards Put writing but not decisively so. This combination points to a mixed to neutral sentiment from option writers.

**SENSEX** shows a PCR(OI) of 0.99, indicating a nearly balanced book of outstanding Call and Put options. However, today's flow ΔPCR is significantly higher at 1.44. This suggests that the fresh writing activity today was heavily skewed towards Put options, with more Puts being sold compared to Calls, which historically tends to coincide with a mildly bullish undertone for the day's fresh positioning.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the PCR(OI) is 0.79, similar to Nifty, suggesting a balanced book with a slight inclination towards Call writing. More notably, today's flow ΔPCR is 0.51, which is quite low. This indicates that a significant majority of new positions added today were Call options being written, suggesting option sellers are expecting the index to face resistance and potentially move lower.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that particular price. It's often considered a "magnet" because the underlying index sometimes tends to gravitate towards this strike price as expiry approaches, as option writers try to minimise their collective losses.

For **NIFTY 50**, the Max Pain strike is at 24,600, which is approximately 0.8% above the current spot price of 24,395.85. This suggests that option writers are collectively positioned for the index to potentially move higher towards this level by expiry.

**SENSEX** has its Max Pain strike at 78,000, which is very close to its current spot price of 78,079.96. When Max Pain is near the spot price, it often implies that the market might remain range-bound around this level as expiry nears.

**BANK NIFTY** also shows its Max Pain at 58,000, which is about 0.6% above its current spot price of 57,635.25. This indicates a potential upward pull towards 58,000 for Bank Nifty from the perspective of option writers.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held a long ratio of **12.3%** in Index futures, with 27,487 long contracts against 196,189 short contracts. A low long ratio in index futures has historically tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish stance from these institutional players, suggesting they are predominantly short on index futures.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of **54.9%**, with 3,528,485 long contracts versus 2,900,054 short contracts. A long ratio above 50% in stock futures typically suggests a moderately bullish stance on individual stocks.

## What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

Considering the various data points, the overall picture for tomorrow's session appears to be one of mixed signals with a slight bullish lean for Nifty and Sensex, while Bank Nifty shows signs of caution.

For **Nifty 50**, the mildly bullish bias from the 8-vote check, coupled with Max Pain suggesting an upward pull and fresh Put writing near current levels, indicates that option writers are positioning for the index to potentially hold its ground or see some upward movement. Historically, when Max Pain is above the spot and fresh Put writing is observed at lower levels, it has tended to coincide with a supportive undertone for the index.

**Sensex** also carries a mildly bullish bias, primarily supported by a strong ΔPCR today, which suggests fresh Put-heavy writing. While the Call wall is close, the significant fresh Put writing at lower levels, combined with Max Pain near spot, historically tends to coincide with the index attempting to consolidate or see a slight positive bias, with option writers expecting a floor to hold.

However, **Bank Nifty** presents a mildly bearish bias. The low ΔPCR, indicating heavy fresh Call writing, and the concentration of Call OI at 58,000, suggests that option sellers are expecting this level to act as a strong resistance. Historically, when fresh Call writing dominates the daily flow and significant Call OI builds up at nearby strikes, it has tended to coincide with the index facing downward pressure or struggling to move higher.

The FII index futures positioning, with a low long ratio, adds a layer of caution from institutional players, which historically tends to coincide with a more guarded market outlook overall, despite some individual index biases.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret market movements better. You can access options Open Interest data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex. Here, you'll find the total Call and Put OI for various strike prices, which helps identify potential resistance and support levels (the "walls" and "bases" we discussed).

To calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR), simply sum up the total Put OI and divide it by the total Call OI from the option chain. For today's flow (ΔOI), you'll need to look at the "Change in OI" column. FII derivative positioning data is also available on the NSE website under "Participant-wise Open Interest." By regularly tracking these data points and observing how they change day-to-day, you can begin to identify patterns and understand the collective positioning of market participants, helping you form your own informed perspective on market tendencies.
