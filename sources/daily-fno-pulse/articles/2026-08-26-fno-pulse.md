# India F&O Pulse — 26 Aug 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

*   **SENSEX:** Similar to Nifty, Sensex also shows a mildly bearish lean, driven by prevalent Call writing and a lower Put-Call Ratio.
*   **BANK NIFTY:** The banking index appears to be positioned for a range-bound session, with balanced Put and Call Open Interest suggesting support and resistance clustered closely.

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, like Calls and Puts, that have not yet been settled. When we look at the highest Call OI strike, it indicates the price level where the most Call options have been written (sold). Call writers generally expect the market to stay below this level, effectively acting as a resistance. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike signifies the level where the most Put options have been written. Put writers typically believe the market will remain above this point, thus forming a support level.

For the **NIFTY 50**, the heaviest Call Open Interest is concentrated at 24,500, suggesting a strong ceiling. The heaviest Put Open Interest, offering potential support, is seen at 24,200. Today's fresh writing saw significant new Call writing near 24,400, indicating that option sellers are actively capping the upside around these levels. However, fresh Put writing near 23,866 also suggests some participants are expecting a floor to hold further down. The aggregate flow shows a net Call OI build of 55.5 million contracts, significantly outweighing the 12.3 million contracts in Put OI build, reinforcing the bearish sentiment from option writers.

On the **SENSEX**, the highest Call OI stands at 78,000, acting as a key resistance. The strongest Put OI is at 77,000, providing a base. Fresh Call writing was heaviest near 77,900, with writers anticipating the index to struggle past this mark. Simultaneously, fresh Put writing near 76,733 suggests some expectation of support at lower levels. The overall flow saw 25.1 million contracts added to Call OI, much higher than the 5.6 million added to Put OI, pointing to a bearish leaning.

For **BANK NIFTY**, both the heaviest Call OI and Put OI are clustered around 57,500, indicating that option writers are positioning for the index to trade within a tight range around this level. Today's fresh Put writing near 58,066 suggests that some participants are expecting the index to find support even if it moves higher, while the overall flow was more balanced.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR below 1.0 generally suggests more Call writing, indicating potential resistance and a cautious or bearish outlook. A PCR above 1.0 suggests more Put writing, often seen as support and a more bullish or confident outlook.

For **NIFTY 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) is 0.69, which is below 1.0 and suggests a dominance of Call writers, implying potential upside limitations. The PCR based on today's fresh flow (ΔOI) is even lower at 0.22, indicating that today's new option writing was heavily skewed towards Calls, reinforcing the bearish sentiment.

The **SENSEX** also shows a PCR(OI) of 0.67, similar to Nifty, pointing to a Call-heavy option book. Its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) is 0.22, suggesting that fresh positions added today were predominantly Call options, further strengthening the view of resistance ahead.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the PCR(OI) is 1.1, which is above 1.0 and indicates a more balanced or slightly Put-heavy book, suggesting robust support. The PCR(today's flow ΔOI) is 1.29, implying that today's fresh option writing saw more Puts being written than Calls, which can be interpreted as participants expecting the current levels to act as a floor.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is a concept in options theory that refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that specific price. It's often considered a magnetic point that the underlying index tends to gravitate towards as the expiry date approaches, as option writers, who hold the majority of outstanding contracts, often try to manage their positions to minimise their overall losses.

For **NIFTY 50**, the Max Pain point is at 24,500. This is approximately 1.2% above the current spot price of 24,207.75. The upward pull from this Max Pain point suggests that option writers might benefit if the index moves towards this higher level by expiry.

The **SENSEX** has its Max Pain at 78,100, which is about 0.8% above its spot price of 77,472.94. This also implies an upward pull, aligning with the idea that expiry might see the index gravitate higher from its current position.

However, for **BANK NIFTY**, the Max Pain is at 57,500, which is about 0.5% below its current spot price of 57,783.75. This suggests a potential downward pull towards 57,500 as expiry nears, indicating that option writers would prefer the index to settle around this lower level.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often offers insights into broader market sentiment. Today, FIIs held a long ratio of **10.8%** in Index futures (25,651 long contracts versus 211,711 short contracts). Historically, such a low long ratio for FIIs in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish sentiment among these large institutional players. In stock futures, their long ratio stood at **54.5%** (3,425,528 long contracts against 2,857,765 short contracts), indicating a more balanced but slightly bullish stance on individual stocks.

When a combination of factors like a low Put-Call Ratio (PCR), significant fresh Call writing, and a lower FII index futures long ratio align, as seen today for Nifty and Sensex, the next session has historically tended to coincide with a cautious market sentiment. ## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements more effectively. You can access options Open Interest data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, typically in the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in an easily digestible format.

When reviewing the data, pay close attention to the strikes with the highest Open Interest for both Calls and Puts, as these act as key resistance and support levels. Also, observe the change in Open Interest (ΔOI) for the day to see where fresh positions are being built. A significant increase in Call OI at a particular strike, especially when the index is near that level, often indicates strong selling pressure. Similarly, a rise in Put OI suggests support. Calculating the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI gives you an overall sentiment gauge. A PCR below 1.0 suggests a bearish bias, while above 1.0 leans bullish. Combining these insights with FII positioning data, also available on the NSE website, can provide a comprehensive picture of the market's underlying structure for the upcoming session.
