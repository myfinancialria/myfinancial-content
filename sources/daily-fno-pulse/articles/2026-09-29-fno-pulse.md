## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. When we look at the highest Call OI strike, it typically indicates a level where a large number of call options have been sold (written). Call writers profit if the market stays below this strike, so it often acts as a potential resistance level, a ceiling the market may struggle to cross. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike signifies a level where many put options have been sold. Put writers profit if the market stays above this strike, making it a potential support level, a floor the market may find difficult to breach.

Today's fresh writing activity offers a closer look at where new positions are being taken. For **Nifty 50**, the heaviest fresh Call (CE) writing was observed near 23,066. This suggests that call sellers believe the Nifty might struggle to move significantly above this level in the near term. On the Put (PE) side, the heaviest fresh writing was near 22,433, indicating that put sellers anticipate the index finding support around this level.

In **Sensex**, fresh Call writing was most prominent near 73,166, implying a potential cap on upward movement from the perspective of call writers. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 71,500, suggesting that put sellers are comfortable with the index staying above this mark.

For **Bank Nifty**, the data shows significant fresh Put writing near 54,500. This indicates that put sellers expect the index to find a floor around this level. The provided data did not specify the heaviest fresh CE writing strike for Bank Nifty, but the overall picture from fresh put writing points to a belief in support.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total open interest of put options to that of call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests more put writing than call writing, which can be interpreted as a bullish sentiment (as put writers expect prices to rise or stay stable). A PCR below 1 indicates more call writing than put writing, often seen as a bearish signal (as call writers expect prices to fall or stay stable).

For **Nifty 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.83, suggesting a slightly higher concentration of call options compared to put options, indicating a relatively balanced but leaning bearish book. However, today's flow, measured by the change in Open Interest (ΔPCR), is 0.69. This lower figure implies that fresh writing today was significantly call-heavy, with more new call options being sold than put options. This combination points towards a cautious or bearish sentiment from today's option writers.

**Sensex** shows a PCR(OI) of 1.07, indicating a slightly higher number of put options compared to call options in total open interest, suggesting a balanced to mildly bullish stance. The ΔPCR for today's flow is 1.54, which is quite high. This means that today's fresh writing was heavily skewed towards put options, with many more new put options being sold than call options. This strong put-heavy fresh writing suggests a bullish sentiment among today's option writers for Sensex.

**Bank Nifty** has a PCR(OI) of 0.9, similar to Nifty, indicating a slightly higher call open interest and a balanced to mildly bearish overall book. However, today's flow ΔPCR is 0.54, which is quite low. This signifies that fresh writing today was predominantly call-heavy, with a significantly higher number of new call options being sold compared to put options. This suggests a bearish sentiment from today's option writers for Bank Nifty, despite some other bullish signals.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that specific price. It's often considered a magnetic point because, as expiry approaches, the market tends to gravitate towards this level. Option writers, who hold a significant portion of the open interest, might try to influence the market to expire at or near this point to maximise their profits or minimise losses.

For **Nifty 50**, the Max Pain level is at 23,000. This is approximately +1.2% above the current spot price of 22,716.20. The fact that Max Pain is above the current spot suggests a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry draws closer.

**Sensex** has its Max Pain at 72,600, which is very close to the current spot price of 72,529.07. When Max Pain is near the spot, it often suggests that the market might consolidate around current levels, with option writers positioned for minimal movement.

**Bank Nifty** shows a Max Pain level of 55,700. This is a significant +2.7% above its current spot price of 54,259.95. A Max Pain level considerably higher than the spot price can indicate a strong potential for the index to be pulled upwards towards this level, especially as the expiry date approaches.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can empower you to form your own informed perspective on market movements. You can typically access option chain data, which includes Open Interest for various strikes, directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in a user-friendly format. Look for the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Sensex, or Bank Nifty.

To calculate the PCR, you'll need to sum up the total Put Open Interest and total Call Open Interest for all available strikes for a given expiry. Dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI gives you the PCR(OI). For "today's flow" PCR (ΔPCR), you would do the same calculation but only using the *change* in Open Interest for puts and calls from the previous day's close to today's close. FII positioning data is also available on the NSE website, usually under the "Participant-wise Open Interest" section in the archives. By consistently tracking these metrics and observing how the market reacts, you can begin to identify patterns and understand the collective sentiment of option writers and large institutional players.
