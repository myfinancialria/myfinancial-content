# India F&O Pulse — 19 Oct 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

**Indian Indices See Call Writing Dominance Amidst Broad Market Weakness**

**TL;DR**
*   **NIFTY 50:** The day's option data, particularly fresh call writing, points towards a mildly bearish sentiment, with significant resistance building above current levels.
*   **SENSEX:** Similar to Nifty, Sensex also saw heavy call writing today, suggesting a potential cap on upward movement in the near term.
*   **BANK NIFTY:** While some put support is visible, overall option activity, especially fresh call additions, indicates a cautious to mildly bearish outlook for the banking index.

## What the indices did today

The Indian equity markets witnessed a broad-based decline today, with key indices closing in the red. The **NIFTY 50** ended the session at 22,888.95, down by 1.11%. This close placed the index well below its At The Money (ATM) strike of 22900, with significant call option resistance forming near 23300, suggesting a potential ceiling for upward moves.

The **SENSEX** followed suit, closing at 73,852.90, a decline of 1.17%. The index settled below its ATM strike of 73900, with option chain data indicating strong call writing activity that could act as a resistance level around the 74600 mark.

The **BANK NIFTY** also experienced a downturn, shedding 0.64% to close at 55,488.10. While closing slightly below its ATM of 55500, the index found some support near the 55000 Put OI wall, but faced a strong call wall at 56000, indicating a range-bound or capped movement.

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, rose by 5.05% to 14.77, reflecting increased market uncertainty following today's declines.

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options contracts provides a crucial insight into where market participants, particularly option sellers (writers), are positioning themselves. The strike with the highest Call Open Interest typically acts as a significant **resistance level**, as many participants expect the underlying asset not to rise beyond this point. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put Open Interest often serves as a **support level**, indicating where many expect the asset to find a floor.

For the **NIFTY 50**, the heaviest fresh Call (CE) writing today was observed near the 23300 strike. This suggests that option sellers believe the Nifty might struggle to cross this level in the near future, effectively placing a potential cap above the current spot price. On the other hand, fresh Put (PE) writing was comparatively lower. The aggregate flow saw a net Call OI build of 46.7 million contracts, significantly outweighing the 21.4 million Put OI build, reinforcing the call-heavy sentiment.

In the **SENSEX**, today's option activity also showed a clear preference for fresh Call writing. While specific strike details for fresh writing aren't provided, the overall aggregate flow indicates a net Call OI build of 13.0 million contracts against a mere 4.7 million Put OI build. This strong call-side activity suggests that option writers are positioning for limited upside movement.

The **BANK NIFTY** witnessed its heaviest fresh Call writing near the 55700 strike, implying that option sellers anticipate the index might find it challenging to move substantially higher from current levels. The aggregate flow for Bank Nifty showed a net Call OI build of 1.3 million contracts, with virtually no fresh Put OI build (0.0 million), indicating a clear lean towards bearish positioning by option writers.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely used indicator that helps gauge market sentiment by comparing the total Open Interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR value above 1 generally suggests more Put writing (often seen as bullish by option writers who expect prices to rise or stay stable), while a value below 1 indicates more Call writing (often seen as bearish, as writers expect prices to fall or stay capped).

For the **NIFTY 50**, the overall PCR (OI) stands at 0.85, which suggests a relatively balanced book with a slight lean towards more call writing. However, today's flow, measured by the change in PCR (ΔOI), was a more significant 0.46. This low figure indicates that fresh option writing today was heavily skewed towards Calls, signalling a distinctly bearish sentiment from today's new positions.

The **SENSEX** recorded an overall PCR (OI) of 0.73, also pointing to a relatively balanced book but with a slightly higher proportion of call writing than Nifty. More notably, its PCR (today's flow ΔOI) was 0.36, indicating a strong influx of new Call writing relative to Put writing, reinforcing a bearish bias for the day's fresh positions.

For the **BANK NIFTY**, the overall PCR (OI) is 0.89, suggesting a balanced book. However, its PCR (today's flow ΔOI) was 0.0, which means there was a complete dominance of Call writing over Put writing in today's fresh additions. This zero value strongly suggests that new option writers are overwhelmingly betting against significant upward movement.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is a concept in options theory that refers to the strike price at which the maximum number of options will expire worthless, causing the greatest financial loss for option buyers and the maximum profit for option sellers. It's often considered a "magnet" for the underlying index as expiry approaches, as option writers (who collectively hold significant power) tend to push the price towards this level.

For the **NIFTY 50**, the Max Pain strike is currently at 23100. This is approximately 0.9% above today's spot close of 22,888.95. This suggests that if the "Max Pain" theory holds true, there might be an upward pull towards this level as the weekly expiry draws closer.

The **SENSEX** has its Max Pain strike at 74600, which is about 1.0% above its current spot price of 73,852.90. This also indicates a potential upward pull, or at least a resistance point, that option writers might aim for by expiry.

For the **BANK NIFTY**, the Max Pain strike is at 55800, approximately 0.6% above its spot close of 55,488.10. Similar to the other indices, this suggests a slight upward bias from the Max Pain perspective, implying option writers might prefer the index to settle around this level. It's important to remember that the magnetic pull of Max Pain strengthens significantly as the expiry date gets closer.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs showed a **long ratio of 10.4% in Index futures**, meaning only 36,661 contracts were long compared to 316,591 short contracts. This very low long ratio, with a substantial net short position, historically tends to coincide with a cautious or outright bearish outlook from these large institutional players on the broader market indices.

In **Stock futures**, FIIs maintained a more balanced stance, with a long ratio of 55.7% (3,613,429 long contracts versus 2,875,948 short contracts). This suggests that while their sentiment on the overall index might be bearish, they are maintaining a relatively neutral to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks.

## What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

Today's derivatives data presents a broadly cautious to mildly bearish picture for the Indian equity markets. The consistent heavy fresh Call writing across Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty, coupled with low Put-Call Ratios for today's flow, indicates that option writers are actively positioning for limited upside or potential downside. The significant net Call OI build over Put OI build further reinforces this sentiment, suggesting that market participants expect current levels to act as a cap.

Historically, when such a strong dominance of call writing is observed after a market decline, it has tended to coincide with either a consolidation phase or continued weakness in the subsequent session. The very low FII Index futures long ratio also aligns with a cautious outlook from institutional investors, which has often preceded periods of market pressure. While Max Pain points slightly higher for all indices, its magnetic pull typically becomes stronger closer to expiry, and it might be counteracted by the immediate pressure from fresh call writing. Therefore, when these signals align in this direction, the next session has historically tended to see indices facing resistance on any upward moves, with a bias towards consolidation or further downside.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements more effectively. You can access options data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the 'Derivatives' section, specifically the 'Option Chain' for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and other indices. Your stockbroker's platform (like Fyers, Zerodha, etc.) also often provides simplified views of this data.

To start, look for the 'Open Interest' column on the Call and Put sides of the option chain. Identify the strikes with the highest OI on both sides – these are your key resistance and support levels. Next, observe the 'Change in Open Interest' column; a significant positive number here indicates fresh writing or accumulation at that strike. If fresh Call OI is building rapidly at strikes above the current market price, it suggests a ceiling. If fresh Put OI is building at strikes below the current market price, it suggests a floor. Calculate the Put-Call Ratio by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI, and track how it changes daily. By combining these observations with the FII data (also available on the NSE website under 'Participant-wise Open Interest'), you can start forming your own informed perspective on market sentiment.
