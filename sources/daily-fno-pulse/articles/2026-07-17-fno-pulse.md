## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, like options, that have not yet been settled. When we look at the highest Call OI strike, it often indicates a level where many call options have been sold (written), and these writers would profit if the price stays below this level. Therefore, it tends to act as a potential **resistance** for the underlying index. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike represents a level where many put options have been sold, and these writers profit if the price stays above this level. This strike often functions as a potential **support** level.

For the **Nifty 50**, the heaviest fresh Put writing today was observed near the 24,166 strike. This suggests that put sellers, also known as put writers, are expecting the Nifty to find a floor around this level, indicating a belief that the index will not fall significantly below it. This fresh writing is below the current spot, reinforcing a potential support base.

On the **Sensex**, fresh Put writing was heaviest near the 77,766 strike, while fresh Call writing was concentrated near 77,966. The substantial put writing below the spot suggests confidence among put sellers that the market will hold above this level. However, the call writing slightly above spot indicates some sellers are also capping the upside around that zone. The aggregate flow shows a much larger build-up in Put OI (6.5 million contracts) compared to Call OI (2.7 million contracts) today, which typically points to option sellers expecting an upward bias or at least a strong floor.

For **Bank Nifty**, the heaviest fresh Call writing was seen near the 58,900 strike, while the heaviest fresh Put writing was near 57,833. This positioning suggests that option writers are establishing both a potential ceiling around 58,900 and a floor around 57,833, indicating expectations of the index trading within this defined range.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that compares the total Open Interest of put options to that of call options. A PCR (OI) value above 1 generally suggests that more put options have been written than call options, which can be interpreted as option sellers collectively expecting the market to either rise or at least not fall significantly, thus forming a bullish or supportive bias. Conversely, a PCR (OI) below 1 suggests more call options have been written, potentially indicating a bearish or capping bias from option sellers. The PCR (today flow ΔOI) looks at the change in open interest for the day, giving insight into fresh positioning.

The **Nifty 50** recorded a PCR (OI) of 1.42, which is well above 1. This indicates a significant dominance of put options in the open interest, suggesting that option writers are collectively positioned for the downside to be well-protected. However, today's flow saw a ΔPCR of -8.94, which suggests a mixed or balanced flow of fresh writing during the day, not strongly favouring either calls or puts in new positions.

For the **Sensex**, the PCR (OI) stands at 1.4, also comfortably above 1. This points to a strong protective layer of put options, with option sellers anticipating the index to hold firm or move higher. The PCR (today flow ΔOI) was 2.41, indicating that today's fresh writing was heavily skewed towards puts, further reinforcing a bullish or supportive sentiment from option sellers.

The **Bank Nifty** had a PCR (OI) of 0.79, which is below 1, indicating a more balanced book with a slight lean towards call options in the overall open interest. Today's flow ΔPCR was -1.61, suggesting a mixed or balanced fresh flow of options, not giving a strong directional signal from new positions. This combination points towards a more neutral or range-bound outlook from option writers.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the least financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that level. As an expiry date approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this "max pain" strike, acting like a magnetic pull. This effect becomes more pronounced closer to the weekly or monthly expiry.

For the **Nifty 50**, the max pain point is at 24,050, which is approximately 1.2% below the current spot price of 24,334.30. This suggests a potential downward pull towards this level if the magnet effect were to dominate as expiry approaches.

The **Sensex** has its max pain at 77,600, about 0.7% below its spot price of 78,151.45. Similar to Nifty, this indicates a potential magnetic pull slightly lower than the current market level.

The **Bank Nifty** stands out with its max pain at 58,600, which is very close to its current spot price of 58,521.40. When max pain is near the spot, it often suggests that the index might consolidate around these levels, as option writers are most comfortable with the price expiring in this vicinity.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Today, FIIs held a long ratio of **11.0%** in Index futures. This means that out of their total open index futures positions, only 11.0% were long contracts, while the remaining 89.0% were short. Historically, such a low long ratio in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from FIIs on the broader market.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of **54.0%**, indicating a slight preference for long positions over short positions in individual stock futures. This suggests a relatively balanced to mildly bullish stance on specific stocks, contrasting with their more cautious approach in index futures.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals daily can empower you to form your own informed perspective. You can access much of this data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, specifically in the F&O section for option chain data and participant-wise open interest. Many brokerage platforms also provide user-friendly interfaces to view option chains.

To combine these data points, start by looking at the Option Chain for the relevant expiry. Identify the strikes with the highest Call OI and Put OI to understand the broad resistance and support levels. Then, observe the "Change in OI" column to see where fresh positions (writing) are being added for the day. Calculate the PCR (OI) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI, and similarly for the daily change (ΔOI). Compare the current spot price to the "Max Pain" strike. Finally, review the FII derivative positioning data, available on the NSE archives, to gauge institutional sentiment in futures. By consistently tracking these metrics, you can begin to identify patterns and understand the collective positioning of market participants, helping you interpret potential market behaviour.
