## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. When we look at options, **Call Open Interest (Call OI)** represents the total number of open Call option contracts at various strike prices. A high Call OI at a particular strike price often indicates that many traders have *written* (sold) Call options at that level, expecting the price to stay below it. This strike then acts as a potential resistance or 'ceiling'. Conversely, **Put Open Interest (Put OI)** is the total number of open Put option contracts. High Put OI at a strike suggests many traders have *written* (sold) Put options there, expecting the price to stay above that level, thus forming a potential support or 'floor'.

For the **Nifty 50**, today saw a significant build-up in Call Open Interest, with net CE OI build at 97.1 million contracts, vastly outweighing the 0.4 million in Put OI build. This heavy fresh Call writing suggests that option sellers are positioning for the upside to be capped, reinforcing the idea of resistance at higher levels.

On the **Sensex**, the aggregate flow showed a net Call OI build of 5.3 million contracts against 1.9 million in Put OI. The heaviest fresh Call writing today was observed near the 85,000 strike. This indicates that option writers believe the Sensex might struggle to move significantly above the 85,000 mark, establishing it as a strong potential ceiling. Interestingly, the heaviest Call OI wall is at 84,000, which is quite close to the current spot, suggesting immediate resistance.

For **Bank Nifty**, while there was fresh Call writing today, the heaviest fresh Put writing was noted near the 62,500 strike. This suggests that Put sellers are confident that the Bank Nifty will find support around or above this level, establishing a potential floor for the index. The overall Put base proximity at 65,000, though further away, also suggests underlying support.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely watched indicator that compares the volume of Put options to Call options. It helps gauge overall market sentiment. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests more Put buying/Call selling (often interpreted as bullish or hedging), while a value below 1 indicates more Call buying/Put selling (often interpreted as bearish). We look at two types: PCR(OI) which uses total Open Interest, and PCR(today's flow ΔOI) which uses only today's change in Open Interest.

For the **Nifty 50**, the PCR(OI) stands at 0.6, which is below 0.7, indicating that Call options dominate the overall Open Interest. This generally suggests that option writers believe the upside is capped. Today's flow ΔPCR was 0.0, implying a relatively balanced fresh flow between Calls and Puts during the session, despite the overall Call-heavy book.

The **Sensex** also recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.6, similar to Nifty, pointing to a Call-dominated options book and suggesting limited upside potential. Furthermore, today's flow ΔPCR was 0.35, which is quite low. This clearly indicates that fresh writing activity was heavily tilted towards Calls, reinforcing the bearish sentiment for the day.

In the case of **Bank Nifty**, the PCR(OI) is 0.96, which is close to 1. This suggests a relatively balanced options book in terms of overall Open Interest, indicating a neutral stance from a long-term perspective. However, today's flow ΔPCR was 0.53, implying that fresh writing activity for the day was more skewed towards Calls, which is a short-term bearish signal for the session's flow.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It's often seen as a "magnet" because option writers, who collectively hold a significant portion of the market, tend to try and push the price towards this level as expiry approaches to maximise their profits.

For the **Nifty 50**, the Max Pain level is at 26,050, which is approximately 1.7% above its current spot of 25,607.20. This indicates that option writers, on aggregate, would prefer the Nifty to gravitate upwards towards 26,050 by expiry.

The **Sensex** has its Max Pain point at 84,300, sitting about 1.2% above its closing spot of 83,339.26. Similar to Nifty, this suggests a potential upward pull towards 84,300 from the perspective of option writers.

For **Bank Nifty**, the Max Pain is located at 64,000, which is roughly 1.5% higher than its spot price of 63,040.90. This also points to an upward magnetic pull towards 64,000 as expiry nears.

It's important to remember that this "magnet effect" tends to become stronger and more influential as the weekly or monthly expiry date draws closer.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are often considered significant players in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment. Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of **22.1%**. This means that out of their total Index futures positions, only 22.1% were long contracts, while the remaining 77.9% were short contracts (long 36,644 contracts vs. short 129,516 contracts).

Historically, such a low FII Index futures long ratio tends to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from these large institutional participants. A higher long ratio would typically suggest a more bullish stance. This data point from the FII derivative positioning indicates a prevailing bearish sentiment among these large players in the index futures segment.

In stock futures, FIIs had a long ratio of **57.1%** (long 3,397,359 contracts vs. short 2,555,574 contracts), suggesting a relatively more balanced to slightly bullish stance in individual stocks compared to indices.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals daily can empower you to better interpret market movements. You can start by visiting the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website (nseindia.com) or your broker's trading platform (like Fyers, which provides API data). Look for the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty.

On the Option Chain, you'll find the Open Interest (OI) for both Call and Put options at various strike prices. Identify the strike with the highest Call OI and the highest Put OI – these are your potential resistance and support levels, respectively. Next, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI. A PCR below 1 suggests Call dominance (potential resistance), while above 1 suggests Put dominance (potential support). Also, keep an eye on the "change in OI" column to see where fresh buying or selling (writing) activity is concentrated for the day. Finally, track the FII derivative positioning, usually available on the NSE website under "Participant-wise Open Interest," to understand how institutional investors are positioning themselves in index futures. Combining these data points daily can help you form a more informed perspective on the market's underlying sentiment.
