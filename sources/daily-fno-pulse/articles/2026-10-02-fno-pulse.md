## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been settled. When we look at the highest Call OI strike, it indicates a level where a significant number of call options have been sold (or "written"). Option sellers, or "writers," profit if the index stays below this strike, suggesting they believe the index might struggle to move above this level. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike signifies a level where many put options have been sold, implying that option writers anticipate the index finding strong support around this point.

For **NIFTY 50**, the heaviest fresh Call writing today was observed near the 20,950 strike. This suggests that call sellers believe the index might not move significantly above this level in the near term, potentially capping upside movements. On the Put side, there was no dominant fresh writing signal provided, indicating a more balanced flow.

In the case of **SENSEX**, today's heaviest fresh Call writing was concentrated near the 69,000 strike. This positioning by call sellers implies an expectation that the index could face resistance around or below this level. The option chain also shows a significant Call wall at 72,500, which is quite close to the current spot price, reinforcing the idea of a potential ceiling.

For **BANK NIFTY**, fresh Call writing was most prominent near the 51,600 strike. This suggests that some call sellers expect the index to remain below this level. However, the data also highlights a strong Put base at 56,000, suggesting robust support from put writers at that level.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a key indicator that compares the total Open Interest (OI) of Put options to that of Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests that more put options have been written than call options, which can be interpreted as a mildly bullish sentiment from option writers, as they expect the market to find support. Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more call writing, often seen as a mildly bearish signal, as writers expect resistance.

For **NIFTY 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.75. This figure, being below 1, suggests a slightly higher concentration of call writing, implying that option writers are leaning towards a cap on upward movement. The PCR based on today's flow of Open Interest (PCR(today flow ΔOI)) was 0, indicating a balanced or mixed activity between fresh put and call writing during the session.

Similarly, **SENSEX** recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.93, which is also below 1, pointing to a slight dominance of call writing. Its PCR(today flow ΔOI) was also 0, signalling a neutral fresh flow for the day.

**BANK NIFTY** had a PCR(OI) of 0.92, again below 1, suggesting a slight edge for call writing. Its PCR(today flow ΔOI) was 0, indicating balanced fresh activity. For all three indices, the balanced fresh flow (ΔPCR of 0) suggests that while the overall book might have a lean, today's new positions did not significantly alter that bias in one direction.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is a concept in options trading that refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that level. It often acts as a magnetic point, drawing the index towards it, especially as the weekly or monthly expiry date draws closer.

For **NIFTY 50**, the Max Pain strike is at 22,750. This level is approximately 1.5% above the current spot price of 22,421.95. The data suggests that option writers would incur the least loss if Nifty were to settle around 22,750, potentially drawing the index higher towards this point as expiry approaches.

**SENSEX** has its Max Pain strike at 72,500, which is about 0.8% above its current spot of 71,909.70. This also implies a potential upward pull towards 72,500, where option writers are most comfortably positioned.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the Max Pain strike is at 55,500, a significant 1.9% above its current spot of 54,450.75. This relatively higher Max Pain level suggests a stronger potential magnetic pull towards the upside for Bank Nifty as expiry nears.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment.

As per yesterday's data (today's NSE archive not yet published), the FII Index futures long ratio stood at 8.0%. This indicates that FIIs held significantly more short positions (339,779 contracts) than long positions (29,605 contracts) in index futures. Historically, a very low long ratio in index futures has often coincided with periods of cautious or negative market sentiment from these large participants, as they are net short on the broader market direction.

In stock futures, the FII long ratio was 54.3%, with 3,393,649 long contracts against 2,858,568 short contracts. This suggests a slightly bullish stance by FIIs in individual stock futures, indicating selective buying interest in specific companies.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals daily can empower you to better interpret market movements. You can begin by looking at the Open Interest (OI) data for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty options, which is freely available on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section. Pay close attention to the strikes with the highest Call OI and Put OI, as these often act as key resistance and support levels.

Next, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI. You can also track the change in OI for calls and puts during the day to understand fresh positioning. Finally, observe the "Max Pain" strike, which many brokers and financial websites calculate, to gauge where option writers are most comfortably positioned. Combining these data points with FII activity, also available on the NSE website, can help you form a more comprehensive view of the market's underlying structure and potential short-term biases.
