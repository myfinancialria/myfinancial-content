# India F&O Pulse — 30 Jul 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Indian Indices Show Mixed Signals with Underlying Bullish Leaning from Options Data

**TL;DR**
*   **NIFTY 50:** Options data suggests a mildly bullish bias, with significant fresh put writing indicating support at lower levels, even as max pain points slightly lower.
*   **SENSEX:** The overall options structure for Sensex also leans mildly bullish, driven by a high Put-Call Ratio and fresh put writing, despite a close call wall.
*   **BANK NIFTY:** Bank Nifty displays a mildly bullish sentiment, primarily supported by its max pain level being significantly above the current spot price and fresh put writing.

## What the indices did today

The **NIFTY 50** closed the day at 24,317.15, marking a modest gain of +0.28%. The index ended the session comfortably within its implied option-chain range of 24,000 to 24,600, suggesting that option writers currently anticipate the index to remain within these boundaries for the near term.

The **SENSEX** also registered a positive day, climbing +0.35% to settle at 77,928.15. The index finished very close to its upper option-chain wall of 78,000, indicating that this level could act as an immediate point of interest for market participants.

In contrast, the **BANK NIFTY** experienced a slight dip, closing down -0.10% at 57,147.50. The index found itself significantly below its implied option-chain range, which currently shows strong resistance and support converging around the 58,000 mark.

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not been settled. Analysing OI helps us understand where market participants, particularly option writers (sellers), are positioning themselves.

The highest Call Open Interest (OI) strike typically indicates a level where a significant number of call options have been sold. Call options give the holder the right, but not the obligation, to buy an underlying asset at a specified price (strike price). Call writers profit if the price stays below their strike, so a high Call OI strike often acts as a perceived resistance level, suggesting where call sellers expect the market to find a ceiling.

Conversely, the highest Put Open Interest (OI) strike signifies a level where a large number of put options have been sold. Put options give the holder the right, but not the obligation, to sell an underlying asset at a specified price. Put writers profit if the price stays above their strike, making a high Put OI strike a potential support level, indicating where put sellers expect the market to find a floor.

For **NIFTY 50**, today's fresh Call writing was heaviest near 24,583. This suggests that call sellers anticipate the Nifty might struggle to move significantly above this level. On the Put side, the heaviest fresh Put writing was observed near 23,966, indicating that put sellers are building a base, expecting the index to find support around this point. The aggregate flow showed a net Put OI build of 29.4 million contracts, significantly higher than the 11.6 million Call OI build, suggesting a stronger conviction among put writers.

For **SENSEX**, fresh Call writing was concentrated near 78,000, which is very close to the day's closing price and acts as a potential cap. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 77,800, suggesting a floor is being established just below the current spot.

In **BANK NIFTY**, fresh Call writing data was not explicitly highlighted for specific strikes, but the overall flow indicated call-heavy writing. However, fresh Put writing was notable near 56,533, suggesting that put writers are establishing a support level well below the current spot price.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that compares the total Put Open Interest to the total Call Open Interest (PCR(OI)), or the total Put volume to the total Call volume for a specific period (PCR(today's flow ΔOI)). A PCR above 1 generally suggests that more put options are being written compared to call options, which can be interpreted as a bullish signal because put writers expect prices to rise (or at least not fall significantly). Conversely, a PCR below 1 often indicates more call writing, suggesting a bearish sentiment.

For **NIFTY 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stood at 1.18, which is considered a balanced book, leaning slightly towards puts. However, the PCR based on today's fresh flow (ΔOI) was a robust 2.54. This significantly high value indicates that today's new option writing was heavily skewed towards puts, suggesting that fresh participants are actively selling puts, anticipating the index to hold or move higher.

The **SENSEX** displayed a PCR(OI) of 1.54, which is quite high and suggests that put options significantly dominate call options in the overall open interest. This typically points to a well-protected downside. The PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was also 1.54, reinforcing the idea that fresh writing today was predominantly in puts, further strengthening the bullish sentiment from an options perspective.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the PCR(OI) was 0.79, indicating a relatively balanced book but with a slight tilt towards call writing in the overall open interest. The PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was even lower at 0.64, suggesting that today's fresh option writing was predominantly in calls. This specific flow implies that new writers are expecting the Bank Nifty to face resistance or potentially move lower in the short term.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that particular price. It's often viewed as a "magnet" for the underlying price, especially as the weekly or monthly expiry date approaches. The market price tends to gravitate towards this level to inflict the least pain on the majority of option writers.

For **NIFTY 50**, the max pain level is currently at 24,150. With the spot price at 24,317.15, this means max pain is about -0.7% below the current spot. This suggests that, if the market were to expire today, the optimal level for option writers would be slightly lower than the current spot.

The **SENSEX** has its max pain level at 77,600. Compared to its spot price of 77,928.15, max pain is approximately -0.4% below the current level. Similar to Nifty, this indicates a slight downward pull from the max pain perspective.

Interestingly, **BANK NIFTY** shows a max pain level of 58,000, which is a significant +1.5% above its current spot of 57,147.50. This suggests that option writers would benefit most if Bank Nifty were to move upwards towards the 58,000 mark by expiry.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are major participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning is closely watched. Their actions can often provide insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held a long ratio of **10.4%** in Index futures. This means that out of their total Index futures positions, only 10.4% were long contracts, while the remaining 89.6% were short. Historically, such a low long ratio in FII Index futures has often tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from these large institutional players on the broader market indices. Their stock futures long ratio, however, stood at **54.9%**, indicating a more balanced to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks.

## Stocks where money moved today

Observing individual stock movements within the F&O segment can highlight areas of concentrated activity.

Among the top gainers today from the F&O watchlist, **MARUTI** led the pack with a +1.72% rise, closing at ₹14,188.00, accompanied by a volume of 626,000. **RELIANCE** also saw a respectable gain of +1.33% to ₹1,292.90, with significant volume of over 1.2 crore shares. **SBIN** advanced +1.14% to ₹1,025.30.

On the losing side, **TCS** declined by -0.60% to ₹2,431.80. **AXISBANK** was down -0.53% at ₹1,228.90, and **HINDUNILVR** slipped -0.47% to ₹2,107.90. These movements are purely observational and reflect where buying or selling interest was concentrated today.

## What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

Synthesising the various signals, the overall options data for Nifty and Sensex suggests a mildly bullish leaning for tomorrow's session. Both indices saw significant fresh put writing today, as reflected in their high daily flow PCRs, indicating that option writers are establishing support levels and potentially expecting the market to hold or move higher. While max pain for Nifty and Sensex points slightly lower, the strong put writing activity, especially for Sensex with its high overall PCR(OI), suggests underlying strength. Bank Nifty also shows a mildly bullish bias, primarily driven by its max pain level being considerably above the current spot, which historically tends to act as an upward magnet as expiry approaches.

When these signals align with strong fresh put writing and elevated PCRs, the next session has historically tended to see attempts to hold current levels or a modest upward bias, especially if the broader market sentiment remains supportive. However, the FII index futures positioning remains on the cautious side, which could temper significant upside moves.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market dynamics can be a valuable skill. You can access much of this data yourself daily. The National Stock Exchange (NSE) website is a primary source for Open Interest data. Navigate to the 'Derivatives' section and look for 'Option Chain' for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex (via the BSE derivatives section for Sensex options). Here, you can see the total Call and Put OI at various strike prices.

To assess fresh writing, you'll want to look at the 'Change in Open Interest' column. A significant positive change in Call OI at a particular strike indicates fresh Call writing (sellers expecting resistance), while a large positive change in Put OI indicates fresh Put writing (sellers expecting support). You can also calculate the PCR by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI. For FII positioning, the NSE publishes daily participant-wise Open Interest data in CSV format, usually available in the 'Reports' section under 'Derivatives'. By regularly tracking these data points and observing how they evolve, especially closer to expiry, you can begin to form your own informed interpretations of market sentiment.
