## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled or closed. It gives us a snapshot of how many contracts are currently "open" in the market.

The highest Call OI strike typically represents a resistance level where option writers (sellers) believe the index might struggle to move above. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike often acts as a support level, indicating where option writers expect the index to find a floor. These levels are sometimes referred to as "walls" because they can act as barriers to price movement.

For **NIFTY 50**, the heaviest Call OI is observed at 24350, just slightly above the spot price, while the heaviest Put OI is at 24300, slightly below. This suggests a narrow, contested zone. Today's fresh Call writing was heaviest near 24650, indicating that call sellers anticipate a cap on further upside. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing concentrated near 24233, suggesting put sellers expect this level to hold as a floor.

In **SENSEX**, the strongest Call wall is at 78000, with a Put base at 77500. Fresh Call writing today was most significant near 79000, implying that call writers expect the index to remain below this level. On the Put side, heavy fresh writing occurred near 76500, suggesting conviction among put sellers that this level will act as support.

**BANK NIFTY** shows a very tight range, with both the heaviest Call OI and Put OI concentrated at 57500, right around the spot price. Today's fresh Call writing was most prominent near 57600, indicating a potential cap. Conversely, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 57400, suggesting a floor is anticipated there.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 generally suggests that more Put options are open compared to Call options, which some market participants interpret as a potentially bullish signal, as put writers often expect the market to stay above their strike prices. Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more Call options are open, which can sometimes be seen as a bearish signal.

For **NIFTY 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 1.07, suggesting a relatively balanced book with a slight lean towards put options. However, the PCR based on today's fresh flow of Open Interest (PCR(today flow ΔOI)) was -0.29, indicating that today's fresh writing activity saw more Call options being written than Put options, which points to a mixed or slightly bearish flow for the day.

**SENSEX** exhibits a strong PCR(OI) of 1.37, indicating a significant dominance of put options and potentially robust downside protection. This bullish sentiment was further reinforced by today's flow, with PCR(today flow ΔOI) at a high 7.96, signifying a substantial amount of fresh put writing compared to call writing.

**BANK NIFTY** has a PCR(OI) of 0.83, suggesting a more balanced options book, leaning slightly towards call options. However, today's fresh flow showed a strong bullish signal with PCR(today flow ΔOI) at 4.52, indicating that fresh put writing significantly outpaced fresh call writing during the session.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying asset expires at that level. It's often viewed as a potential magnet for the index's expiry price, as option writers, who typically hold larger positions, may try to influence the price towards this point to minimise their losses. This "magnet effect" tends to become stronger as the expiry date approaches.

For **NIFTY 50**, the Max Pain point is at 24250, which is approximately -0.3% below the current spot price. This suggests a potential pull towards slightly lower levels as expiry nears.

**SENSEX**'s Max Pain is located at 77300, about -0.5% below its spot price. This also indicates a potential downward pull for the index towards that level.

**BANK NIFTY**'s Max Pain is at 57600, which is very close to its current spot price. This suggests that the index might gravitate around its current levels as expiry approaches.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can empower you to form your own daily market perspective. You can access options Open Interest data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" for each index. Look for the total Call OI and Put OI at various strike prices to identify the strongest resistance (highest Call OI) and support (highest Put OI) levels.

To calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR), simply divide the total Put OI by the total Call OI. You can also track the change in OI (ΔOI) throughout the day to see where fresh positions are being built, which provides insight into the "flow" of the day's activity. Max Pain calculators are available on various financial websites or through your broker's platform. For FII derivative positioning, the NSE archives publish daily participant-wise Open Interest data, typically by the end of the trading day or early the next morning. By consistently reviewing these data points and observing how they align or diverge, you can develop a more informed understanding of market sentiment and positioning.
