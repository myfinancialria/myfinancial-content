## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides insight into the total participation and conviction behind a particular strike price.

For options, the strike price with the highest Call Open Interest (CE OI) is often seen as a significant resistance level, as many Call option sellers (writers) believe the index will not rise above that point. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put Open Interest (PE OI) is typically viewed as a strong support level, as Put option sellers expect the index to stay above that price.

Today's options activity saw significant fresh Call writing near the 78600 level for Sensex, suggesting that Call sellers anticipate the index might find a cap around this region. Simultaneously, fresh Put writing was observed near 75833 for Sensex, indicating that Put sellers expect this level to act as a floor. For Bank Nifty, fresh Call writing was concentrated near 56500, implying a potential cap, while fresh Put writing near 55566 suggests expectations of a floor. These fresh writing activities highlight where option sellers are actively positioning themselves, betting against price movements beyond these levels.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR(OI) value above 1 typically suggests more Put writing than Call writing, which can be interpreted as a bullish sentiment (Put writers expect prices to rise or stay stable). A value below 1, conversely, suggests more Call writing, often interpreted as a bearish sentiment (Call writers expect prices to fall or stay stable).

For **Nifty 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.82, suggesting a balanced options book with a slight inclination towards Call writing. However, the PCR based on today's fresh flow (ΔOI) is 1.03, indicating that today's new option writing was relatively balanced to slightly Put-heavy. This combination points to a mixed sentiment.

For **Sensex**, the PCR(OI) is 0.8, again indicating a slight dominance of Call writing in the overall book. The PCR from today's flow (ΔPCR) is 1.01, suggesting a balanced fresh writing activity. This also points to a mixed and range-bound outlook.

For **Bank Nifty**, the PCR(OI) is 0.85, showing a balanced overall options book. Crucially, today's flow ΔPCR is significantly higher at 1.94, indicating that fresh Put writing heavily outweighed fresh Call writing today. This strong Put-heavy fresh writing suggests a mildly bullish bias from option sellers for Bank Nifty.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that specific price. It's often thought of as a "magnet" for the index, especially as the weekly or monthly expiry approaches, because option writers collectively have a vested interest in the price expiring near this point.

Today, the Max Pain for **Nifty 50** is at 23900, which is very close to its spot close of 23,960.60. Similarly, for **Sensex**, Max Pain is at 77400, also quite near its spot close of 77,301.40. For **Bank Nifty**, Max Pain is at 56000, again close to its spot close of 55,913.60. When Max Pain is close to the current spot price, it suggests that options writers are positioned for the index to consolidate around these levels by expiry. The closer we get to expiry, the stronger this "magnet effect" tends to be.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held a relatively low Index futures long ratio of **13.6%**, with 34,839 long contracts against 220,818 short contracts. Historically, such a low long ratio in Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish sentiment from this segment of market participants. In contrast, their Stock futures long ratio was higher at **58.4%**, indicating a more balanced to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks. This divergence often suggests FIIs might be hedging their equity holdings or taking selective long positions in specific stocks while maintaining a cautious outlook on the broader index.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily movements. To read this yourself, you can start by looking at the Open Interest (OI) data, which is freely available on the NSE website under the "Derivatives" section. Focus on the Option Chain for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty.

Look for the strike prices with the highest Call OI and Put OI; these act as your immediate resistance and support levels. Next, track the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI. Also, try to observe changes in OI during the day (ΔOI) to see where fresh positions are being built. Many brokerage platforms also provide these data points in an easily digestible format. By combining these observations with the Max Pain level (which you can calculate or find on various financial portals) and FII positioning data (also available on NSE), you can start forming your own informed perspective on where the market might be headed in the short term, always remembering that these are probabilities, not certainties.
