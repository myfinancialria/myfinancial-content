# India F&O Pulse — 15 Sep 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Indian Indices Close Lower Amidst Mixed Options Data, FIIs Show Caution in Futures

*   **Nifty 50:** The options data for Nifty 50 suggests a largely range-bound to mixed outlook for the upcoming session, despite today's decline.
*   **Sensex:** Sensex positioning indicates a mildly bearish bias, with significant fresh call writing observed today.
*   **Bank Nifty:** Bank Nifty data leans towards a mildly bullish sentiment, supported by the "max pain" level and put base proximity.

The **Nifty 50** concluded the day at 23,118.60, registering a decline of 1.19%. The index closed within its option-chain implied range of 21,700 to 24,000, suggesting that market participants generally expect it to operate within these broad boundaries.

The **Sensex** also saw a dip, ending the session at 74,003.82, down 1.04%. Similar to Nifty, Sensex closed comfortably within its implied option-chain range of 71,000 to 77,000, indicating defined boundaries by option writers.

Meanwhile, the **Bank Nifty** experienced a sharper fall of 1.43%, settling at 55,794.75. The index closed below its heaviest Put Open Interest (OI) at 57,500, suggesting that this level, which was previously seen as a strong floor, was breached today.

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been settled. The strike price with the highest Call OI often acts as a significant resistance level, meaning many participants believe the index may struggle to move above it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI typically serves as a strong support level, where a large number of participants expect the index to find a floor.

For **Nifty 50**, the option chain suggests significant resistance around the 24,000 level, where Call OI is concentrated, while strong support appears to be building near 21,700. Today's fresh writing activity, which refers to newly added option contracts, was notably call-heavy. This indicates that a greater number of new Call options were sold compared to Put options, particularly around the current spot levels, suggesting expectations of limited upside.

On **Sensex**, the option chain points to 77,000 as a key resistance level and 71,000 as a crucial support. Today's flow also showed a dominant presence of fresh Call writing, particularly at strikes above the current spot, reinforcing the view that market participants are positioning for a capped upward movement.

For **Bank Nifty**, the implied range from option walls is quite tight, with significant OI at 57,500 on both the Call and Put side. However, today's heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near the 56,500 strike. This suggests that option sellers believe Bank Nifty may face resistance around this level in the near term.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests a bullish sentiment, as more Put options are being written (sold) than Call options, implying that writers expect the market to rise or remain stable. Conversely, a PCR below 1 often indicates a bearish sentiment, with more Call options being written, suggesting expectations of a fall or limited upside.

For **Nifty 50**, the overall PCR(OI) stands at 0.86, which is a balanced book leaning slightly towards a bearish sentiment. However, today's fresh flow, reflected in the ΔPCR of 0.65, indicates that new writing was predominantly in Call options, suggesting a bearish tilt in today's activity.

The **Sensex** shows an overall PCR(OI) of 0.76, which is on the lower side, indicating a more bearish stance from option writers. The ΔPCR for today's flow was even lower at 0.37, signifying a strong bias towards fresh Call writing, reinforcing a bearish sentiment for the day.

**Bank Nifty** has an overall PCR(OI) of 0.92, suggesting a relatively balanced options book, perhaps with a slight bearish lean. The ΔPCR for today's flow was -0.29, indicating a mixed or balanced activity in fresh writing, with no strong directional bias emerging from the day's new positions.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset's price expires at that level. It's often considered a magnet, as the underlying index tends to gravitate towards this point, especially as the expiry date approaches.

For **Nifty 50**, the max pain level is currently at 23,450. This is approximately 1.4% above the current spot price, suggesting that option writers, on aggregate, would prefer the index to expire higher than its current level.

**Sensex** has its max pain point at 75,000, which is about 1.3% above its current spot. This also indicates an upward pull from the perspective of option writers.

**Bank Nifty's** max pain is significantly higher at 57,200, approximately 2.5% above its current spot. This suggests a stronger upward pull from the option writers' perspective, potentially indicating expectations for a recovery or stability at higher levels.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 12.1%, with 46,258 long contracts against 337,310 short contracts. Historically, such a low long ratio in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from FIIs on the broader market indices. This indicates that a substantial majority of their index futures positions are on the short side.

In contrast, their Stock futures long ratio stood at 53.9%, with 3,431,913 long contracts versus 2,931,900 short contracts. This suggests a more balanced to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks, indicating selective buying interest in specific companies even amidst a cautious view on the broader indices.

When various options and futures data points present a mixed picture, as they did today, with Nifty suggesting range-bound movement, Sensex showing a mild bearish lean, and Bank Nifty indicating a mild bullish bias, market movements have historically tended to be characterised by increased volatility and potentially sideways consolidation. Historically, such a combination of factors has often led to sessions where indices may attempt to recover from declines but could face overhead resistance, resulting in a volatile yet potentially range-bound trading day.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements more effectively. You can access much of this data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" and "Participant-wise Open Interest" reports. Many brokerage platforms also provide these data points in an easily digestible format.

When reviewing the data, pay close attention to the Open Interest (OI) at different strike prices for both Call and Put options. Look for strikes with unusually high OI, as these often act as key support and resistance levels. Track the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) for both total OI and the change in OI for the day (ΔOI) to gauge the immediate sentiment from option writers. Additionally, monitoring FII positioning in index and stock futures can provide insight into the sentiment of large institutional players. By combining these different perspectives, you can develop a more comprehensive understanding of market positioning, rather than relying on single indicators.
