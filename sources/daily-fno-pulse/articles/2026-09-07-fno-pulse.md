## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) provides a crucial look into the positions held by market participants. When we talk about the **highest Call OI strike**, we are referring to the strike price where the maximum number of Call options are currently open and outstanding. This level is often interpreted as a significant resistance point, as Call option writers (sellers) believe the underlying index is unlikely to rise above this level by expiry. They profit if the index stays below this strike.

Conversely, the **highest Put OI strike** indicates the strike price with the maximum number of open Put options. This level is typically seen as a strong support zone, as Put option writers believe the index will not fall below this point. Their profit comes if the index remains above this strike.

Looking at today's fresh writing activity, which refers to the new positions added during the day:

For **Nifty 50**, the heaviest fresh Call (CE) writing was observed near the 23,850 strike. This suggests that Call sellers are actively building positions at levels slightly above the current spot, anticipating that the upside might be capped around here. On the Put side, the heaviest fresh Put (PE) writing was seen near 23,616, indicating that Put sellers are establishing a potential floor below the current spot.

In **Sensex**, fresh Call writing was most concentrated around 76,400, again pointing to Call sellers expecting resistance at these higher levels. Simultaneously, fresh Put writing was significant near 76,000, reinforcing this level as a potential support.

For **Bank Nifty**, the heaviest fresh Call writing was noted near 57,733. This indicates that Call writers are positioning themselves for the index to stay below this mark, despite the overall balanced PCR.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely used indicator that helps gauge market sentiment by comparing the total Open Interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests that more Put options are open than Call options, which can be interpreted as a potentially bullish sentiment from option writers who expect prices to rise. Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more Call options are open, often signalling a potentially bearish sentiment as Call writers anticipate prices to fall.

For **Nifty 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.57. This value, being significantly below 1, clearly indicates that Call options dominate the open interest, suggesting a prevailing cautious or mildly bearish sentiment among option writers who are positioning for capped upside. The PCR based on today's flow of Open Interest (ΔPCR) was -0.24, which, while indicating a mixed or balanced flow for the day, doesn't significantly alter the overall call-heavy bias.

The **Sensex** PCR(OI) is 0.61, also firmly below 1, reinforcing the dominance of Call options and a bearish undertone. Today's flow ΔPCR was 0.08, which suggests that fresh writing activity for the day was also tilted towards Calls, further strengthening the bearish signal from the overall OI.

**Bank Nifty** presents a more neutral picture with a PCR(OI) of 0.94. This value is close to 1, indicating a relatively balanced book between Put and Call open interest, suggesting a neutral to range-bound sentiment. The ΔPCR for today's flow was -0.01, further confirming a mixed or balanced flow of fresh positions for the day.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is a fascinating concept in options analysis. It refers to the strike price at which the maximum number of option buyers (both Call and Put) would incur the greatest financial loss if the index were to expire at that level. Conversely, it is also the strike price where option sellers (writers) would realise their maximum profit. As expiry approaches, the underlying index is often observed to gravitate towards this "Max Pain" strike, acting like a magnet.

For **Nifty 50**, the Max Pain strike is at 24,100. This is approximately 1.3% above the current spot price, suggesting a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry draws nearer.

The **Sensex** Max Pain strike is at 77,000, which is about 1.1% above its current spot. This also indicates a potential upward gravitational pull, similar to Nifty.

**Bank Nifty's** Max Pain is positioned at 57,500, approximately 0.7% above its spot price. This implies a potential tendency for the index to move towards this level. It's important to remember that the "magnet effect" of Max Pain tends to become stronger as the weekly or monthly expiry date gets closer.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements more effectively. To read this yourself daily, you would typically look at several key data points.

Firstly, always check the **spot price** of the indices and compare them to the **Option Chain data**. Specifically, identify the strike prices with the highest Call Open Interest (the "Call wall") and the highest Put Open Interest (the "Put base"). These levels often act as immediate resistance and support. You can find the Option Chain on the NSE India website under the "Derivatives" section, or through your broker's trading platform.

Secondly, calculate or find the **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)**, both for total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) and for the change in Open Interest (ΔPCR) for the day. A PCR below 1 suggests more Call writing (bearish), while above 1 suggests more Put writing (bullish). Also, look for the **Max Pain** strike, which can be found on various options analytics platforms. Finally, keep an eye on **FII derivative positioning** data, which is published daily on the NSE India website under "Participant-wise Open Interest". By combining these data points – observing where the index closed relative to OI walls, understanding the sentiment from PCR, noting the Max Pain magnet, and seeing how large institutions are positioned – you can build your own informed perspective on the market's likely short-term direction.
