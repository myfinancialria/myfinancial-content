## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides valuable insight into market sentiment and potential price levels where significant activity is concentrated.

When we talk about the **highest Call OI strike**, we are looking at the price level where the maximum number of Call option contracts are currently open. This strike often acts as a strong resistance point, as Call option sellers (also known as writers) profit if the index stays below this level. Their collective positioning suggests they do not expect the index to rise significantly past this point. Conversely, the **highest Put OI strike** indicates the price level with the most open Put option contracts. This level typically serves as a strong support, as Put option sellers profit if the index remains above this strike, implying they expect the index not to fall below it.

For the Nifty 50, the heaviest Call OI is concentrated at the 23,000 strike, which is just above today's closing price. This indicates a strong ceiling for potential upward moves. Today's fresh Call option writing was notably heavy near the 22,966 level, suggesting that new sellers are anticipating the Nifty to remain capped around this region.

In the Bank Nifty, the heaviest Put OI is observed at the 55,000 strike, which was almost exactly where the index closed today, suggesting it could act as an immediate floor. However, fresh Call option writing was concentrated near the 55,300 strike, indicating that new sellers are expecting resistance just above current levels.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a key sentiment indicator calculated by dividing the total Open Interest (OI) of Put options by the total OI of Call options. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests that more Put options are open than Call options, which can be interpreted as a bullish signal, as Put sellers expect prices to rise. Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more Call options are open, often signalling a bearish sentiment as Call sellers expect prices to fall.

For the Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.68. This value, being well below 0.7, suggests a dominance of Call options, indicating that option writers are collectively positioned for limited upside. Furthermore, today's fresh option writing, reflected by the PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR), is significantly lower at 0.37, reinforcing a strong call-heavy bias for the day's new positions.

The Sensex shows an even lower PCR(OI) of 0.58, indicating a pronounced dominance of Call options and a strong bearish signal from the overall open interest. Today's fresh writing also shows a call-heavy bias with a ΔPCR of 0.42.

Bank Nifty's PCR(OI) is at 0.85, which is closer to a balanced book, suggesting a more neutral overall sentiment from existing positions. However, today's fresh option writing, with a ΔPCR of 0.04, is extremely call-heavy, indicating that new option sellers are strongly betting against significant upward movement.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is a concept in options trading that refers to the strike price at which the maximum number of options will expire worthless, causing the least financial loss for option writers (sellers). As expiry approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this "max pain" strike, as option writers collectively try to minimise their losses. It acts like a magnetic pull on the index.

For the Nifty 50, the max pain point is currently at 23,200, which is approximately 1.7% above today's spot price. This suggests a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry nears.

The Sensex has its max pain at 74,700, about 1.3% above its current spot. This also indicates an upward magnetic pull.

Bank Nifty's max pain is situated at 55,500, roughly 1.0% above its spot price. This similarly suggests a potential upward drift towards this strike.

It's important to remember that while max pain indicates a potential magnetic pull, especially closer to expiry, it needs to be viewed in conjunction with other signals like PCR and OI walls to get a complete picture.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Historically, such a low long ratio in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish sentiment from these large institutional players regarding the broader market outlook.

In stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of 56.0%, indicating a net long position (3,660,271 long contracts versus 2,878,721 short contracts). This suggests a relatively more positive stance on individual stocks compared to the overall index.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market dynamics can empower you to interpret daily F&O data. You can access the raw data from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website. Navigate to the 'Derivatives' section, then 'Equity Derivatives', and look for the 'Daily Bhavcopy' or 'Option Chain' for specific indices like Nifty or Bank Nifty.

To calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR), simply sum up the Open Interest for all Put options and divide it by the sum of Open Interest for all Call options for a given expiry. You can also look at the 'Change in Open Interest' column in the option chain to identify where fresh buying or selling (writing) has occurred for the day. For FII positioning, the NSE archives often provide 'Participant-wise Open Interest' data. By observing these data points daily and understanding what each signal suggests, you can develop your own perspective on market sentiment and potential support and resistance levels. Remember, it's about combining multiple data points to form a holistic view, rather than relying on any single indicator.
