## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. For options, the strike price with the highest Call OI typically acts as a significant resistance level, as many call option sellers (writers) believe the price will not rise above this level. Conversely, the strike price with the highest Put OI often serves as a strong support level, as put option sellers expect the price to remain above it.

Today's data provides interesting insights into where option writers are positioning themselves. For the NIFTY 50, the heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near 24,616, indicating that call sellers anticipate the index might struggle to move significantly higher than this level. Simultaneously, the heaviest fresh Put writing was seen near 24,350, suggesting that put sellers expect this level to act as a floor. The aggregate flow for Nifty showed a substantial net Put OI build of 45.7 million contracts, significantly outweighing the 18.3 million in Call OI build, which tends to be a bullish signal as it implies more participants are betting against a downside move.

On the SENSEX, the heaviest fresh Call writing was near 78,133, implying a potential cap on upward movement. However, fresh Put writing was concentrated around 78,100, suggesting a strong immediate floor.

For BANK NIFTY, fresh Call writing was heaviest near 57,800, indicating a potential resistance. The heaviest fresh Put writing was near 57,233, suggesting a nearby support. Notably, the aggregate flow for Bank Nifty showed a net Call OI build of 0.4 million, while Put OI build was negligible, which could be interpreted as a slight bearish leaning from today's fresh positioning.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that helps gauge market sentiment. It is calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests a bullish sentiment, as there are more put options outstanding than call options, implying that more market participants are expecting prices to rise or are hedging against a fall. Conversely, a PCR below 1 often indicates a bearish sentiment.

For the NIFTY 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at a robust 1.34. This value, being well above 1, suggests that put options significantly outnumber call options, indicating a strong underlying bullish sentiment and potentially well-protected downside. The PCR based on today's fresh flow of Open Interest (PCR(today flow ΔOI)) is even higher at 2.49, which means that today's new option writing was heavily skewed towards puts. This combination points towards a strong conviction among option writers that the Nifty is unlikely to see a significant fall in the near term.

The SENSEX recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.92, which is close to 1, suggesting a relatively balanced options book. The PCR(today flow ΔOI) was 0.94, also indicating a mixed or balanced flow of fresh writing for the day. This suggests that option writers are not showing a strong directional bias for Sensex.

For the BANK NIFTY, the PCR(OI) was 0.75, which is below 1, indicating a slight dominance of call options. The PCR(today flow ΔOI) was a very low 0.03, implying that today's fresh writing was overwhelmingly concentrated in call options. This combination, particularly the low daily flow PCR, suggests that option writers are anticipating limited upside for the Bank Nifty.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option buyers would collectively experience the maximum financial loss if the underlying asset were to close at that price on expiry. It's often considered a magnet, as the underlying index or stock price tends to gravitate towards this level as the expiry date approaches. This is because option writers, who typically hold larger positions, benefit when the price closes near the max pain point.

For the NIFTY 50, the max pain point is at 24,150. With the spot price at 24,383.60, this means max pain is about 1.0% below the current spot. This suggests a potential downward pull towards 24,150 as expiry nears.

The SENSEX's max pain is precisely at its ATM (At The Money) strike of 78,100, which is very close to its spot price of 78,094.64. When max pain is at or very near the spot price, it often indicates a tendency for the index to consolidate around current levels.

For the BANK NIFTY, max pain is at 58,000, which is 1.3% above its spot price of 57,264.85. This indicates a potential upward pull towards 58,000 as expiry approaches.

It's important to remember that the magnet effect of max pain becomes stronger as the weekly or monthly expiry date draws closer.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides clues about market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held a long ratio of **11.1%** in Index futures, meaning that only 11.1% of their total index futures positions were long, while the remaining 88.9% were short. This suggests a predominantly bearish stance in index futures from the FII segment. Historically, a very low FII index futures long ratio has tended to coincide with cautious or negative market sentiment.

In contrast, FIIs held a long ratio of **55.1%** in Stock futures, with 3,504,120 contracts long against 2,859,657 short. This indicates a slightly bullish bias in individual stock futures, suggesting selective buying interest in specific companies.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can empower you to form your own informed perspective on market movements. You can begin by looking at the Open Interest (OI) data, which is readily available on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the derivatives section, or through your broker's trading platform. Pay close attention to the strike prices with the highest Call OI and Put OI for the current expiry; these often act as key resistance and support levels.

Next, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI. Also, look at the change in OI for calls and puts to understand fresh positioning. Finally, observe the FII (Foreign Institutional Investor) and DII (Domestic Institutional Investor) derivative data, also available on the NSE website, to see how these significant market participants are positioning themselves in index and stock futures. By regularly tracking these metrics and understanding their historical tendencies, you can develop a clearer picture of the market's underlying sentiment and potential direction for the upcoming sessions.
