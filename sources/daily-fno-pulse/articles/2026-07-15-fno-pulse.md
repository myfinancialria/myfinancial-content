## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding option contracts that have not yet been settled. When we talk about "highest Call OI" or "highest Put OI," these strikes are often seen as significant levels of resistance and support, respectively. A **Call OI wall** indicates a strike where a large number of call options have been sold, implying that many market participants expect the price to struggle to move above this level. Conversely, a **Put OI wall** signifies a strike where numerous put options have been sold, suggesting that a strong base of support might exist below which the price is unlikely to fall easily.

For the Nifty 50, the heaviest Call OI is observed at the 24,200 strike, which is just 0.5% above the current spot price. This suggests a strong ceiling in the immediate vicinity. Today's fresh writing in Nifty was notably concentrated in Call options, particularly around the 24,283 strike. This indicates that new call sellers entered the market, positioning for the index to remain capped around these levels.

On the Sensex, the option chain shows a significant Call OI wall at 78,000. Fresh Call writing today was heaviest near the 77,700 strike, reinforcing the view that option sellers anticipate resistance around this zone. Interestingly, there was also heavy fresh Put writing near 76,233, suggesting that some participants are also creating a floor for the index at lower levels.

For Bank Nifty, the heaviest Put OI is noted at 59,000, which is considerably above the current spot price. Today's fresh Call writing was most prominent near 58,400, implying a potential cap. However, there was also significant fresh Put writing around 56,866, suggesting that option sellers are also building a support base below the current levels.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely watched indicator that helps gauge overall market sentiment by comparing the total Open Interest of Put options to that of Call options. A PCR value below 1 typically suggests that more Call options have been written (sold) than Put options, often interpreted as a bearish sentiment as call writers profit if the market stays below or falls from their strike. Conversely, a PCR above 1 suggests more Put writing, which can be seen as a bullish signal.

For the Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.69. This value, being well below 1, clearly indicates a dominance of Call options being written, suggesting that market participants are largely positioned for limited upside. Furthermore, today's flow, measured by the change in Open Interest (ΔPCR), is even lower at 0.58. This indicates that fresh option writing today was heavily skewed towards Calls, reinforcing the bearish undertone.

The Sensex also exhibits a similar pattern, with its PCR(OI) at 0.69. This again points to a call-heavy options book, implying that upside movements might encounter significant resistance. Today's fresh flow ΔPCR for Sensex is 0.66, further confirming that new option writers are predominantly selling Calls.

Bank Nifty presents a slightly different picture. Its PCR(OI) is 0.78, which, while still below 1, is closer to a balanced book compared to Nifty and Sensex. Today's flow ΔPCR for Bank Nifty is 0.86, suggesting a more mixed or balanced flow of fresh writing, with a slight tilt towards Put writing compared to the other indices. This indicates a relatively more neutral to mildly bullish sentiment among Bank Nifty option writers.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is a concept in options trading that refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the option contract were to expire at that specific price. As expiry approaches, the underlying asset's price often tends to gravitate towards this "max pain" strike, acting like a magnet. This is because option writers, who are typically large institutional players, have a vested interest in the price expiring at a point where their collective losses are minimised.

For the Nifty 50, the max pain strike is currently at 24,300. This is approximately 0.9% above the current spot price of 24,078.50. This suggests that the "pin pull" or magnetic effect of max pain is currently pointing towards an upward movement for expiry.

The Sensex's max pain strike is at 77,800, which is about 0.8% above its current spot price of 77,185.43. Here too, the max pain point indicates a potential upward pull for the index as expiry draws closer.

For the Bank Nifty, the max pain strike is significantly higher at 58,500, representing a 1.3% premium over its spot price of 57,757.85. This suggests a stronger upward magnetic pull for Bank Nifty compared to the other indices, aligning with its mildly bullish signals from other data points. It is important to remember that while max pain offers a directional hint, it is just one of many indicators to consider.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are often considered significant players in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Today's data shows that FIIs hold a very low Index futures long ratio of just **8.4%**. This means that for every 100 index futures contracts held by FIIs, only 8.4 are long (expecting prices to rise), while the vast majority (91.6%) are short (expecting prices to fall). Specifically, FIIs hold 26,357 long index futures contracts against a substantial 289,069 short contracts. Historically, such a low long ratio in FII index futures has tended to coincide with periods of caution or downward pressure on the broader market indices.

In contrast, FIIs' positioning in Stock futures shows a more balanced picture, with a long ratio of **53.7%**. This indicates a slight bullish bias in individual stock futures, with 3,770,340 long contracts against 3,252,827 short contracts. This suggests that while FIIs are cautious on the broader market indices, they maintain a relatively neutral to slightly positive view on specific stocks.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can help you form your own perspective on market sentiment. You can access options data from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, typically under the "Derivatives" section, where you'll find the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and other indices. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in an easily digestible format.

To interpret the data, first, look at the **Option Chain** for the current expiry. Identify the strike prices with the highest **Open Interest (OI)** for both Calls and Puts. These are your potential resistance (Call wall) and support (Put wall) levels. Next, observe the **change in Open Interest (ΔOI)** for the day. Significant increases in Call OI at specific strikes indicate fresh selling pressure (expecting prices to fall or stay below), while increases in Put OI suggest fresh buying (expecting prices to rise or stay above). Calculate the **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI. A PCR below 1 often signals bearish sentiment, while above 1 leans bullish. Finally, check the FII derivative positioning data, usually available on the NSE website, to see how institutional players are positioned in index futures. Combining these data points can provide a comprehensive view of market participants' collective expectations for the upcoming session.
