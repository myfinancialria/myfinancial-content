## Decoding today's open interest

In the options market, **Open Interest (OI)** refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been closed. When we talk about the highest Call OI strike, it represents the price level where the maximum number of Call options have been sold (written) and are still open. Call writers typically sell options when they anticipate the underlying index's price will *not* rise above that strike. Therefore, a high Call OI strike often acts as a resistance level, indicating a potential ceiling for the index.

Conversely, the highest Put OI strike denotes the price level where the maximum number of Put options have been sold and remain open. Put writers generally sell options when they expect the underlying index's price will *not* fall below that strike. Thus, a high Put OI strike frequently functions as a support level, suggesting a potential floor for the index.

For the **Nifty 50**, today's fresh writing saw a substantial build-up in Call Open Interest, with 42.8 million contracts added, significantly outweighing the 7.5 million in fresh Put Open Interest. The heaviest Call OI wall is positioned very close to the spot price at 24,600, just 0.1% above the closing level, indicating strong and immediate resistance.

In the **Sensex** options, fresh Call writing was notably heavy near 78,866, suggesting that Call sellers anticipate the index may struggle to move much higher. Simultaneously, fresh Put writing was significant near 77,333, implying that Put sellers expect the index to find support around this level. The heaviest Call and Put OI walls are both concentrated at 78,500, right at the spot price, pointing to a very narrow expected range.

For **Bank Nifty**, the heaviest Call and Put OI walls are both positioned at 58,000, which is approximately 0.4% above the current spot price. This suggests that option writers are positioning for the index to potentially gravitate towards this level, where both resistance and support are currently concentrated.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The **Put-Call Ratio (PCR)** is a widely used sentiment indicator, calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests more Put options are open than Calls, which some interpret as a bullish signal (as Put sellers expect prices to rise). Conversely, a value below 1 indicates more Call options are open, often seen as a bearish signal (as Call sellers expect prices to fall). We also look at the PCR based on today's fresh flow (ΔPCR) to understand immediate sentiment.

For **Nifty 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.66. This figure, being below 0.7, indicates a dominance of Call options, suggesting potential upside resistance. The PCR based on today's fresh flow (ΔPCR) is even lower at 0.17, highlighting that today's new option writing was heavily skewed towards Calls, reinforcing a bearish sentiment from fresh positioning.

The **Sensex** shows a PCR(OI) of 0.76, which is relatively balanced, suggesting a neutral stance from overall open interest. However, today's flow ΔPCR is 0.54, indicating that fresh writing today was more Call-heavy, which could imply a bearish bias from the day's new positions.

**Bank Nifty's** PCR(OI) is 0.8, also indicating a relatively balanced book of overall open interest. The ΔPCR for today's flow is -0.52, which suggests a mixed or balanced flow in fresh writing, not strongly favouring either Calls or Puts on a net basis for the day.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

**Max Pain** is the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that specific price. It's often considered a "magnet" for the index, as option writers, particularly large institutions, have a vested interest in the price expiring near this level to maximise their profits or minimise losses. This magnetic pull tends to strengthen as the expiry date approaches.

For **Nifty 50**, the Max Pain is identified at 24,900, which is approximately 1.3% above the current spot price. This suggests that, from the perspective of option writers, there might be an upward pull towards this level by expiry.

**Sensex's** Max Pain is at 78,900, about 0.5% above the current spot. This indicates a potential upward drift towards this level as expiry nears, according to the positioning of option writers.

For **Bank Nifty**, the Max Pain is located at 58,000, which is roughly 0.4% above the current spot price. This suggests a mild upward bias towards this strike from the option writers' perspective.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can empower you to form your own perspective on market sentiment. You can access Open Interest data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically looking for "Option Chain" for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in an easily digestible format.

To interpret the data, first identify the highest Call and Put Open Interest strikes to gauge potential resistance and support levels. Then, observe the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) to understand the overall sentiment – a PCR below 1 often suggests a bearish tilt, while above 1 can indicate a bullish bias. Finally, look at the Max Pain strike to see where option writers are positioned, as this can act as a magnetic pull for the index. Combining these elements daily can help you build a clearer picture of market participants' expectations.
