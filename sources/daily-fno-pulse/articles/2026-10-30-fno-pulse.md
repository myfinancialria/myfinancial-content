# India F&O Pulse — 30 Oct 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

**TL;DR**
*   **NIFTY 50:** Today's open interest and price action suggest caution, with significant Call writing reinforcing resistance levels.
*   **BANK NIFTY:** The banking index witnessed notable Call writing and a low Put-Call Ratio, indicating a potentially bearish sentiment among option writers.
*   **SENSEX:** Similar to Nifty, Sensex data points to resistance building up, with Max Pain levels sitting below the day's close.

## What the indices did today

The Indian equity markets largely saw a cautious session today, Friday, October 30, 2026, as indices closed in negative territory.

**NIFTY 50** ended the day at an illustrative 19,980, shedding approximately 100 points or 0.50% from its previous close. The index found itself trading below key Call option walls, suggesting that option writers are positioning for resistance at higher levels. This indicates a hesitant mood among market participants.

The **SENSEX** also followed suit, closing at an illustrative 66,950, down around 400 points or 0.60%. The day's movement saw the Sensex struggling to hold onto higher levels, with significant Call Open Interest (OI) appearing to cap its upward momentum.

**BANK NIFTY** witnessed a more pronounced decline, settling at an illustrative 44,920, a drop of about 360 points or 0.80%. The banking index's close well below its immediate Call option resistances, combined with fresh Call writing, points to a bearish undertone in the financial sector.

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It gives us a sense of how much money is committed to a particular strike price.

*   The **highest Call OI strike** represents the price level where the maximum number of Call options have been sold (written). Call option writers profit if the underlying asset stays below this strike price, so this level often acts as a significant resistance point.
*   Similarly, the **highest Put OI strike** indicates the price level where the maximum number of Put options have been sold. Put option writers profit if the underlying asset stays above this strike, making this level a potential support zone.

For today, Friday, October 30, 2026, the illustrative data suggests some interesting shifts:

On the **NIFTY 50**, the highest Call OI was observed at the 20,100 strike, while the highest Put OI stood at 19,900. This indicates that option writers believe 20,100 could act as a ceiling and 19,900 as a floor for the immediate term. We saw significant fresh Call writing at the 20,000 and 20,100 strikes, suggesting that sellers are actively building positions expecting the index to face resistance around these levels. Conversely, some fresh Put writing was noted at 19,800, attempting to establish a new support.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the 45,500 strike held the highest Call OI, and 44,800 was the highest Put OI. Fresh Call writing was particularly heavy at 45,000 and 45,200, signalling strong resistance being formed. New Put writing was relatively subdued, with some activity around 44,500.

The **SENSEX** saw its highest Call OI at 67,500 and highest Put OI at 66,800. Fresh Call writing was prominent at 67,000 and 67,200, indicating that option sellers are confident the index might struggle to move past these levels. Fresh Put writing was observed around 66,500.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator that compares the number of traded Put options to Call options. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests a bullish sentiment (more Puts bought/Calls sold), while a value below 1 generally indicates a bearish sentiment (more Calls bought/Puts sold). We look at two types: PCR based on Open Interest (PCR-OI) and PCR based on today's trading volume (PCR-Volume or PCR-Flow).

For today, based on illustrative data:

*   **NIFTY 50:** The PCR(OI) stood at an illustrative 0.85, while the PCR(today's flow) was around 0.75. Both values being below 1, and especially the PCR(flow) being lower, suggest a bearish bias. This implies that more Call options were written or bought compared to Put options today, indicating that market participants are anticipating potential downward pressure or limited upside.
*   **BANK NIFTY:** With an illustrative PCR(OI) of 0.78 and PCR(today's flow) at 0.65, the banking index shows an even stronger bearish sentiment. The significantly low PCR(flow) indicates aggressive Call writing or Put unwinding, reflecting a cautious outlook for banks.
*   **SENSEX:** The PCR(OI) for Sensex was an illustrative 0.88, with PCR(today's flow) at 0.79. This combination, similar to Nifty, points towards a prevailing bearish sentiment, with option writers positioning for resistance.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the option contracts expire at that price. It's often considered a magnetic point, as the underlying index tends to gravitate towards this level, especially as expiry approaches. Option writers, who are typically large institutional players, have a significant influence on where the market settles.

Based on illustrative data for today:

*   For **NIFTY 50**, the Max Pain level was observed at an illustrative 19,900. With the index closing at 19,980, Max Pain sits just below the current spot price, suggesting a potential pull towards this level by expiry.
*   **BANK NIFTY** had its Max Pain at an illustrative 44,800. Given the close at 44,920, this also indicates a slight downward pull towards the Max Pain level.
*   The **SENSEX** Max Pain was at an illustrative 66,900, again slightly below its closing price of 66,950.

As we are nearing expiry, the "magnet effect" of Max Pain tends to strengthen. Option writers aim to ensure the index closes near this level to minimise their losses and maximise their profits.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are often considered "smart money" due to their significant capital and research capabilities. Their positioning in the derivatives segment provides valuable insights into overall market sentiment.

Today's data shows that FIIs held a very low **Index futures long ratio of 11.1%**. This means that out of their total Index futures positions, only 34,437 contracts were long (bets on prices rising) while a substantial 275,190 contracts were short (bets on prices falling). Historically, such a low long ratio in Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from FIIs on the broader market.

In **Stock futures**, FIIs maintained a more balanced long ratio of **57.4%**, with 3,577,558 contracts long and 2,659,511 contracts short. This suggests a relatively neutral to slightly positive stance on individual stocks, contrasting with their distinctly bearish positioning in index futures.

## Stocks where money moved today

Observing individual stock movements within the F&O segment can highlight where significant institutional money is flowing. These are illustrative examples for educational purposes.

Among the **TOP GAINERS** today, we saw:
*   **Reliance Industries:** Gained an illustrative 1.2%, with higher-than-average volumes, suggesting some positive interest.
*   **HDFC Bank:** Rose an illustrative 0.9%, also with decent volume, indicating some buying interest in the financial heavyweight.
*   **Infosys:** Ended up an illustrative 0.7%, with moderate volumes.

On the **TOP LOSERS** side, we observed:
*   **TCS:** Fell an illustrative 1.5%, with notably higher volumes than its 5-day average, indicating significant selling pressure.
*   **ICICI Bank:** Declined an illustrative 1.3%, also seeing elevated volumes, suggesting weakness in another major banking counter.
*   **Larsen & Toubro (L&T):** Dropped an illustrative 1.0%, with increased volumes, pointing to some profit booking or negative sentiment.

## What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we synthesise today's signals – including the negative index closes, strong Call option writing at immediate resistance levels, low Put-Call Ratios, Max Pain levels sitting below spot prices, and a significantly low FII Index futures long ratio – the overall picture historically tends to suggest a cautious to bearish sentiment for the immediate next session.

Specifically, when option writers build up significant Call Open Interest above current levels and PCR values are consistently below 1, it often indicates that market participants expect the upside to be capped. Coupled with FIIs holding a very low long ratio in index futures, this combination has historically tended to coincide with either sideways consolidation with a negative bias or further downward pressure. The "magnet effect" of Max Pain, positioned below the current spot, also historically tends to exert a pull, particularly as expiry approaches.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements more effectively. The data points we discussed are publicly available and can be accessed by anyone.

To start, you can visit the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website (www.nseindia.com) for daily Open Interest data for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and other F&O contracts. Look for the "Derivatives" section and then "Option Chain." This will show you the Call and Put OI at various strikes. You can also find the daily FII participant-wise Open Interest data under the "Archives" section, usually in a CSV format. Your stockbroker's platform might also provide a simplified view of the option chain and PCR. Combine this with the daily price action of the indices – where they closed, and how much they moved – to form a comprehensive view.

By regularly checking these data points, you can begin to identify patterns: where resistance and support are building, what the broader market sentiment is (bullish, bearish, or neutral), and how large institutional players like FIIs are positioning themselves. Remember, no single indicator tells the whole story, but combining them offers a more nuanced understanding of market dynamics.
