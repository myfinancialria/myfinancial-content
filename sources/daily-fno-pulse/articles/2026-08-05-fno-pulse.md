## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding Call and Put option contracts that have not yet been closed or exercised. It provides a snapshot of where market participants, particularly option writers (sellers), are positioning themselves.

The highest Call OI strike typically represents a significant resistance level, as many Call options have been sold at this price, implying writers believe the underlying asset will not go above this point. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike acts as a strong support level, as Put writers expect the asset to stay above this price.

For **Nifty 50**, the overall open interest data indicates a stronger presence of Call writers, suggesting a potential cap on upside movement. Today's fresh writing further reinforced this, with a notable increase in Call selling compared to Put selling. However, there was also significant fresh Put writing observed near the 24,266 level, indicating that some participants expect this to act as a floor.

In **SENSEX**, the dominance of Call options in the overall OI also points to resistance overhead. The fresh writing activity for the day was more balanced, but still leaned slightly towards Call writing.

For **BANK NIFTY**, the heaviest Call OI is observed at 58,000, which is very close to the current spot price, indicating a potential ceiling. Simultaneously, the heaviest Put OI is also near 58,000, suggesting a strong base. Today's fresh Call writing was most prominent near 58,800, indicating expectations of a cap at higher levels, while fresh Put writing was concentrated around 57,100, suggesting a floor is anticipated there.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator calculated by dividing the total Open Interest of Put options by the total Open Interest of Call options. A PCR above 1.0 generally suggests bullish sentiment (more Puts written than Calls), while a ratio below 1.0 often indicates bearish sentiment (more Calls written than Puts). We also look at the PCR based on today's fresh flow (ΔOI) to gauge immediate sentiment.

For **Nifty 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.6, which is below 0.7, typically signalling that Call options dominate, and upside movement might be capped. Today's fresh flow PCR (ΔPCR) was even lower at 0.54, further indicating that new option writers were predominantly selling Calls.

**SENSEX** also showed a PCR(OI) of 0.68, again below 0.7, suggesting a similar Call-heavy positioning. However, its fresh flow ΔPCR was 0.75, which is closer to a balanced flow but still indicates a slight preference for Call writing today.

**BANK NIFTY** presented a PCR(OI) of 0.73, which is considered more balanced. Its fresh flow ΔPCR was 1.15, suggesting that today's new writing activity saw more Puts being written than Calls, which can be interpreted as a mildly bullish sign for the immediate session.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It's often seen as a magnetic point, with the underlying index tending to gravitate towards it as expiry approaches.

For **Nifty 50**, the Max Pain level is at 24,950, which is approximately 1.3% above its current spot price of 24,624.65. This suggests a potential upward pull towards this level.

**SENSEX** has its Max Pain at 79,100, about 0.7% above its spot of 78,581.00, also indicating a mild upward bias from this indicator.

**BANK NIFTY's** Max Pain is at 58,000, roughly 0.5% above its closing spot of 57,739.95. This aligns with the idea of a potential upward drift towards this crucial level. As expiry draws nearer, the influence of the Max Pain strike tends to strengthen.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can empower you to form your own informed perspective on market movements. You can typically find Open Interest data on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and other indices. Your broker's trading platform might also provide a simplified view of this data.

To interpret it, look for the strikes with the highest Call OI and Put OI – these are your key resistance and support levels. Track the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) for overall sentiment. A PCR below 1.0 often suggests more Call writing (bearish), while above 1.0 indicates more Put writing (bullish). Also, pay attention to "Max Pain," which you can often find on various financial data websites; it's a theoretical point of minimum pain for option writers. Combining these elements, along with FII positioning data (also available on the NSE website under "Participant-wise Open Interest"), can help you gauge the market's pulse each day.
