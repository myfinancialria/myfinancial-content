## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options contracts provides valuable insights into where market participants, particularly option writers (sellers), expect the underlying index to find support or face resistance. The strike with the highest Call OI typically acts as a strong resistance level, a ceiling that the index may struggle to cross. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI often serves as a robust support level, a floor below which the index may find it difficult to fall.

For the **NIFTY 50**, today's heaviest fresh Call writing, where new sellers entered the market, was observed near the 24,250 strike [cite: data]. This suggests that call sellers anticipate the index might struggle to move significantly above this level, effectively placing a potential cap on upward movement [cite: data]. On the Put side, the heaviest fresh writing was seen near the 23,850 strike, very close to the spot closing price [cite: data]. This indicates that put sellers expect this level to act as a floor, preventing further significant downside [cite: data]. The aggregate flow for Nifty showed a substantial net Call OI build of 21.8 million contracts, significantly outweighing the 2.1 million net Put OI build, which leans towards a bearish sentiment from fresh positioning [cite: data].

In **SENSEX**, the heaviest fresh Call writing today was concentrated around the 76,400 strike, which is right at the spot closing price [cite: data]. This suggests that call writers believe the index may face immediate resistance at current levels [cite: data]. On the other hand, fresh Put writing was most prominent near the 76,300 strike, just below the spot, indicating that put sellers are forming a base around this level [cite: data].

For **BANK NIFTY**, fresh Call writing was heaviest near the 56,766 strike [cite: data]. This implies that call sellers are positioning for the index to face a ceiling around this level [cite: data]. Conversely, the heaviest fresh Put writing was observed near the 56,233 strike, indicating that put sellers are establishing a potential support base there [cite: data].

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that helps gauge market sentiment. It is calculated by dividing the total Open Interest of Put options by the total Open Interest of Call options (PCR(OI)), or by comparing the fresh Put writing to fresh Call writing for the day (PCR(today flow ΔOI)). A PCR above 1 generally suggests a bullish sentiment as more puts are being written (expecting prices to rise), while a PCR below 1 often indicates a bearish sentiment as more calls are being written (expecting prices to fall). A PCR close to 1 suggests a balanced market.

For the **NIFTY 50**, the PCR(OI) stood at 0.7 [cite: data]. This figure, being below 1, suggests that overall, there is a higher Call Open Interest compared to Put Open Interest, leaning towards a slightly bearish long-term outlook from existing positions [cite: data]. More notably, today's flow ΔPCR was 0.1 [cite: data], which is significantly below 1. This indicates that the fresh option writing for the day was heavily skewed towards Call options, reinforcing a bearish signal for the immediate term [cite: data].

The **SENSEX** presented a PCR(OI) of 0.94 [cite: data], which is close to 1, suggesting a relatively balanced book from an aggregate open interest perspective [cite: data]. However, today's flow ΔPCR was 1.57 [cite: data]. This figure, being well above 1, suggests that a significant amount of fresh Put writing occurred today compared to Call writing, indicating a bullish bias from the day's fresh positioning [cite: data].

For **BANK NIFTY**, the PCR(OI) was 0.77 [cite: data], similar to Nifty, suggesting a slight bearish tilt from the overall open interest [cite: data]. Today's flow ΔPCR was -0.24 [cite: data]. While a negative value here might seem unusual, it signifies a strong imbalance where Call writing significantly outpaced Put writing, or there was significant Put unwinding, leading to a negative change in the ratio. This indicates a mixed to bearish flow for the day, despite the overall bias leaning bullish from other indicators [cite: data].

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers, collectively, would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that specific price. It's often considered a "magnet" because as expiry approaches, the underlying index tends to gravitate towards this level, as option writers try to minimise their collective losses.

For the **NIFTY 50**, the Max Pain strike is at 24,200 [cite: data]. This is approximately 1.4% above the current spot price of 23,869.60 [cite: data]. This suggests that option writers are positioned for a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry nears [cite: data].

The **SENSEX** has its Max Pain strike precisely at 76,400 [cite: data], which is right at its current spot price of 76,391.39 [cite: data]. When Max Pain is at or very close to the spot, it often indicates a market that option writers expect to consolidate around current levels [cite: data].

For **BANK NIFTY**, the Max Pain strike is significantly higher at 57,900 [cite: data]. This is about 2.3% above its current spot price of 56,592.00 [cite: data]. This substantial difference suggests a strong potential upward pull for the Bank Nifty towards this level, especially as expiry approaches [cite: data].

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements more effectively. You can access options open interest data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) India website, usually under the "Derivatives" section, where you'll find option chains for various indices and stocks. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in an easily digestible format.

To calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR), simply divide the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest for a given expiry. For "Max Pain," you would typically look for the strike price where the sum of losses for all outstanding Call and Put options is at its minimum. Some advanced charting tools and broker platforms offer this calculation readily. By regularly tracking these data points – PCR, Max Pain, and fresh OI builds at specific strikes – and observing FII positioning, you can develop a more holistic understanding of market sentiment and the potential range of movement expected by large participants. Remember to look at multiple data points together, as no single indicator tells the whole story.
