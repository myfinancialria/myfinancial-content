# India F&O Pulse — 24 Nov 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Index Options Data Suggests Mixed-to-Range-Bound Outlook for Nifty and Sensex, While Bank Nifty Shows Mildly Bullish Cues After Today's Decline

**TL;DR**
*   **Nifty 50:** The options data for Nifty 50 points towards a range-bound or mixed outlook for the upcoming session, despite today's decline.
*   **Sensex:** Similar to Nifty, Sensex options positioning indicates a largely range-bound or mixed sentiment among participants.
*   **Bank Nifty:** Despite a significant drop today, Bank Nifty's options data leans towards a mildly bullish bias, with key put support appearing close.

## What the indices did today

Today, November 24, 2026, saw India's benchmark indices close lower, with volatility, as measured by the India VIX, rising by 2.19% to 17.7.

The **Nifty 50** closed at 25,349.65, marking a 1.75% decline for the day. This close placed the index below its Max Pain point of 25,650, but still comfortably within the broader option-chain implied range of 24,000 to 26,000, suggesting that option writers anticipate the index staying within these boundaries for the current expiry.

The **Sensex** also experienced a significant drop, closing at 82,435.99, down 1.77%. Its closing level was below its Max Pain point of 82,900. The option chain for Sensex indicates a broad implied range between 79,500 and 85,000, within which the index settled today.

The **Bank Nifty** witnessed the sharpest decline among the major indices, falling 3.17% to close at 62,585.35. Despite this drop, its Max Pain point stands at 63,100, slightly above the current spot. The option chain suggests a strong concentration of open interest around 65,000, which could act as a significant level.

## Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides insights into the liquidity and sentiment surrounding different strike prices.

For options, the strike price with the highest Call Open Interest typically acts as a significant resistance level, meaning many participants expect the index to struggle to move above it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put Open Interest often serves as a strong support level, where participants anticipate the index finding a floor.

Today, for **Nifty 50**, the heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near the 23,900 strike. "Call writing" means selling Call options, and it suggests that these sellers believe the Nifty 50 will likely not rise significantly above this level, effectively placing a potential cap on upward movement. This is notably below the current spot, indicating some cautious sentiment. The highest Put OI, which acts as a support, is within the 24,000-26,000 range.

In **Sensex**, the heaviest fresh Call writing was seen near the 79,500 strike. This indicates that Call sellers are positioning themselves for the index to potentially face resistance around this level, which is also below the current spot. The highest Put OI is within the 79,500-85,000 range.

For **Bank Nifty**, the heaviest fresh Call writing was near the 59,700 strike, again below the current spot, implying that Call sellers anticipate a potential ceiling there. However, a significant Put base, or the heaviest Put Open Interest, is noted at 65,000. This level is only about 3.9% below today's closing spot, suggesting a relatively close floor of support from Put writers.

## PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the number of Put options traded or outstanding to the number of Call options. It helps gauge overall market sentiment. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests a bullish bias, as more Put options are being written (sold) than Calls, implying expectations of stability or upward movement. A value below 1 often indicates a bearish bias, while a value around 1 suggests a neutral or balanced sentiment.

For **Nifty 50**, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.83. This value, being below 1, suggests a slightly cautious or balanced sentiment in the overall options book. Today's flow, as measured by the change in PCR (ΔPCR), was 0, indicating a mixed or balanced activity from option participants during the day.

The **Sensex** recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.0, which points to a perfectly balanced options book, with an equal number of Puts and Calls outstanding. Its ΔPCR for today was also 0, reflecting mixed activity.

**Bank Nifty** has a PCR(OI) of 1.03. This value, slightly above 1, suggests a mildly bullish bias in the overall options positioning for the banking index. Its ΔPCR was also 0, indicating balanced activity for the day.

## Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would experience the minimum cumulative loss if the underlying asset expires at that level. It's often considered a magnetic point, as the index tends to gravitate towards this strike as expiry approaches, as option writers aim to minimise their collective payouts.

For **Nifty 50**, the Max Pain point is 25,650. This is approximately 1.2% above today's spot close, suggesting that option writers are collectively positioned for the index to potentially move higher towards this level by expiry.

The **Sensex** Max Pain point is 82,900, which is about 0.6% above its current spot. This also indicates a slight upward pull towards the Max Pain level.

**Bank Nifty** has its Max Pain at 63,100, approximately 0.8% above its spot close. This suggests that option writers in Bank Nifty are collectively positioned for a slight upward movement towards this strike.

It's important to remember that the "magnet effect" of Max Pain tends to strengthen as the expiry date draws closer.

## FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are often referred to as "big money" due to their significant capital deployment in the Indian markets. Their derivative positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

As of yesterday, November 23, 2026 (today's NSE archive not yet published), FIIs held a relatively low Index futures long ratio of **20.1%**. This means that out of their total Index futures positions, only 20.1% were long (bets on price increase), while the majority were short (bets on price decrease). Historically, such a low long ratio in Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish sentiment from FIIs regarding the broader market direction.

In Stock futures, FIIs had a long ratio of **56.7%**, indicating a slightly bullish bias in their individual stock-specific bets.

Today's trading saw some notable movements in F&O watchlist stocks:

Among the top gainers were **TCS**, which rose 3.53% to ₹2460.00 with a volume of 7.38 million shares, and **TITAN**, up 1.30% to ₹4680.00. **ITC** gained 0.64% to ₹301.00, seeing significant volume of over 27.5 million shares, while **INFY** was up 0.49% to ₹1103.40.

On the losing side, several banking stocks saw declines. **AXISBANK** fell 3.97% to ₹1388.60, with a volume of over 10.8 million shares. **HDFCBANK** dropped 3.33% to ₹817.80, recording the highest volume among the losers at over 55.6 million shares. **ICICIBANK** was down 3.32% to ₹1585.50, and **SBIN** declined 2.79% to ₹1132.65. **LT** also saw a drop of 2.59% to ₹4099.90. These movements are observational and reflect where significant trading activity occurred today.

## What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

Synthesising the various signals from today's options and futures data, the overall picture for tomorrow's session appears to be largely range-bound for Nifty 50 and Sensex, with a mildly bullish undertone for Bank Nifty.

For Nifty and Sensex, the balanced PCR values, coupled with fresh Call writing below current spot levels, suggest that option writers are not anticipating a significant upward surge. However, the Max Pain points being above current spot levels indicate a potential upward pull. When these signals align with a mixed or range-bound bias, the next session has historically tended to see the indices consolidate or trade within a defined range, rather than making sharp directional moves.

For Bank Nifty, despite today's sharp decline, the slightly bullish PCR and a Max Pain point above spot, combined with a relatively close Put base at 65,000, suggest that option writers might be anticipating some recovery or stabilisation. Historically, when such a combination of factors is present after a significant fall, the index has tended to find support and potentially consolidate or see a mild upward bounce in the subsequent session.

The FIIs' low long ratio in Index futures indicates a cautious stance from institutional players, which historically tends to coincide with limited upside potential in the near term for the broader market.

## How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these daily F&O movements can empower you to better interpret market sentiment. To read this data yourself, you can start by visiting the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, specifically the "Derivatives" section. Here, you'll find the daily Bhavcopy and the "Participant-wise Open Interest" reports.

Look for the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty to identify the highest Call Open Interest (resistance) and highest Put Open Interest (support). You can also track the change in Open Interest (ΔOI) for specific strikes to see where fresh buying or selling (writing) is occurring. For the Put-Call Ratio (PCR), many financial websites and broker platforms provide this calculated value, or you can calculate it by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI. Max Pain can also be found on various options analytics platforms. By consistently observing these data points and how they change relative to the index's spot price, you can begin to form your own informed perspective on the market's underlying sentiment.
