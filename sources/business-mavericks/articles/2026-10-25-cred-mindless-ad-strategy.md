The year was 2021. The Indian Premier League (IPL) was in full swing, and millions of eyes were glued to their screens. Then, a commercial dropped that momentarily eclipsed the on-field action. It featured Rahul Dravid, "The Wall" of Indian cricket, known for his unshakeable calm and gentlemanly demeanor, in a fit of unexpected, hilarious road rage. "Indiranagar ka gunda hoon main!" he roared, smashing a car's rearview mirror with his bat, a stark contrast to his public persona. The ad, for a then-nascent fintech platform called CRED, went instantly viral, spawning memes and discussions across social media. It was absurd, unexpected, and seemingly had little to do with paying credit card bills. But this was exactly the point.

### The World Before

Before CRED entered the scene in 2018, paying credit card bills in India was a chore, a mundane banking transaction devoid of any aspiration or reward. Users typically navigated clunky bank portals, net banking interfaces, or generic payment apps like PhonePe and Google Pay. The market was undifferentiated, focused on utility, and certainly not on creating a "premium experience" for financially responsible individuals. Fintech companies generally vied for transaction volume across a broad user base, often through aggressive discounts or cashback offers. The idea of building a "members-only" platform, deliberately excluding the majority of potential users, and then spending astronomical sums on celebrity ads that barely explained the product, was considered foolish, if not suicidal, by conventional marketing wisdom.

### The Crack

Kunal Shah, CRED's founder, had a different vision. Having previously co-founded FreeCharge, Shah understood the Indian consumer landscape deeply. He identified a significant, yet underserved, segment: India's most creditworthy individuals. These were people with high credit scores (750 or above), who paid their bills on time, yet received little to no recognition or reward from the traditional financial system. CRED wasn't just another payments app; it was designed to be an exclusive club for these financially disciplined users, turning membership into a coveted status symbol.

The actual story of CRED's rise began with a bold, almost counter-intuitive marketing strategy centered on absurdist humor and high-profile celebrity endorsements. In September 2020, CRED became an official partner of the Indian Premier League (IPL) in a three-season deal reported to be around ₹120 crore. This partnership provided a massive platform for their unconventional ad campaigns.

Their first major campaign during IPL 2020, titled "Not Everyone Gets It," featured Bollywood icons from the 90s like Anil Kapoor, Madhuri Dixit, and Bappi Lahiri. These ads depicted celebrities humorously "auditioning" for a CRED commercial or performing in bizarre, self-deprecating scenarios that subverted their established public personas. The ads made no direct attempt to explain CRED's product features, instead focusing on entertainment and intrigue. The tagline itself, "Not Everyone Gets It," subtly reinforced the brand's exclusivity, implying that if you didn't understand the humor, perhaps the platform wasn't for you either.

But it was the IPL 2021 campaign, "Great for the Good," that truly cemented CRED's place in popular culture. The "Indiranagar ka Gunda" ad featuring Rahul Dravid losing his cool in traffic became a cultural phenomenon. This 23-second spot, which first aired on April 9, 2021, quickly garnered millions of views and trended on social media. The genius lay in the stark contrast: the calmest man in Indian cricket portrayed as an angry, bat-wielding hooligan. Other ads in this series featured celebrities like Jackie Shroff, Kapil Dev, and Javagal Srinath in equally unexpected roles, relying on nostalgia and casting against type to create memorable, shareable content.

The strategy was simple: sell the ad, not the product directly. By creating intriguing, entertaining, and highly shareable content, CRED aimed to generate massive brand recall and word-of-mouth. The ads were designed to be "talked about as cultural content, not merely consumed as commercial messaging." This approach involved a substantial financial commitment. In FY21 (April 2020 - March 2021), CRED spent ₹418 crore on marketing. This figure surged to an astounding ₹976 crore in FY22 (April 2021 - March 2022), the year of the viral Rahul Dravid campaign. To put this into perspective, in FY22, for every rupee CRED earned in revenue, it spent nearly ₹2.50 on advertising.

### What Happened Next

The massive marketing investment paid off in terms of brand awareness and user acquisition. Following the initial IPL 2020 campaign, CRED reported a 6 to 7 times increase in daily sign-ups and app downloads surged by approximately 700%. The "Indiranagar ka Gunda" ad alone helped the company achieve significant virality.

This brand building translated quickly into investor confidence and a rapid increase in valuation. CRED achieved unicorn status (a valuation of $1 billion or more) in April 2021, just days after the Dravid ad went viral, with a valuation of $2.2 billion. By June 2022, its valuation peaked at $6.4 billion. Despite the significant marketing expenditures contributing to substantial losses (₹1,280 crore loss in FY22 against ₹394 crore revenue), the company's user base continued to grow, reaching over 12 million members by 2024.

In more recent times, while still reporting losses, CRED has shown progress in narrowing them. In FY25, operating revenue reached ₹2,735 crore, and operating loss fell by 51% to ₹298 crore, with gross margins at about 70%. The company even reported its first profitable quarter in January-March 2026. As of June 2026, CRED's valuation stood at $4.5 billion, following a $900 million Series H funding round led by Meta Platforms. The core strategy of attracting high-quality users allowed CRED to expand its offerings into various financial services like lending (CRED Mint), wealth management (through Kuvera acquisition), and commerce, deepening its monetization potential beyond just bill payments.

### Lessons to Take Away

1.  **Exclusivity as a Powerful Differentiator:** In a crowded market, deliberately limiting your target audience can create immense aspiration and a sense of belonging for those who qualify. CRED's 750+ credit score entry barrier made membership a status symbol, transforming a mundane task into a premium experience.
2.  **Brand Building Trumps Immediate Feature Explanation (for the right segment):** While most startups focus on performance marketing and explaining product features, CRED invested heavily in building an aspirational brand identity through emotional, shareable content. For a premium audience, the feeling of being part of an exclusive club can be more compelling than a list of features.
3.  **The Power of Absurdist Humor and 'Against Type' Casting:** CRED's use of celebrities in unexpected, self-deprecating, and humorous roles generated massive earned media. By subverting public personas (like Rahul Dravid's calm demeanor), they created content that people *wanted* to talk about and share, amplifying their message far beyond paid reach.
4.  **Strategic Patience and Deep Funding are Crucial for Long-Term Brand Plays:** Spending hundreds of crores on marketing while incurring significant losses is not feasible for every business. CRED's ability to execute this strategy was underpinned by substantial early funding, allowing them to prioritize brand building and user acquisition over immediate profitability, with a clear long-term monetization roadmap for its high-value user base.
5.  **Nostalgia and Cultural Resonance Drive Engagement:** The choice of 90s celebrities like Anil Kapoor, Madhuri Dixit, and even Rahul Dravid, tapped into a strong sense of nostalgia for CRED's target demographic of financially established adults aged 25-40. This emotional connection created deeper resonance and memorability for the campaigns.
