June 24, 2022. The board of Zomato, India's burgeoning food delivery giant, had just approved the acquisition of quick-commerce startup Blinkit (formerly Grofers) for ₹4,447 crore, an all-stock deal valued at $568 million. The news sent Zomato's stock spiraling, shedding over 20% in six straight sessions and nearly $1 billion in market capitalization. Analysts openly questioned the rationale: why would a loss-making company acquire another cash-strapped venture in a segment notorious for burning capital?

Deepinder Goyal, Zomato's co-founder and CEO, remained unfazed. He'd later articulate the decision as one driven by "survival, not long-term strategy", framing quick commerce as a critical, synergistic extension of Zomato's core food delivery business. He envisioned a future where Blinkit would not just complement Zomato, but potentially outgrow it. This audacious bet on 10-minute grocery delivery, at a time when the market was littered with cautionary tales, marked a pivotal moment for Zomato and would ultimately redefine its trajectory.

## The World Before: A Race to the Bottom

The quick commerce landscape in India in 2022 was nothing short of a battlefield. Players like Swiggy Instamart, Zepto, Dunzo, BigBasket, and Amazon Pantry were locked in an intense race for market share, promising consumers groceries and essentials in unheard-of delivery times – often within 10 to 30 minutes. However, this hyper-speed came at a steep price. The business model was inherently challenging, plagued by razor-thin profit margins and exorbitantly high logistics costs.

Delivering hundreds of thousands of small-value orders, often under ₹300, across India's congested metro cities meant navigating complex traffic, narrow lanes, and tight delivery windows. Last-mile logistics alone could account for 12-15% of the transaction value. Operational costs—including rider wages, fuel, rent for micro-warehouses (known as "dark stores"), and packaging—further squeezed profitability. Most quick-commerce platforms reported negative unit economics, with delivery costs per order frequently exceeding ₹50–₹70.

Globally, the sector was already witnessing a shake-out. Many quick-commerce startups in Western markets, once valued in the billions, had collapsed by 2024, serving as stark warnings of the inherent difficulties in the model. Blinkit itself, then known as Grofers, had been struggling. After pivoting from a next-day delivery model to 10-minute delivery in 2021, it faced significant financial and operational challenges, including laying off 1,600 employees in March 2022 and relying on a $150 million loan from Zomato just before the acquisition. The market perception was clear: quick commerce was a cash-burning machine, and Zomato's decision to dive deeper into it was met with widespread skepticism, reflected in its plummeting share price.

## The Crack: A Calculated Bet on Density

Despite the prevailing headwinds, Deepinder Goyal saw an opportunity, or perhaps a necessity. "Quick commerce has been our stated strategic priority since the last one year," he announced. He reasoned that the grocery market was significantly larger than food delivery, and even a small share could substantially boost Zomato's revenues. Rather than building from scratch, Zomato chose to acquire Blinkit for two key reasons: speed and an experienced team. Blinkit offered an established technology platform, an optimized dark store network, and a team with extensive experience in the quick commerce space.

The acquisition, an all-stock deal for ₹4,447 crore ($568 million), was formally completed in August 2022. While initially met with investor disapproval, Goyal believed in the long-term synergy between food and quick grocery delivery, aiming to increase customer "wallet share" and optimize costs by potentially sharing delivery fleets.

The secret sauce for Blinkit's 10-minute delivery promise, as Goyal clarified, wasn't reckless driving by delivery partners. Instead, it was a meticulously designed system built on a dense network of "dark stores"—small warehouses strategically located close to high-demand areas. When an order is placed, items are picked and packed within a mere 2.5 minutes. The delivery partner then travels an average distance of under 2 kilometers, typically completing the delivery in about 8 minutes at an average speed of 15 kmph. Crucially, delivery partners do not have timers on their apps, shifting the focus from individual speed to systemic efficiency.

The integration wasn't without its challenges. Deepinder Goyal candidly revealed that he twice asked Blinkit founder Albinder Dhindsa to step down shortly after the acquisition, citing Dhindsa's struggle to adapt to the new quick commerce model and its demands for different thinking and organizational rebuilding. However, Dhindsa ultimately remained at the helm, a testament to his resilience and Goyal's eventual belief in his leadership.

## What Happened Next: The Unfolding Success

The gamble paid off spectacularly. By 2024, Blinkit had not only stabilized but thrived, becoming a significant growth engine for Zomato. Its revenue from operations nearly tripled in the financial year ending March 31, 2024 (FY24), surging from ₹806 crore in FY23 to ₹2,301 crore. More importantly, Blinkit achieved a critical milestone: it turned adjusted EBITDA positive in March 2024, ahead of expectations.

The growth accelerated further. In the first quarter of FY25 (April-June 2024), Blinkit's revenue increased by 22% quarter-on-quarter to ₹942 crore, with its Gross Order Value (GOV) – the total value of customer orders – rocketing by 130% year-on-year to ₹4,923 crore. By the March quarter of FY24, Blinkit's GOV had already surged 97% year-on-year to ₹4,027 crore. Such was its momentum that Deepinder Goyal, in March 2024, stated his expectation for Blinkit to become larger than Zomato's core food delivery business within the next year. This prediction came true in mid-2025, when Blinkit indeed overtook food delivery to become Eternal's (Zomato's parent company) biggest business.

Blinkit’s aggressive expansion of its dark store network was key to this growth. It added 75 new dark stores in Q4 FY24, bringing the total to 526. The company set ambitious targets, aiming for 1,000 stores by the end of FY25 and 2,000 stores by 2026. By Q1 FY27 (June 2026), Blinkit's network had grown to 2,443 stores. This strategic density allowed it to gain significant market share, capturing approximately 40% of India's quick commerce market by GMV in early 2024, surpassing competitors like Swiggy Instamart. By September 2025, Blinkit's market share exceeded 50%.

Zomato's overall financial health also reflected Blinkit's success. The company reported its first full-year profit of ₹351 crore for FY24, a dramatic turnaround from a loss of ₹971 crore the previous year. For Q4 FY24, Zomato's consolidated net profit stood at ₹175 crore, with revenue from operations reaching ₹3,562 crore. In Q1 FY27, Eternal (Zomato's parent) reported a consolidated net profit of ₹92 crore, with total revenue climbing to ₹20,211 crore, significantly driven by Blinkit’s ₹15,664 crore contribution to the top line. Blinkit's Net Order Value (NOV) in this quarter jumped 86% year-on-year to ₹17,132 crore. Meanwhile, competitors like Swiggy Instamart continued to grapple with lower Average Order Values and negative contribution margins, lagging behind Blinkit's growth and unit economics.

## Lessons to Take Away

1.  **Bold Bets Can Redefine Trajectories:** Deepinder Goyal’s decision to acquire Blinkit was initially met with significant investor backlash and skepticism. However, his conviction that quick commerce was a strategic imperative, even for a food delivery company, proved prescient. Sometimes, the riskiest-looking moves, when rooted in a deep understanding of market shifts and strategic synergies, can unlock exponential growth.
2.  **Acquire for Speed and Existing Capabilities:** Instead of building a quick commerce infrastructure from scratch, Zomato acquired Blinkit. This inorganic growth strategy allowed them to instantly gain an established technology platform, a functional dark store network, and an experienced team, saving invaluable time and resources in a rapidly evolving, competitive market.
3.  **Unit Economics are Paramount, and Achievable:** The quick commerce model is inherently challenging due to high operational costs. Blinkit's success demonstrates that profitability in this segment hinges on meticulous system design, particularly a dense network of dark stores to minimize delivery distances and optimize inventory management. This focus on efficiency, rather than simply speed, was key to turning adjusted EBITDA positive.
4.  **Leadership Agility in Integration:** The post-acquisition period was not smooth, with Deepinder Goyal even considering replacing Blinkit's founder. This highlights the critical importance of leadership adaptability and the willingness to make tough decisions, or have tough conversations, to ensure the acquired entity aligns with the new strategic vision and operational demands.
