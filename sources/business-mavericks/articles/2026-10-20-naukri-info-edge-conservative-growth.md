## Naukri.com: How Sanjeev Bikhchandani Built India's Most Profitable Internet Company by Saying No

In October 1996, at the IT Asia Exhibition in New Delhi, Sanjeev Bikhchandani encountered something that would change the course of his life and India's internet landscape: the "www". He asked a stall owner what it meant and was told it stood for the "world wide web." Intrigued, Bikhchandani learned about the internet, and when he discovered there were around 14,000 internet users in India at the time, he thought, "Wow! That's a lot of people." This seemingly small encounter sparked the idea for Naukri.com, a venture that would defy the prevailing internet startup ethos of its time, choosing a quiet path to profitability over the frenzied chase for valuations, ultimately building India's most enduring and profitable internet conglomerate.

### The World Before the "No"

The late 1990s and early 2000s were a turbulent yet exhilarating time for the internet globally. The dot-com bubble was inflating, driven by a surge in internet adoption, abundant venture capital, and an almost irrational exuberance for new online businesses. Companies, often with little to no revenue, were achieving astronomical valuations, fueled by the mantra of "get big fast" and prioritizing user acquisition or gross merchandise value (GMV) over actual profits. Many believed that profitability could wait, a distant concern to be addressed once market dominance was achieved. This led to massive cash burns and, eventually, a spectacular crash that wiped out trillions in market value and shuttered countless startups by 2002.

In India, the internet was still in its infancy, with broadband access limited and the concept of online classifieds largely unfamiliar. Launching an internet business in such an environment, especially one focused on a niche like job listings, seemed an even more daunting proposition, prone to the same speculative pitfalls as its Western counterparts.

### The Crack: A Contrarian Path to Profit

Sanjeev Bikhchandani, a graduate of IIM Ahmedabad, had spent years in advertising and marketing before quitting his stable job in 1990, driven by an entrepreneurial spirit. The idea for Naukri.com, launched in 1997, wasn't born out of a grand vision for disruption, but from a simple observation: his colleagues at HMM Ltd. (now GlaxoSmithKline Consumer Healthcare) meticulously scanning the classified job pages of *Business India* magazine. He realized there was a clear need for a more efficient way for people to find jobs and for companies to find talent.

Operating out of a small room in Delhi, Info Edge (Naukri's parent company) started with minimal capital. Bikhchandani famously went without a salary for nearly six years while his wife supported the household. The early days were a struggle; in its first year (1997-98), Naukri.com earned a modest revenue of ₹2.35 lakh. However, by the second year (1998-99), as more businesses began to pay for listings, revenue jumped to ₹18 lakh, signaling that this "experiment" could indeed be a serious business.

Unlike many of its dot-com peers, Naukri.com chose a path of deliberate, organic growth, focusing on a robust classifieds business model. Its revenue streams primarily came from B2B services offered to employers and recruitment consultants, including job listings, resume database access (through products like RESDEX), and email marketing. This meant charging companies for value-added services, ensuring revenue generation from day one. Bikhchandani's philosophy was clear: "The purpose of a business is to earn a profit." He actively "said no" to the prevailing wisdom of burning cash to acquire users at any cost, instead focusing on achieving positive unit economics early on.

This disciplined approach, while perhaps appearing "boring" amidst the dot-com frenzy, proved to be its greatest strength. While competitors splurged on advertising and unsustainable expansion, Naukri.com steadily built its database of job seekers and relationships with corporate clients. By 2006, Naukri.com became the first Indian dot-com company to launch an Initial Public Offering (IPO) on an Indian stock exchange, a testament to its sustainable business model.

### What Happened Next

Naukri.com's quiet, profitable rise laid the foundation for Info Edge to become a formidable internet conglomerate. Today, Naukri.com remains India's leading job site, holding the highest market share and brand awareness among both job seekers and recruiters. As of June 2026, the platform boasts a database of 118 million resumes and nearly 700,000 job listings, serving over 49,000 billed corporate customers.

Beyond its flagship brand, Info Edge strategically diversified, leveraging its cash flows and deep market understanding. It owns other prominent classifieds platforms like 99acres.com (real estate), Jeevansathi.com (matrimony), and Shiksha.com (education). Crucially, Info Edge also became one of India's most successful venture capital investors, making early bets on future unicorns like Zomato (food delivery) and Policybazaar (insurance marketplace). Info Edge invested in Policybazaar in 2008 and Zomato in 2010 when their valuations were a mere ₹21 crore and ₹9.4 crore, respectively. Both companies went public in 2021, delivering significant returns.

Info Edge's disciplined investment strategy has yielded impressive results. Since starting its financial investing activity in 2007, it has achieved an estimated 36% gross Internal Rate of Return (IRR) across all its investments. Its overall investment portfolio, including stakes in Zomato and Policybazaar, was valued at approximately $3.7 billion (₹31,500 crore) as of March 31, 2025.

Financially, Info Edge has demonstrated remarkable consistency. Over the past five years (FY21-FY25), its billings have grown at a healthy compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 25.1%, with revenues growing at 23.8%. The company reported a net profit of ₹13,099 million in FY25, up 120.3% from FY24. This sustained performance has compounded Info Edge's market capitalization, making Sanjeev Bikhchandani India's first internet billionaire without resorting to excessive cash burn.

### Lessons to Take Away

1.  **Profitability isn't a dirty word:** While the startup world often champions "growth at all costs," Naukri's journey proves that focusing on unit economics and achieving profitability early can build a resilient, long-lasting business. Prioritizing positive cash flow allows a company to weather market downturns and fund future growth internally, rather than being beholden to external investors.
2.  **Patience is a virtue in nascent markets:** Bikhchandani's willingness to operate without a salary for years and build Naukri slowly in a market with limited internet penetration showcased immense patience and conviction. In emerging markets, it often takes time for user behavior to shift and for business models to mature. Rushing this process with unsustainable spending can be fatal.
3.  **Strategic restraint creates opportunities:** Saying "no" to aggressive acquisitions, overseas adventures, or chasing every fleeting trend allowed Info Edge to conserve capital and focus on its core strengths. This disciplined approach enabled them to make strategic, high-impact investments like Zomato and Policybazaar when others might have been overextended or distracted.
4.  **Classifieds are powerful, resilient businesses:** Naukri.com's success highlights the enduring value of a well-executed classifieds model. By connecting buyers and sellers (job seekers and employers) efficiently and charging for value-added services, it built a high-margin business with strong network effects that has proven difficult for competitors to dislodge.
