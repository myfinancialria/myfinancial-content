On June 24, 2022, the boardroom at Zomato buzzed with an unusual tension. Deepinder Goyal, the company’s co-founder and CEO, was pushing for a decision that many investors, and even some analysts, considered sheer madness: acquiring Blinkit, a struggling 10-minute grocery delivery startup, for $568 million (₹4,447 crores) in an all-stock deal. Zomato, itself a loss-making entity at the time, was proposing to buy another company hemorrhaging cash at an alarming rate – Blinkit was losing ₹100-150 crore monthly. The market reaction was swift and brutal; Zomato’s stock crashed by 10% on the news. Critics called it a "terrible decision," a "bailout disguised as acquisition," and warned it would only delay Zomato's own path to profitability. Yet, Goyal had an unwavering conviction, a "survival instinct," as he later described it. He believed this audacious bet was not just necessary, but a game-changer for Zomato’s future.

## The World Before

The quick commerce landscape in India circa 2022 was a wild west of venture capital and burning cash. Companies like Swiggy Instamart, Zepto, and Dunzo were locked in a fierce battle to deliver groceries and essentials in unheard-of timelines – often 10 to 20 minutes. While consumers revelled in the convenience, the underlying economics were widely questioned. Setting up "dark stores" (small warehouses in residential areas) and maintaining a hyper-efficient delivery fleet was incredibly capital-intensive. Average order values were often low, and delivery costs high, making unit economics – the profitability of each individual order – look unviable. Many experts believed that the quick commerce model had not proven its profitability at scale anywhere globally.

Zomato, primarily a food delivery platform, had its own struggles. While it had grown rapidly, the food delivery business was becoming commoditised, and competition with Swiggy was intense. Both companies were burning cash to acquire and retain customers, leading to thin margins. The pandemic-driven boom in food delivery was showing signs of slowing, and Zomato needed a new growth engine to justify its valuation and long-term prospects. Entering quick commerce was seen as a high-risk gamble, especially by acquiring a company like Blinkit (formerly Grofers), which had reportedly fired 5% of its workforce and was struggling to raise further funds. Analysts from Kotak Institutional Equities even downgraded Zomato's stock, citing that Blinkit would require even more investments amidst rising competition.

## The Crack

Despite the widespread skepticism, Deepinder Goyal saw not a burning hole, but a strategic imperative. He argued that quick commerce wasn't just a complementary business but an essential evolution of last-mile delivery. "Getting into last-mile delivery was something we needed to do to survive," Goyal later stated. He envisioned a future where customers expected everything, not just food, delivered within minutes. Acquiring Blinkit, rather than building from scratch, offered a crucial acceleration into this burgeoning market.

The acquisition, valued at $568.1 million, was completed on August 10, 2022. Goyal addressed concerns about corporate governance, especially given that a senior Zomato leader was married to Blinkit's CEO, stating that 97% of shareholders approved the transaction, aligning his financial outcomes directly with other shareholders as his compensation was stock-linked. He defended the 10-minute delivery promise, clarifying it wasn't about riders speeding dangerously, but about "system design". Blinkit's strategy relied on a dense network of dark stores, ensuring that delivery partners only had to cover short distances (an average of under 2 km) at moderate speeds (around 15 km/h). Orders were picked and packed within 2.5 minutes, making the 10-minute window achievable through efficiency, not recklessness. Delivery partners, he noted, didn't even have timers on their apps.

Under Zomato's wing, Blinkit underwent a significant operational overhaul. The focus shifted to improving unit economics by optimizing dark store operations, reducing waste, and increasing order density. Zomato’s existing delivery fleet infrastructure could also be leveraged, potentially improving fleet utilisation and reducing delivery costs across both food and grocery segments, especially during complementary peak hours. This synergy was a core part of Goyal's rationale.

## What Happened Next

The turnaround was nothing short of remarkable. By 2024, the "terrible" acquisition had transformed into Zomato's fastest-growing segment, dramatically reshaping the company's financial narrative.

Blinkit's growth exploded. In Q1 FY25 (April-June 2024), Blinkit's revenue surged 2.4 times year-on-year to ₹942 crore (approximately $113 million). Its Gross Order Value (GOV) also increased by 2.3X to ₹4,923 crore. Crucially, Blinkit moved from a loss of ₹105 crore in Q1 FY23 to an adjusted EBITDA profit of ₹43 crore in Q1 FY25. By March 2024, Blinkit had turned operationally profitable.

This rapid rise of Blinkit played a pivotal role in Zomato's overall financial health. For the full financial year 2023-24, Zomato reported its first-ever consolidated net profit of ₹351 crore, a significant turnaround from a loss of ₹971 crore in the previous year. Zomato's consolidated revenue for FY24 grew by 67% to ₹12,961 crore. Deepinder Goyal publicly expressed gratitude that "the bet that we took on Blinkit worked out just fine and we are not at a point where Akshant (Goyal, CFO) and I are getting fired for an expensive acquisition gone wrong".

By Q2 FY25 (July-September 2024), Blinkit's revenue continued its upward trajectory, reaching ₹1,156 crore, a 129% year-on-year growth. Its EBITDA loss narrowed significantly to ₹8 crore from ₹125 crore a year earlier. Such was Blinkit's dominance that by Q1 FY26 (April-June 2025), its net order value surpassed Zomato's food delivery business, reaching ₹9,203 crore against ₹8,967 crore, marking a fundamental shift in the company's primary growth driver. By Q2 FY27 (July-September 2026), Blinkit’s revenue of ₹19,152 crore was 5.6 times that of Zomato’s food delivery business in the same quarter, solidifying its position as the main engine of the newly rebranded Eternal Ltd (Zomato changed its corporate name in March 2025 while retaining Zomato for the food delivery app). Blinkit had not only outgrown Swiggy Instamart in market share, but it had also become the driving force behind Zomato's profitability and valuation.

## Lessons to Take Away

1.  **Conviction in the Face of Criticism:** Deepinder Goyal's unwavering belief in the long-term potential of quick commerce, despite immediate market backlash and significant cash burn, proved critical. Sometimes, the most contrarian bets yield the biggest returns when backed by a clear strategic vision and operational execution.
2.  **Synergy is Key, Not Just Scale:** The acquisition wasn't just about buying market share; it was about leveraging Zomato's existing last-mile delivery infrastructure and expertise to improve Blinkit's unit economics. Identifying and executing on these operational synergies transformed a loss-making entity into a profitable growth engine.
3.  **Operational Excellence Drives Profitability:** The success of 10-minute delivery wasn't magic, but meticulous "system design" and a dense network of dark stores. Focusing on efficiency in packing, rider routes, and inventory management turned a seemingly impossible business model into a viable one. Unit economics, often dismissed in growth-at-all-costs phases, became the bedrock of Blinkit's turnaround.
4.  **Adaptability to Market Shifts:** Zomato recognized that food delivery, while significant, might not be the sole future of hyperlocal commerce. By making a bold move into quick commerce, they diversified their offerings and positioned themselves for a broader market, demonstrating an ability to adapt to evolving consumer expectations for instant gratification.
