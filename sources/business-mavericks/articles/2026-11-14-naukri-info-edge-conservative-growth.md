### Naukri.com: How Sanjeev Bikhchandani Built India's Most Profitable Internet Company by Saying No

In 1990, a young brand manager named Sanjeev Bikhchandani found himself standing in the Madurai heat, comparing two Horlicks packaging designs. He’d spent years studying, culminating in an IIM Ahmedabad MBA, only to be deciphering consumer preferences in a dusty market. "I didn't study so much to do this," he recalled thinking. "I didn't want to be a prisoner of EMIs and visiting cards." Within months, he quit his secure job at Hindustan Milkfood Manufacturers (now GlaxoSmithKline Consumer Healthcare), moved into the servant's quarters above his father’s garage in East Delhi, and embarked on an entrepreneurial journey with no computer, no investor, and no salary. It would take him seven years to find the idea that truly worked, Naukri.com, and another three before anyone offered him outside money. This was the unglamorous, bootstrapped beginning of what would become Info Edge, India's internet pioneer, a company that compounded at 30%+ for 25 years, not by chasing hype, but by consistently saying "no" to conventional wisdom.

### The World Before

Before the internet cast its sprawling net, job hunting in India was a fragmented, often frustrating affair. The classified pages of national dailies and business magazines like *Business India* were the primary hunting grounds for opportunities, alongside word-of-mouth and recruitment consultants. Economic liberalization in 1991 had spurred the growth of the service sector, particularly in IT, finance, and healthcare, creating a burgeoning demand for talent. Yet, the mechanisms to connect job seekers with employers remained largely analog.

The global internet boom was just beginning to stir in the mid-1990s, but in India, it was a nascent, almost alien concept. In 1997, when Naukri.com was launched, there were only around 14,000 internet users in the entire country. Bandwidth was scarce and expensive. This was a time when many internet startups globally were burning through venture capital, prioritizing rapid user acquisition and grand visions over sustainable revenue. The dot-com bubble was inflating, fueled by exuberance and the belief that profitability could wait. To launch an internet business in such a nascent market, and then to insist on profitability, seemed either foolish or impossibly old-fashioned.

### The Crack

Bikhchandani’s eureka moment wasn't a sudden flash of technological brilliance, but a simple observation. While still at HMM, he noticed that whenever a copy of *Business India* magazine arrived, his colleagues would instinctively flip to the back, devouring the job advertisements, not always because they sought a new role, but out of sheer curiosity. This quiet, universal human interest in opportunities became the seed for Naukri.com.

In 1995, Bikhchandani founded Info Edge. Two years later, on April 2, 1997, Naukri.com officially launched. The initial operation was rudimentary, far from the polished tech companies of today. Without reliable internet access, the first version of Naukri was updated manually. Bikhchandani's team subscribed to 29 different newspapers daily, meticulously extracting job listings. These listings were then entered into floppy disks and ferried to a friend's home, where they could be uploaded to the internet. The website launched with approximately 1,000 job postings.

Funding was non-existent. The monthly server rent of $25 was initially paid by Bikhchandani’s elder brother, a professor in the US. A critical early decision was made: Naukri would never charge job seekers. The goal was to attract as many users as possible to the nascent platform. Instead, revenue would come from charging employers for job postings. In its first financial year (1997-98), Naukri.com generated a modest revenue of INR 2.35 lakh. The following year, it jumped to INR 18 lakh.

Crucially, Bikhchandani resisted the siren call of venture capital and the "growth at all costs" mentality prevalent in the dot-com era. When prospective investors approached, suggesting that "nobody makes a profit on the internet," and that it was a "valuation game," he chose to walk away. His sole ambition was clear: the company should be viable, break even, and make some money. This commitment to profitability taught the team the value of money, forcing them to do more with less and develop a sharp focus on customer needs.

The company truly began to scale after Hitesh Oberoi joined in 2000, helping to build and professionalize the sales operations. Later that year, after years of bootstrapping, Info Edge finally secured its first institutional investment: INR 7.29 crore from ICICI Ventures. By 2002-03, Naukri.com was profitable, a rare feat for an Indian internet company at the time, especially after the dot-com bubble burst.

### What Happened Next

Naukri.com's conservative, profit-first approach paid off handsomely. In November 2006, Info Edge made history by becoming one of the first internet companies to list on Indian stock exchanges. The Initial Public Offering (IPO) saw the company issue 53.24 lakh new shares at a price of INR 320 each, raising INR 170.36 crore. The issue was oversubscribed about 55 times, demonstrating strong investor confidence. On its listing day, November 21, 2006, the stock closed at INR 593, an impressive 85% jump from its issue price, valuing the company at INR 1,619 crore.

With the steady cash flows generated by Naukri.com, Info Edge diversified strategically, launching other successful classifieds platforms like 99acres.com (real estate), Jeevansathi.com (matrimony), and Shiksha.com (education). More remarkably, Info Edge leveraged its financial strength to become a pioneering venture investor in India. Using its own balance sheet, it made early bets on what would become some of India's most iconic internet companies: Zomato (food delivery) in 2010 and Policybazaar (insurance aggregator) in 2008. Info Edge's investment strategy was characterized by "patient capital," a long-term vision, and providing strategic guidance to its portfolio companies. These investments proved transformative, with both Zomato and Policybazaar eventually going public in 2021, becoming multi-billion-dollar companies.

Sanjeev Bikhchandani’s net worth has soared, reaching an estimated $2.8 billion as of October 2026, making him one of India's first internet billionaires. Info Edge continues to demonstrate robust financial health. For the second quarter of fiscal year 2027 (ending September 2026), the company reported revenue of ₹9.04 billion, an increase of 12.2% year-on-year, and a net profit of ₹4.80 billion, up 51.6% year-on-year. For the trailing twelve months leading up to November 2026, Info Edge reported a revenue of INR 34.73 billion and a net income of INR 17.63 billion. Naukri.com remains India's largest employment website, a testament to its enduring market leadership built on fundamental business principles.

### Lessons to Take Away

1.  **Profitability is a Feature, Not a Bug:** In an era obsessed with growth at any cost, Naukri.com proved that sustainable businesses are built on strong unit economics and a clear path to profitability. Sanjeev Bikhchandani's early refusal to accept investor money when it meant compromising on this principle allowed Info Edge to control its destiny and build a robust, cash-generating machine.
2.  **Patience is a Strategic Asset:** The journey from a garage startup to an IPO took over a decade, and even the initial institutional investment only came after years of bootstrapping. Info Edge's "patient capital" approach, both in building its core business and in its venture investments, highlights that true wealth creation in nascent markets often requires a long-term, disciplined outlook, rather than chasing quick exits.
3.  **Customer-Centricity Over Hype:** Naukri.com succeeded by solving a real, unmet need in the Indian job market: providing a centralized, searchable database of opportunities. By choosing not to charge job seekers, it prioritized user adoption and value, building a critical mass that ultimately attracted paying employers. This focus on fundamental customer problems, rather than technological wizardry alone, secured its market dominance.
4.  **Cash Flow Fuels Opportunity:** The consistent profitability of Naukri.com allowed Info Edge to become a strategic investor, making early, impactful bets on companies like Zomato and Policybazaar. This demonstrates how a strong, self-sustaining core business can create a powerful ecosystem for future growth and diversification, without relying solely on external funding cycles.
