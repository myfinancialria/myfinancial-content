## Why Deepinder Goyal Bet Zomato on 10-Minute Grocery Delivery When Everyone Said It Was Impossible

In August 2022, the Indian financial markets watched in disbelief. Zomato, the food delivery giant, announced it was acquiring Blinkit, then known as Grofers, for a staggering $568 million in an all-stock deal. The reaction was swift and brutal: Zomato's stock crashed 10% in a single day. Analysts and investors alike questioned the sanity of the move. Here was a company still struggling for its own profitability, buying a quick-commerce startup that was bleeding cash at an alarming rate, reportedly ₹100-150 crore every month, with no clear path to break-even. Critics called it a "terrible decision," a "poison pill" that would sink Zomato. Deepinder Goyal, Zomato's co-founder and CEO, however, saw something others didn't – a vision of survival and explosive growth that would, in less than two years, turn Blinkit into Zomato's fastest-growing segment and a primary driver of its profitability.

### The World Before: A Sinking Ship in a Sea of Red Ink

The quick-commerce landscape in India was, to put it mildly, a minefield in 2022. Several players, including Swiggy Instamart, Zepto, and Dunzo, were locked in a fierce battle for market share, promising groceries and essentials in 10-30 minutes. While consumers loved the convenience, the business model itself was capital-intensive, plagued by high operational costs, logistical complexities, and unproven unit economics. Most quick-commerce companies were burning through investor money at an unsustainable pace, prioritizing growth at all costs over profitability.

Blinkit, originally Grofers, was a prime example of this struggle. Founded in 2013, it had pivoted to quick commerce in 2021, promising 10-minute deliveries. Despite raising approximately $630 million from investors like SoftBank and Tiger Global by the end of 2021, the company found itself in dire straits. In March 2022, just months before the acquisition, a cash-strapped Blinkit laid off 1,600 employees, roughly 5% of its workforce. It had even failed to raise funds at a ₹6,000 crore valuation, making Zomato's ₹4,447 crore ($568 million) acquisition price seem bewilderingly high to many.

Meanwhile, Zomato itself faced its own set of challenges. Food delivery, its core business, was becoming commoditized. Competition with Swiggy was brutal, leading to intense cash burn and slowing growth as the post-pandemic boom tapered off. The unit economics of food delivery were deteriorating, with rising delivery costs and capped restaurant commissions. Zomato needed a new growth engine, something with more promising economics to justify its market valuation and ensure long-term relevance. Many saw quick commerce as a black hole for capital, not a solution.

### The Crack: A Bet on Density, Not Speed

Deepinder Goyal, however, saw quick commerce as a "stated strategic priority" and a necessary "survival instinct" for Zomato. He believed that the quick delivery business was synergistic with Zomato's existing logistics network and offered a "right to win" in the long term. Despite the public outcry and a dip in Zomato's stock, the acquisition was completed on August 10, 2022.

The core of Goyal's audacious bet lay in a fundamental redefinition of "10-minute delivery." It wasn't about pushing riders to reckless speeds, a concern that had sparked public debate and safety worries. Instead, it was an intricate game of operational efficiency and hyper-local density. As Goyal explained, Blinkit's 10-minute promise was achieved by strategically locating "dark stores"—small, tech-enabled micro-warehouses stocked with fast-moving goods—in densely populated neighborhoods. Orders are picked and packed within 2.5 minutes, and riders then cover an average distance of less than 2 kilometers, driving at a safe average speed of about 15 kmph. Crucially, delivery partners don't even have a timer on their app indicating the promised delivery time, removing pressure for unsafe driving.

Under Zomato's wing, Blinkit's operational strategy was revamped. Deepinder Goyal, despite initial reservations where he even asked Blinkit founder Albinder Dhindsa to step down twice, ultimately entrusted Dhindsa with leading the quick commerce vertical, leveraging his deep understanding of supply chains. Zomato provided the much-needed capital to sustain losses during the aggressive expansion phase and improve unit economics, which refers to the revenue and costs associated with a single unit (in this case, one order).

The turnaround hinged on several key decisions:
*   **Dark Store Expansion:** Blinkit aggressively expanded its network of dark stores. From approximately 400 stores at the time of acquisition, it reached 526 by Q4 FY24, with plans to hit 1,000 stores by the end of FY25. This expansion drastically reduced delivery distances and times.
*   **Inventory Management:** Leveraging data, Blinkit optimized its inventory within each dark store to stock high-demand products, ensuring quick picking and packing.
*   **Category Expansion:** Beyond groceries, Blinkit broadened its offerings to include categories like electronics, medicines, and even office supplies, increasing average order value (AOV) and customer stickiness.
*   **Integrated Logistics:** Zomato aimed to leverage its existing delivery fleet for both food and quick commerce, optimizing rider utilization and reducing overall delivery costs.

### What Happened Next: Vindication and Explosive Growth

The skeptics were systematically proven wrong. Blinkit, once a cash-burning liability, transformed into Zomato's star performer.

By March 2024, Blinkit achieved adjusted EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization) profitability, a significant milestone that few in the quick-commerce sector had managed. This metric, Adjusted EBITDA, shows a company's operating performance before non-cash expenses and financing costs, giving a clearer picture of its core business health.

The numbers spoke volumes:
*   **Revenue Surge:** Blinkit's revenue from operations nearly tripled year-on-year, growing from ₹806 crore in FY23 to ₹2,301 crore in FY24. For the January-March quarter of FY24 alone, Blinkit reported revenues of ₹769 crore.
*   **Order Growth:** Monthly orders soared from 3.5 million in August 2022 to over 20 million by late 2024. By FY25, Blinkit processed 424 million orders.
*   **Zomato's Profitability:** Driven by Blinkit's turnaround and robust performance in its core food delivery business, Zomato posted a consolidated net profit of ₹175 crore in Q4 FY24, a dramatic swing from a loss of ₹188 crore in the same period a year ago. Zomato's consolidated revenue from operations for Q4 FY24 reached ₹3,562 crore, up from ₹2,056 crore year-on-year.
*   **Market Leadership:** Blinkit rapidly gained market share, now accounting for approximately 45-50% of India's quick-commerce market. It demonstrably outpaced its primary competitor, Swiggy Instamart, processing significantly more orders and expanding its dark store network more aggressively.
*   **Soaring Valuation:** In a remarkable vindication, Goldman Sachs valued Blinkit at an astonishing $13 billion by April 2024. This valuation was not only a six-fold increase from March 2023 but also surpassed the valuation of Zomato's own food delivery business, highlighting the profound shift in market perception. Deepinder Goyal himself stated his belief that "Blinkit will be larger than Zomato in one-tenth the time."

### Lessons to Take Away

1.  **Strategic Vision Trumps Short-Term Metrics:** The Blinkit acquisition was widely criticized for adding a loss-making entity to an already unprofitable Zomato. However, Goyal's long-term vision for quick commerce as a necessary growth engine and a synergistic fit proved more powerful than immediate financial concerns. Sometimes, betting on future potential over current profitability can unlock significant value.
2.  **Redefine the Problem, Redefine the Solution:** The "10-minute delivery" promise, initially perceived as reckless, was re-engineered by Blinkit to be a function of operational design and density, not rider speed. By focusing on dark store networks and efficient packing, they tackled the challenge structurally, making the impossible seem routine and safe.
3.  **Capital Allocation for Long-Term Moats:** Zomato, despite its own unprofitability, had the balance sheet to fund Blinkit's cash-intensive expansion. This strategic capital allocation allowed Blinkit to build a robust network of dark stores, creating a significant competitive moat—a sustainable competitive advantage that makes it difficult for rivals to compete—in a rapidly growing market.
4.  **Embrace Synergies, Even if Unconventional:** The decision to acquire a grocery delivery service by a food delivery company was initially questioned. However, the inherent synergies in last-mile logistics, customer acquisition, and technology proved invaluable, allowing Zomato to leverage its existing strengths and expand its total addressable market.
