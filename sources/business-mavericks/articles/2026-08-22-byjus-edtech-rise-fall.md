## BYJU'S: How India's Most Valuable Startup Wiped Out $22 Billion in 18 Months

In March 2022, Byju Raveendran, the son of school teachers from Kerala, stood at the pinnacle of India's startup ecosystem. His edtech company, BYJU'S, had just raised $800 million, pushing its valuation to a staggering $22 billion, making it the country's most valuable startup. Raveendran himself infused $400 million into this round, increasing his stake and solidifying his image as a visionary who had built a global education empire. The world watched, impressed by the rapid ascent of a company that seemed to embody the limitless potential of India's digital future. Fast forward to October 2024, and Raveendran would admit a stark truth: "The company is worth zero now.". This dramatic collapse, from a $22 billion behemoth to near insolvency in just 18 months, is a masterclass in the perils of growth at all costs.

## The World Before

Before BYJU'S transformed the landscape, India's education sector was largely traditional. Learning was confined to classrooms, textbooks, and a burgeoning but fragmented private coaching industry for competitive exams. Online learning, while present, was nascent and often viewed as a supplement, not a replacement, for conventional methods. The idea of a digital platform catering to K-12 students with engaging, interactive content seemed ambitious, almost impossible, to many. Traditional pedagogy emphasized rote learning, and the penetration of digital tools in homes, especially for educational purposes, was limited. The market was ripe for disruption, but the challenge lay in making online education accessible, affordable, and, most importantly, effective enough to convince skeptical parents and students.

Byju Raveendran, a former engineer and a gifted teacher, began by coaching students for competitive exams like the Common Admission Test (CAT) in 2008. His unique teaching methods, which focused on conceptual understanding rather than memorization, quickly gained popularity. In 2011, he founded Think & Learn Private Limited, the parent company of BYJU'S, shifting focus to the K-12 segment. The launch of "BYJU'S – The Learning App" in 2015, featuring video lessons and interactive exercises, was a game-changer. It tapped into India's growing smartphone penetration and a hunger for better educational outcomes, offering a fresh, exciting approach to learning. Early funding from investors like Sequoia Capital and the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative validated its model and fueled its initial growth.

## The Crack

BYJU'S's growth trajectory was meteoric, particularly during the COVID-19 pandemic when schools shut down and online learning became a necessity rather than an option. User numbers surged, and funding rounds became larger and more frequent. The company embarked on an aggressive acquisition spree, spending heavily to expand its offerings and global footprint.

Key acquisitions that underscored this "growth-at-all-costs" strategy included:

*   **WhiteHat Jr (2020):** Acquired for $300 million, this online coding platform for young children was meant to diversify BYJU'S's portfolio. However, WhiteHat Jr soon became embroiled in controversy due to aggressive advertising campaigns making dubious claims, leading to public backlash and legal disputes. The company faced accusations of misleading parents and even filed defamation lawsuits against critics. By December 2023, WhiteHat Jr was sued by Code.org for unpaid dues related to a licensing agreement.
*   **Aakash Educational Services (2021):** This was BYJU'S's largest acquisition, integrating the traditional test preparation giant for nearly $1 billion. While strategically sound on paper, the funding for this acquisition and its integration later became a source of significant financial strain and legal challenges.

These acquisitions, alongside others like Epic! (US-based digital reading platform for $500 million) and Great Learning (professional learning platform for $600 million), aimed to solidify BYJU'S's position as a global edtech powerhouse. However, this rapid expansion, fueled by massive venture capital inflows, masked underlying operational and financial issues.

The cracks began to widen as reports of **aggressive sales tactics** emerged. Former employees and reports alleged that sales teams were pushed to aggressively market expensive educational packages to families, sometimes encouraging financing arrangements that later became difficult for customers to manage. This pressure-driven work culture and allegations of mis-selling tarnished the company's reputation and eroded trust.

A major red flag appeared with **opaque accounting practices and audit delays**. Deloitte Haskins & Sells, BYJU'S's statutory auditor since 2016, resigned in June 2023 with "immediate effect," citing inordinate delays in the company's financial statements for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2022. Deloitte stated it had not been able to commence the audit due to these delays, which would significantly impact its ability to complete the audit according to standards. This delay was crucial, as audited financial statements for FY22 were due by September 30, 2022. BYJU'S eventually released its FY22 results, reporting a staggering loss of ₹8,245 crore (approximately $860 million at current rates) on a revenue of ₹5,298 crore (approximately $550 million), a significant increase in losses from FY2021. The company had recognized over half of multi-year subscription revenue upfront, a practice that raised concerns.

Adding to the financial woes, BYJU'S defaulted on a $1.2 billion Term Loan B secured in November 2021. Lenders accused BYJU'S Alpha, a US subsidiary, of breaching the loan agreement and unlawfully transferring $533 million out of the United States. A US court later confirmed BYJU'S was in default, allowing lenders to take control of Byju's Alpha Inc.. In November 2025, a US court issued a default judgment holding Byju Raveendran personally liable for over $1 billion for repeatedly defying discovery orders in this case.

## What Happened Next

The aftermath has been brutal. From its peak valuation of $22 billion in March 2022, BYJU'S's value plummeted dramatically. By January 2024, US investor BlackRock had slashed its valuation of BYJU'S to approximately $1 billion—a staggering 95% drop. Other investors followed suit; Prosus NV marked down its valuation to under $3 billion by November 2023, and later wrote off its entire 9.6% stake, citing $493 million in losses. In June 2024, HSBC also stated its 10% stake in the company was worth zero. By October 2024, Byju Raveendran himself acknowledged the company was "worth nothing". Forbes' Billionaire Index 2024 reflected Raveendran's net worth plunging to zero from $2.1 billion a year prior.

Massive job cuts became a recurring theme. In October 2022, BYJU'S announced plans to lay off 2,500 employees, about 5% of its 50,000-strong workforce. Further layoffs of 1,500 employees followed in February 2023, primarily from design, engineering, and production verticals. More job cuts, impacting hundreds of employees, continued throughout 2023 and 2024, totaling nearly 2,500 layoffs since June 2023.

The company entered insolvency proceedings in India, with the Supreme Court ordering continuation of these proceedings in October 2024. Legal battles intensified across India, Singapore, and the United States, including a Singapore court sentencing Byju Raveendran to six months in jail for contempt for failing to comply with court orders related to asset disclosure in May 2026. The company, once a symbol of India's startup ambition, became buried under debt, lawsuits, and unpaid dues.

## Lessons to Take Away

1.  **Sustainable Growth Over Hyper-Growth:** BYJU'S prioritized rapid expansion through aggressive acquisitions and marketing over building a sustainable, profitable business model. This "growth-at-all-costs" philosophy led to a skyrocketing burn rate, where expenditures far outpaced revenue growth, making the company overly dependent on continuous external funding.
2.  **Due Diligence in M&A:** The acquisition spree, particularly of companies like WhiteHat Jr, brought significant reputational and financial baggage. Inadequate due diligence on the acquired entities' business practices and financial health can quickly unravel the core company, especially when integrating disparate business models.
3.  **Transparency and Governance are Non-Negotiable:** The delays in financial reporting, the resignation of Deloitte, and the subsequent allegations of accounting irregularities and fund diversion severely eroded investor confidence and attracted regulatory scrutiny. Strong corporate governance and transparent financial practices are fundamental for long-term trust and stability, especially for a company of BYJU'S's scale.
4.  **Ethical Sales and Customer Trust:** Aggressive sales tactics and alleged mis-selling not only damaged BYJU'S's brand reputation but also led to numerous customer complaints and regulatory actions. In the long run, customer trust built on ethical practices is more valuable than short-term sales targets.
5.  **Beware the Pandemic Bubble:** The surge in online learning during the pandemic created an artificial growth environment. Companies that failed to adapt quickly to the post-pandemic normalization, where offline models regained relevance and demand for online solutions slowed, found their "structural weaknesses" exposed as revenue growth weakened sharply.
