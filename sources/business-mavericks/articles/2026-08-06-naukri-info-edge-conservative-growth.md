## Naukri.com: How Sanjeev Bikhchandani Built India's Most Profitable Internet Company by Saying No

In 1997, from a modest servant's quarter above his father's garage in Delhi, Sanjeev Bikhchandani launched Naukri.com. India's internet landscape then was rudimentary, with a mere 14,000 internet users across the entire nation. Connections were slow, expensive dial-up affairs, and the very idea of an online business seemed like a gamble. While the dot-com bubble was inflating globally, promising quick riches to those who "burned cash" for growth, Bikhchandani chose a different path – one defined not by aggressive expansion or glamorous acquisitions, but by a stubborn insistence on profitability and a disciplined "no" to fleeting trends. This quiet conviction would lay the foundation for Info Edge, Naukri's parent company, to become an internet powerhouse, compounding at over 30% for 25 years and making Bikhchandani India's first internet billionaire without chasing fleeting valuations.

### The World Before the Web

Before Naukri.com, India's job market was firmly rooted in print. Job seekers meticulously scanned the classified pages of newspapers and magazines like Business India, where opportunities were scattered and difficult to navigate. For companies, recruitment was a slow, expensive process, reliant on placing advertisements in multiple publications. The internet, publicly launched in India by VSNL in 1995, was largely an unknown entity, a niche technology for the technically savvy and research communities.

The prevailing skepticism about the internet as a viable business platform was immense. Infrastructure was poor, digital literacy was low, and trust in online transactions was virtually non-existent. Many believed that Indians would never truly embrace online services, let alone pay for them. The idea of an online job portal, aggregating information that was already available in print, seemed not just challenging but foolish to many. This was an era where the concept of "e-recruitment" was alien, and the path to profitability for an internet company in India was utterly unproven.

### The Crack of Discipline

Sanjeev Bikhchandani, an IIM-Ahmedabad graduate who had worked in marketing at companies like GlaxoSmithKline (then HMM Ltd), had observed a peculiar habit among his colleagues: they would eagerly devour the job advertisements tucked away in the back pages of Business India magazine. It was a simple, yet profound, insight: jobs were a "high interest category" even for those who already had one. He realized that if this fragmented information could be aggregated and made searchable online, it would be invaluable.

In 1995, Bikhchandani founded Info Edge, and two years later, in 1997, Naukri.com was born. His initial seed capital was a meagre Rs 2,000. Faced with virtually no marketing budget, Bikhchandani employed a clever, bootstrapped strategy. Instead of paying for advertisements, Naukri.com approached newspapers that lacked strong recruitment sections. Naukri offered to provide these publications with job listings, effectively enhancing their content, in exchange for visibility and branding. This ingenious barter system provided Naukri with valuable exposure without burning precious cash.

While the dot-com bubble of the late 1990s saw many internet startups raising huge sums and spending lavishly in a race for market share, Bikhchandani remained steadfastly focused on fundamentals. He famously advised, "Bootstrap as long as possible; it teaches fiscal discipline". His philosophy was clear: "Investor money is not a guarantee of a viable business, customer money is." This fiscal conservatism was Naukri's lifesaver. In 2000, just before the global dot-com bubble burst, Info Edge raised Rs 7 crore from ICICI Ventures. While 90% of internet startups collapsed in the ensuing bust, Naukri survived and even thrived, largely due to its "sound economics". Bikhchandani consistently emphasized robust unit economics, noting that each new salesperson could generate Rs 50,000 in monthly revenue at a cost of Rs 22,000.

This disciplined approach extended to Info Edge's investment strategy as well. As an investor, Bikhchandani became known for his "anti-portfolio" – a list of successful startups like Flipkart, Ola, Snapdeal, and BigBasket that he chose *not* to invest in. His rationale often stemmed from a belief that these businesses dealt in "atoms" (physical goods with complex logistics) rather than "bytes" (information aggregation), or that their valuations didn't align with his disciplined investment philosophy. He famously dodged early VC meetings, preferring to keep the business simple and self-sustaining until external capital became truly necessary. This unwavering commitment to sustainable growth, even when others chased hyper-growth at all costs, set Naukri.com apart.

### What Happened Next

Sanjeev Bikhchandani's patient, profitable approach culminated in a historic moment for the Indian internet industry. In November 2006, Info Edge (India) Limited went public, becoming one of India's first internet companies to be listed on the stock exchanges. The IPO was a book build issue of ₹170.36 crores, with shares priced at ₹320 each.

From its core offering, Naukri.com rapidly diversified. Info Edge expanded its portfolio to include 99acres.com for real estate, Jeevansathi.com for matrimony, and Shiksha.com for education, applying its successful classifieds-aggregation model to other fragmented Indian markets. Naukri.com solidified its position as India's leading job site, boasting a database of 113 million resumes by Q3FY26.

Info Edge's financial performance reflects its enduring profitability. The company has consistently maintained a debt-free financial position with substantial cash reserves. For the financial year ending March 2026, Info Edge reported a consolidated net profit of Rs 566 crore for the March quarter and total revenue from operations of Rs 869 crore. Annually, Info Edge (India) Ltd recorded a revenue of INR 32.85 billion (approx Rs 3,285 crore) and a net income of INR 14.50 billion (approx Rs 1,450 crore) for FY26. The company has shown a robust profit growth of 137.89% over the past three years and an impressive average operating margin of 37.90% over the last five years.

On the stock market, Info Edge (NSE: NAUKRI) has delivered exceptional returns, showcasing the power of sustainable growth. The company's stock has compounded significantly over the years, with a 10-year return of 660.24% as of August 2026. Sanjeev Bikhchandani, the visionary behind this empire, has seen his net worth soar to an estimated $2.8 billion as of March 2026, solidifying his status as an Indian internet pioneer and earning him the Padma Shri in 2020.

### Lessons to Take Away

1.  **Bootstrapping Builds Resilience:** Naukri.com's early years, marked by extreme frugality and internal funding, instilled a deep fiscal discipline that became a competitive advantage. Operating without a large war chest forced innovation in marketing (like the barter system with newspapers) and a relentless focus on unit economics, making the company resilient when the dot-com bubble burst.
2.  **Customer Revenue Validates, Investor Money Accelerates:** Bikhchandani's mantra that "the best funding is from your customer; it's called sales" highlights the importance of proving product-market fit and a viable business model with customer payments before seeking external capital. Investor money, in this view, should amplify an already working model, not subsidize an unproven one.
3.  **Strategic "No" Creates Focus:** The ability to decline seemingly attractive opportunities, whether for acquisitions or investments, allowed Info Edge to remain focused on its core strengths and avoid distractions that didn't align with its long-term vision or profitability metrics. This discerning approach prevented the company from succumbing to the "fear of missing out" (FOMO) that often plagues growth-focused ventures.
4.  **Profitability is Permanent, Valuation is Temporary:** While many internet companies chased sky-high valuations, Info Edge prioritized consistent revenue growth and strong profit margins. This focus on fundamental business health over speculative market capitalisation ensured its long-term viability and allowed it to become a self-sustaining enterprise, able to invest wisely in new ventures from its own accruals.
5.  **Long-Term Vision in a Short-Term World:** Naukri.com's journey from a small startup to a listed company took 16 years. Bikhchandani's story is a testament to the power of patience and a long-term perspective in building a sustainable business, resisting the pressure for immediate gratification and hyper-growth that often leads to spectacular failures.
