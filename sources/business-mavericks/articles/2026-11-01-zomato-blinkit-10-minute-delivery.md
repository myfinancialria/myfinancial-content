The board meeting on June 24, 2022, was anything but celebratory for Zomato. The company had just announced its decision to acquire quick-commerce platform Blinkit (formerly Grofers) for ₹4,447 crore (approximately $568 million) in an all-stock deal. The market reacted with swift brutality. Zomato's stock crashed 10% in a single day, and analysts across the board decried the move as a desperate bailout, a "terrible decision" for a company already battling to achieve profitability in its core food delivery business. Many saw it as one cash-burning entity buying another, a recipe for disaster. Yet, years later, Deepinder Goyal, Zomato's co-founder and CEO, would look back at that audacious gamble and call it a "survival instinct," stating that without it, Zomato might not have a future in the next decade. This is the story of how Deepinder Goyal bet Zomato on 10-minute grocery delivery when nearly everyone said it was impossible, and how that bet spectacularly paid off.

## The World Before

In 2022, the quick commerce landscape in India was a precarious battleground, largely seen as a black hole for investor capital. The promise of delivering groceries and essentials in 10-30 minutes was alluring to consumers, but the unit economics were a nightmare. Logistical complexities in India’s densely populated cities, high operational costs including rider wages, fuel, and dark store rents, and severe supply chain vulnerabilities made profitability seem like a distant dream. Quick commerce was synonymous with "cash burn".

Blinkit, which had rebranded from Grofers in December 2021 to align with its new 10-minute delivery focus, was struggling. Despite raising around $630 million from investors like SoftBank and Tiger Global by the end of 2021, the company was reportedly burning ₹100 crore monthly and lacked a clear path to profitability. In March 2022, cash-strapped Blinkit laid off 1,600 employees, nearly 5% of its workforce, and Zomato extended a $150 million loan to keep it afloat, signaling its distress.

Meanwhile, Zomato itself faced challenges in its core food delivery business. Competition with Swiggy was fierce, leading to commoditization and brutal cash burn for market share. Growth was slowing after the pandemic boom, and the unit economics of food delivery were becoming increasingly difficult. Zomato needed a new growth engine, a category with better potential to justify its valuation. Swiggy had already launched Instamart, demonstrating the rapid growth and customer love for quick grocery delivery. Zomato risked ceding an entire burgeoning category to its rival if it didn't act decisively.

## The Crack

The decision to acquire Blinkit, announced on June 24, 2022, for $568 million in an all-stock deal, was Zomato's bold move to enter the quick commerce space. It was a strategic imperative, a "stated strategic priority" for Zomato since its initial investment in Blinkit a year prior. Deepinder Goyal saw quick commerce as a business "synergistic with our core food business, giving Zomato the right to win in the long term". The rationale was multi-faceted: gaining entry into the rapidly growing quick commerce market, increasing customer "wallet share" by offering both food and groceries on one platform, and potentially reducing overall delivery costs by leveraging a shared fleet.

The acquisition, completed by August 2022, was just the beginning. The real work was in turning Blinkit around. Zomato's leadership, particularly CEO Deepinder Goyal and Blinkit CEO Albinder Dhindsa (who continued to lead the quick commerce business), focused on fundamental operational improvements.

One of the most critical shifts was Blinkit's pivot to an inventory-led model, rather than just a marketplace connecting customers to local stores. This meant setting up its own "dark stores" – small warehouses strategically located in densely populated neighborhoods – to stock high-demand essentials directly. By managing inventory directly, Blinkit gained control over the supply chain, product quality, and pricing, ensuring consistent availability and faster deliveries. The network of dark stores was crucial for achieving the 10-minute delivery promise, enabling delivery partners to fulfill orders rapidly from nearby locations.

Technology played a pivotal role. Blinkit implemented AI-powered demand forecasting to predict popular items based on neighborhood buying trends, ensuring dark stores were optimally stocked and minimizing waste. Route optimization algorithms assigned orders to the nearest delivery agents, maximizing efficiency and minimizing idle time. Zomato also leveraged its existing delivery network, creating synergies by using its fleet for both food and grocery deliveries, especially during non-peak hours, thereby increasing fleet utilization and reducing overall logistics costs.

Furthermore, Blinkit expanded its product offerings beyond just groceries to include high-margin items like electronics, beauty products, and home appliances, enhancing its average order value (AOV). The introduction of private labels, which constituted about 20% of its total revenue, also contributed to higher profit margins by cutting out intermediaries. Advertising revenue from brands paying for sponsored listings and top-of-search visibility became another significant income stream.

## What Happened Next

The results of this strategic pivot and operational overhaul were astounding. Blinkit, once a distressed asset, became Zomato's fastest-growing segment and a significant driver of its valuation.

*   **Revenue Growth:** Blinkit's financial performance soared. In Q1 FY25 (April-June 2024), Blinkit's revenue surged by 2.4X year-on-year to ₹942 crore, up from ₹384 crore in Q1 FY24. For the full fiscal year 2024, Blinkit's revenue from operations was ₹2,301 crore, nearly tripling from ₹806 crore in FY23. By FY25, it clocked ₹5,206 crore in revenue.
*   **Profitability Milestone:** A crucial turning point came in March 2024, when Blinkit achieved adjusted EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization) positivity, a significant milestone for a business previously known for its heavy cash burn. This operational profitability signaled that the quick commerce model, when executed efficiently, could indeed be sustainable. In Q1 FY25, Blinkit's profit (excluding non-cash elements like ESOPs and depreciation) stood at ₹43 crore, a 23X quarter-on-quarter jump.
*   **Market Leadership:** Blinkit rapidly gained market share, surpassing its rivals. As of mid-2025, Blinkit held the largest market share in India's quick commerce sector, estimated at around 44-46%, significantly outperforming Swiggy's Instamart at 23-25% and Zepto at 29-30%. Its net order value (NOV) reached ₹20,940 crore, growing 79% year-on-year, while Instamart's was ₹6,590 crore, up 35%.
*   **Store Expansion and Order Volumes:** Blinkit's dark store network expanded aggressively, reaching 639 stores by Q1 FY25, up from 383 a year prior, with plans to hit 2,000 stores by late 2026. Average Gross Order Value (GOV) throughput per store grew from ₹6 lakh per day to ₹10 lakh per day. Monthly orders grew from 3.5 million in August 2022 to over 20 million by late 2024.
*   **Zomato's Consolidated Performance:** Blinkit's resurgence fueled Zomato's overall financial health. In Q1 FY25, Zomato's consolidated net profit surged to ₹253 crore, a substantial improvement from ₹2 crore in the same period last year, driven by high demand for both food and grocery delivery. For the fiscal year ending March 2024, Zomato's overall revenue jumped 71% to ₹12,114 crore. In Q1 FY26 (April-June 2025), quick commerce net order value (NOV) exceeded food delivery NOV for the first time.

The "expensive acquisition gone wrong" transformed into a brilliant strategic move, demonstrating that sometimes the craziest-looking decisions are the ones that see the future others miss.

## Lessons to Take Away

1.  **Embrace "Survival Instinct" Over Conventional Wisdom:** Deepinder Goyal's decision to acquire a struggling quick-commerce player, despite widespread criticism and a plummeting stock price, was rooted in a belief that food delivery alone wasn't enough for long-term survival. Sometimes, a bold, counter-intuitive move is necessary to secure future relevance, even if it means short-term pain and market skepticism.
2.  **Operational Excellence Drives Profitability in "Cash Burn" Industries:** Quick commerce was notorious for burning cash. Blinkit's turnaround wasn't just about Zomato's capital infusion; it was about a relentless focus on operational efficiency – switching to an inventory-led model, optimizing dark store networks, leveraging AI for demand forecasting, and integrating logistics. These granular improvements transformed a loss-making entity into a profitable growth engine.
3.  **Synergies Are More Than Buzzwords:** The integration of Blinkit with Zomato wasn't just theoretical. Sharing delivery fleets, cross-selling to existing customer bases, and integrating backend operations like warehousing with Hyperpure created tangible cost efficiencies and expanded revenue streams. These real, executable synergies were critical to Blinkit's success.
4.  **Category Leadership Justifies Strategic Overpayment:** While Zomato paid a premium for Blinkit at a time when its valuation was dropping, the acquisition secured Zomato's position as a leader in a rapidly growing category. Building quick commerce from scratch would have taken years and immense capital with no guarantee of success. Acquiring an established (albeit struggling) player allowed Zomato to leapfrog competition and capture market share quickly.
