## Why CRED Spent ₹976 Cr on 'Useless' Celebrity Ads — and Cracked Premium Distribution

It was April 2021. As the Indian Premier League (IPL) season kicked off, a new advertisement began to dominate prime time, featuring a usually calm and composed Rahul Dravid, the legendary cricketer, in an uncharacteristic fit of road rage. "Indiranagar ka gunda hoon main!" he bellowed, smashing car mirrors with a bat, flinging milkshakes, and generally behaving like a maniac. The internet exploded. Memes flooded social media. People wondered if the "Wall" had finally cracked, or if the ad agency had lost its mind. This was just one in a series of intentionally absurd commercials from a relatively new fintech company called CRED, featuring other venerable celebrities like Anil Kapoor in a bizarre dance routine and Madhuri Dixit struggling with a retro phone.

The ads were mocked, debated, and dissected. They seemed to defy every conventional marketing playbook, barely explaining the product and instead relying on surreal humor. Yet, behind the apparent madness, Kunal Shah's CRED was executing a calculated, audacious strategy that would see it spend a staggering ₹976 crore on marketing in that fiscal year alone, an amount more than double its total revenue for the period. This wasn't about selling a product; it was about building a brand so exclusive, so aspirational, that simply being a user became a status symbol.

### The World Before: A Sea of Sameness

Before CRED's aggressive entry, India's credit card landscape was, for most users, a largely uninspired affair. While the number of credit cardholders was steadily growing, especially among digitally fluent urban professionals, the act of paying bills remained a transactional chore. Banks offered various reward points, but these systems were often opaque, fragmented, and rarely felt genuinely rewarding. There was no single platform that celebrated or incentivized responsible financial behavior in a meaningful way.

The broader fintech market was dominated by players like PhonePe, Google Pay, and Paytm, which focused on mass-market adoption and transactional breadth through UPI (Unified Payments Interface). These platforms competed on convenience and volume, aiming to capture as many users as possible. Creditworthiness, as a concept, was a quiet metric used by banks for lending decisions, not a badge of honor to be flaunted.

Kunal Shah, a serial entrepreneur known for co-founding FreeCharge, saw this gap. He founded CRED in 2018 with a simple yet disruptive thesis: to create a platform that rewarded financially disciplined individuals. But not just any individuals. CRED deliberately targeted a niche: users with a high credit score, typically 750 or above (CAC, or Customer Acquisition Cost, is the cost of convincing a potential customer to buy a product or service; EBITDA, or Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization, is a measure of a company's overall financial performance; Unit economics refers to the direct revenues and costs associated with a business's fundamental unit). This "invite-only" model, initially relying on word-of-mouth, was designed to cultivate an aura of exclusivity from day one. However, to truly scale this premium segment, CRED needed to make noise – a lot of noise.

### The Crack: Absurdity as Aspiration

The turning point came with CRED's decision to become an official sponsor of the IPL, India's biggest sporting spectacle, from 2020 to 2022. This move alone cost the company an estimated ₹120 crore over three years. It was a massive investment for a relatively young company, signaling an intent far beyond simple utility.

Then came the ads. Masterminded with input from comedian Tanmay Bhatt and others, the campaigns that launched during the 2020 and 2021 IPL seasons defied all logic. Instead of explaining how CRED allowed users to pay credit card bills and earn rewards, the commercials showcased beloved celebrities like Rahul Dravid, Madhuri Dixit, Anil Kapoor, Jackie Shroff, Govinda, and Kapil Dev in scenarios that were utterly unexpected and often ridiculous. The "Indiranagar ka Gunda" ad featuring Rahul Dravid, for instance, garnered 4 million views in just four days.

The strategy was deliberate. As Kunal Shah himself articulated, CRED was building a "trust brand" rather than a "product brand." For its target audience—affluent, financially savvy individuals—the core utility of paying bills was understood. What they craved, perhaps subconsciously, was recognition and a sense of belonging to an elite club. The ads, rather than focusing on features, focused on building brand recall and an aspirational image, positioning CRED as a "luxury brand that is both cool and aspirational."

This was "exclusionary marketing" at its finest. By making the ads absurd and self-deprecating, CRED invited people to question what this mysterious brand was all about. It tapped into nostalgia for the 90s, appealing to the 36-50 age group that constitutes a large share of credit card holders. The high entry barrier (a 750+ credit score) meant that not everyone could join. Those who could, however, felt a heightened sense of privilege. "People didn't use CRED to save money. They used it to feel exclusive," notes one analysis. The campaigns created a "Unique Brag-worthy Proposition" (UBP), turning the act of being a CRED member into a status signal.

The financial commitment was staggering. In FY22 (April 2021 - March 2022), the year the Rahul Dravid campaign reached peak virality, CRED's marketing and promotion expenses soared to ₹976 crore. In contrast, its revenue from operations for the same period stood at just ₹394 crore, resulting in a net loss of ₹1,280 crore. For every rupee earned, CRED was spending more than two rupees on advertising. This unconventional spending, however, was a calculated bet on long-term brand equity and premium user acquisition.

### What Happened Next: Valuation Soars, Ecosystem Expands

The "useless" ads, far from being a waste, proved remarkably effective. The viral campaigns translated directly into a significant surge in user acquisition: a 6 to 7 times jump in daily signups and an 8-fold spike in app downloads shortly after the Rahul Dravid ad's release.

CRED's valuation skyrocketed. In April 2021, right after the initial celebrity campaigns, the company achieved a valuation of $2.2 billion, attaining unicorn status. By October 2021, this had nearly doubled to $4.01 billion, and by June 2022, it peaked at $6.4 billion.

The user base grew dramatically too. From 5.9 million users in 2021, CRED reached 12 million users by 2022. By mid-2026, it boasted 17 million monthly active users and processed over 40% of India's credit card bill payments.

Beyond bill payments, CRED began to strategically diversify its offerings, transforming from a single-product app into a full-spectrum financial ecosystem. It introduced features like CRED Cash (short-term credit), CRED Mint (peer-to-peer lending), rent payments via credit card, a commerce platform with premium brands, insurance distribution, and even vehicle management (CRED Garage). These new revenue streams began to improve its financial metrics. While net losses remained substantial (₹1,644 crore in FY24), operating revenues saw impressive growth, climbing from ₹95.5 crore in FY21 to ₹2,397 crore in FY24 and ₹2,735 crore in FY25. More importantly, operating losses narrowed significantly in FY25, indicating a path towards efficiency.

In a significant development in June 2026, Kunal Shah stepped down from the CRED board to lead WhatsApp globally for Meta, which simultaneously invested $900 million in CRED. This investment, however, came at a valuation of $4.5 billion, a correction from its peak $6.4 billion in 2022, reflecting broader market shifts in fintech.

### Lessons to Take Away

1.  **Status is a Potent Currency:** CRED demonstrated that for a specific, affluent demographic, a product's utility can be secondary to the status it confers. By making membership exclusive and amplifying that exclusivity through aspirational, unconventional marketing, CRED tapped into a deep psychological need for recognition and belonging.
2.  **Break the Rules to Break the Clutter:** In a crowded market, conventional advertising often gets lost. CRED's deliberately absurd, product-agnostic ads cut through the noise precisely because they were unexpected and generated conversation. They didn't explain; they intrigued.
3.  **Long-Term Brand Building Can Justify Short-Term Burn:** The substantial marketing spend, which initially far outstripped revenue, was a calculated investment in building enduring brand equity. Kunal Shah consciously adopted a luxury brand-building approach, prioritizing long-term trust and recall over immediate transactional ROI.
4.  **Niche Focus is a Springboard for Ecosystem Expansion:** By initially focusing on a highly specific, high-value user segment (750+ credit score), CRED built a loyal, engaged base. This premium audience then became the foundation upon which an entire ecosystem of financial products and services could be built, demonstrating the power of deep penetration within a niche before expanding horizontally.
