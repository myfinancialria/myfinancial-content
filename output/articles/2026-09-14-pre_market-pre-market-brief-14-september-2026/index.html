Pre-Market Brief — 14 September 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets presented a mixed picture, with US indices closing higher but Asian peers showing varied performance, including a dip in Japan.
  • GIFT Nifty is indicating a slightly negative opening bias for the Indian equity markets today.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) have been consistent sellers in September, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continue to provide crucial buying support.
  • The biggest news revolves around increasing expectations of a US Federal Reserve rate hike, driven by inflation concerns and a hawkish stance from the new Fed Chair.
  • For Nifty, the 23300-23400 range, particularly the CPR pivot at 23359.2 and Put OI at 23300, will be a key zone to watch for support.
  • The day's main risks include surging crude oil prices and rising US 10-year bond yields, which could intensify FII outflows and exert downward pressure on Indian equities.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Overnight, US markets closed on a positive note, with all three major indices — Dow, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 — registering gains. This positive momentum from Wall Street could provide some early tailwinds. However, Asian markets are showing a mixed trend this morning, with Japan's Nikkei 225 trading lower, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng saw modest gains and China's Shanghai Composite was marginally down. This mixed global sentiment suggests a cautious start for Indian markets, despite the positive US close.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) strengthened slightly against the US Dollar, closing at ₹95.54, a marginal gain of 0.16%. The Dollar Index, however, saw an increase of 0.40% to 99.51, indicating broader dollar strength globally. Crude oil prices witnessed a significant surge, with Brent Crude jumping 3.15% to $107.90 and WTI Crude rising 3.11% to $103.16. Gold and Silver both traded lower, declining by 0.34% and 0.98% respectively. The US 10-Year Yield increased by 0.63% to 4.97%, reflecting growing concerns about inflation and potential rate hikes.

Interpretation: A stronger Rupee is generally positive for importers and helps curb imported inflation. However, the rising Dollar Index suggests global capital flows might favour the dollar. The sharp increase in crude oil prices is a significant concern for India, potentially putting pressure on oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors, and could fuel domestic inflation. Rising US 10-Year Yields typically make emerging markets like India less attractive for foreign investors, posing a risk to FII flows.

GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling

The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 23483.00, down by 0.38%.

Interpretation: This indicates that the Indian Nifty 50 is likely to open with a slight gap-down today, reflecting a cautious sentiment ahead of the main trading session.

FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, offloading Indian equities worth ₹931 crore in the cash segment yesterday. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net buyers, injecting ₹1,968 crore into the market, providing a crucial cushion against foreign outflows. Over the last five sessions, FIIs have been net sellers for four sessions, while DIIs have consistently been net buyers, demonstrating their continued support for the domestic market.

Option-chain signals (OI) — where support & resistance sit

For Nifty, the highest Call OI is concentrated at the 24000 strike (13,668,500 contracts), followed by 23800 and 23700, indicating strong resistance at these higher levels. On the Put side, the highest OI is at 23300 (13,802,300 contracts), followed by 23000 and 23200, suggesting robust support around these levels. The Nifty PCR (Put-Call Ratio) is 1.1, which leans towards a supportive or bullish-to-neutral sentiment.

Bank Nifty sees the highest Call OI at 57500 (2,136,210 contracts), followed by 58000, acting as immediate resistance. The highest Put OI is also at 57500 (1,671,750 contracts), with 56000 and 57000 also showing significant Put OI, indicating a battleground around 57500. The Bank Nifty PCR is 0.98, which is neutral to slightly cautious.

For Sensex, the 75000 strike holds the highest Call OI (778,200 contracts), making it a key resistance. On the support side, 74000 has the highest Put OI (725,200 contracts). The Sensex PCR is 1.25, indicating a supportive bias.

Interpretation: Combining technical and OI data, for Nifty, the 23300-23400 zone is critical support, with the 23300 Put OI aligning with the CPR bottom and S1. Resistance is likely to be encountered around 23700-23800. For Bank Nifty, 57500 is a crucial pivot, acting as both resistance (Call OI) and initial support (Put OI), suggesting potential volatility around this level. Sensex has strong support at 74000 and resistance at 75000. A PCR above 1, as seen in Nifty and Sensex, generally suggests that more puts have been written than calls, indicating underlying support, while a PCR near 1 (Bank Nifty) points to a balanced view. Traders should respect these levels today, as a break on either side could dictate the day's trend.

Stock & sector news that matters

Goldman Sachs expects Fed to hike rates amid inflation fears Goldman Sachs has reversed its earlier forecast and now expects the US Federal Reserve to raise interest rates by 25 basis points in September. This shift is attributed to rising oil prices, stronger producer inflation, and a hawkish tone from the new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh. Why it matters: This is a significant global development. Higher US interest rates typically lead to a stronger US Dollar and make emerging markets less attractive for foreign capital. This could exacerbate FII outflows from India and put pressure on Indian equities, especially rate-sensitive sectors.

FIIs sell Indian shares worth ₹14,475 crore in Sept; analyst warns soaring bond yields may deepen selloff Foreign investors have sold Indian equities worth ₹14,475 crore in September so far. This selling is linked to surging oil prices and rising global bond yields, compounded by escalating Middle East tensions. Analysts caution that further increases in US Treasury yields could intensify this selloff. Why it matters: This news directly impacts Indian market sentiment. Sustained FII selling, driven by global yield and crude price pressures, can cap upside potential and lead to market corrections. Investors should monitor FII activity closely, as their continued outflows could weigh heavily on benchmark indices.

HDFC Bank shares hit 52-week lows over consecutive sessions while analysts scream Buy. Has the stock hit its bottom? HDFC Bank shares have been consistently hitting fresh 52-week lows, despite numerous 'Buy' recommendations from brokerages. Analysts point to attractive valuations and potential for margin improvement, even as technical indicators remain weak. Why it matters: HDFC Bank is a heavyweight in the Indian banking sector and a significant component of benchmark indices. Its underperformance can drag down the broader market, especially the financial sector. While analysts see value, the continued price decline suggests investor caution, and its movement will be a key indicator for the banking space.

India beats a hasty retreat from a crucial market reform India’s market-closing auction reform, introduced by SEBI to curb price manipulation, has faced significant volatility and investor backlash within weeks of its implementation. The regulator is now reportedly considering weakening or abandoning it, highlighting deeper issues. Why it matters: This news reflects regulatory uncertainty and potential challenges in implementing market reforms. While the specific impact on daily trading might be limited, it could affect investor confidence in the regulatory environment and the efficiency of market mechanisms in the long run.

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  1. US Federal Reserve Rate Path: The increasing expectations of a US Fed rate hike, as indicated by Goldman Sachs and the hawkish tone of the new Fed Chair, will be a major global event to watch this week. Markets typically react with increased volatility around Fed policy announcements, especially if there's a shift in monetary policy stance, as higher rates can impact global liquidity and capital flows.
  2. FII Flow Trends: The continuous selling by FIIs in September, coupled with rising US yields, makes their daily flow data critical. Markets tend to react negatively to sustained FII outflows, often leading to downward pressure on benchmark indices, while a reversal in this trend could provide significant support.
  3. Crude Oil Price Movement: The sharp rise in Brent Crude prices is a key concern for India, a major oil importer. Markets typically react to significant crude price movements, with sustained increases often leading to inflationary pressures, impacting sectors like OMCs, aviation, and chemicals, and potentially dampening overall economic sentiment.

What to watch in today's session

  1. GIFT Nifty's opening indication: The slight gap-down suggested by GIFT Nifty will set the initial tone for the Indian market.
  2. Nifty's 23300-23400 support zone: Watch how Nifty reacts around its CPR pivot of 23359.2 and the strong Put OI at 23300. A breach could signal further downside.
  3. Crude Oil Prices: Any further escalation in crude oil prices will be closely monitored for its potential impact on inflation and specific sectors.
  4. FII/DII activity: The trend of FII selling versus DII buying will continue to dictate market direction and resilience.
  5. HDFC Bank's performance: As a major banking heavyweight, its price action will be crucial for the financial sector and the broader market.
  6. Global bond yields: Keep an eye on the US 10-year yield; a continued rise could intensify FII selling pressure.

Bottom line

Indian markets are poised for a cautious start, with GIFT Nifty indicating a slight gap-down. The key battleground for Nifty will be around the 23300-23400 zone, where strong option chain support meets technical pivots. Traders should remain vigilant regarding the rising crude oil prices and the hawkish stance from the US Federal Reserve, as these global factors could continue to drive FII outflows and inject volatility into the market.
