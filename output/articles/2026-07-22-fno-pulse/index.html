Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been settled. For Call options, the strike with the highest OI often acts as a resistance level, indicating where option writers (sellers) believe the price is unlikely to rise beyond. Conversely, for Put options, the strike with the highest OI typically serves as a support level, suggesting where put writers expect the price to hold.

For Nifty 50, the heaviest Call OI is observed at the 24200 strike, suggesting it could act as a significant resistance point. The heaviest Put OI is concentrated at 24000, which might provide immediate support. Looking at today's fresh activity, significant new Call writing was seen near the 24050 strike, indicating that call sellers are positioning for a potential cap on upside movement around this level. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was notable near 23816, suggesting put sellers anticipate a floor forming there. The aggregate flow for Nifty showed a net Call OI build of 62.6 million contracts, significantly higher than the 24.7 million net Put OI build, reinforcing a bearish bias from today's fresh positions.

On the Sensex, the heaviest Call OI is at the 77500 strike, which could act as a strong ceiling. Fresh Call writing was most prominent near 76833, suggesting that option sellers expect the index to face resistance around this point. Conversely, fresh Put writing was concentrated near 76166, indicating potential support from put sellers. The aggregate flow for Sensex also showed a higher net Call OI build of 20.7 million compared to 6.6 million for Puts, pointing to a bearish sentiment from today's activity.

For Bank Nifty, the option chain shows a unique situation with the maximum OI walls for both calls and puts indicated at 59000, suggesting this strike could be a crucial pivot point. Heaviest Put OI is indeed at 59000, reinforcing its role as a potential strong support. PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that compares the total Put Open Interest to the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 generally suggests that there are more outstanding Put options than Call options, which is often interpreted as a bullish signal (put writers expecting prices to rise). Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more Call options than Put options, often seen as a bearish signal (call writers expecting prices to fall). We also look at the PCR of today's fresh Open Interest (ΔOI) to gauge the immediate sentiment.

For Nifty 50, the overall PCR(OI) stands at 0.7, indicating a relatively balanced book with a slight tilt towards calls. However, today's flow, represented by ΔPCR of 0.39, shows a significant inclination towards fresh Call writing, suggesting a more bearish sentiment from today's participants.

The Sensex has an overall PCR(OI) of 0.63, which is below 0.7, indicating that Call options significantly dominate the outstanding contracts, generally interpreted as a bearish outlook. Today's flow, with a ΔPCR of 0.32, further reinforces this, showing a strong preference for fresh Call writing.

Bank Nifty recorded an overall PCR(OI) of 0.77, suggesting a relatively balanced options book. Interestingly, today's flow, with a ΔPCR of -0.05, indicates a mixed or balanced fresh writing activity, without a strong directional bias from new positions.

Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the least financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that specific price. It is often seen as a magnetic force, where the index tends to gravitate towards this level as the expiry date approaches.

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain level is identified at 24300. This is approximately 1.3% above the current spot price, suggesting a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry draws closer.

The Sensex Max Pain point is at 77600, which is about 1.1% above its current spot price. This indicates that option writers are positioned for the index to potentially move higher towards this strike.

Bank Nifty has its Max Pain level at 58000, approximately 1.5% above its current spot price. This suggests a notable upward pull for Bank Nifty towards this strike as expiry nears.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to form your own informed perspective. You can access the raw data yourself daily from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website or through your broker's platform.

To begin, look for the "Option Chain" on the NSE website for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty. Here, you can identify the strikes with the highest Open Interest for both Calls and Puts – these are your potential resistance and support levels. Also, pay attention to the "Change in OI" column to see where fresh positions (Call or Put writing) are being added or unwound. Next, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI; this gives you a broad market sentiment. Finally, check the FII derivative positioning data, usually available in the "Participant-wise Open Interest" section on the NSE, to see how institutional players are positioned in index and stock futures. Combining these data points can help you build a comprehensive picture of the market's underlying sentiment.
