Optimise NPS for Higher Returns & a Bigger Retirement Corpus

TL;DR

  • The National Pension System (NPS) offers a low-cost, market-linked avenue for retirement planning.
  • Optimising your asset allocation, especially increasing equity exposure early, can significantly boost your corpus.
  • Regular voluntary contributions beyond mandatory deductions enhance your savings and provide additional tax benefits.
  • Periodically reviewing and rebalancing your portfolio ensures it aligns with your risk profile and financial goals.
  • Choosing a well-performing Pension Fund Manager (PFM) and understanding their charges is crucial for long-term growth.

What this means in plain terms

The National Pension System (NPS) is a government-backed retirement scheme designed to help individuals build a substantial corpus for their post-retirement years. It's a market-linked product, meaning your returns depend on how the underlying investments perform. Unlike traditional fixed-income options, NPS offers the potential for higher growth, especially over long horizons, but also comes with market risks.

Optimising your NPS means making smart choices about how your money is invested and how much you contribute, to ensure it works as hard as possible for you. This isn't about complex trading strategies, but rather a few fundamental decisions that can make a big difference to your final retirement fund. By understanding and actively managing these aspects, you can potentially accumulate a much larger sum, providing greater financial security when you stop working.

Strategic Asset Allocation for Growth

One of the most impactful decisions in NPS is your asset allocation – how your contributions are split across different asset classes. NPS offers four primary asset classes:

  • Asset Class E (Equity): Invests predominantly in equity market instruments.
  • Asset Class C (Corporate Debt): Invests in fixed income instruments like corporate bonds.
  • Asset Class G (Government Securities): Invests in government bonds and related instruments.
  • Asset Class A (Alternative Investments): A small portion can be allocated to alternative assets like REITs and InvITs.

You have two main choices for managing this allocation:

Active Choice

Under the Active Choice option, you decide the percentage of your contribution to be invested in each asset class. For Asset Class E (equity), the maximum allocation allowed is 75% until age 50. After age 50, this equity exposure gradually reduces annually, with a maximum of 50% by age 60. This option is suitable for individuals who understand market dynamics and prefer to manage their risk exposure actively. For those with a long investment horizon and a higher risk appetite, allocating a higher percentage to equity (Asset Class E) in the initial years can significantly enhance potential returns, leveraging the power of compounding.

Auto Choice (Lifecycle Fund)

If you prefer a hands-off approach, Auto Choice automatically adjusts your asset allocation based on your age. There are three lifecycle funds:

  • LC75 (Aggressive): Starts with 75% equity exposure, suitable for those with high-risk tolerance.
  • LC50 (Moderate): Starts with 50% equity exposure.
  • LC25 (Conservative): Starts with 25% equity exposure, suitable for those with low-risk tolerance.

    • Your equity exposure automatically reduces as you age, de-risking your portfolio closer to retirement. While convenient, reviewing if the chosen auto-choice fund aligns with your actual risk profile and goals is essential. For many, especially younger investors, starting with LC75 might be more beneficial than LC50 or LC25, to maximise growth potential during their prime earning years.

Maximising Contributions and Tax Benefits

Beyond the mandatory contributions, leveraging voluntary contributions can substantially boost your retirement corpus and offer additional tax advantages.

Tier I and Tier II Accounts

NPS offers two types of accounts:

  • Tier I Account: This is the primary retirement account with restricted withdrawals. Contributions to this account are eligible for tax benefits under Section 80C, 80CCD(1B), and 80CCD(2) of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
  • Tier II Account: This is a voluntary savings account, offering more flexibility for withdrawals. While contributions to Tier II generally do not offer tax benefits, government employees can claim a deduction under Section 80C for contributions up to ₹1.5 lakhs, provided there is a lock-in period of three years. For others, it acts like a low-cost mutual fund for additional savings.

Leveraging Tax Benefits

NPS provides significant tax incentives that can reduce your taxable income while building your retirement fund:

  • Section 80C: Contributions to Tier I NPS are eligible for deduction within the overall ₹1.5 lakh limit under Section 80C.
  • Section 80CCD(1B): An exclusive additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 is available for contributions to Tier I NPS, over and above the Section 80C limit. This is a powerful tool to save more tax and invest more for retirement.
  • Section 80CCD(2): If you are a salaried employee, your employer's contribution to your NPS account (up to 10% of your basic salary + Dearness Allowance) is deductible from your taxable income. For central government employees, this limit is 14%. This benefit is over and above the limits under Section 80C and 80CCD(1B).

By making full use of these sections, especially the ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B) and encouraging employer contributions, you can significantly reduce your tax outgo while simultaneously increasing your retirement savings.

Regular Monitoring and Rebalancing

An NPS portfolio, like any other investment, isn't a "set it and forget it" solution. Regular monitoring and periodic rebalancing are crucial to ensure your portfolio remains aligned with your financial goals and risk tolerance.

Why Monitor?

Market conditions change, and so do your personal circumstances. What was an appropriate asset allocation five years ago might not be ideal today. Monitoring allows you to:

  • Assess the performance of your chosen Pension Fund Manager (PFM) and asset classes.
  • Identify if your risk appetite has changed due to life events (e.g., marriage, children, nearing retirement).
  • Ensure your portfolio is on track to meet your retirement corpus target.

The Rebalancing Act

Rebalancing involves adjusting your asset allocation back to your desired proportions. For instance, if your equity investments have performed exceptionally well, their percentage in your portfolio might have grown beyond your initial target. Rebalancing would mean selling some equity units and buying more debt units to restore your original allocation. This helps in booking profits and maintaining your intended risk level.

NPS allows you to change your asset allocation, Pension Fund Manager (PFM), and investment choice (Active or Auto) once a year. Use this flexibility wisely. For instance, as you get closer to retirement, you might consider gradually shifting more of your corpus from equity to less volatile debt instruments to protect your accumulated gains. This strategy is often referred to as "glide path" investing, which the Auto Choice option inherently provides.

A real example

Meet Priya, 35, ₹20L CTC, Mumbai. Priya started investing in NPS at 25, contributing ₹50,000 annually (₹4,167/month).

  • Scenario 1: Passive Approach (LC50 Auto Choice)

    • * Priya chose the Auto Choice LC50 fund. * Assumed average annual return: 9% (due to lower initial equity exposure and auto-de-risking). * After 25 years (until age 50), her corpus would be approximately ₹52.4 lakhs. * If she continues till age 60, with further de-risking, the corpus might reach around ₹1.4 crores.

  • Scenario 2: Optimised Approach (Active Choice, Higher Equity + Voluntary Contributions)

    • * Priya chose Active Choice with 75% Equity, 15% Corporate Debt, 10% Government Securities until age 50. * She also decided to contribute an additional ₹50,000 annually under Section 80CCD(1B), making her total annual contribution ₹1,00,000 (₹8,333/month). * Assumed average annual return: 11% (due to higher equity exposure). * After 25 years (until age 50), her corpus would be approximately ₹1.33 crores. * If she continues till age 60, maintaining a slightly higher equity exposure (e.g., 60% till 50, then gradually reducing to 40% by 60 through rebalancing), her corpus could potentially reach ₹4.2 crores.

Difference in Corpus at age 60: ₹4.2 crores (Optimised) - ₹1.4 crores (Passive) = ₹2.8 crores

This example clearly illustrates how strategic asset allocation, higher equity exposure in younger years, and maximising voluntary contributions can lead to a significantly larger retirement corpus. The additional ₹50,000 contribution also saves Priya ₹15,600 in taxes annually (assuming 31.2% tax bracket, including cess).

What to do this week

  1. Check your current NPS asset allocation: Log in to your NPS account and review your current investment choice (Active or Auto) and the percentage allocated to Asset Class E (Equity).
  2. Evaluate your risk profile and investment horizon: Consider if your current equity exposure aligns with your age, risk tolerance, and years left until retirement. If you're young and have a high-risk appetite, consider increasing your equity allocation (up to 75% in Active Choice or switch to LC75).
  3. Assess your contributions: Determine if you are already contributing the additional ₹50,000 under Section 80CCD(1B). If not, set up a standing instruction or make a one-time payment to fully utilise this tax benefit.
  4. Review your Pension Fund Manager's performance: Compare the historical returns of your current PFM against others on the NPS Trust website to ensure you're getting competitive returns.
  5. Set a reminder for annual review: Schedule a yearly reminder to review your NPS portfolio, asset allocation, and PFM performance, making adjustments if necessary.

FAQ

Can I change my Pension Fund Manager (PFM) in NPS?

Yes, you can change your PFM once in a financial year. You can do this by logging into your NPS account online and submitting a request.

What happens if I don't choose an asset allocation?

If you don't make an active choice, your funds will automatically be invested under the Auto Choice (Lifecycle Fund) LC50, which starts with 50% equity exposure and gradually reduces it with age.

Are Tier II NPS contributions tax-deductible?

Generally, Tier II contributions are not tax-deductible for private sector employees. However, government employees can claim a deduction under Section 80C for Tier II contributions with a three-year lock-in period.

What are the withdrawal rules for NPS at maturity?

At age 60, you can withdraw up to 60% of your corpus as a lump sum, which is entirely tax-exempt. The remaining 40% must be used to purchase an annuity from an IRDAI-regulated life insurance company, which provides a regular pension.

How often should I rebalance my NPS portfolio?

You are allowed to change your asset allocation once in a financial year. It's advisable to review your portfolio annually and rebalance if your actual allocation deviates significantly from your target, or if your risk profile changes.

Can NRIs invest in NPS?

Yes, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) can open an NPS account. The contributions are subject to FEMA regulations and income tax provisions applicable to NRIs.
