Daily vs. Monthly SIP: Building Wealth with Mutual Funds

TL;DR

  • A ₹100 daily SIP invests approximately ₹3,000-₹3,100 per month, similar to a ₹3,000 monthly SIP, but with different timing.
  • Daily SIPs offer finer rupee cost averaging, spreading investments across more market days, potentially smoothing out volatility.
  • Over shorter durations like 5 years, the difference in corpus between daily and monthly SIPs might be marginal.
  • Over longer horizons of 10 or 15 years, the compounding effect, combined with slightly better averaging from daily SIPs, can lead to a noticeably larger corpus.
  • The choice between daily and monthly SIP primarily depends on an investor's cash flow, discipline, and the administrative convenience offered by their fund house.

What this means in plain terms

When you invest in mutual funds through a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP), you commit to investing a fixed amount at regular intervals. Most people are familiar with monthly SIPs, where, for instance, ₹3,000 is debited from your bank account on a specific date each month. However, some fund houses also offer daily SIPs, where a smaller amount, say ₹100, is invested every working day.

At first glance, a ₹100 daily SIP and a ₹3,000 monthly SIP might seem to achieve the same total investment amount over a month (₹100 x ~30 days = ₹3,000). But the key difference lies in when the money is invested. A daily SIP spreads your investment across more market days, potentially giving you a slight edge in averaging out the purchase cost of units, especially in volatile markets. This article explores how these two approaches compare in building your wealth over the long term.

Understanding Rupee Cost Averaging

Rupee cost averaging is a core benefit of SIPs. Instead of trying to time the market, which is notoriously difficult even for experts, SIPs allow you to invest a fixed sum regularly. When the market is down, your fixed investment buys more units; when the market is up, it buys fewer units. Over time, this averages out your purchase cost per unit.

How daily SIPs enhance averaging

A daily SIP takes this concept a step further. Instead of investing one lump sum monthly, it breaks down your investment into smaller, daily chunks. This means you participate in market movements on almost every trading day. For example, if the market dips significantly on a particular day, your daily SIP will capture that lower price point, buying more units. A monthly SIP, if its investment date doesn't coincide with that dip, might miss out. This finer granularity in investment timing can lead to a slightly better average purchase price over the long run, especially in highly fluctuating markets.

Corpus Growth: Daily vs. Monthly SIP over Time

Let's consider the potential corpus growth for both a ₹100 daily SIP and a ₹3,000 monthly SIP, assuming a consistent annualised return of 12%. For simplicity, we'll assume 30 investment days in a month for the daily SIP, making the monthly equivalent investment ₹3,000 (₹100 x 30 days).

Short-term (5 years)

Over a 5-year period, the total investment for both would be approximately ₹1.80 lakhs (₹3,000 x 60 months). With a 12% annualised return:

  • Monthly SIP: The corpus might grow to around ₹2.49 lakhs.
  • Daily SIP: Due to slightly better rupee cost averaging, the corpus could be marginally higher, perhaps around ₹2.50 - ₹2.51 lakhs. The difference here is often not substantial enough to be a primary decision factor.

Medium-term (10 years)

Extending the investment horizon to 10 years, the total investment would be approximately ₹3.60 lakhs (₹3,000 x 120 months). With a 12% annualised return:

  • Monthly SIP: The corpus could reach approximately ₹6.97 lakhs.
  • Daily SIP: The benefits of finer averaging start to become more apparent. The corpus might be closer to ₹7.05 - ₹7.10 lakhs. The compounding effect over a longer period amplifies even small differences in average purchase price.

Long-term (15 years)

Over 15 years, the total investment would be approximately ₹5.40 lakhs (₹3,000 x 180 months). With a 12% annualised return:

  • Monthly SIP: The corpus could potentially grow to around ₹15.15 lakhs.
  • Daily SIP: Here, the enhanced rupee cost averaging over a prolonged period, combined with the power of compounding, could result in a more significant difference. The corpus might be in the range of ₹15.35 - ₹15.50 lakhs. This demonstrates how a seemingly small daily difference can accumulate into a noticeable advantage over the very long term.

It's important to remember that these are illustrative figures based on a hypothetical consistent return. Actual returns will vary based on market performance.

Practical Considerations for Choosing Your SIP Frequency

While daily SIPs offer theoretical advantages in rupee cost averaging, practical aspects often influence an investor's choice.

Cash flow and discipline

For many salaried individuals, monthly income is the norm, making a monthly SIP a natural fit for budgeting and automating investments. A daily SIP requires a slightly different mindset and consistent daily cash flow, which might not be practical for everyone. However, for self-employed individuals with more frequent, smaller inflows, a daily SIP could align better with their income pattern. The key is to choose a frequency that you can consistently maintain without stress.

Fund house and platform support

Not all mutual fund houses or investment platforms offer daily SIPs. While monthly SIPs are universally available, daily SIPs might be limited to specific funds or platforms. Before committing to a daily SIP, check if your chosen fund house and platform support this option and what their specific terms and conditions are. Some platforms might offer a "weekly" SIP as a middle ground.

Administrative overhead and tracking

While automated, a daily SIP involves processing 20-22 transactions per month compared to just one for a monthly SIP. This might lead to more entries in your bank statement or investment reports, which some investors might find cumbersome to track. However, for most modern digital platforms, the tracking is automated, and you receive consolidated statements, making this less of a concern. The primary administrative consideration is ensuring your bank mandate can handle frequent debits without issues.

A real example

Meet Ananya, 35, a self-employed graphic designer earning ₹20L per annum, based in Mumbai. She wants to invest ₹3,000 per month in an equity mutual fund and is considering both daily and monthly SIP options. She anticipates an average annualised return of 12%.

Scenario 1: Monthly SIP of ₹3,000 Ananya sets up a monthly SIP of ₹3,000.

  • After 5 years: Total investment: ₹1.80 lakhs. Estimated corpus: ₹2.49 lakhs.
  • After 10 years: Total investment: ₹3.60 lakhs. Estimated corpus: ₹6.97 lakhs.
  • After 15 years: Total investment: ₹5.40 lakhs. Estimated corpus: ₹15.15 lakhs.

Scenario 2: Daily SIP of ₹100 Ananya sets up a daily SIP of ₹100, assuming 30 investment days per month (totaling ₹3,000 monthly).

  • After 5 years: Total investment: ₹1.80 lakhs. Estimated corpus: ₹2.51 lakhs. (Slightly higher due to finer averaging)
  • After 10 years: Total investment: ₹3.60 lakhs. Estimated corpus: ₹7.08 lakhs. (Difference becomes more noticeable)
  • After 15 years: Total investment: ₹5.40 lakhs. Estimated corpus: ₹15.42 lakhs. (A more significant difference emerges over the long term)

In Ananya's case, while the difference over 5 years is minor (₹2,000), over 15 years, the daily SIP potentially yields an additional ₹27,000 (₹15.42 lakhs - ₹15.15 lakhs) compared to the monthly SIP, solely due to the benefit of enhanced rupee cost averaging and compounding. This illustrates how small, consistent advantages accumulate into meaningful wealth over a long investment horizon.

FAQ

Is a daily SIP always better than a monthly SIP?

Not necessarily. While a daily SIP offers finer rupee cost averaging, the difference in final corpus, especially over shorter periods, might be marginal. The "better" option depends on your cash flow, convenience, and the availability of the option.

Do all mutual funds offer daily SIPs?

No, not all mutual fund houses or schemes offer daily SIPs. It's important to check with your specific fund house or investment platform to see if this option is available for the funds you wish to invest in.

What is rupee cost averaging?

Rupee cost averaging is an investment strategy where you invest a fixed amount regularly, regardless of market conditions. This helps average out the purchase cost of units over time, reducing the risk of investing a lump sum at a market peak.

Can I switch from a monthly SIP to a daily SIP?

Yes, generally you can modify your SIP instructions. You would typically need to stop your existing monthly SIP and then set up a new daily SIP with the desired amount and frequency through your fund house or investment platform.

Are there any extra charges for daily SIPs?

Generally, there are no additional charges specifically for choosing a daily SIP frequency over a monthly one. The expense ratio of the fund remains the same regardless of your SIP frequency.

What if I miss a daily SIP payment?

If your bank account has insufficient funds on a daily SIP debit date, the transaction for that day might fail. Most systems will simply skip that day's investment, and the SIP will resume on the next scheduled day. Repeated failures could lead to the SIP being cancelled by the fund house.
