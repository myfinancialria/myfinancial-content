Market Wrap — 23 July 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices extended their losing streak for a fourth consecutive session, with Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing significantly lower.
  • Rising global oil prices, fueled by escalating Middle East tensions, and sustained selling by both FIIs and DIIs were the primary drivers of today's market decline.
  • NTPC Green Energy Ltd. emerged as the top gainer among Nifty 500 stocks, surging over 6%.
  • CIE Automotive India Ltd. was the biggest laggard, plummeting more than 11% today.
  • Both Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers in the cash market, withdrawing ₹819 crore and ₹418 crore respectively.
  • Data for Futures & Options (F&O) build-up is unavailable for today's session.

How the market behaved today

Indian benchmark indices witnessed a fourth consecutive day of declines, as Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closed notably lower. The market opened with a negative bias and remained under pressure throughout the session, reflecting broad-based selling. The Nifty 50 ended at 23,869.60, down 0.53%, while the Bank Nifty shed 0.94% to close at 56,592.00. Midcap and Smallcap indices also experienced significant cuts, indicating a widespread bearish sentiment.

What moved the market — the factors

Today's market downturn was primarily influenced by a confluence of global and domestic factors. A significant concern was the surge in global crude oil prices, with Brent crude approaching the $100 per barrel mark earlier in the day, driven by escalating US-Iran tensions and fears of a widening conflict in the Middle East. This development stoked inflation worries and weighed heavily on investor sentiment, particularly for energy-sensitive sectors.

Adding to the bearish mood, both Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) turned net sellers in the cash market, contributing to the selling pressure. This combined institutional outflow indicated a lack of conviction among major players.

On the corporate front, Infosys's decision to trim the upper end of its FY27 revenue growth guidance, despite reporting higher quarterly profits, negatively impacted the IT sector and overall market sentiment. While the IT sector managed to stay relatively resilient compared to other losers, the news from a sector heavyweight created a cautious environment. Heavyweight stocks like IndusInd Bank and Adani Green Energy also saw significant declines, pulling down their respective sectors. The broad-based selling was evident in the market breadth, which was overwhelmingly negative, with nearly four declining stocks for every advancing one.

The market breadth was overwhelmingly negative today, reflecting broad-based selling pressure across segments. This indicated that the bearish sentiment was widespread and not confined to a few specific stocks or sectors.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's market session was not dominated by a single, defining event like a central bank policy decision or major economic data release. Instead, it was primarily influenced by a combination of global geopolitical developments and key corporate earnings announcements.

The most significant external factor was the escalating tensions in the Middle East, which pushed Brent crude oil prices towards $100 a barrel earlier in the day. This development immediately raised concerns about inflationary pressures and potential impacts on corporate margins, leading to a cautious stance among investors. The energy sector, despite the rise in crude prices, saw some selling, while sectors sensitive to input costs or consumer spending faced headwinds.

Domestically, the Q1 earnings season continued to unfold, with Infosys's results being a major highlight. While the IT major reported a healthy profit increase, its decision to trim the upper end of its revenue guidance for FY27 disappointed investors, causing its ADRs to fall and casting a shadow over the entire IT sector. Similarly, IndiGo's reported loss due to higher operating costs also weighed on the aviation sector. These corporate updates, coupled with the sustained selling by both FIIs and DIIs, created a challenging environment for the Indian equities market throughout the day.

Setup for tomorrow

As we head into tomorrow's session, global cues will continue to play a crucial role. The trajectory of crude oil prices, particularly in light of ongoing Middle East tensions, will be a key monitorable. While Brent crude closed lower today, the earlier surge towards $100/barrel and the significant rise in WTI crude (+4.47%) suggest volatility in energy markets. The US Dollar Index showed a slight uptick, and the US 10-year Treasury yield also increased, which could influence capital flows.

Investors will be closely watching for any further developments on the geopolitical front and how global markets react overnight. Domestically, the performance of the IT sector following Infosys's guidance will remain in focus. Broader market sentiment will likely hinge on the continuation or reversal of institutional selling, and any fresh cues from ongoing Q1 earnings reports.
