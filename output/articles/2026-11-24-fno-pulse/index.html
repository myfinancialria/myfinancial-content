India F&O Pulse — 24 Nov 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Indian Indices See Bearish Signals in Nifty and Sensex, While Bank Nifty Shows Mild Bullish Undercurrents.

TL;DR

  • Nifty 50: Today's options data, particularly fresh call writing, suggests a mildly bearish outlook for the upcoming session, with resistance expected around higher levels.
  • Sensex: The overall positioning appears range-bound, though recent call writing indicates potential capping of upside moves.
  • Bank Nifty: Despite a significant fall today, the options data, especially active put writing, points towards a mildly bullish bias, with support potentially forming at lower strikes.

What the indices did today

The Nifty 50 closed the day at 25,349.65, marking a 1.75% decline. This close places it well below its Max Pain point of 25,650, and within the broader implied range of 24,000 to 26,000 suggested by the option chain. The significant selling pressure observed today indicates a cautious sentiment among market participants.

The Sensex also experienced a notable drop, ending at 82,435.99, down 1.77%. Similar to Nifty, Sensex closed beneath its Max Pain level of 82,900, but remained comfortably within the wider option-chain implied range of 79,500 to 85,000. Today's movement suggests a lack of strong conviction for an immediate upward bounce.

The Bank Nifty witnessed the sharpest decline among the major indices, falling 3.17% to close at 62,585.35. Despite this significant fall, its Max Pain level of 63,200 remains above the current spot, and the option chain suggests a broad range, though the heaviest Put Open Interest (OI) is relatively close, hinting at potential support.

Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. When we look at the highest Call OI strike, it indicates the price level where the most Call options have been sold. Call option sellers, also known as writers, profit if the underlying asset stays below that strike price. Therefore, the highest Call OI strike often acts as a significant resistance level, suggesting where market participants believe the upside might be capped. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike signifies the price level where the most Put options have been sold. Put writers profit if the underlying asset stays above that strike price, making the highest Put OI strike a strong support level, indicating where the market expects a floor to be.

For the Nifty 50, today's fresh Call writing was heaviest near the 25,766 strike. This suggests that Call option sellers believe Nifty might struggle to move significantly above this level in the near term, positioning it as a potential resistance point.

For Bank Nifty, we observed fresh Call writing concentrated near 63,500, indicating that Call sellers anticipate this level to act as a cap. However, there was also significant fresh Put writing near 62,666, suggesting that Put sellers are actively creating a floor around current levels, anticipating the index to hold above this mark.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a popular indicator that compares the total open interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR(OI) value above 1 typically suggests more Put options are open than Call options, which can be interpreted as a bullish sentiment by some, as Put writers are betting on the market staying above certain levels. A value below 1, conversely, indicates more Call options are open, potentially hinting at a bearish sentiment.

For Nifty 50, the overall PCR(OI) stands at 0.83, suggesting a slightly higher concentration of Call options open interest, indicating a somewhat balanced to mildly cautious stance among option writers. However, today's flow, measured by the change in PCR (ΔPCR) at 0.69, shows that fresh writing activity was heavily tilted towards Call options, reinforcing the expectation of resistance.

The Sensex has a PCR(OI) of 1.0, indicating a perfectly balanced book in terms of outstanding Put and Call options. Yet, today's fresh flow ΔPCR of 0.52 signals that new writing was predominantly in Call options, implying that recent activity leans towards capping upside movements.

Bank Nifty shows a PCR(OI) of 1.03, suggesting a slight dominance of Put open interest, which could be interpreted as a mildly supportive setup. The ΔPCR for today's flow is 1.06, indicating that fresh Put writing was slightly more active than Call writing, aligning with a more balanced to mildly bullish sentiment despite the day's sharp fall.

Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would experience the least financial loss if the index were to expire at that level. It's often considered a magnetic point because, as expiry approaches, the index sometimes tends to gravitate towards this level, as option writers try to manage their positions for minimal loss.

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain is at 25,650, which is about 1.2% above today's spot close. This suggests that option writers, on aggregate, would prefer the Nifty to expire higher than its current level.

The Sensex Max Pain is at 82,900, approximately 0.6% above its current spot. This also indicates a slight upward pull from the perspective of option writers.

Bank Nifty has its Max Pain at 63,200, which is roughly 1.0% above its closing price. This suggests that despite the day's significant decline, option writers are positioned for a potential recovery towards this level. The closer we get to expiry, the stronger this "magnet effect" can become.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment. Looking at yesterday's data (November 23, 2026), FIIs held a low Index futures long ratio of 20.1%. This means that out of their total Index futures positions, only a fifth were 'long' (betting on an increase), while the vast majority were 'short' (betting on a decrease). Historically, a consistently low FII index futures long ratio has tended to coincide with periods of market caution or downside pressure.

In Stock futures, their long ratio stood at 56.7%, indicating a slight preference for long positions in individual stocks. It's important to remember that this data reflects yesterday's activity, as today's NSE archive for participant-wise open interest is not yet published.

Stocks where money moved today

In today's F&O watchlist, we saw some significant movements. Among the top gainers were TCS, which rose 3.53% with a volume of over 73 lakh shares, TITAN gaining 1.30%, ITC up 0.64%, and INFY increasing by 0.49%.

On the losing side, banking stocks were prominently featured. AXISBANK fell 3.97%, HDFCBANK declined 3.33% with a substantial volume of over 5.5 crore shares, ICICIBANK was down 3.32%, and SBIN dropped 2.79%. LT also saw a decline of 2.59%. The concentrated selling in banking names suggests a sector-specific cautiousness today.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we combine these various signals, a nuanced picture emerges for the upcoming session. The Nifty 50 and Sensex, with their negative daily closes, fresh call writing dominance, and lower PCR(OI) values (for Nifty), suggest that market participants are positioning for potential resistance at higher levels. Historically, such a combination of factors has tended to coincide with cautious market sentiment and could indicate that any upward moves might face selling pressure.

Conversely, Bank Nifty, despite its sharp fall, shows a mildly bullish undercurrent from its Max Pain being above spot and a balanced to slightly put-heavy fresh flow. While the immediate reaction to today's decline might be negative, the options data suggests that put writers are attempting to establish a floor, which has historically tended to coincide with attempts at recovery or consolidation after a sharp fall. Overall, the data points towards a potentially mixed day, with broader indices facing headwinds, while the banking sector might see attempts at finding support.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to better interpret daily movements. You can start by visiting the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, specifically the "Derivatives" section, to find the daily option chain data for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty. Look for the "Open Interest" column for both Call and Put options across various strike prices. The strikes with the highest OI indicate potential support and resistance levels.

Additionally, pay attention to the "Change in Open Interest" column, which tells you where fresh buying or selling (writing) activity occurred today. If there's a significant increase in Call OI at a particular strike, it suggests new resistance. If there's a large increase in Put OI, it points to new support. You can also calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI. Many brokers also provide these data points within their trading platforms, making it easier to track. By observing these trends daily, you can develop your own understanding of how market participants are positioning themselves.
