What moved the market — the factors

The primary driver for today's market sentiment was the outcome of the US midterm elections. Democrats secured control of both the Senate and the House of Representatives, a development that eased concerns over major policy shifts and spurred a rally in US stocks. This global optimism translated into positive cues for Indian markets.

A significant impact of the US election results was the sharp decline in crude oil prices. Brent Crude fell by 8.01% to close at $95.30, while WTI Crude dropped by 7.70% to $88.69. Lower oil prices are generally a boon for India, a major oil importer, as they help reduce import bills and inflationary pressures. This directly contributed to a rally in Indian government bonds, which saw their best performance since April.

Furthermore, the US dollar slid against other currencies, with the Dollar Index closing down by 0.39% at 101.47. A weaker dollar typically makes emerging markets like India more attractive to foreign investors. This was reflected in robust buying by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs), who pumped in a net ₹6,837 crore into Indian equities. Conversely, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers, offloading ₹3,679 crore. The strong FII inflow provided significant support to the market.

Globally, the Democrat win led to a surge in renewable energy and healthcare stocks, while AI data center stocks dipped, as investors anticipated potential tighter regulation on artificial intelligence.

News that moved stocks

Here are some of the key news items that influenced market sentiment and specific stocks today:

Democrat Sweep in US Midterm Elections Fuels Global Rally Democrats secured control of both the Senate and the House of Representatives in the US midterm elections, easing concerns over major policy shifts. This outcome led to a rally in US stocks, with the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq advancing, and provided a significant positive impetus to global markets, including India.

Oil Prices Slide Post US Midterm Verdict, Boosting Indian Bonds Following the US midterm election results, crude oil prices experienced a sharp decline, with Brent Crude falling over 8% and WTI Crude dropping over 7%. This significant drop in oil prices led to a strong rally in Indian government bonds, marking their best performance since April, as lower oil prices are beneficial for India's economy.

AI Data Center Stocks Dip, Renewables Surge After US Election The Democrat sweep in the US midterms led to a dip in AI data center stocks globally, while renewable energy and healthcare shares rallied. This reaction came as investors evaluated the potential for tighter regulation on artificial intelligence under the new political landscape.

Aptus Value Housing Finance Q2 Profit Rises 18% to ₹267 crore Aptus Value Housing Finance India reported a robust 18% increase in net profit to ₹267 crore for the second quarter, with total income surging to ₹634 crore. This strong financial performance is positive for the housing finance sector.

Keystone Realtors Q2 Profit Jumps 10-fold to ₹101 crore Keystone Realtors announced a ten-fold increase in consolidated net profit for the September quarter, reaching ₹100.75 crore. This impressive growth was primarily attributed to a significant profit share from joint ventures, indicating strong performance in the real estate development space.

SBI Group Q2 Results Show Mixed Performance State Bank of India (SBI) reported a 14.28% annual profit growth in Q2 FY27, while SBI Cards' profit jumped 75%, and SBI Life's rose 11%. Analysts noted that SBI Cards beat estimates, SBI was broadly in line, and SBI Life missed expectations, leading to varied reactions across the SBI Group stocks.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's market session was largely dominated by the aftermath of the US midterm election results. The news that Democrats secured control of both the Senate and the House of Representatives was a major global event.

This outcome was perceived positively by global markets, leading to a rally in US stocks, with the Dow Jones, S&P 500, and Nasdaq all advancing significantly. For India, the most direct and beneficial impact came from the sharp decline in crude oil prices, which plummeted over 7-8% for both Brent and WTI. This fall in oil prices immediately translated into a strong rally in Indian government bonds, which saw their best performance since April.

The global sentiment also led to a weakening of the US dollar, making emerging markets more attractive. This was evident in the substantial net buying by FIIs in the Indian equity market. While specific Indian index data is unavailable, the news of several stocks hitting 52-week highs (like Ather Energy, PVR Inox, Marico, and Indian Hotels) suggests a broad-based positive reaction in the Indian equity space. Globally, sectors like renewable energy and healthcare saw gains, while AI data center stocks dipped due to concerns over potential tighter regulation.

Setup for tomorrow

With the positive global cues from the US midterm election results and the significant drop in crude oil prices, Indian markets are likely to open with a positive bias tomorrow. US markets rallied strongly today, and a weaker dollar combined with lower oil prices typically bodes well for India. Investors will continue to monitor global developments, including any further reactions to the US Treasury's updated language regarding potential changes in auction sizes for treasury securities. Key sectors to watch include those sensitive to oil prices, as well as stocks that demonstrated strength today, such as those that hit 52-week highs. The performance of banking stocks, particularly those from the SBI Group, will also be in focus following their Q2 results.
