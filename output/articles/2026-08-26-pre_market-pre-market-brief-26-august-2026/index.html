Pre-Market Brief — 26 August 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets closed on a positive note, setting a firm tone for Asian peers.
  • GIFT Nifty indicates a slightly positive open for the Indian benchmark indices.
  • FIIs and DIIs both remained net buyers in the cash segment yesterday, continuing a supportive trend.
  • Crude oil prices have seen a significant decline of over 6% in two days, easing inflation concerns.
  • Nifty's key levels to watch are 24400-24500 as resistance and 24200-24180 as support.
  • The primary risk for today remains global sentiment and sustained FII flows.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Global markets displayed a positive sentiment overnight, with major US indices closing in the green. Asian markets are also largely trading higher this morning, reflecting a continuation of this positive momentum. This broad-based optimism from international bourses is likely to provide a favourable backdrop for the Indian market open.

Currency, commodities and yields

The USD/INR pair closed at ₹95.40, down 0.34%, indicating a slight strengthening of the Rupee against the US Dollar. The Dollar Index also saw a minor dip of 0.04% to 98.96. In commodities, Brent Crude plunged significantly by 7.71% to $85.06, while WTI Crude fell 5.73% to $80.14. This marks a substantial two-day decline in oil prices. Gold saw a healthy gain of 1.45% to $4707.90, and Silver was up 1.16% to $69.33. The US 10-Year Yield dropped by 1.38% to 4.64%.

Interpretation: A stronger Rupee is generally positive for the economy, potentially easing import costs. The sharp fall in crude oil prices is a significant positive for India, which is a major oil importer. This could reduce inflationary pressures and benefit sectors like OMCs, paint, aviation, and logistics. Rising gold prices often indicate a flight to safety or inflation hedging, while falling US yields could make emerging markets like India more attractive to foreign investors.

Option-chain signals (OI) — where support & resistance sit

NIFTY: The highest Call OI is concentrated at 24500 (5,465,980 contracts), followed by 25000 and 24800. This suggests strong resistance around the 24500 level. On the Put side, the highest OI is at 24200 (7,325,500 contracts), followed by 24300 and 24000, indicating robust support around 24200. The PCR (Put-Call Ratio) is 0.96. BANK NIFTY: Highest Call OI is at 57500 (1,813,230 contracts), followed by 58000. Highest Put OI is also at 57500 (1,696,740 contracts), followed by 58000. The PCR is 1.1. SENSEX: Highest Call OI is at 78000 (1,387,920 contracts), followed by 79000. Highest Put OI is at 77500 (1,735,280 contracts), followed by 77000. The PCR is 1.05.

Interpretation: For Nifty, the PCR of 0.96 is neutral-to-slightly cautious, with 24500 acting as a ceiling and 24200 as a floor. For Bank Nifty, a PCR of 1.1 suggests a supportive bias, with 57500 being a crucial pivot where both calls and puts are concentrated. For Sensex, a PCR of 1.05 also indicates a supportive-to-neutral stance, with 78000 as resistance and 77500 as strong support. Combined with technical levels, Nifty needs to decisively cross 24400-24500 for sustained upside, while 24200-24180 will be key support to respect today. Bank Nifty's narrow CPR and high OI at 57500 point to a potential breakout or breakdown from this level.

Bottom line

The Indian market is poised for a firm to slightly positive open today, buoyed by positive global cues and a significant drop in crude oil prices. The sustained buying by both FIIs and DIIs offers a supportive backdrop. Nifty's ability to hold above 24200 and decisively breach the 24400-24500 resistance zone will be crucial for the day's direction, while Bank Nifty's narrow CPR suggests a potential trending move. Investors should remain cautious of any sudden shifts in global sentiment or FII flows.
