Your Digital Rupee Just Got a Push: What Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman Said

TL;DR

  • Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman recently urged the RBI to speed up the adoption of India's Digital Rupee (e₹).
  • The Digital Rupee is India's own digital currency, issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).
  • It works like digital cash in a special wallet on your phone, allowing person-to-person and person-to-merchant payments.
  • The pilot project is expanding, with over 60 lakh users and new features like offline transactions and programmability being tested.
  • This push aims to make digital transactions more efficient, reduce cash handling costs, and improve financial inclusion.

What's this about?

India's Finance Minister, Nirmala Sitharaman, recently called on the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) to accelerate the rollout and adoption of the Digital Rupee (e₹). The Digital Rupee is India's own Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC), which is essentially a digital form of our physical currency, the Indian Rupee. It's designed to function just like the cash you hold in your wallet, but in a virtual format.

This digital currency is a direct liability of the RBI, meaning it carries the same trust and guarantee as the paper notes and coins we use every day. It's currently in a pilot phase, being tested by a select group of users and merchants, and the Finance Minister's statement signals a strong government intent to see it adopted more widely and quickly.

Why it's in the news right now

Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman made these remarks at the Global Fintech Fest in Mumbai in September 2026. She specifically urged the RBI to "further advance and also develop what it has initiated through both the wholesale and retail CBDC pilots." This intervention highlights the government's focus on modernising India's payment systems and leveraging digital currency for various purposes, including retail payments and government disbursements.

The retail Digital Rupee (e₹-R) pilot, which began in December 2022, has been gradually expanding. As of late 2026, the central bank is advancing the pilot towards a wider rollout, with a target for broader adoption by late 2026. The Finance Minister's statement serves as a significant policy signal, indicating that accelerating this transition is now a high-level priority.

How it actually works

The Digital Rupee (e₹) is a digital token that represents legal tender, just like a ₹100 note. Here's how it works:

  1. Issued by RBI: The RBI creates and issues the Digital Rupee electronically. It's not like cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, which are decentralised. The e₹ is a sovereign currency, fully backed by the government.
  2. Distributed via Banks: Commercial banks participating in the pilot distribute the Digital Rupee to their customers. As of end-March 2025, the pilot included 17 banks.
  3. Digital Wallet: Users download a special Digital Rupee app from their participating bank. This app acts as a digital wallet on their mobile phone or device.
  4. Loading Money: You can load your e₹ wallet by transferring money from your linked bank account. This is similar to adding money to a prepaid wallet, but here, you're essentially converting your bank deposits into digital cash.
  5. Transactions: Once in your wallet, you can use the e₹ for two main types of transactions:

    6. * Person-to-Person (P2P): Sending money directly to another individual's e₹ wallet. * Person-to-Merchant (P2M): Paying for goods and services at shops or online by scanning a QR code (either a specific CBDC QR code or even a UPI QR code).

  6. Advanced Features: The RBI is also testing advanced features like:

    7. * Offline Functionality: Allowing transactions even in areas with poor or no internet connectivity. * Programmability: This means the e₹ can be designed for specific uses. For example, government welfare benefits could be issued as programmable e₹ that can only be spent on certain goods (like food) at approved stores.

As of June 2024, the retail CBDC pilot had approximately 5 million users and 420,000 participating merchants. The number of users had grown to over 60 lakh by end-March 2025 across 17 banks.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine you have a physical wallet where you keep your cash. The Digital Rupee is like having a digital version of that cash in a special, secure pocket on your phone. When you use it, it's like handing over a digital ₹100 note directly, instead of using your bank account to send money. Just as you can give a ₹100 note to a friend or a shopkeeper, you can send e₹ directly from your digital wallet to theirs.

Why it matters to you

  1. New, Efficient Payment Option: The Digital Rupee offers another way to pay, complementing existing digital payment methods like UPI. It could make transactions even faster and simpler, especially for small value payments, potentially without needing a PIN for amounts up to ₹500.
  2. Financial Inclusion: With features like offline functionality, the e₹ could bring digital payments to remote areas where internet access is patchy, helping more people participate in the digital economy.
  3. Potential for Targeted Benefits: Programmable e₹ could allow the government to disburse welfare benefits or subsidies that can only be used for specific purposes (e.g., buying food or medicines). This ensures the funds reach the intended beneficiaries and are used as planned.
  4. Reduced Cash Handling Costs: For the country as a whole, a wider adoption of digital currency can reduce the significant costs associated with printing, transporting, and managing physical cash.
  5. Enhanced Security: Being a direct liability of the RBI and built on secure technology, the Digital Rupee offers the trust and finality of central bank money, potentially reducing risks like counterfeiting.

Common confusions cleared up

Myth: The Digital Rupee is just like UPI. Fact: While both are digital payment methods, they are fundamentally different. UPI facilitates payments from your bank account. The Digital Rupee, on the other hand, is the digital form of money itself, held in a separate wallet, much like physical cash. When you use e₹, you're directly transferring digital currency, not sending an instruction to your bank.

Myth: The Digital Rupee is a cryptocurrency like Bitcoin. Fact: No. Cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin are decentralised and their value can fluctuate wildly. The Digital Rupee is a Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC), issued and regulated by the RBI. It has a stable value, always equal to one Indian Rupee, and carries the full backing of the government.

Myth: The Digital Rupee will replace physical cash. Fact: The RBI has clarified that the Digital Rupee is meant to be an additional payment avenue, not a replacement for physical currency. It aims to offer more choices and convenience for digital transactions.

FAQ

What is the Digital Rupee (e₹)?

The Digital Rupee, or e₹, is India's official Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC), issued by the Reserve Bank of India. It is a digital form of the Indian Rupee, designed to be used like cash but in a virtual format.

Who issues the Digital Rupee?

The Digital Rupee is issued directly by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). It is a direct liability of the central bank, just like physical currency.

How can I get a Digital Rupee wallet?

Currently, you can get an e₹ wallet if you are a customer of one of the participating banks in the pilot program. You would download the specific Digital Rupee app from your bank and register using your linked bank account.

Is it safe to use?

Yes, e₹ wallets are designed to be safe and secure. The RBI ensures a robust cybersecurity framework to protect the digital currency in your wallet.

Does the Digital Rupee earn interest?

No, like physical cash, the Digital Rupee itself does not earn any interest. It is a medium of exchange and a store of value, not an investment instrument.
