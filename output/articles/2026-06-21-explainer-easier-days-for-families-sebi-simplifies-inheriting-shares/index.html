Easier Days for Families: SEBI Simplifies Inheriting Shares

TL;DR

  • SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey led a recent Board meeting that approved new rules to make inheriting shares much simpler.
  • If an investor passes away, their family can now claim shares and other securities with less paperwork and faster.
  • This includes a new "Quick Transmission Processing" for smaller amounts, speeding up claims for sums up to ₹30,000.
  • Limits for simplified documentation for larger amounts have also been significantly increased, reducing the need for court orders.
  • These changes aim to ease a difficult process for grieving families and boost investor confidence in the market.

What's this about?

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), India's market regulator, recently announced important changes to how families can claim shares and other investment securities after an investor passes away. This process is called "transmission" of securities. Previously, it could be a long and complicated journey, often requiring extensive legal documents and court orders. The new rules, approved by the SEBI Board on June 19, 2026, aim to make this process much smoother, faster, and less burdensome for legal heirs.

Why it's in the news right now

SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey, who took office on March 1, 2025, for a three-year term, presided over a crucial SEBI Board meeting on June 19, 2026. During this meeting, a wide range of reforms were approved, including significant simplifications for the transmission of securities. This decision is a direct response to feedback from investors and market participants about the difficulties faced by families during a sensitive time. It’s part of SEBI's ongoing effort to improve ease of doing business and strengthen investor protection in India's capital markets.

How it actually works

When an investor passes away, their legal heirs need to get the ownership of the deceased's shares, mutual fund units, or other securities transferred to their names. This is called transmission. Here’s how SEBI is simplifying it:

  1. Quick Transmission Processing (QTP) for Smaller Amounts: For smaller holdings, SEBI has introduced a "Quick Transmission Processing" category. If the value of physical shares (shares held as paper certificates) is up to ₹10,000, or dematerialised (demat) shares (shares held electronically in a demat account) is up to ₹30,000, families can now claim them with minimal documentation.
  2. Higher Limits for Simplified Documentation: For larger holdings, the limits for simplified documentation have been significantly increased. Earlier, for physical shares, simplified documentation was allowed up to ₹5 lakh. This limit has now been doubled to ₹10 lakh. For demat holdings, the limit has been increased from ₹15 lakh to ₹30 lakh. This means more families can use a simpler process without needing a lengthy court process.
  3. Reduced Document Requirements: Importantly, SEBI has removed the requirement for a Permanent Account Number (PAN) and mandatory probate of wills for these simplified processes. Probate is a legal process where a will is proved in court and accepted as a valid public document. Removing this for certain cases will save a lot of time and legal costs.
  4. Role of Depositories and Registrars: The process involves working with the Depository Participant (DP) where the demat account was held, or the Registrar and Share Transfer Agent (RTA) for physical shares. They will guide the legal heirs through the updated, simpler documentation.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine a family where the main earning member passes away suddenly. They had a bank account, some property, and also some shares in different companies. Earlier, getting access to the bank account or transferring the property would be a long process with lots of forms, visits to different offices, and sometimes even court hearings. Now, think of transferring shares like transferring money from a small savings account.

With SEBI's new rules, for smaller amounts of shares, it's like the bank saying, "Just show us a few basic documents, and we'll transfer the money to the rightful heir quickly." For larger amounts, it's like the bank saying, "You still need to show us proper proof, but we've made the list of documents shorter and removed some very difficult ones like needing a judge's stamp for everything." This makes a sad and difficult time a little less stressful for the family.

Why it matters to you

  1. Less Stress During a Difficult Time: Losing a loved one is hard enough. These new rules significantly reduce the emotional and financial burden on families trying to access inherited investments, making a complex legal process much more humane.
  2. Faster Access to Funds: With simpler procedures and reduced documentation, families can gain access to the deceased's investments much quicker. This can be crucial for meeting immediate financial needs.
  3. Reduced Costs: Avoiding lengthy court procedures like obtaining a probate can save families substantial legal fees and other associated expenses, which can run into lakhs of rupees.
  4. Boosts Investor Confidence: Knowing that their investments can be easily passed on to their families provides greater peace of mind for current and prospective investors, encouraging more people to participate in the securities market.
  5. Simplified for Small Investors: The increased limits for simplified documentation mean that even middle-class families with moderate shareholdings can benefit from the easier process, without needing to navigate complex legalities.

Common confusions cleared up

  • Myth: You always need a court order (like a probate or succession certificate) to inherit shares.

    • * Fact: Not anymore for many cases! With the new SEBI rules, for physical shares up to ₹10 lakh and demat shares up to ₹30 lakh, you can use simplified documentation without a mandatory court order.

  • Myth: "Transmission" of shares is the same as "transfer" of shares.

    • * Fact: They are different. "Transmission" happens when shares are passed on to legal heirs due to the death of the original owner. "Transfer" usually refers to a sale or gift of shares from one living person to another.

  • Myth: These new rules only apply to shares bought after June 19, 2026.

    • * Fact: The new rules apply to existing holdings as well, making the process easier for all eligible securities, regardless of when they were purchased.

FAQ

What is "transmission" of securities?

Transmission of securities is the process by which ownership of shares, mutual fund units, or other investments is passed from a deceased investor to their legal heirs.

What is the difference between physical and demat holdings?

Physical holdings refer to shares held as paper certificates. Demat holdings (short for dematerialised) are shares held electronically in a demat account, which is like a bank account for securities.

What kind of "simplified documentation" is needed now?

Typically, this might include a death certificate, a copy of the will (if any), an affidavit, a no-objection certificate (NOC) from other legal heirs, and an indemnity bond. The exact list can vary but is significantly less cumbersome than full court processes.

How quickly can the process be completed now?

While SEBI has simplified the rules, the actual time taken can still depend on the completeness of documents and the efficiency of the Depository Participant or Registrar and Share Transfer Agent. However, the aim is to complete the process much faster than before, especially with the Quick Transmission Processing for smaller amounts.

Does this apply to all types of investments?

These rules primarily apply to listed shares and other securities like mutual fund units. For other assets like property or bank accounts, different rules apply, though the general principle of simplifying inheritance is gaining traction across various financial sectors.
