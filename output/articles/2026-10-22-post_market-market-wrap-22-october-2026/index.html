Market Wrap — 22 October 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian equities witnessed a negative close today, with Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty both ending in the red.
  • Global cues, particularly concerns over AI spending in the US and rising crude oil prices, contributed to the cautious sentiment.
  • Tata Teleservices (Maharashtra) Ltd. emerged as the top Nifty 500 gainer, soaring over 18%.
  • KEI Industries Ltd. was the biggest Nifty 500 loser, declining over 8%.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were both net buyers in the cash market today.

How the market behaved today

Indian markets closed lower today, extending losses through the session after a relatively flat open. The Nifty 50 ended down by 0.54%, while the Bank Nifty also registered a modest decline of 0.09%. Broader markets, represented by the Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100, saw sharper cuts, indicating a broad-based weakness across segments.

What moved the market — the factors

The Indian market's cautious mood today was largely influenced by a combination of global cues and broad-based selling pressure. US stocks opened lower on Thursday, driven by renewed concerns over the outlook for artificial intelligence spending, which weighed on growth stocks. This global sentiment seemed to spill over into domestic trading. Additionally, rising crude oil prices, with Brent Crude up over 2% and WTI Crude up over 1%, also contributed to investor jitters, as higher oil prices can impact inflation and corporate margins.

Domestically, while both Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net buyers today, the broader market breadth was negative, with declines significantly outnumbering advances. This indicated underlying weakness and profit-booking across a wide range of stocks. Sectors like Media, FMCG, and Energy faced significant selling pressure, pulling down the overall indices.

Sectors — winners and losers

Today saw a mixed bag for sectors, with Pharma and PSU Banks showing resilience, while Media and FMCG faced headwinds.

Top 3 Gainer Sectors:

  • PHARMA: +0.12% - The sector managed a marginal gain, possibly on defensive buying interest.
  • PSU BANK: +0.07% - Public sector banks showed slight positive momentum.
  • BANK: -0.09% - The broader banking sector, including private banks, ended marginally in the red.

Top 3 Loser Sectors:

  • MEDIA: -2.35% - This sector was the top laggard, experiencing significant selling pressure.
  • FMCG: -1.41% - Fast-moving consumer goods companies saw considerable declines.
  • ENERGY: -1.05% - Despite rising crude oil prices, the domestic energy sector closed lower.

News that moved stocks

  • SEBI warns investors against WhatsApp stock tips. The market regulator has cautioned investors against unauthorized stock tips and fake profit claims spread through exclusive WhatsApp groups, flagging tactics like astroturfing and the use of overseas numbers. This news serves as a general warning for investors across the market.
  • NCDEX launches NDEX and FINNDEX to expand into equity benchmarks. NCDEX has introduced two new equity benchmarks, NDEX and FINNDEX, designed to track 20 large, liquid Indian stocks and 15 financial sector companies, respectively. This development marks an expansion of NCDEX into equity benchmarks and could impact how market performance is tracked.
  • CreditAccess Grameen Q2 profit jumps fourfold to Rs 502 crore. CreditAccess Grameen reported a substantial increase in its Q2 net profit, driven by higher income and improved asset quality. This positive earnings report could provide a boost to the financial services sector and specifically to CreditAccess Grameen shares.
  • Record galore! Jio IPO claims crown for highest QIB demand among India’s 5 largest issues, edges past NSE. Reliance Jio Platforms' ₹30,213 crore IPO has garnered exceptional Qualified Institutional Buyer (QIB) subscription, surpassing previous records. This strong demand highlights robust investor confidence in Jio Platforms and could set a positive tone for future large-cap IPOs.
  • Colgate-Palmolive India Q2 Results: Profit rises 4.3% to Rs 341.6 crore. Colgate-Palmolive India reported a modest increase in net profit for the September quarter, along with higher sales. This news provides an update on the performance of a key player in the FMCG sector.
  • Hatsun Agro Product Q2 Results: Profit rises 5.6% on strong sales. Hatsun Agro Product also announced a growth in net profit for the September quarter, supported by strong total income. This indicates steady performance in the consumer goods segment.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's session was primarily driven by broad market sentiment and global cues rather than a single defining domestic event. Concerns over AI spending jitters in the US market and the rebound in crude oil prices created a cautious environment globally, which translated into selling pressure on Indian equities. The negative market breadth further underscored the widespread nature of this sentiment, as investors opted for profit-booking across various sectors. While some companies like CreditAccess Grameen, Hatsun Agro Product, and Colgate-Palmolive India announced positive Q2 results, these individual earnings reports did not significantly alter the overall cautious tone of the market.

Setup for tomorrow

As we head into tomorrow's session, global cues will continue to be a key determinant. US stocks slid today due to AI spending jitters, and this sentiment could carry forward. The Dollar Index remained relatively stable, while the US 10-year yield saw a slight dip. However, rising crude oil prices (Brent at $111.10 and WTI at $98.24) could be a point of concern, potentially impacting inflation outlooks. Investors will also be watching the USD/INR movement, which closed at 94.81 today. Sectors that saw significant selling today, such as Media, FMCG, and Energy, might remain under pressure, while Pharma and PSU Banks could offer some resilience.
