Business Mavericks · 2026-09-25

The year was 1997. India’s internet landscape was a sparse, nascent frontier, home to perhaps a mere 14,000 users. Bandwidth was a luxury, and the idea of a digital business making money seemed almost laughable. Yet, from a modest servant’s quarter above his father’s garage in Delhi, Sanjeev Bikhchandani was about to launch something that would defy all expectations: Naukri.com. With a seed capital of roughly ₹2,000, his vision was simple, almost mundane: aggregate the job listings scattered across countless newspapers and magazines into one searchable online database. Most people were busy chasing the dot-com gold rush, but Bikhchandani was quietly laying the groundwork for India’s most profitable internet company, not by saying "yes" to every trend, but by mastering the art of saying "no."

The World Before

Before Naukri.com, finding a job in India was a tedious, analog affair. Job seekers would pore over the classified sections of dozens of daily newspapers and specialized business magazines, each a fragmented piece of a larger, opaque market. Companies, in turn, placed advertisements in these publications, hoping to catch the eye of suitable candidates. It was a slow, inefficient process, often relying on word-of-mouth or headhunters for unadvertised roles. The internet, though emerging, was hardly seen as a viable platform for serious business. It was a novelty, an academic tool, not a marketplace. Many early internet ventures were characterized by exorbitant spending, aggressive expansion, and, ultimately, spectacular crashes during the dot-com bust of the early 2000s. The prevailing wisdom was "growth at all costs," often at the expense of profitability, a notion Bikhchandani would soon challenge.

The Crack

Sanjeev Bikhchandani, an IIM Ahmedabad graduate, founded Info Edge in 1995, and two years later, in March 1997, Naukri.com officially launched. The earliest version of the site featured around 1,000 job postings, painstakingly collected from 29 different newspapers by data entry operators. Revenue in the first year, 1997-98, was a modest ₹2.35 lakh, with the first cheque arriving only after six months, largely because they hadn't bothered to ask for money earlier. Bikhchandani himself operated without a salary for nearly six years during these "drifting years," sustained by his wife's income.

The turning point came when sales jumped to ₹18 lakh in the second year, 1998-99, revealing the immense potential of online aggregation. But unlike many of his contemporaries who burned through cash in pursuit of market share, Bikhchandani adopted a fundamentally different approach: a relentless focus on profitability and sustainable growth. Info Edge achieved its first profit of ₹1.80 lakh in 1999-2000, a stark contrast to the prevalent loss-making internet companies of the era. By 2002-03, Naukri.com was consistently profitable.

This profitability was not accidental; it was a deliberate strategy rooted in what Bikhchandani would later articulate as his "saying no" philosophy. He famously resisted the urge for aggressive inorganic growth. "We are builders, not buyers," he once stated, acknowledging his conservative approach to large acquisitions. This meant passing on what would become some of India's biggest startup success stories. He declined to invest in Flipkart at the pre-Series A stage, passed on Ola, and rejected Snapdeal twice, alongside BigBasket. While these might be seen as missed opportunities by some, Bikhchandani views them differently. He believes no investor gets every decision right; what truly matters is having clear reasons, learning from misses, and backing enough winners.

This conservative, long-term mindset also extended to Info Edge's foray into venture investing. It wasn't a proactive strategy to become a venture capitalist, but rather a need for "compliance" in 2007, when the company had capital but saw no suitable large acquisition targets in the internet space at a decent price. This led to investing in early-stage startups, a form of inorganic growth. Bikhchandani's investment philosophy emphasizes identifying category-defining companies and backing strong founders with integrity, commitment, and a clear vision for solving unsolved problems, rather than chasing specific sectors or inflated valuations. This patient approach led to early bets on companies like Zomato (then FoodieBay) and Policybazaar, despite initial skepticism from his own board about investing in restaurant directories or nascent insurance comparison platforms.

What Happened Next

The disciplined, long-term approach paid off handsomely. In 2006, Info Edge became the first Indian internet company to go public, with its IPO oversubscribed more than 54 times. By 2011, Naukri.com commanded a 55% market share, solidifying its position as India's largest job search site. Today, it remains India's number one job portal, consistently dominating recruitment listings and serving as a major revenue engine for its parent company, Info Edge.

Info Edge diversified its classifieds portfolio, launching Jeevansathi.com (matrimonial) in 1998, 99acres.com (real estate) in 2005, and Shiksha.com (education) in 2008. As of September 2026, Info Edge boasts a market capitalization of approximately ₹80,657 crore (over $10 billion).

The company's financial performance reflects its sustained profitability. In FY25, Info Edge reported revenues of ₹39,236 million, a 33% increase from FY24, with a 5-year CAGR of 32.4%. Net profit in FY25 surged to ₹13,099 million, a 120.3% increase year-on-year. The company maintains impressive operating profit margins, reaching 35.8% in FY25, and net profit margins of 47.4% as of June 2026.

Perhaps the most compelling testament to Bikhchandani's patient investment philosophy is Info Edge's venture portfolio. From cumulative investments of approximately ₹4,900 crore across 135 startups since 2007, the portfolio was valued at nearly ₹41,300 crore as of June 2026, delivering a remarkable 743% return. Info Edge's initial ₹147 crore investment in Zomato, for instance, grew to an astonishing ₹18,300 crore, a 124x gain. The combined value of its shareholding in Zomato and Policybazaar alone exceeds ₹31,500 crore. Sanjeev Bikhchandani, through his stake in Info Edge and its investments, has achieved a net worth of approximately $2.7 billion as of September 2026, making him one of India's wealthiest self-made entrepreneurs and an internet pioneer.

Lessons to Take Away

  1. Profitability Over Hype: In an era often defined by "growth at all costs," Naukri.com demonstrated that a relentless focus on cash flow and achieving profitability early on can build a resilient business that outlasts market fluctuations and dot-com busts.
  2. Strategic Conservatism is a Superpower: By consciously "saying no" to aggressive, ego-driven acquisitions or jumping on every trending opportunity, Info Edge conserved capital, maintained focus, and allowed its core businesses to mature sustainably, even if it meant missing out on some high-profile bets.
  3. Patience as a Core Asset: From bootstrapping for years without a salary to making long-term, calculated bets on early-stage startups, Bikhchandani exemplified extreme patience. This long-term horizon allowed investments to compound significantly and core businesses to entrench themselves.
  4. Invest in Founders, Not Just Sectors: Info Edge's successful venture strategy wasn't about identifying "hot" sectors but rather about backing founders with integrity, capabilities, and a genuine drive to solve unsolved problems, even if their initial ideas seemed niche or unconventional.
  5. Build Deep Moats, Then Diversify: Naukri.com first built an unassailable leadership position in online recruitment before venturing into other classifieds and, eventually, strategic investments. This approach ensured a strong cash-generating core to fund future growth and diversification.
