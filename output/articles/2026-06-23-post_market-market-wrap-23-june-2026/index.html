Market Wrap — 23 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed significantly lower, shedding over 1% each.
  • Broader markets also saw declines, with Midcap 100 down over 1% and Smallcap 100 losing nearly half a percent.
  • Pharma emerged as the top-gaining sector, while Metal, IT, and PSU Bank sectors witnessed sharp declines.
  • Global cues, particularly a deep sell-off in US tech stocks, weighed heavily on market sentiment.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support.

Today's session saw a clear divergence in sectoral performance, with healthcare-related sectors showing resilience amidst a broader market downturn.

  • PHARMA: +0.92% - This sector showed strength today, with several healthcare stocks featuring among the top Nifty 500 gainers.
  • FMCG: -0.60% - While technically a loser, it was among the least impacted sectors today.
  • AUTO: -0.77% - Similar to FMCG, the auto sector saw a relatively smaller decline compared to the broader market.

Top 3 Loser Sectors:

  • METAL: -3.22% - The metal sector was the biggest laggard, with major players experiencing significant declines.
  • IT: -2.23% - Global tech sell-off sentiment likely contributed to the sharp fall in Indian IT stocks.
  • PSU BANK: -1.97% - Public sector banks also faced selling pressure, contributing to the overall market weakness.

Market breadth

The market breadth was overwhelmingly negative today, with 387 declines against a mere 113 advances out of 500 stocks. This clearly indicates that selling pressure was widespread across the market, affecting a large number of stocks.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the Indian equity market today, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided some buying support.

What moved markets today

Global Tech Sell-off and AI Spending Worries: Global markets, particularly US tech stocks, experienced a significant downturn today, which had a ripple effect on Indian equities. Nasdaq futures sank nearly 3% amid concerns over AI spending and hawkish Federal Reserve bets. Elon Musk's fortune took a substantial hit as SpaceX shares tumbled, contributing to a broader tech sell-off that saw the Nasdaq 100 on track to lose over $1 trillion in market value. This global sentiment weighed heavily on India's IT sector.

Rupee Weakens as Fed Rate Expectations Boost Dollar: The Indian rupee slipped against the US dollar today, aligning with global currency movements. The dollar climbed to its highest point in a year, fueled by renewed expectations of Federal Reserve rate hikes. This strengthening dollar and the prospect of higher US interest rates typically lead to capital outflows from emerging markets like India.

Easing Oil Prices and Bond Yields: In a contrasting development, Indian government bond yields dipped to a three-month low today. This was primarily buoyed by easing global oil prices and receding geopolitical tensions. Lower bond yields can sometimes signal a more favourable environment for corporate borrowing, but today's market was dominated by other negative cues.

SEBI Proposes Common Advertising Code: The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has proposed a common advertising code for various regulated entities, including brokers and mutual funds. This move aims to simplify compliance and enhance investor protection. While a regulatory development, its immediate market impact is likely limited.

Setup for tomorrow

The Indian market is likely to remain influenced by global cues, especially the ongoing sentiment in US tech stocks and the trajectory of the US dollar. Overnight, the USD/INR closed higher at 94.72, and the Dollar Index also strengthened, suggesting continued pressure on the rupee. Brent Crude and WTI Crude both saw declines, which could offer some relief on the inflation front. US 10-year yield rose slightly. Investors will be closely watching for any further developments in global markets and statements from central banks. Sectors like IT and Metals might continue to face headwinds, while defensive sectors like Pharma could see sustained interest.
