Foreign Salary in NRE Account Not Taxable for NRIs: ITAT Clarifies

TL;DR

  • The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) Ahmedabad has ruled that foreign salary earned by an NRI outside India is not taxable in India.
  • This non-taxable status holds even if the foreign salary is credited to an NRE (Non-Resident External) account in India.
  • The mere deduction of TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) using an Indian TAN (Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number) on foreign income does not make it taxable in India.
  • The ruling reinforces the principle that an NRI's foreign-sourced income is generally exempt from Indian income tax.
  • NRIs should ensure their residential status is correctly determined to avoid such tax demands.

What this means in plain terms

For many Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), especially those working in the Gulf or other countries, a common question arises: is the salary they earn abroad taxable in India if they send it back home? The recent ruling by the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT) in Ahmedabad provides significant clarity and relief on this front. It states unequivocally that salary earned by an NRI for services rendered outside India is not taxable in India.

This holds true even if that foreign-earned salary is deposited into an NRE bank account in India. The tax department cannot demand tax on such income simply because it lands in an Indian NRE account or because some form of TDS might have been erroneously deducted using an Indian TAN. The core principle is that income earned by an NRI outside India, from sources outside India, is typically not subject to Indian income tax. This ruling safeguards NRIs from potential unwarranted tax demands on their legitimate foreign earnings.

Understanding NRE Accounts and Tax Benefits

NRE (Non-Resident External) accounts are bank accounts opened by NRIs in India to deposit their foreign earnings. These accounts are special because they allow for the free repatriation of both the principal amount and the interest earned, back to the foreign country without any restrictions.

The key benefit of an NRE account, from a tax perspective, is that the interest earned on deposits in these accounts is fully exempt from income tax in India. This exemption is crucial for NRIs looking to save or invest their foreign income in India without incurring additional tax liabilities on the interest component. The principal amount, being foreign-sourced income, is also treated distinctly.

Key Features of NRE Accounts:

  • Currency: Maintained in Indian Rupees (INR).
  • Source of Funds: Only foreign currency remittances from abroad or funds legitimately earned abroad can be deposited.
  • Repatriability: Both principal and interest are fully repatriable.
  • Taxation: Interest earned on NRE accounts is exempt from income tax in India.

Residential Status and Tax Liability for NRIs

Under the Indian Income Tax Act, an individual's tax liability in India is primarily determined by their residential status, not their citizenship. The Act defines various categories of residential status: Resident, Non-Resident (NR), and Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR).

Determining Residential Status:

The determination of residential status depends on the number of days an individual spends in India during a financial year (April 1 to March 31) and in preceding financial years.

  • Non-Resident (NR): An individual is generally considered a Non-Resident if they are in India for less than 182 days in a financial year, or less than 120 days if their Indian-sourced income exceeds ₹15 lakhs.
  • Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR): If an individual meets certain conditions regarding their stay in India over current and past years.
  • Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR): This status applies to individuals who meet some, but not all, conditions for ROR, often including NRIs who have recently returned to India.

Tax Implications based on Residential Status:

  • Resident and Ordinarily Resident (ROR): Taxable on their global income (income earned anywhere in the world).
  • Non-Resident (NR): Taxable only on income that accrues or arises in India, or is deemed to accrue or arise in India. Foreign-sourced income is generally not taxable.
  • Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR): Taxable on income earned in India and income from a business or profession controlled or set up in India. Foreign income not connected to an Indian business/profession is generally exempt.

This distinction is crucial for NRIs, as their foreign salary, being income earned outside India, falls outside the purview of Indian taxation as long as they maintain their Non-Resident status.

The ITAT Ruling: Clarifying Foreign Salary Taxation

The recent ruling by the ITAT Ahmedabad has provided significant relief by clarifying the taxability of foreign salaries for NRIs. The case involved an individual who was a Non-Resident Indian and earned salary for services rendered outside India. This foreign salary was credited to their NRE bank account in India. The Income Tax Department had raised a demand for tax, contending that since the money was credited to an Indian account and TDS was reportedly deducted using an Indian TAN, it should be taxable in India.

The ITAT, however, rejected this contention, citing the fundamental principles of the Income Tax Act. It reiterated that for a Non-Resident, only income that accrues or arises in India, or is deemed to accrue or arise in India, is taxable. Income earned for services rendered outside India, by an individual who is a Non-Resident, does not fall into this category.

Key Takeaways from the Ruling:

  • Source of Income is Paramount: The decisive factor is where the income is earned and where the services are rendered. If the services are rendered outside India by an NRI, the income is considered foreign-sourced.
  • NRE Account Credit is Irrelevant for Taxability: Simply crediting foreign-earned salary into an NRE account in India does not change its character from foreign-sourced income to Indian-sourced income. NRE accounts are specifically designed for repatriating foreign earnings.
  • Erroneous TDS/TAN Does Not Create Tax Liability: If TDS was mistakenly deducted using an Indian TAN on foreign salary, or if an Indian TAN was involved in the transaction, it does not automatically make the foreign income taxable in India. Such deductions would be considered erroneous.
  • Reinforces NRI Tax Principles: The ruling reinforces the well-established principle that NRIs are taxed only on their Indian-sourced income.

This ruling provides a strong precedent and clarity, ensuring that NRIs are not unfairly taxed on their legitimate foreign earnings merely due to banking practices or administrative errors.

A real example

Meet Sanjay, 42, a software engineer working in Dubai, earning AED 30,000 per month. Sanjay has been working in Dubai for 10 years and visits India for about 30 days each year. This makes him a Non-Resident (NR) for income tax purposes in India.

Sanjay regularly remits a portion of his salary, say AED 15,000 (approximately ₹3.4 lakhs at current exchange rates), to his NRE savings account in India every month. Over a financial year, he credits about ₹40.8 lakhs of his foreign salary to this NRE account.

In a hypothetical scenario, the Income Tax Department issues a notice to Sanjay, demanding tax on this ₹40.8 lakhs, arguing that since the money was credited to an Indian bank account, it constitutes taxable income in India. They might even point to an erroneous TDS deduction by an overseas employer's Indian payroll entity, if such a situation arose.

Based on the ITAT ruling:

  1. Sanjay's Residential Status: He is a Non-Resident, as he spends less than 182 days in India.
  2. Source of Income: His salary is earned for services rendered entirely in Dubai. This is foreign-sourced income.
  3. NRE Account: The NRE account is designed for holding foreign earnings, and merely crediting funds to it does not convert foreign income into Indian income.
  4. Taxability: As a Non-Resident, Sanjay is only taxable on income that accrues or arises in India. His foreign salary does not fall into this category.

Therefore, the tax demand on Sanjay's ₹40.8 lakhs foreign salary would be invalid according to the ITAT ruling. He would be able to successfully contest the demand, citing his residential status and the foreign source of his income, reinforced by this judicial precedent.

What to do this week

  1. Verify Your Residential Status: Reconfirm your residential status (NR, RNOR, ROR) for the current financial year based on your days of stay in India. This is foundational for your tax liability. You can refer to the Income Tax Department's guidelines on their official portal for clarity.
  2. Review Bank Statements: Check if any foreign salary remittances have been mistakenly categorised or had TDS deducted by an Indian entity. Keep clear records of the source of funds credited to your NRE accounts.
  3. Consult a Financial Advisor: If you have received any tax notices regarding foreign income credited to an NRE account, or if you are unsure about your tax position, consult a Certified Financial Planner (CFP) or a tax expert specialising in NRI taxation.
  4. Maintain Documentation: Keep meticulous records of your employment contract, salary slips from your foreign employer, entry/exit stamps in your passport, and bank statements showing remittances from abroad to your NRE account. These will serve as proof of your residential status and the foreign source of income.

FAQ

Is all income earned by an NRI tax-free in India?

No, only income earned outside India for services rendered outside India is generally tax-free for NRIs. Income that accrues or arises in India (e.g., rental income from Indian property, interest from NRO accounts, capital gains from Indian assets) is taxable in India.

What if my foreign employer has an Indian entity and uses an Indian TAN for my salary?

The ITAT ruling clarifies that the mere use of an Indian TAN or erroneous TDS deduction does not make foreign-sourced income taxable for an NRI. The key is where the services are rendered and your residential status.

Can I transfer funds from my NRE account to an NRO account?

Yes, you can transfer funds from an NRE account to an NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) account. However, remember that interest earned on NRO accounts is taxable in India.

What is the difference between an NRE and an NRO account?

NRE accounts are for depositing foreign earnings, and both principal and interest are fully repatriable and interest is tax-exempt. NRO accounts are for managing Indian-sourced income (e.g., rent, dividends) and foreign income, but interest is taxable and only the current income (not principal) is generally repatriable after tax.

Does this ruling apply to income other than salary?

The specific ruling pertains to foreign salary. However, the underlying principle that foreign-sourced income of an NRI is not taxable in India generally extends to other forms of foreign income (e.g., foreign capital gains, foreign business income) as long as they are truly foreign-sourced and the individual maintains NR status.
