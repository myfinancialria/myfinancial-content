Pre-Market Brief — 22 May 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets closed mostly positive, with Asian indices showing strong gains, led by Japan's Nikkei.
  • Indian rupee strengthened against the US Dollar yesterday, but the RBI is reportedly considering measures to stabilise the currency amidst its recent decline.
  • Crude oil prices saw a significant jump, which could fuel inflationary concerns and impact India's current account deficit.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak in Indian equities for the third consecutive session, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net buyers.
  • A significant number of companies, including L&T, LTTS, and Havells, are trading ex-dividend or ex-buyback today.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) strengthened by 0.61% to close at ₹95.94 against the US Dollar yesterday. This appreciation comes amidst reports of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) considering measures to stabilise the currency after its recent record lows, which had increased import costs. A stronger rupee is generally positive for import-dependent India as it makes imports cheaper and can help curb imported inflation.

The Dollar Index, which measures the US Dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies, saw a marginal increase of 0.06% to 99.25. This indicates a slight strengthening of the dollar globally, despite the rupee's appreciation.

In commodities, crude oil prices witnessed a notable surge. Brent Crude climbed by 2.36% to $105.00 per barrel, while WTI Crude rose by 1.84% to $98.12 per barrel. Rising crude oil prices are a significant concern for India, as the country imports a large portion of its oil requirements. This increase could lead to higher fuel prices, potentially fuelling inflation and widening the current account deficit.

Gold prices dipped by 0.35% to $4523.90, while Silver saw a modest gain of 0.42% to $76.74. This indicates mixed movements in precious metals.

The US 10-Year Treasury Yield increased by 0.31% to 4.59%. A rise in US bond yields can make dollar-denominated assets more attractive, potentially leading to capital outflows from emerging markets like India. It also reflects expectations of higher interest rates or inflation in the US.

Recent FII/DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling trend in Indian equities, offloading shares worth ₹1,891 crore yesterday. This marks the third consecutive session of net selling by FIIs. Conversely, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained strong buyers, injecting ₹2,492 crore into the market, thereby cushioning the impact of FII outflows.

| Investor Type | Net Cash Position (₹ crore) | | :------------ | :-------------------------- | | FII | -1,891 | | DII | +2,492 |

Over the last five sessions, FIIs have consistently been net sellers, with total outflows exceeding ₹5,900 crore, while DIIs have provided consistent buying support, with total inflows over ₹8,200 crore.

Overnight news that matters

Rupee plunge sees India turn to 2013 taper tantrum playbook India's central bank is reportedly considering various measures to stabilise the rupee, which has recently fallen to a record low. This move to prevent further depreciation and control import costs indicates the RBI's proactive stance. Why it matters to Indian markets: A stable rupee is crucial for economic stability, managing inflation, and attracting foreign investment. Any concrete steps by the RBI could impact currency markets and investor sentiment towards Indian assets.

Indian stock market to remain on FPIs 'sell' radar this year; here's why Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) have sold Indian equities worth over ₹2,20,000 crore so far in 2026, following significant outflows last year. Analysts suggest this selling trend might continue, contributing to the Nifty 50's 3% decline year-to-date. Why it matters to Indian markets: Persistent FPI selling can create downward pressure on Indian equities. While DIIs have been providing support, sustained foreign outflows could cap market upside and increase volatility.

MTAR Tech shares soar 24% in just three sessions. What’s triggering this renewed buying spree? MTAR Technologies shares have seen a significant rally, jumping 24% over three days, after securing a ₹467.3 crore order from an international client. The company stated this deal is part of ongoing business. Why it matters to Indian markets: This news highlights stock-specific action driven by strong order wins. Companies with robust order books and execution capabilities can attract investor interest, potentially leading to significant price movements.

JSW Cement shares gain 12% as Q4FY26 profit jumps to Rs 362 crore JSW Cement shares surged over 11% following a strong Q4FY26 earnings report, which showed a remarkable 2,162% year-on-year profit jump. This was attributed to robust revenue growth and improved operational efficiency. Why it matters to Indian markets: Strong quarterly results, especially with significant profit growth, can lead to positive sentiment and upward price revisions for individual stocks and potentially the broader sector. This indicates strength in the cement sector.

Why it matters to Indian markets: The IT sector is a major component of Indian indices. Divided opinions on its outlook suggest potential for selective buying or continued consolidation. Investors will be watching for signs of demand recovery or clarity on future growth drivers.

What to watch in today's session

  1. RBI's Stance on Rupee Stability: Monitor any further announcements or actions from the Reserve Bank of India regarding currency stabilisation measures, as this will directly impact the USD/INR pair and broader market sentiment.
  2. Crude Oil Price Movement: Keep a close eye on Brent and WTI crude oil prices. Continued upward pressure could reignite inflation concerns and impact sectors reliant on crude, such as paints, chemicals, and logistics.
  3. FII/DII Flow Dynamics: Observe the FII and DII activity today. Persistent FII selling, if not adequately absorbed by DII buying, could lead to market volatility.
  4. Stock-Specific Actions: Watch out for stocks like MTAR Technologies and JSW Cement, which saw significant moves based on recent news. Also, companies going ex-dividend/buyback today, such as L&T, LTTS, and Havells, might see increased trading activity.
  5. Sectoral Performance: Pay attention to the IT sector, given the mixed views on its valuation and growth prospects. Also, the pharma sector is being highlighted by some experts, so watch for any momentum there.

Bottom line

The Indian market is likely to open with a mixed bias today. While global cues are largely positive, the persistent selling by FIIs and rising crude oil prices remain key concerns. The rupee's recent volatility and the RBI's potential intervention will be closely watched. Investors should remain cautious and focus on fundamentally strong companies amidst these evolving macro and micro factors.
