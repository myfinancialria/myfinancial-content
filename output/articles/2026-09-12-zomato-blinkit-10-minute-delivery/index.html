Why Deepinder Goyal Bet Zomato on 10-Minute Grocery Delivery When Everyone Said It Was Impossible

The air in June 2022 was thick with skepticism. Zomato, the Indian food delivery giant, had just announced its audacious plan to acquire Blinkit, a struggling quick-commerce startup formerly known as Grofers. Investors reacted with a shudder: Zomato's stock plummeted by over 20% in the days following the announcement, sinking from ₹70.50 to around ₹55 apiece within six trading sessions. Analysts widely derided the ₹4,447 crore (approximately $568 million) all-stock deal as a "terrible decision," warning that a loss-making company was acquiring another "money-losing business" in a cash-burning sector. Yet, Deepinder Goyal, Zomato's founder and CEO, stood firm. He believed this seemingly reckless bet on 10-minute grocery delivery wasn't just strategic – it was the future.

The World Before: A Cash Bonfire

Before Zomato's bold move, the quick commerce landscape in India was a battleground defined by aggressive expansion and even more aggressive cash burn. Companies like Swiggy Instamart, Zepto, Dunzo, and BigBasket were locked in a fierce race to deliver groceries and essentials to customers in under 30 minutes, often even quicker. This nascent industry, projected to grow exponentially from $300 million in 2022 to $5 billion by 2025, was also notorious for its abysmal unit economics.

The fundamental challenge lay in the high operational costs. Quick commerce relied on "dark stores" – small warehouses strategically located in densely populated areas – and a vast fleet of delivery riders. Delivering hundreds of thousands of small-value orders, often under ₹300, across congested metro traffic meant last-mile logistics alone accounted for 12-15% of the transaction value. Rider wages, fuel, dark store rent, and packaging further squeezed already thin margins. Most platforms reported negative unit economics, with delivery costs often exceeding ₹50-₹70 per order.

Grofers, which rebranded to Blinkit in December 2021 to align with its 10-minute delivery vision, was no exception. Despite pioneering express grocery delivery and raising significant capital from investors like SoftBank and Tiger Global, it found itself in dire straits. By March 2022, the company was cash-strapped, having fired 1,600 employees – nearly 5% of its workforce. It was a clear signal of the quick commerce segment's struggle for sustainable profitability, making Zomato's acquisition appear to many as a bailout rather than a strategic play.

The Crack: A Bet on Density and Discipline

Deepinder Goyal saw not a dying venture, but a synergistic opportunity. He had been vocal about quick commerce being a strategic priority for Zomato since 2021, recognizing its potential to expand customer "wallet share" and complement the existing food delivery business. The peak demand times for food delivery and quick commerce often differed, meaning a shared delivery fleet could be utilized more efficiently, driving down overall delivery costs. Critically, Goyal believed that only 10-minute delivery truly made sense in the quick commerce space. "Thirty minutes is not quick. 10 is quick," he asserted.

The acquisition of Blinkit for ₹4,447 crore, which included a $100 million investment made in March 2022 for over 10% stake, was completed by August 2022. Zomato also acquired Blinkit's warehousing and ancillary services business, Hyperpure, for ₹60.7 crores, aiming for greater supply chain integration.

The core of Blinkit's turnaround strategy under Zomato hinged on operational discipline and system design. Goyal vehemently defended the 10-minute delivery promise, clarifying that it wasn't achieved by pressuring riders to speed. Instead, it was enabled by a high density of dark stores located close to customers. Once an order was placed, items were picked and packed within 2.5 minutes. The delivery partner would then travel an average of under 2 kilometers in roughly 8 minutes, maintaining an average speed of about 15 kmph. Crucially, riders didn't even have an in-app timer showing the promised delivery time, removing the direct pressure to rush.

This focus on hyper-local density and efficient internal processes was complemented by a significant shift in Blinkit's business model. It transitioned from a pure marketplace model, where sellers managed inventory, to a 1P (first-party) inventory-led model. This meant Blinkit started buying and managing inventory directly, allowing for better control over stock, faster picking, and improved margins. The number of dark stores rapidly expanded, further shrinking delivery radii and enhancing speed.

What Happened Next: A Remarkable Ascent

The market's initial skepticism soon gave way to a remarkable turnaround. By 2024, Blinkit had not only shed its "cash-burning" label but had also become Zomato's fastest-growing segment, significantly contributing to the parent company's profitability.

The numbers tell a compelling story of growth:

  • Profitability: Zomato achieved its first full-year consolidated net profit of ₹351 crore in FY24, a dramatic swing from a loss of ₹971 crore in FY23. A major contributor to this was Blinkit turning adjusted EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization) positive in March 2024 (Q4 FY24), and reporting a profit of ₹43 crore in Q1 FY25.
  • Revenue Growth: Blinkit's revenue from operations nearly tripled, surging from ₹806 crore in FY23 to ₹2,301 crore in FY24. In Q1 FY25, Blinkit's revenue soared to ₹942 crore, marking an exceptional 2.4x year-on-year growth.
  • Gross Order Value (GOV): Blinkit's GOV, a key metric representing the total value of orders, nearly doubled to ₹4,027 crore in Q4 FY24 and further surged to ₹4,923 crore in Q1 FY25, a 2.3x increase year-on-year.
  • Order Volume & Market Share: Blinkit's monthly orders grew exponentially, from 3.5 million in August 2022 to over 20 million by late 2024. In Q1 FY25, Blinkit processed 78.8 million orders. Blinkit firmly established itself as the market leader in India's quick commerce segment, capturing an estimated 41-50% market share in 2024. By Q1 FY25, it held 46% of the market, significantly ahead of Swiggy Instamart (25%) and Zepto (29%). In Q1 FY26, Blinkit's quick commerce Net Order Value (NOV) surpassed Zomato's food delivery NOV for the first time.
  • Network Expansion: Blinkit expanded its dark store network to 526 stores by Q4 FY24, with ambitious plans to reach 1,000 stores by March 2025.

Zomato's (renamed Eternal Limited in 2024 for its broader portfolio) stock price also saw a significant recovery, peaking at over ₹300 towards the end of 2024, a testament to the market's renewed confidence in the quick commerce strategy.

Lessons to Take Away

  1. Bold Bets on Synergy can Yield Outsized Returns: Deepinder Goyal's decision to acquire a struggling quick commerce player was met with widespread criticism. However, his conviction in the underlying synergy between food delivery and quick commerce, particularly in optimizing delivery fleets and increasing customer engagement, proved prescient. Sometimes, a seemingly counter-intuitive acquisition can unlock massive value if the strategic fit is strong.
  2. Operational Excellence is the Unsung Hero of "Quick": The success of 10-minute delivery wasn't about reckless speed, but meticulous system design. By focusing on store density, efficient in-store packing, and optimized short-distance routes, Blinkit demonstrated that speed could be achieved sustainably without compromising safety or rider welfare. This highlights that "quick" in quick commerce is more about process efficiency than sheer velocity.
  3. Adaptability in Business Model is Crucial: Blinkit's transition from a marketplace to a 1P inventory-led model was a pivotal operational shift. This move allowed greater control over inventory, better margins, and ultimately, a more robust business. Businesses in dynamic sectors must be willing to fundamentally re-engineer their models to find a sustainable path to profitability.
  4. Profitability Trumps Growth in the Long Run (Especially in "Funding Winter"): In 2022, the quick commerce sector was burning cash indiscriminately. Zomato's acquisition of Blinkit occurred amidst a "funding winter," where investors increasingly scrutinized profitability. Blinkit's subsequent focus on achieving adjusted EBITDA positivity demonstrated that even high-growth, capital-intensive businesses can find their path to financial discipline, earning investor confidence in the process.
