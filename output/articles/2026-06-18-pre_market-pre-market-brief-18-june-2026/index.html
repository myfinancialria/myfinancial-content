Pre-Market Brief — 18 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets largely saw a negative close, with US indices down over 1% and Asian markets mixed.
  • Crude oil prices experienced a significant drop, while gold and silver also traded lower.
  • The Indian Rupee depreciated against the US Dollar, influenced by a hawkish US Federal Reserve stance.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were marginal net buyers yesterday, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their strong buying trend.
  • The NSE's long-awaited IPO filing is a key development, potentially boosting market capitalisation and impacting related stocks like New India Assurance.

Where global markets closed

Global equity markets presented a mixed picture, with major US indices closing lower, reflecting investor caution. The Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all registered declines of over 1%. European markets also ended in the red, while Asian markets showed divergence, with Japan's Nikkei 225 posting gains and Hong Kong's Hang Seng falling sharply.

The overall global tone appears cautious to negative, primarily driven by the US market performance and the Federal Reserve's hawkish signals.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) depreciated by 0.51% to 94.40 against the US Dollar. This weakening comes amid a stronger Dollar Index, which rose by 0.21% to 100.30, and a hawkish stance from the US Federal Reserve. A weaker rupee can make imports more expensive and potentially impact inflation.

In the commodities segment, crude oil prices saw a significant decline. Brent Crude fell by 2.31% to $77.71, and WTI Crude dropped by 3.54% to $74.07. Lower crude prices are generally positive for India, a major oil importer, as it can ease inflationary pressures and reduce the import bill. Gold and Silver also traded lower, with Gold down 0.77% to $4325.30 and Silver down 2.49% to $68.93.

The US 10-Year Yield increased by 0.54% to 4.49%. A rising US yield can make dollar-denominated assets more attractive, potentially leading to capital outflows from emerging markets like India and putting pressure on Indian bond yields.

Overnight news that matters

US Fed's hawkish tone shuts door on India bond rally for now The US Federal Reserve's recent hawkish stance, signaling potential rate hikes due to persistent inflation risks, has influenced global bond markets. This shift has led to a slight dip in Indian government bonds, as the prospect of higher US rates can impact capital flows and investor sentiment towards emerging market debt.

NSE IPO Effect: New India Assurance shares rally 14% on plans to offload over 1 crore shares in blockbuster offer The National Stock Exchange (NSE) has filed its Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) for its long-awaited Initial Public Offering (IPO), a significant milestone for Indian capital markets. This development has positively impacted New India Assurance shares, which surged over 14% as the government plans to offload over 1 crore shares in the blockbuster offer. The NSE IPO, potentially India's largest, could attract foreign capital and boost overall market capitalisation by nearly 1%.

India-UK FTA levels the playing field for apparel exporters, says Pearl Global's Pallab Banerjee The new UK Free Trade Agreement is expected to significantly boost India's apparel exports. This agreement removes a long-standing 10-12% tariff disadvantage that Indian exporters faced against competitors like Bangladesh and Cambodia, creating a more level playing field for the sector. This could lead to increased orders and revenue for Indian apparel manufacturers.

JAL shares delist from BSE and NSE today: Will its 6.5 lakh shareholders get anything? Jaiprakash Associates (JAL) shares were delisted from the BSE and NSE today, marking the conclusion of one of India's longest-running insolvency cases. This event is significant for its 6.5 lakh shareholders, as the delisting follows the acquisition of the debt-laden company.

Vedanta Iron and Steel shares hit 5% upper circuit for fourth straight day, surge 20% since listing Vedanta Iron & Steel shares have continued their strong performance, hitting the 5% upper circuit for the fourth consecutive session. The stock has surged over 20% since its listing, driven by robust investor interest and strategic investments. This indicates strong post-demerger sentiment for this specific Vedanta entity.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Global Cues: Monitor the performance of US and European markets, especially given the hawkish stance from the US Federal Reserve, which could influence investor sentiment in India.
  2. Crude Oil Prices: The significant drop in Brent and WTI crude oil prices yesterday will be crucial to watch for sustained trends, as lower crude is beneficial for India.
  3. FII/DII Flows: Continue to observe institutional investment patterns. While FIIs were marginal buyers yesterday, DIIs have been consistently supportive, and their sustained activity will be key.
  4. NSE IPO Developments: The market will closely watch for further details and market reactions related to the NSE's IPO filing, and its potential impact on related stocks like New India Assurance.
  5. Specific Stock Movements: Keep an eye on stocks that have been in the news, such as Yes Bank (due to its recent rally), Vedanta Iron & Steel (for continued post-listing momentum), and Vedanta Power (after falling below its listing price).

Bottom line

Indian markets are likely to open with a cautious bias today, influenced by the negative global cues from US markets and the hawkish tone from the US Federal Reserve. While lower crude oil prices offer some relief, the depreciation of the rupee and rising US bond yields could temper enthusiasm. Domestic institutional buying continues to provide a strong floor, but investors should remain vigilant regarding global developments and their impact on capital flows.
