Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding options contracts that have not yet been closed or exercised. When we look at the highest Call OI strike, it indicates the price level where the most Call option contracts are currently open. This typically acts as a significant resistance level, as many Call writers (who profit if the price stays below their strike) expect the underlying index not to rise above this point. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike represents the price level where the most Put option contracts are open, often serving as a strong support level, as Put writers (who profit if the price stays above their strike) anticipate the index will not fall below it.

Today's fresh writing activity gives us insights into where new sellers are positioning themselves. For Nifty 50, the heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near 25,566, suggesting that new Call sellers anticipate the upside to be capped around this level. Simultaneously, significant fresh Put writing was seen near 25,066, indicating that Put sellers expect this level to act as a floor.

In Sensex, the data highlighted heavy fresh Put writing near 80,333, implying that Put sellers are establishing a strong support base at this strike.

For Bank Nifty, we saw substantial fresh Call writing near 62,000, suggesting a ceiling is being formed. Interestingly, there was also heavy fresh Put writing near 60,500, which is very close to the current spot price, indicating a strong battleground around this level. The aggregate flow for Bank Nifty showed a net Put OI build of 2.0 million contracts against a Call OI build of 0.8 million contracts today, suggesting a relatively stronger conviction from Put writers.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to that of Call options. A PCR value above 1 generally suggests that there are more Put options open than Call options, which can be interpreted as a bullish signal by some, as Put writers expect prices to rise. Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more Call options open, often seen as a bearish signal as Call writers anticipate prices to fall.

For Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.65, indicating that Call options dominate the overall open interest, which typically suggests a bearish undertone or an expectation of capped upside. Today's flow, measured by the change in Open Interest (ΔPCR), is 0.5, reinforcing the call-heavy fresh writing and suggesting a continuation of this sentiment for the immediate future.

Sensex also shows a PCR(OI) of 0.62, similar to Nifty, with Call options dominating. However, today's flow ΔPCR is 0.71, which is closer to neutral, suggesting a more balanced fresh writing activity for the day compared to the overall positioning.

Bank Nifty has a PCR(OI) of 0.78, which is closer to the neutral zone compared to the other two indices, indicating a more balanced book between Put and Call open interest. Notably, today's flow ΔPCR is significantly higher at 2.51, suggesting that fresh writing for the day was heavily skewed towards Put options, often interpreted as a bullish signal from new Put writers.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would experience the least amount of financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that particular price. It's often considered a "magnet" for the index price as expiry approaches, as option writers, who collectively hold a substantial position, may try to steer the market towards this level to maximise their profits or minimise losses.

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain strike is at 25,500, which is approximately 1.0% above its current spot price of 25,255.05. This suggests a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry nears.

Sensex has its Max Pain at 82,800, about 1.1% above its spot price of 81,879.40. This also indicates a potential upward bias or a magnet effect towards higher levels.

In contrast, Bank Nifty's Max Pain is at 60,500, which is approximately 0.5% below its current spot price of 60,792.40. This suggests a potential downward pull for Bank Nifty towards the Max Pain level. The closer the expiry, the stronger this "magnet effect" tends to be.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning is closely watched. Today, the FII Index futures long ratio stands at 31.0%. This means that out of their total open index futures contracts, 31.0% are long positions (expecting prices to rise) and the remaining 69.0% are short positions (expecting prices to fall). With 57,226 long contracts versus 127,639 short contracts, FIIs exhibit a net short position in index futures. Historically, a lower long ratio in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or slightly bearish sentiment from these large institutional players.

In stock futures, FIIs show a long ratio of 58.5%, with 3,575,532 long contracts against 2,540,154 short contracts, indicating a net long position. This suggests a more optimistic view on individual stocks compared to the broader indices.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these daily F&O movements can empower you to interpret market sentiment. To read this data yourself, you would typically start by visiting the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, specifically the "Derivatives" section. Here, you can find the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex (BSE also provides Sensex options data).

From the option chain, you can identify the highest Call OI and Put OI strikes, which indicate major resistance and support levels. You can also calculate the PCR(OI) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI. To gauge today's fresh flow (ΔPCR), you would compare today's OI data with yesterday's to see where new contracts were added or closed. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in an easily digestible format. By tracking Max Pain, FII positioning (available in the participant-wise Open Interest CSV on the NSE archives), and significant stock movements, you can build a comprehensive picture of market sentiment and potential price behaviour for the upcoming session. Remember, this is about understanding the market's pulse, not predicting exact movements.
