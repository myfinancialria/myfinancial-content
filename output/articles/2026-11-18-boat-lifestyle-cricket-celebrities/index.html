How boAt Built a ₹4,000 Cr Audio Brand Using Cricketers Instead of TV Ads

In 2016, Aman Gupta and Sameer Mehta embarked on a journey that, to many seasoned veterans of India's consumer electronics market, seemed destined for failure. They launched boAt, an audio accessories brand, with a paltry ₹30 lakh of their own money, split equally between them. Their initial product wasn't even a pair of headphones, but a durable charging cable for Apple devices. This was not a splashy, venture-backed debut. This was two men in their thirties, betting everything on a hunch: that young Indians wanted stylish, affordable, and robust audio gear, and that they could reach them without spending a fortune on traditional advertising. Their ambition was to build a brand for people who couldn't, or wouldn't, pay international prices for premium audio.

The World Before

Before boAt, the Indian audio market was a tale of two extremes. At one end, global behemoths like Sony, JBL, and Bose dominated, offering high-quality products but at price points often inaccessible to the average Indian consumer. Their brand narratives were often rooted in technical specifications and sonic fidelity, appealing to audiophiles but alienating a broader, budget-conscious audience. These international players had established distribution networks and decades of brand trust, making them formidable incumbents.

At the other end were numerous unorganized players offering cheap, often substandard audio accessories. These products might have been affordable, but they typically lacked in durability, design, and consistent quality. There was a significant gap: a gaping chasm for aesthetically pleasing, reliable audio products that wouldn't break the bank. India's consumer electronics market was growing, driven by a rising middle class and increasing disposable incomes, yet the audio segment remained underserved for a large segment of the population, particularly the youth. Smartphone adoption was soaring, and with it, the demand for accompanying accessories like earphones and headphones, but the options were either too expensive or too flimsy. Most new entrants relied on the standard playbook of heavy advertising across TV and print to establish credibility – a costly affair that required deep pockets and long runways. This made boAt's digital-first, low-budget approach seem audacious, if not outright foolish, in a market where trust was often built through expensive, traditional media campaigns.

The Crack

Aman Gupta, who had previously worked as Sales Head at JBL, had a keen understanding of the audio industry and the significant market gap. He observed that young Indians wanted products that were fashionable, durable, and, crucially, affordable. Rather than competing on pure technological superiority like the global giants, boAt focused on understanding and catering to these specific consumer preferences.

Their initial product, the durable Apple charging cable, was a quiet success, proving that consumers were willing to pay for quality and durability at a reasonable price. This gave them the confidence to pivot into audio. But their real "crack" in the market came from a radically different marketing strategy: skipping traditional, expensive TV advertisements in favor of micro-priced celebrity endorsements and digital-first campaigns.

boAt strategically tapped into India's two biggest cultural obsessions: cricket and Bollywood. Instead of aiming for a single, prohibitively expensive A-list ambassador, they built a roster of popular cricketers like Hardik Pandya, KL Rahul, Shikhar Dhawan, and Rishabh Pant, and Bollywood stars such as Jacqueline Fernandez, Kiara Advani, and Kartik Aaryan. These were not just passive endorsements; the celebrities actively integrated boAt products into their social media presence, match-day appearances, and music videos. This strategy helped position boAt products not merely as electronics, but as lifestyle accessories, an extension of personal style and passion.

Aman Gupta famously stated that 90% of their marketing budget went to digital channels. They leveraged platforms like Instagram, YouTube, and e-commerce marketplaces to connect directly with their target audience – millennials and Gen Z. By partnering with IPL cricket teams, boAt gained massive visibility among millions of cricket fans, effectively making their ads visible to potential buyers without the exorbitant costs of prime-time TV slots. The brand cultivated a community of "boAtheads" – customers who didn't just buy products but identified with the brand's youthful, energetic vibe.

This digital-first, influencer-led approach was far more cost-effective than traditional advertising. For example, roping in Hardik Pandya in 2018 significantly boosted brand awareness and added an aspirational value, leading to substantial revenue generation. The average ticket size for boAt products also grew, indicating a greater willingness among consumers to invest in the brand. By focusing on affordability (products often priced between ₹350 and ₹2,000 for earphones and speakers), quality, and stylish designs, boAt forced established brands like JBL to re-evaluate their pricing strategies in India.

The numbers quickly reflected the efficacy of this strategy. From revenues of ₹27 crore in FY2017, boAt saw a four-fold increase to ₹108 crore in FY2018. The brand claimed to sell over 6,000 units per day, with four units sold every minute, by 2018.

What Happened Next

The shrewd marketing and keen understanding of the Indian consumer propelled boAt into a meteoric rise. By 2020, boAt had become India's #1 wearable brand by volume. In Q3 2020, they captured an 18.3% market share in the True Wireless (TWS) earphones category, surpassing global players like Xiaomi, Realme, JBL, and Apple.

The company continued its aggressive growth trajectory. In FY2020, boAt's revenue stood at $99 million (approximately ₹700 crore), with a profit of $6.92 million (approximately ₹49 crore). By FY2022-23, the parent company, Imagine Marketing, reported a staggering revenue of ₹4,000 crore from net sales, a two-fold increase from ₹2,000 crore in FY21-22. This made boAt one of India's largest digital-first consumer technology companies. By 2023, boAt was recognized as the world's 5th largest wearable brand and India's #1, maintaining a dominant position in the overall Indian market with a 29.7% share. Their market share in the Indian wearables and hearables segment by shipments stood at approximately 27%.

boAt has diversified its product portfolio beyond audio to include smartwatches, power banks, and grooming products, expanding into high-growth adjacencies. The company initially planned a ₹2,000 crore IPO in 2022, which was later withdrawn due to unfavorable market conditions. However, they refiled a confidential draft red herring prospectus (DRHP) with SEBI in 2025, aiming to raise ₹2,000 crore, later revising it to ₹1,500 crore. As of November 2025, boAt commanded a 26% value share and 34% volume share in fiscal 2025 in India's branded personal audio market. Their current annual revenue is more than ₹1,000 crore as on March 31, 2026. While boAt's revenue peaked in FY2023 at $400 million, it saw a slight decline in FY2024 to $375 million, with a net loss of $9.5 million, down from $15.5 million in FY2023. However, the company returned to profit in FY25 with ₹61 crore, even as revenue continued to see marginal declines.

Lessons to Take Away

  1. Understand the "Why" Behind Consumer Choices, Not Just the "What": boAt didn't just sell earphones; it sold a lifestyle. While competitors focused on technical specifications, boAt understood that for young Indians, audio accessories were also fashion statements and extensions of their identity. This deep insight into cultural nuances allowed them to create products and marketing that resonated emotionally, rather than just functionally.
  1. Strategic Brand Association Trumps Generic Reach: Instead of broad, expensive mainstream advertising, boAt selectively partnered with cricketers and Bollywood stars, aligning their brand with India's most popular cultural touchstones. This micro-targeted, celebrity-driven approach created aspirational value and achieved high brand awareness digitally and through word-of-mouth, especially in Tier-II cities, far more efficiently than traditional TV ads.
  1. Digital-First is a Distribution and Marketing Advantage: By prioritizing online marketplaces and social media, boAt bypassed the massive costs and complexities of building extensive offline distribution and traditional advertising networks from scratch. This digital-first model allowed for direct engagement with their target demographic, rapid product iteration based on feedback, and cost-effective scaling.
  1. Community Building as a Moat: boAt fostered a sense of belonging by referring to its customers as "boAtheads". This community-driven approach encouraged user-generated content and organic advocacy, turning customers into brand evangelists. In a competitive market, a loyal community can be a powerful, sustainable advantage that goes beyond product features or price points.
