Decoding today's open interest

Understanding the distribution of Open Interest (OI) in the options chain can provide valuable insights into market participants' expectations. The highest Call OI strike represents the strike price where the maximum number of Call options are currently open. Option writers (sellers) typically sell Calls when they believe the index is unlikely to cross that level, effectively setting an overhead resistance. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike is the strike price with the maximum open Put options. Put writers sell Puts when they anticipate the index will not fall below that level, thereby establishing a potential support base.

Looking at today's fresh writing activity:

  • Nifty 50: Heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near 23,800, indicating that new Call sellers are positioning for the index to remain below this mark. On the Put side, significant fresh writing occurred near 23,033, suggesting new Put sellers are confident the index will find support around this level.
  • Sensex: Today's heaviest fresh Call writing was concentrated near 75,000, reinforcing the idea of a ceiling at this level. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was notable around 74,800, indicating new support expectations.
  • Bank Nifty: Fresh Call writing was most active near 57,133, suggesting a potential cap. On the Put side, new Put writing was heaviest near 55,633, establishing a fresh floor.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that compares the total Open Interest (OI) of Puts to the total OI of Calls. A PCR above 1 generally suggests more Put writing (a bullish sentiment from writers expecting prices to rise), while a PCR below 1 indicates more Call writing (a bearish sentiment from writers expecting prices to fall).

  • Nifty 50: The overall PCR(OI) stands at 0.69, which is below 1, suggesting that Call writers currently dominate the Open Interest, implying a potential cap on upside movement. However, today's fresh flow PCR(ΔOI) is 0.81, indicating a more balanced flow of new Call and Put writing for the day, suggesting mixed sentiment from recent activity.
  • Sensex: With an overall PCR(OI) of 1.08, the Sensex has a balanced book of Call and Put Open Interest. More notably, today's fresh flow PCR(ΔOI) is a high 5.6, indicating a significant dominance of new Put writing over Call writing today. This points to a strong bullish sentiment from option writers in the current session, expecting the index to hold or move higher.
  • Bank Nifty: The Bank Nifty's overall PCR(OI) is 0.87, which is below 1 but closer to neutrality than Nifty, suggesting a relatively balanced book with a slight lean towards Call writing. Today's fresh flow PCR(ΔOI) is -0.67, which implies that more Calls were written than Puts in today's session, indicating a mixed to slightly cautious sentiment from daily activity.

Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the index were to expire at that level. It often acts as a magnetic point, especially as expiry approaches, as option writers tend to adjust their positions to minimise losses.

  • Nifty 50: The Max Pain for Nifty 50 is at 23,800, which is about 1.4% above its current spot of 23,477.80. This suggests a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry nears.
  • Sensex: For Sensex, the Max Pain is precisely at its current spot of 74,900. When Max Pain aligns with the spot price, it often indicates that the market expects the index to hover around this level towards expiry.
  • Bank Nifty: Bank Nifty's Max Pain is at 57,500, a significant 1.8% above its spot of 56,471.95. This substantial distance suggests a strong upward magnetic pull, potentially drawing the index higher as expiry approaches.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding the market's pulse from derivatives data can be a valuable skill. You can access this information daily from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the 'Derivatives' section, specifically looking at 'Option Chain' and 'Participant-wise Open Interest' data. Many brokerage platforms also provide user-friendly interfaces to view option chain data in real-time. Key data points to look for include the Open Interest (OI) for Calls and Puts at various strike prices, the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) based on total OI, and the change in OI for the day.

To interpret this data, start by identifying the strikes with the highest Call and Put OI, as these often act as immediate resistance and support levels. Then, observe where fresh Call and Put writing is concentrated relative to the current spot price, as this reveals where new positions are being built. Calculate or note the PCR for both total OI and daily change in OI to gauge the overall sentiment of option writers. Finally, look at the Max Pain level and its distance from the spot price, as this can indicate a potential magnetic pull. Combining these different perspectives helps paint a more comprehensive picture of market participants' expectations, rather than relying on any single indicator.
