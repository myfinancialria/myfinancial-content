Planning Ahead: Understanding Bank Holidays from June 25 to 28

TL;DR

  • Indian banks will observe regional holidays and weekends, leading to closures for up to four days between June 25 and June 28, 2026.
  • These closures are state-specific; it is crucial to consult the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) holiday calendar for your particular region.
  • While physical bank branches will be shut, most digital banking services like UPI, IMPS, and online net banking will remain operational.
  • Cheque clearing, NEFT, and RTGS transactions might experience delays if processed during the holiday period, affecting settlement times.
  • Planning your financial transactions, especially large transfers or bill payments, well in advance is advisable to avoid inconvenience.

What this means in plain terms

Next week, many banks across India, including major players like SBI and HDFC Bank, will be closed for up to four days between June 25 and June 28, 2026. This extended closure is due to a combination of regional public holidays, such as Muharram, and regular weekend offs. It's important to understand that these aren't nationwide closures; rather, they vary from state to state depending on local observances.

For bank customers, this primarily means that physical branch services—like cash deposits, withdrawals over the counter, or cheque submissions—will be unavailable on these specific days. While this might seem inconvenient, the good news is that most digital banking channels will continue to function smoothly. However, certain transactions like cheque clearing or large-value transfers might experience delays in processing and settlement, as these often depend on bank working hours. Therefore, proactive planning of your financial activities is key to navigating these holidays without any hitches.

Understanding the RBI Holiday Calendar

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) categorises bank holidays under several heads, ensuring a structured approach to closures across the nation. These categories help both banks and customers understand the nature and scope of each holiday.

Types of Bank Holidays

Bank holidays in India are broadly classified into three main types as per the RBI guidelines:

  1. Holidays under the Negotiable Instruments Act: These are usually public holidays declared by the Central or State Governments, such as Republic Day, Independence Day, Gandhi Jayanti, and major festivals like Diwali, Eid, or Christmas. Banks are closed for all public transactions on these days.
  2. Holidays under the Negotiable Instruments Act and Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) Holiday: On these days, while physical bank branches are closed, RTGS services might also be unavailable. However, other digital payment systems often continue to operate.
  3. Bank's Closing of Accounts: This is an annual holiday (typically April 1st) when banks close for their financial year-end accounting, and public transactions are usually suspended.

The upcoming closures between June 25 and June 28, 2026, largely fall under the "Negotiable Instruments Act" category, combined with weekend offs. Muharram, a significant religious observance, is a state-specific holiday, meaning its impact will be felt differently across various regions. For instance, a bank might be closed in Hyderabad for Muharram but open in Mumbai on the same day. It is always advisable to refer to the official RBI website's holiday calendar for the most accurate and up-to-date information for your specific state.

Digital Banking: Operational Status During Holidays

In an increasingly digital economy, the impact of bank holidays on financial transactions has significantly reduced, thanks to various electronic payment systems. Most of these services operate 24x7, irrespective of physical branch closures.

Uninterrupted Digital Services

Several key digital banking services continue to function seamlessly even during bank holidays:

  • Unified Payments Interface (UPI): This instant real-time payment system is available round the clock, every day of the year. Whether you're paying a vendor or transferring money to a friend, UPI transactions will go through instantly.
  • Immediate Payment Service (IMPS): Similar to UPI, IMPS facilitates instant interbank electronic fund transfers 24x7. This is particularly useful for urgent transfers during holidays.
  • Net Banking and Mobile Banking: Most online banking services, including checking account balances, viewing statements, paying bills, and initiating fund transfers (via UPI/IMPS), remain fully functional.
  • ATMs: Automated Teller Machines (ATMs) will be operational for cash withdrawals and other services. However, it's prudent to plan cash needs in advance, especially if there's a prolonged holiday period, as ATMs might run out of cash in certain high-demand areas.

Services with Potential Delays

While instant payment systems are unaffected, some services might experience delays in settlement or processing:

  • National Electronic Funds Transfer (NEFT): While NEFT operates 24x7, 365 days a year since December 2019, the final settlement and crediting to the beneficiary account might be affected if the receiving bank's internal systems or specific processes are tied to working hours. For practical purposes, while you can initiate NEFT, the actual reflection in the beneficiary account might be delayed until the next working day if the holiday falls on a settlement cycle.
  • Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS): RTGS, primarily used for large-value transactions (₹2 lakh and above), also operates 24x7. However, similar to NEFT, the final processing and crediting could see delays if the receiving bank's operational staff are unavailable to complete the final steps during a holiday.
  • Cheque Clearing: This is perhaps the most affected service. Cheques deposited during a holiday period will only be processed on the next working day, leading to delays in funds being credited to your account.
  • Fixed Deposit/Recurring Deposit Maturity: If an FD or RD matures on a bank holiday, the proceeds will typically be credited on the next working day.

Planning Your Finances Around Bank Holidays

Being proactive is the best way to manage your financial commitments and avoid last-minute stress during extended bank holidays. A little planning can ensure smooth operations, even when branches are closed.

Key Dates and Regional Variations

Between June 25 and June 28, 2026, several states will observe bank holidays. The primary reason for specific closures during this period is Muharram, which falls on a weekday. This, combined with the subsequent weekend, results in an extended break for banks in affected regions. For example, if Muharram is observed on Thursday, June 25, and then Friday, June 26, is also a holiday in some states, followed by Saturday (second or fourth, which are typically bank holidays) and Sunday, this creates a four-day closure. It is crucial to check the RBI's official holiday list for June 2026 specifically for your state to confirm exact dates.

Practical Steps for Financial Management

  • Prioritise Urgent Transactions: Identify any critical payments or transfers that need to be completed before the holidays begin. This could include EMIs, credit card bills, rent payments, or urgent business transactions.
  • Leverage Digital Channels: Familiarise yourself with and utilise UPI, IMPS, net banking, and mobile banking apps for most of your routine transactions. These are your best friends during bank holidays.
  • Manage Cash Needs: If you anticipate needing a significant amount of cash, withdraw it a day or two before the holidays. While ATMs are open, heavy usage might lead to temporary cash shortages in some locations.
  • Schedule Cheque Deposits/Clearances: If you are expecting a cheque payment or need to deposit one, do so well in advance. Account for the delayed clearing time. Similarly, if you've issued a post-dated cheque that falls on a holiday, ensure sufficient funds are available on the next working day.
  • Verify Bill Payment Due Dates: Double-check the due dates for utility bills, loan EMIs, and credit card payments. Many service providers offer online payment options, but if you rely on direct debit or physical payments, ensure they are scheduled to clear before the holiday period.
  • Communicate with Business Partners: If your business involves frequent bank transactions, inform your clients or partners about potential delays during the holiday period to manage expectations.

A real example

Let's consider Rahul, 38, earning ₹28 lakhs CTC, based in Bengaluru. He needs to make a few financial transactions next week.

Rahul has the following tasks:

  1. Pay his monthly house rent of ₹45,000. The landlord prefers a bank transfer via NEFT, and the due date is June 26.
  2. Transfer ₹1.5 lakhs to his sister in Chennai for her medical expenses. This is urgent.
  3. Deposit a cheque of ₹75,000 he received from a client for freelance work.
  4. Pay his credit card bill of ₹32,000 due on June 27.

Checking the RBI holiday calendar for Karnataka, Rahul finds that banks in Bengaluru will be closed on June 25 for Muharram, and then on June 28 (Sunday). June 26 and 27 are working days.

Here's how Rahul plans:

  • House Rent (₹45,000): Since the due date is June 26 and June 25 is a holiday, Rahul decides to initiate the NEFT transfer on June 24. This ensures the funds are credited to his landlord's account well before the due date, avoiding any last-minute stress. Even though NEFT operates 24x7, doing it on a working day provides an extra buffer.
  • Transfer to Sister (₹1.5 lakhs): This is urgent. Rahul immediately uses IMPS via his mobile banking app on June 24. IMPS is instant and works 24x7, so the funds are credited to his sister's account within minutes, regardless of the upcoming holiday.
  • Client Cheque (₹75,000): Rahul received the cheque on June 24. Knowing June 25 is a holiday, he decides to deposit the cheque at an ATM or a drop box on June 24. He understands that the cheque will only be sent for clearing on June 26 (the next working day) and the funds will likely be credited to his account by June 27 or 28, depending on the clearing cycle. He adjusts his immediate spending plans accordingly.
  • Credit Card Bill (₹32,000): With a due date of June 27, Rahul opts to pay his credit card bill using net banking's bill pay feature on June 26. This ensures the payment is processed on a working day, and his account is updated promptly, avoiding late payment charges.

By planning ahead and leveraging digital banking channels, Rahul successfully manages all his financial tasks despite the upcoming bank holidays.

What to do this week

To ensure your finances remain uninterrupted during the upcoming bank holidays, here are 3-5 concrete actions you can take this week, each taking about 30 minutes:

  1. Check Your State's Holiday Calendar: Visit the official RBI website (rbi.org.in) and navigate to their holiday calendar section. Specifically look for the list of bank holidays for your state for June 2026. Note down the exact dates your local banks will be closed. This will clarify which days you need to plan around.
  2. Review Upcoming Payments and Due Dates: Go through your calendar or financial planner for the next two weeks. Identify any loan EMIs, credit card bills, utility payments, or rent due dates that fall between June 25 and June 28. If any critical payment is due on or around these dates, plan to complete it by June 24.
  3. Ensure Digital Banking Readiness: Confirm you have activated and know how to use your bank's net banking, mobile banking app, UPI, and IMPS services. If you haven't used them recently, do a small test transaction (e.g., ₹10 transfer to a friend) to ensure everything is working smoothly.
  4. Assess Cash Requirements: Briefly think about any cash needs you might have for the extended weekend. If you anticipate needing cash for small expenses or emergencies, make a withdrawal from an ATM by June 24 to avoid potential queues or temporary cash shortages during the holidays.
  5. Initiate Pending Cheque Transactions: If you have any cheques to deposit or are expecting a cheque payment, try to complete the deposit or follow up on the payment by June 24. This will allow for processing on the earliest possible working day.

FAQ

How do I know if my bank is closed in my city?

You should check the official holiday calendar published by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on its website (rbi.org.in). These calendars specify state-wise bank holidays under the Negotiable Instruments Act.

Will online transactions like UPI and IMPS work during bank holidays?

Yes, digital payment systems such as UPI (Unified Payments Interface) and IMPS (Immediate Payment Service) operate 24x7, 365 days a year, including bank holidays. You can use them for instant fund transfers.

What about NEFT and RTGS during bank holidays?

NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer) and RTGS (Real-Time Gross Settlement) also operate 24x7. While you can initiate these transactions, the final crediting to the beneficiary account might experience slight delays if the receiving bank's internal processing is tied to working hours.

Can I withdraw cash from ATMs during bank holidays?

Yes, ATMs will be operational for cash withdrawals and other services during bank holidays. However, it's advisable to plan your cash needs in advance, as some ATMs might temporarily run out of cash due to higher demand during extended closures.

What happens if my cheque clearing date falls on a bank holiday?

If your cheque clearing date falls on a bank holiday, the cheque will typically be processed on the next immediate working day. This means the funds will be credited to your account with a corresponding delay.

Will my EMI or bill payments be affected if due on a bank holiday?

If you have set up automatic debits (NACH mandates) for EMIs or bill payments, they might be processed on the next working day. However, it's always best to ensure sufficient funds are available in your account a day before the holiday to avoid any missed payments or charges.

Where can I find the official RBI holiday calendar?

The official RBI holiday calendar is available on the Reserve Bank of India's website, rbi.org.in, usually under the "Notifications" or "Press Releases" section, or by searching for "Bank Holidays."
