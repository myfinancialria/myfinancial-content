India F&O Pulse — 04 Aug 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Indian Indices See Mixed-to-Bearish Signals from Options Data as NIFTY 50 Stays Range-bound, While SENSEX and BANK NIFTY Lean Mildly Bearish.

TL;DR

  • NIFTY 50: Options data suggests the NIFTY 50 is likely to remain range-bound, with strong resistance and support levels very close to today's closing.
  • SENSEX: The SENSEX option chain indicates a mildly bearish sentiment, with significant Call writing activity and Max Pain positioned above the current spot.
  • BANK NIFTY: BANK NIFTY also shows a mildly bearish undertone, driven by fresh Call writing and its key resistance level aligning closely with its Max Pain point.

What the indices did today

The NIFTY 50 closed the day at 24,614.90, down 0.64%. The index found itself trading within a tight range, with the heaviest Call Open Interest (OI) at 24,650 acting as a ceiling and the strongest Put OI at 24,600 providing a floor, indicating a potential consolidation phase.

The SENSEX ended its session at 78,428.95, a decline of 0.27%. While the index saw some downside, its broader option-chain implied range of 77,000 to 80,000 suggests room for movement, although today's fresh writing activity leaned towards caution.

Meanwhile, the BANK NIFTY settled at 57,907.20, down 0.58%. The index appears to be navigating a very narrow implied range, with both its heaviest Call and Put OI concentrated around the 58,000 mark, suggesting a potential pivot point for upcoming sessions.

Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. In options, the strike price with the highest Call OI typically acts as a significant resistance level, meaning many participants believe the index may struggle to move above this point. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI often serves as a strong support level, suggesting a floor below which the index may find it difficult to fall.

For the NIFTY 50, the heaviest Call OI is observed at 24,650, which is just 0.1% above today's spot close, indicating a very close overhead resistance. The heaviest Put OI is at 24,600, a mere 0.1% below spot, suggesting immediate support. Today's fresh Call writing, where new Call options were sold, was concentrated near 24,533. This suggests that Call sellers (writers) anticipate the NIFTY 50 might struggle to move significantly higher from this level. On the other hand, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 24,566, with Put sellers expecting the index to find a floor around this point.

The SENSEX saw its option-chain implied range between 77,000 and 80,000. Today's heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near 79,666. This indicates that Call writers are positioning themselves for the SENSEX to potentially face a cap around these higher levels, suggesting a cautious outlook on significant upside.

For the BANK NIFTY, the heaviest Call OI is at 58,000, just 0.2% above spot, acting as a clear resistance. The heaviest Put OI is also at 58,000, 0.2% below spot, indicating a strong support. Today's fresh Call writing was most prominent at 58,000, reinforcing this level as a potential ceiling. Conversely, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 57,300, suggesting some participants expect a floor to hold around that lower level. The aggregate flow for BANK NIFTY showed a net Call OI build of 0.8 million contracts, significantly higher than the 0.1 million net Put OI build, further highlighting a leaning towards resistance building.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value below 1 generally suggests that more Call options have been written than Put options, which can be interpreted as a bearish sentiment among option writers who expect the market to either fall or remain range-bound with limited upside. Conversely, a PCR above 1 suggests more Put writing, often seen as a bullish signal.

For the NIFTY 50, the PCR(OI) stands at 0.93, suggesting a relatively balanced book with a slight tilt towards Call writing. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) was -38.96, which is an unusually large negative number, indicating a significant reduction in Put OI relative to Call OI, or a very strong bias towards Call writing today, contributing to a mixed or balanced flow for the day.

The SENSEX recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.71, indicating a clear dominance of Call writing over Put writing in the overall open interest. Today's flow ΔPCR was 0.33, suggesting that fresh writing activity during the day was also tilted towards Call options, reinforcing a cautious or mildly bearish sentiment.

The BANK NIFTY has a PCR(OI) of 0.72, similar to SENSEX, pointing to a higher concentration of Call writing. The PCR based on today's flow ΔPCR was 0.15, which implies that fresh writing activity today was predominantly in Call options, further contributing to a mildly bearish outlook.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the index were to expire at that specific level. It's often considered a magnetic point, as the index tends to gravitate towards this level, especially as the expiry date approaches, because option writers typically have a vested interest in the index closing near this point to minimise their losses.

For the NIFTY 50, the Max Pain point is at 24,600, which is very close to its spot close of 24,614.90. This suggests that option writers are positioned for the NIFTY 50 to potentially expire around this level, reinforcing the idea of a tight range.

The SENSEX has its Max Pain at 78,900, which is approximately 0.6% above its current spot of 78,428.95. This positioning of Max Pain above the spot suggests a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry nears, which could be seen as a mildly bullish signal within an otherwise cautious setup.

For the BANK NIFTY, the Max Pain is at 58,000, aligning closely with its spot of 57,907.20. This indicates that option writers are expecting the index to settle around this key level, which also coincides with the heaviest Call and Put OI, making it a crucial pivot point.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment. Today, FIIs held a long ratio of 12.5% in Index futures, meaning that for every 100 contracts, only 12.5 were long positions, while the rest were short. This is a relatively low long ratio, with 25,701 long contracts against 179,474 short contracts. Historically, such a low FII Index futures long ratio has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from these large participants on the broader index movements.

In Stock futures, FIIs held a long ratio of 54.9%, with 3,492,641 long contracts versus 2,865,103 short contracts. This indicates a more balanced to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks, contrasting with their more bearish positioning in index futures.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

The close proximity of both Call and Put walls, coupled with Max Pain at spot, suggests that the index might continue to consolidate within a tight band. The very low FII Index futures long ratio further supports a cautious stance from institutional players on the broader market.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily movements better. To read this data yourself, you'll primarily need to look at the Open Interest (OI) data for options, which is readily available on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain." Many stockbrokers also provide this data through their trading platforms.

You would typically focus on the strike prices with the highest Call OI and Put OI to identify potential resistance and support levels. Observing where fresh Call and Put writing (change in OI) occurs can tell you where new positions are being built, indicating where market participants expect ceilings or floors. Calculating the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI gives you a quick sentiment check. Finally, monitoring FII derivative positioning, also available on the NSE website, can provide insight into how large institutional players are positioning themselves in index and stock futures. Combining these data points daily can help you form your own informed perspective on the market's probable short-term tendencies.
