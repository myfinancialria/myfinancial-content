Demystifying MDR: UPI vs. Debit and Credit Card Charges

TL;DR

  • Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) is a fee merchants pay to banks and payment processors for accepting digital payments.
  • The National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) has introduced MDR on certain Person-to-Merchant (P2M) UPI transactions above ₹2,000.
  • For these UPI transactions, a 0.4% MDR applies, capped at ₹300 for payments of ₹75,000 and above.
  • Debit card MDRs are capped at up to 0.90%, while credit card MDRs typically range from 1.5% to 2.5% per transaction.
  • Despite the new charges, UPI remains a significantly more affordable digital payment option for merchants compared to traditional cards.

What this means in plain terms

When you pay a shopkeeper using your debit card, credit card, or UPI, the shopkeeper (merchant) doesn't receive the full amount you paid. A small percentage, known as the Merchant Discount Rate (MDR), is deducted. This fee is paid by the merchant to the bank and other entities involved in processing that digital payment. It covers the costs of infrastructure, security, and operations.

Recently, the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI), which manages UPI, introduced MDR for certain UPI transactions. While person-to-person (P2P) UPI transfers and small merchant payments (up to ₹2,000) remain free, a charge now applies to larger payments made to commercial merchants. This move aims to ensure the long-term sustainability and continued development of the UPI ecosystem.

Understanding these charges is crucial for both businesses managing their costs and consumers who might wonder about the underlying economics of their preferred payment methods. This article breaks down how UPI MDR compares to the charges on traditional debit and credit cards, helping you make informed financial decisions.

Understanding Merchant Discount Rate (MDR)

The Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) is essentially the cost a business incurs for accepting digital payments. It's not a tax collected by the government or NPCI, but rather a fee distributed among the various participants in the payment ecosystem. This includes the acquiring bank (the merchant's bank), the issuing bank (your bank), and the card network (like Visa, Mastercard, or RuPay).

MDR covers several components:

  • Interchange Fee: Paid by the acquiring bank to the issuing bank.
  • Scheme Fee: Paid to the card network for using their infrastructure.
  • Acquirer Markup: The fee charged by the acquiring bank for its services.

The specific MDR percentage can vary significantly based on the payment instrument (credit card, debit card, UPI), the type of merchant, their annual turnover, and the transaction value. Merchants often negotiate these rates with their acquiring banks, especially for higher transaction volumes.

UPI Transaction Charges: The Current Landscape

For years, UPI operated under a zero-MDR mandate, largely supported by government subsidies. However, to ensure the long-term sustainability and growth of the digital payment infrastructure, NPCI has revised its framework.

Key UPI MDR Provisions (Effective October 15, 2026)

  • Person-to-Person (P2P) Transactions: All P2P UPI transfers remain completely free, irrespective of the amount.
  • Small Person-to-Merchant (P2M) Transactions: UPI Person-to-Merchant (P2M) transactions up to ₹2,000 continue to be free of MDR. This covers over 95% of the total volume of UPI P2M transactions.
  • Standard P2M Transactions (above ₹2,000): A baseline MDR of 0.4% will apply to P2M UPI transactions exceeding ₹2,000.
  • High-Value P2M Transactions (₹75,000 and above): For transactions of ₹75,000 or more, the 0.4% MDR will be capped at a maximum of ₹300 per transaction. This means even for a ₹1,00,000 transaction, the merchant will pay only ₹300, not ₹400 (0.4% of ₹1,00,000).
  • Exemptions for Micro-Merchants: Small merchants operating under the Person-to-Person-Merchant (P2PM) framework, receiving up to ₹1 lakh per month through UPI QR codes into their personal accounts, are fully exempt from MDR.
  • Special Category Rates: Certain categories like railways, telecom, insurance, fuel, utilities, and agricultural inputs will attract a flat MDR of ₹5 per transaction for amounts above ₹2,000. Capital market transactions (mutual funds, securities) will have a minimal 0.02% MDR, capped at ₹300.

It is important to note that consumers will not be charged any MDR for using UPI. The charge is strictly a merchant-side transaction cost and cannot be passed on to the customer.

Debit Card vs. Credit Card MDR: A Comparison

Traditional card payments have always involved MDR, with rates varying significantly between debit and credit cards.

Debit Card MDR

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) regulates debit card MDRs.

  • For merchants with an annual turnover up to ₹20 lakh, the MDR is capped at 0.4% for physical POS/online transactions and 0.3% for QR-code based transactions.
  • For merchants with an annual turnover above ₹20 lakh, the MDR can be up to 0.9% for physical POS/online transactions and 0.8% for QR-code based acceptance, subject to a maximum of ₹1,000 per transaction.
  • RuPay debit cards are generally exempt from MDR, providing a cost-effective option for merchants.

Credit Card MDR

Credit card MDRs are typically higher than debit card MDRs due to the inherent credit risk and associated rewards programs.

  • Standard credit card MDRs generally range from 1.5% to 2.5% per transaction. Some sources indicate a range of 1% to 3%.
  • The actual rate can depend on the merchant's industry, transaction volume, and their negotiation with the acquiring bank and card network.

Compared to these figures, the new UPI MDR of 0.4% (with a ₹300 cap) is significantly lower, reinforcing UPI's position as a more affordable digital payment acceptance tool for commercial enterprises.

Impact on Merchants and Consumers

The introduction of MDR on specific UPI transactions marks a shift in the digital payments landscape. For merchants, particularly those dealing with higher-value transactions, this means a new cost component to factor into their business operations. While the convenience of UPI is undeniable, businesses in sectors like electronics, jewellery, or travel, where ticket sizes are higher, might re-evaluate their payment mix.

However, the tiered structure and caps ensure that UPI remains competitive. The government and NPCI have also strictly mandated that merchants cannot pass these MDR charges on to customers, ensuring that consumers continue to enjoy free UPI payments. This prohibition protects consumers from any direct impact of the new MDR framework. The collected MDR is crucial for reinvesting in the UPI ecosystem, enhancing infrastructure resilience, innovation, cybersecurity, and customer service.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 42, who runs an electronics store in Pune with an annual turnover of ₹50 lakhs. She accepts payments via UPI, debit cards, and credit cards.

Suppose a customer makes a purchase of a television worth ₹60,000.

  1. Payment via UPI:

    2. * Since the transaction is above ₹2,000, the 0.4% MDR applies. * MDR = 0.4% of ₹60,000 = ₹240. * Priya pays ₹240 to her acquiring bank for this UPI transaction.

  1. Payment via Debit Card:

    2. * Given her turnover, the maximum debit card MDR could be up to 0.9%. * MDR = 0.9% of ₹60,000 = ₹540. * Priya would pay ₹540 for this debit card transaction.

  1. Payment via Credit Card:

    2. * Credit card MDRs typically range from 1.5% to 2.5%. Let's assume an average of 1.8% for Priya's store. * MDR = 1.8% of ₹60,000 = ₹1,080. * Priya would pay ₹1,080 for this credit card transaction.

Comparison: For this ₹60,000 transaction, Priya pays:

  • ₹240 using UPI
  • ₹540 using a debit card
  • ₹1,080 using a credit card

This example clearly illustrates that even with the new MDR, UPI remains significantly more cost-effective for Priya's business compared to accepting payments via debit or credit cards for higher-value transactions.

What to do this week

  1. Review your business's payment mix: If you are a merchant, analyse your average transaction values and the proportion of payments received via UPI, debit cards, and credit cards.
  2. Understand UPI MDR categories: Familiarise yourself with the specific MDR rates for different UPI transaction values and merchant categories (e.g., essential services, capital markets) to accurately project your costs.
  3. Check your payment gateway statements: If you're a merchant, scrutinise your settlement reports to understand the actual MDR being charged for various payment methods.
  4. Educate your staff: Ensure your employees, especially those handling customer payments, are aware that MDR is a merchant cost and cannot be passed on to customers for UPI transactions.
  5. Explore RuPay options: For merchants, consider promoting RuPay debit cards, which generally carry zero MDR, as a cost-effective alternative to other card networks.

FAQ

What is MDR?

MDR, or Merchant Discount Rate, is a fee charged to merchants by banks and payment service providers for processing digital transactions made by customers using instruments like credit cards, debit cards, or UPI.

Will consumers pay MDR for UPI transactions?

No, consumers will continue to use UPI free of cost. The MDR is a charge applicable only to merchants for accepting certain Person-to-Merchant (P2M) UPI payments.

What UPI transactions attract MDR?

MDR of 0.4% applies to Person-to-Merchant (P2M) UPI transactions above ₹2,000. For transactions of ₹75,000 and above, the MDR is capped at ₹300.

Are small merchants affected by UPI MDR?

Small merchants operating under the P2PM framework, receiving up to ₹1 lakh per month through UPI QR codes into their personal accounts, are exempt from MDR.

How does UPI MDR compare to credit card MDR?

UPI MDR (0.4%, capped at ₹300 for high values) is significantly lower than standard credit card MDRs, which typically range from 1.5% to 2.5% per transaction.

What happens if I use a credit card linked to UPI?

Transactions made using a credit card linked to UPI (e.g., RuPay credit cards) will follow the MDR rules applicable to the underlying credit product, not the new 0.4% UPI MDR framework.

Can merchants pass UPI MDR charges to customers?

No, merchants are strictly prohibited from passing on MDR charges to customers while accepting payments through UPI. Consumers must only pay the posted price.
