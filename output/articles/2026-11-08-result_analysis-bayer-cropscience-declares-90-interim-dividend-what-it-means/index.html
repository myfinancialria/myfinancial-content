Bayer CropScience Declares ₹90 Interim Dividend: What It Means

TL;DR

  • Bayer CropScience has announced an interim dividend of ₹90 per equity share for the financial year 2026-27.
  • This dividend translates to a 900% payout based on the face value of ₹10 per share.
  • The record date for determining eligible shareholders for this dividend is November 12, 2026.
  • The company has stated that the dividend payment will be disbursed to shareholders by November 30, 2026.
  • Bayer CropScience also reported a rise in its second-quarter net profit to ₹166.2 crore, up from ₹152.7 crore in the same period last year.

What this means in plain terms

When a company like Bayer CropScience declares a dividend, it's essentially distributing a portion of its profits to its shareholders. The ₹90 per share dividend means that for every share you own, the company will pay you ₹90. The "900% payout" might sound very high, but it's calculated based on the face value of the share, which is often ₹10 for many companies, not its market price. So, 900% of ₹10 is indeed ₹90.

This particular dividend is an "interim" dividend, meaning it's declared and paid out before the company's full annual results are finalised. The "record date" is crucial: you must hold the shares in your demat account by the end of this specific day (November 12, 2026, in this case) to be eligible to receive the dividend. The payment date, November 30, 2026, is when the money will actually be credited to your bank account linked with your demat. Along with this dividend news, the company also shared its second-quarter financial results, showing an increase in net profit, which often signals healthy business performance.

Bayer CropScience's Q2 Performance and Dividend Payout

Bayer CropScience has demonstrated a positive financial trajectory in its recent second-quarter results. The company's net profit for Q2 FY27 saw an increase, reaching ₹166.2 crore compared to ₹152.7 crore in the corresponding quarter of the previous fiscal year. This growth in profitability often provides the financial headroom for companies to reward their shareholders through dividends.

The interim dividend of ₹90 per share represents a significant payout for shareholders. This 900% payout is calculated against the face value of the equity share, which is typically ₹10 for Bayer CropScience. It's important for investors to distinguish this percentage from the dividend yield, which is calculated as the dividend per share divided by the current market price of the share. A higher net profit can indicate robust operational performance, which in turn supports such dividend declarations.

Understanding Dividend Eligibility and Taxation

For investors to be eligible for the announced ₹90 interim dividend, they must ensure their shares are in their demat account by the record date of November 12, 2026. This means that shares would typically need to be purchased at least one trading day before the record date, considering the T+1 settlement cycle for equities in India. The dividend amount will be credited to the shareholder's bank account linked with their demat account by November 30, 2026.

From a taxation perspective, dividends received by individual shareholders in India are taxable in their hands. The Income Tax Act, 1961, stipulates that dividend income is added to the taxpayer's total income and taxed at their applicable slab rates. For instance, if an individual falls into the 30% tax bracket, their dividend income will also be taxed at 30% (plus applicable surcharge and cess). Companies declaring dividends are also required to deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) if the dividend amount exceeds ₹10,000 in a financial year for resident individuals, at a rate of 10%. However, if a shareholder provides a valid PAN, the TDS rate typically remains 10%. If no PAN is provided, the TDS rate can be higher. It is advisable to consult the latest guidelines from the Income Tax Department or a tax advisor for specific situations.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 42, ₹35L CTC, Mumbai, who holds 500 shares of Bayer CropScience.

  1. Dividend Income: Priya owns 500 shares. With an interim dividend of ₹90 per share, her total dividend income will be:

    2. 500 shares * ₹90/share = ₹45,000.

  1. TDS Deduction: Since her dividend income of ₹45,000 exceeds ₹10,000, Bayer CropScience will deduct TDS at 10% (assuming Priya has provided her PAN).

    2. TDS = 10% of ₹45,000 = ₹4,500. Priya will receive ₹45,000 - ₹4,500 = ₹40,500 in her bank account.

  1. Tax Liability: Priya's annual income of ₹35 lakhs places her in the 30% income tax bracket. Her dividend income of ₹45,000 will be added to her total taxable income.

    2. Her final tax liability on the dividend will be 30% of ₹45,000 = ₹13,500 (plus applicable surcharge and cess). Since ₹4,500 has already been deducted as TDS, Priya will need to pay the remaining tax of ₹13,500 - ₹4,500 = ₹9,000 (plus surcharge and cess) when filing her income tax return.

This example illustrates that while the dividend provides direct cash flow, the net amount received and the final tax outgo depend on the individual's tax slab and TDS rules.

FAQ

What is an interim dividend?

An interim dividend is a dividend declared and paid by a company during its ongoing financial year, before the final annual results are announced. It's typically based on the company's performance during a specific quarter or half-year period.

How is the 900% payout calculated?

The 900% payout is calculated based on the face value of the share, not its market price. If the face value of Bayer CropScience shares is ₹10, then a 900% payout means 900% of ₹10, which equals ₹90 per share.

What is the significance of the record date?

The record date (November 12, 2026, for this dividend) is the cut-off date set by the company to determine which shareholders are eligible to receive the dividend. Only those who hold the shares in their demat account by the end of this date will receive the dividend.

Is dividend income taxable in India?

Yes, dividend income received by individual shareholders is fully taxable in India. It is added to your total income and taxed at your applicable income tax slab rates.

Will TDS be deducted on this dividend?

Yes, if the dividend amount you receive from Bayer CropScience exceeds ₹10,000 in a financial year (for resident individuals), the company is mandated to deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) at 10% (if PAN is provided).
