Story 1: New SEBI Rules Simplify Demat and Mutual Fund Account Opening

Topic signal: SEBI simplifies KYC norms for demat and mutual fund accounts

Good news for investors! Starting December 24, 2026, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) is implementing eight key changes to simplify the Know Your Customer (KYC) process for opening demat, trading, and mutual fund accounts. These new rules aim to reduce paperwork and eliminate the need for repeated verification when you deal with multiple financial intermediaries.

Under the revised framework, your KYC information will be centralised, making it easier to port your details across different brokers and financial institutions. This means less hassle when opening new accounts, as intermediaries will upload your Central Know Your Customer Identifier (CKYC ID) and other relevant details.

SEBI has also introduced measures to prevent unnecessary sharing of your KYC information if you no longer have an active account with an intermediary. Stockbrokers and other intermediaries will need to notify KRAs (KYC Registration Agencies) when an account is closed, and mutual funds will do so if an account has no holdings or transactions for six months.

Story 2: EPFO Proposes New Ways to Boost PF Savings for All

Topic signal: EPFO's new proposal explained: How can you build PF savings through customer tips and third-party micro-contributions?

The Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) has proposed significant changes that could make it easier for many more Indians, including gig workers and freelancers, to build their retirement savings. The new proposal aims to allow voluntary and third-party micro-contributions directly into your Provident Fund (PF) account, independent of traditional employer payroll systems.

This means that individuals could potentially deposit irregular earnings, or even customer tips, directly into their Universal Account Number (UAN)-linked PF accounts. For example, a delivery partner receiving a tip could have a portion of it directed to their PF, helping them save for the future.

Currently, PF contributions are primarily tied to salaried employment. This new mechanism would remove that barrier, offering a flexible way for those outside traditional employment to save. The annual self-contribution limit is proposed to rise to ₹5 lakh, aligning with the General Provident Fund for government employees.

These proposed changes could significantly expand access to retirement savings for millions. To understand how these developments might fit into your overall financial strategy, explore MyFinancial's resources on retirement planning.

What to remember: EPFO's new proposal could allow direct, voluntary contributions and third-party micro-contributions to PF accounts, expanding retirement savings options for many.
