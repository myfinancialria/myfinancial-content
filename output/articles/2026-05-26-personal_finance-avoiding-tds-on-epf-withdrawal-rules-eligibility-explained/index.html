Avoiding TDS on EPF Withdrawal: Rules & Eligibility Explained

TL;DR

  • Withdrawing your Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) balance before completing five years of continuous service can lead to Tax Deducted at Source (TDS).
  • TDS is typically levied if the withdrawal amount exceeds ₹50,000 and the service period is less than five years.
  • If your total taxable income for the year is below the basic exemption limit, you can submit Form 15G (for non-senior citizens) or Form 15H (for senior citizens) to avoid TDS.
  • Certain situations, such as termination due to illness, employer closure, or transfer to another EPF account, are exempt from TDS regardless of the service period.
  • Providing your PAN is crucial; without it, a higher TDS rate of 20% will be applied instead of the standard 10%.

What this means in plain terms

When you contribute to your Employees' Provident Fund (EPF), it's essentially a long-term savings scheme designed for your retirement. The government encourages this long-term view by offering tax benefits on contributions and interest earned, provided you stick with it for a reasonable period.

If you decide to withdraw your EPF money prematurely, specifically before completing five years of continuous service, the Income Tax Department may deduct tax at source (TDS) from your withdrawal. This is a mechanism to ensure that the tax benefits provided for long-term savings aren't misused for short-term gains. However, there are specific rules and conditions under which this TDS applies, and equally important, situations where you can avoid it.

Understanding the 5-Year Continuous Service Rule

The cornerstone of EPF taxation on withdrawals is the "five years of continuous service" rule. This rule dictates whether your EPF withdrawal will be considered taxable or exempt.

What constitutes continuous service?

Continuous service doesn't necessarily mean working with the same employer for five years. If you switch jobs, but transfer your EPF balance from your old employer's account to your new employer's account, the service period from both employers is combined. This combined period is considered continuous service for the purpose of the five-year rule. It's crucial to ensure these transfers happen seamlessly to maintain the continuity. If you withdraw your EPF from the old employer and then join a new one, the continuity is broken.

Implications of breaking continuity

If you withdraw your EPF balance before completing five years of continuous service, the withdrawal becomes taxable. This means that not only the employer's contribution and interest earned on it, but also the interest earned on your own contribution, will be added to your income for that financial year and taxed as per your applicable slab rates. Additionally, if TDS was deducted by the EPFO, you would either claim it back during your Income Tax Return (ITR) filing if your total income is below the taxable limit, or adjust it against your final tax liability.

Exemptions to the 5-year rule

There are specific scenarios where the five-year rule is relaxed, and your EPF withdrawal will not attract TDS even if you haven't completed five years of service. These include:

  • Termination of service due to illness: If you have to leave your job because of a severe illness or permanent disability.
  • Closure of the employer's business: If your employer's business is discontinued or closed down.
  • Other reasons beyond the employee's control: The EPFO may consider other genuine reasons on a case-by-case basis.

In these situations, the withdrawal is treated as if the five-year condition was met, and no TDS is levied. For more details on EPF withdrawal rules, refer to the official EPFO website (epfindia.gov.in).

When Does TDS Apply? The ₹50,000 Threshold

Even if you haven't completed five years of continuous service, TDS on EPF withdrawal doesn't automatically apply to all amounts. There's a specific threshold that needs to be crossed.

The ₹50,000 limit

TDS will only be deducted if the EPF withdrawal amount is ₹50,000 or more. If your withdrawal is less than ₹50,000, no TDS will be applicable, even if your service period is less than five years. This threshold was increased from ₹30,000 to ₹50,000 in 2016 to provide relief to small withdrawals.

TDS rates based on PAN availability

The rate of TDS depends on whether you provide your Permanent Account Number (PAN) during the withdrawal process.

  • With PAN: If you provide your PAN, the TDS rate is 10%.
  • Without PAN: If you do not provide your PAN, a significantly higher TDS rate of 20% will be applied. This is a penalty for not linking your financial transactions with your tax identity.

It is always advisable to ensure your PAN is updated with your employer and EPFO records to avoid higher TDS deductions. The Income Tax Department provides guidelines on TDS rates and rules (incometax.gov.in).

Exemptions and Form 15G/15H for Nil Tax Liability

Even if your withdrawal meets the criteria for TDS (over ₹50,000 and less than five years of service), you can still avoid TDS if your total income for the financial year is below the basic exemption limit.

Using Form 15G and Form 15H

These forms are declarations submitted by individuals whose income is below the taxable limit, requesting the deductor (in this case, EPFO) not to deduct TDS.

  • Form 15G: This form is for resident individuals who are not senior citizens (i.e., below 60 years of age). To be eligible to submit Form 15G, your total income for the financial year (including the EPF withdrawal amount) must be less than the basic exemption limit (currently ₹2.5 lakhs for individuals below 60).
  • Form 15H: This form is specifically for resident senior citizens (i.e., 60 years of age or above). For senior citizens, the basic exemption limit is higher (currently ₹3 lakhs for those aged 60-80, and ₹5 lakhs for those aged 80 and above). They can submit Form 15H if their total income is below their applicable exemption limit.

Conditions for submitting Form 15G/15H

  • Total income below taxable limit: The most crucial condition is that your total estimated income for the financial year must be below the basic exemption limit.
  • Tax liability is nil: Consequently, your final tax liability for the year should be zero.
  • Resident Indian: Only resident Indians can submit these forms. NRIs cannot use Form 15G/15H for EPF withdrawals.

By submitting the correct form (15G or 15H) along with your EPF withdrawal application, you declare that your income for the year does not warrant any tax deduction, thereby preventing the EPFO from deducting TDS. Ensure to fill these forms accurately and submit them to the EPFO office or through the online portal as part of your withdrawal request.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 35, a marketing professional in Pune, with a CTC of ₹22L. She worked for her previous company for 3 years and 6 months and then resigned to start her own venture. She had an accumulated EPF balance of ₹4.50 lakhs. After 6 months of being self-employed, she decides to withdraw her entire EPF balance.

Scenario 1: Priya's new venture is not doing well, and her total taxable income for the current financial year (including the ₹4.50 lakhs EPF withdrawal) is estimated to be ₹4 lakhs.

  1. Service Period: Priya's service period is 3 years and 6 months, which is less than 5 years.
  2. Withdrawal Amount: ₹4.50 lakhs, which is more than the ₹50,000 threshold.
  3. TDS Applicability: Based on the rules, TDS would normally apply.
  4. Tax Liability: However, Priya's total estimated income for the year is ₹4 lakhs. Since she is below 60, the basic exemption limit for her is ₹2.5 lakhs. While her total income is above ₹2.5 lakhs, if she has eligible deductions under Section 80C, 80D, etc., her net taxable income might fall below the limit where tax is payable (e.g., after standard deduction of ₹50,000 and Section 80C deductions, her taxable income could be below ₹2.5 lakhs, or within the rebate limit of Section 87A if it's up to ₹5 lakhs).
  5. Action to avoid TDS: If Priya can confidently declare that her final tax liability for the year will be nil (after considering all deductions and rebates), she can submit Form 15G along with her EPF withdrawal application. By doing so, the EPFO will not deduct TDS from her ₹4.50 lakh withdrawal.

Scenario 2: Priya's new venture is successful, and her total taxable income for the current financial year (including the ₹4.50 lakhs EPF withdrawal) is estimated to be ₹12 lakhs.

  1. Service Period & Withdrawal Amount: Same as above, less than 5 years and over ₹50,000.
  2. TDS Applicability: TDS will apply.
  3. Tax Liability: Priya's total income is ₹12 lakhs. This is well above the basic exemption limit, and she will have a significant tax liability.
  4. Action: Priya cannot submit Form 15G as her tax liability will not be nil. The EPFO will deduct TDS at 10% (assuming she has provided her PAN) on the entire ₹4.50 lakhs.

    5. * TDS amount = 10% of ₹4,50,000 = ₹45,000. * Priya will receive ₹4,05,000 (₹4,50,000 - ₹45,000).

  5. ITR Filing: When Priya files her Income Tax Return, she will include the ₹4.50 lakhs EPF withdrawal as part of her income. The ₹45,000 TDS deducted will be reflected in Form 26AS and can be adjusted against her total tax liability for the year. If her final tax liability is less than the TDS deducted, she would receive a refund.

This example illustrates how the rules apply and the importance of Form 15G/15H for those with nil tax liability.

What to do this week

  1. Check your EPF service history: Log in to the EPFO UAN portal (epfindia.gov.in) and verify your total continuous service period across all employers. Ensure all previous EPF accounts were transferred and not withdrawn.
  2. Estimate your annual income: Calculate your projected total income for the current financial year, including any potential EPF withdrawal amount, to determine if your income will fall below the basic exemption limit.
  3. Ensure PAN is linked: Verify that your PAN is updated and linked with your UAN on the EPFO portal. This prevents higher TDS deductions if you do need to withdraw prematurely.
  4. Understand Form 15G/15H: If you anticipate a premature EPF withdrawal and believe your total tax liability for the year will be nil, familiarise yourself with Form 15G or 15H requirements. Have it ready to submit with your withdrawal application.
  5. Consult a professional: If your situation is complex or involves large sums, consider speaking with a Certified Financial Planner or tax advisor to understand the full tax implications of your EPF withdrawal.

FAQ

### What is the primary condition for TDS on EPF withdrawal?

TDS is primarily applicable if you withdraw your EPF balance before completing five years of continuous service and the withdrawal amount exceeds ₹50,000.

### What is the TDS rate if I don't provide my PAN?

If you do not provide your PAN, the TDS rate on EPF withdrawal will be a higher 20% instead of the standard 10%.

### Can Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) use Form 15G or 15H?

No, Form 15G and Form 15H are specifically for resident Indians. NRIs cannot use these forms to avoid TDS on EPF withdrawals.

### Is interest earned on my EPF also subject to TDS if withdrawn prematurely?

Yes, if withdrawn before five years, the entire accumulated balance, including employer contributions, employee contributions, and all interest earned on both, becomes taxable and subject to TDS.

### What if my employer's business closes down? Does TDS still apply?

No, if your service is terminated due to the closure of the employer's business or any other reason beyond your control, TDS will not be deducted, even if your service period is less than five years.

### How do I check if my PAN is linked to my UAN?

You can log in to the EPFO UAN member portal, navigate to the 'Manage' section, and then select 'KYC' to check the status of your PAN linking.

### If I transfer my EPF from one employer to another, does it break the continuous service period?

No, transferring your EPF balance from your old employer's account to your new employer's account ensures continuity of service, and the combined period counts towards the five-year rule.
