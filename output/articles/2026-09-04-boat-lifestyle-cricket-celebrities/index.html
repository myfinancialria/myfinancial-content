How boAt Built a ₹4,000 Cr Audio Brand Using Cricketers Instead of TV Ads

In a market saturated with global giants and aggressive Chinese imports, launching an Indian consumer electronics brand in 2016 seemed, to many, an act of sheer folly. "Indians were getting out. Chinese were coming in," recalled Aman Gupta, co-founder of boAt, describing the prevailing sentiment. "People started writing our obituary even before we were born." Yet, from a small room in Delhi and an initial investment of just ₹30 lakh, Gupta and his partner Sameer Mehta embarked on a journey that would defy expectations, eventually making boAt India's #1 wearable brand by 2020 and a company with a ₹4,000 crore turnover by FY23. Their secret? A playbook that swapped expensive traditional TV ads for the raw, undeniable power of cricket and influencer marketing.

The World Before

Before boAt entered the scene, the Indian audio accessories market was a battleground dominated by two distinct forces. On one side stood established global players like JBL, Sony, and Sennheiser, which commanded the premium segment with their legacy and perceived quality. On the other, a torrent of affordable, often disposable, Chinese imports flooded the entry-level market, leaving little room for local contenders. For the average young Indian consumer, finding stylish, durable audio products that didn't break the bank was a genuine struggle. Many of the existing options were either too expensive, lacked local relevance, or suffered from poor quality.

The prevailing wisdom for consumer brands in India dictated heavy investment in mainstream advertising—television commercials, large print ads, and billboards—to build trust and reach a vast, diverse audience. This was a capital-intensive game, practically impossible for a bootstrapped startup to compete in. Investors, too, were wary of new Indian brands in consumer electronics, viewing it as a high-volume, thin-margin business with formidable incumbents. The earwear market, though growing, was still nascent, with only a few thousand units shipped in 2017, expanding to 1.6 million units in 2018 and 8.5 million in 2019. The landscape seemed firmly set, with little space for a disruptor, especially one without deep pockets for traditional media.

The Crack

Aman Gupta, with his background in launching global brands like Beats Audio and Sennheiser in India, and Sameer Mehta, an IIT-Delhi and IIM-Ahmedabad alumnus with a focus on product and operations, identified a crucial gap. They saw a generation of "boAtheads"—young, on-the-move Indians seeking affordable, durable, and fashionable audio products. In 2016, they officially launched boAt, initially with a simple, yet essential, product: a robust, unbreakable charging cable for iPhones. This seemingly mundane item quickly became a bestseller on Amazon, demonstrating their knack for solving real consumer pain points.

Their true innovation, however, lay in their marketing strategy. Instead of conventional, high-cost television advertising, boAt adopted a "digital-first" approach, focusing on social media and influencer collaborations. They understood that their target audience—millennials and Gen Z—spent more time online than in front of a TV.

The cornerstone of this strategy was leveraging India's twin passions: cricket and Bollywood. Early on, boAt strategically partnered with Indian Premier League (IPL) cricket teams. By 2020, they were the official audio partner for six IPL teams, including Mumbai Indians, Chennai Super Kings, and Kolkata Knight Riders. More importantly, they didn't just sponsor teams; they enlisted cricketers like Hardik Pandya, KL Rahul, Shikhar Dhawan, Shreyas Iyer, Rishabh Pant, Prithvi Shaw, and Jasprit Bumrah as active brand ambassadors, turning them into "boAtheads." These endorsements weren't passive; the cricketers actively promoted boAt products on their social media, in match activations, and during product launches, making the brand synonymous with the swagger and success of modern Indian sports stars.

Simultaneously, boAt tapped into Bollywood's glamour, bringing on board actors like Kiara Advani, Kartik Aaryan, Jacqueline Fernandez, and Rashmika Mandanna. They also roped in musicians like Neha Kakkar and Diljit Dosanjh, and hundreds of micro-influencers across various platforms. This multi-pronged influencer strategy created a constant buzz, making boAt unavoidable on digital feeds. Campaigns like #DoWhatFloatsYourboAt encouraged users to share their own stories, fostering a sense of community around the brand.

This approach was remarkably cost-effective compared to traditional advertising. By investing in micro-priced celebrity endorsements and digital campaigns, boAt achieved widespread visibility and an aura of aspirational yet approachable lifestyle. They weren't just selling earphones; they were selling an identity—a blend of music, fashion, and sport that resonated deeply with the youth. Their products, too, were designed for this audience: stylish, durable (like their early tangle-free 'Bassheads' earphones), and importantly, affordable.

This potent combination of product-market fit and a disruptive marketing strategy yielded phenomenal growth. Within two years of launch, boAt achieved ₹100 crore in sales. By FY20, their revenue had surged to ₹700.44 crore, with profits of ₹48.5 crore. By December 2020, boAt had already become India's number one audio brand by market share. They were selling over 15,000 units every single day.

What Happened Next

The momentum didn't stop. boAt continued its meteoric rise, demonstrating that its success was no fluke. By 2021, the company's revenue had crossed ₹1,000 crore, and it reached approximately ₹3,000 crore by 2022. Their innovative product strategy led to diversification beyond audio, entering categories like smartwatches with products like 'Storm' in 2020, and later expanding into grooming devices, soundbars, and power banks.

In a remarkable testament to their growth, boAt became the fifth-largest wearable brand globally by the third quarter of 2020, surpassing established players like Apple, Samsung, and Xiaomi in India. Their ascent continued, and by Q3 2023, boAt solidified its position as the second-largest wearable brand worldwide in terms of shipment volume, trailing only Apple. The company's valuation reflected this trajectory, reaching ₹10,900 crore as of December 2022.

For the financial year 2025-26, boAt's parent company, Imagine Marketing, reported a profit after tax of ₹84.5 crore on revenues of ₹2,931 crore. Significantly, their wearables business, which had previously recorded a loss, turned profitable, showing a profit of about ₹7 crore in FY26. Imagine Marketing has since filed papers for an Initial Public Offering (IPO) with SEBI, targeting a valuation of around $1.5 billion, signaling their ambition to further consolidate their market leadership and expand into new consumer tech categories and international markets.

Lessons to Take Away

  1. Harness Underpriced Assets: boAt's genius lay in identifying and leveraging the power of cricketers and digital influencers when their endorsement prices were relatively accessible for digital-first brands. This allowed them to achieve massive reach and credibility without the prohibitive costs of traditional media, creating a blueprint for others to follow.
  2. Build a Community, Not Just Customers: By inviting consumers to become "boAtheads" and engaging them through user-generated content and relatable narratives, boAt fostered a loyal community. This community amplified brand messages organically, turning customers into advocates and creating a powerful, self-sustaining marketing engine.
  3. Localise with Lifestyle, Not Just Language: boAt didn't just sell gadgets; it sold a lifestyle perfectly tailored to young India. By integrating its products into popular culture—cricket, Bollywood, music, and social media trends—it made its audio devices fashion statements and essential accessories, rather than mere electronic commodities.
  4. Agility in Product and Channels: Starting with charging cables and quickly pivoting to affordable, stylish audio, then expanding into smartwatches and other wearables, boAt demonstrated remarkable agility. Their initial focus on e-commerce, followed by a strategic expansion into offline retail, ensured they captured market share across diverse consumer segments.
  5. Profitability from Day One, Not Burn-Rate Dominance: In an era often defined by aggressive cash burn to acquire market share, boAt maintained a focus on profitability from its first year. This disciplined approach, coupled with effective marketing, allowed them to grow sustainably and build a robust business without relying solely on venture capital injections.
