Market Wrap — 23 September 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed higher for the day, extending their positive run.
  • Rising US Treasury yields and a stronger dollar acted as a key global headwind, influencing FII outflows.
  • Whirlpool of India Ltd. was the standout gainer, surging 20% in the Nifty 500 pack.
  • Prime Focus Ltd. emerged as the top loser from the Nifty 500, declining over 5%.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the cash market, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong buying support.
  • F&O positioning data is unavailable for today's wrap and will be included from the next session.

How the market behaved today

Indian equities witnessed a positive session today, with the Nifty 50 closing at 23446.80, up 0.50%, and the Bank Nifty ending at 56548.90, gaining 0.59%. The day saw indices opening slightly positive and maintaining an upward trajectory, largely staying within a defined range after an initial push. Both the broader market indices, Nifty Midcap 100 and Nifty Smallcap 100, outperformed the benchmarks, indicating a healthy appetite for mid and small-cap stocks.

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, cooled off significantly, dropping by 5.91% to 10.35. This decline suggests that market participants perceive lower near-term risks, contributing to a more stable trading environment.

What moved the market — the factors

The Indian market today displayed resilience despite challenging global cues, primarily driven by persistent domestic institutional buying. A significant factor was the surge in the 10-year US Treasury yields, which climbed over 5.05% to a 19-year high, fueled by concerns about further Federal Reserve rate hikes following fresh services and manufacturing data. This rise in global bond yields typically makes emerging markets less attractive to foreign investors, leading to FII outflows. Indeed, FIIs were net sellers today, offloading ₹3,810 crore from the Indian cash market.

However, this selling pressure was comfortably absorbed by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), who pumped in a robust ₹4,120 crore, providing crucial support and preventing a broader market decline. The rupee also snapped its four-day winning streak, weakening against the dollar, which is often a consequence of FII selling and broad dollar strength.

Sector-wise, Metal, FMCG, and PSU Bank sectors led the gains, indicating a rotation of funds into these areas. The Metal sector, in particular, saw strong buying interest, rising 2.40%. On the other hand, the IT sector faced headwinds, declining by 0.87%, likely due to concerns over rising US interest rates impacting global growth and technology spending. Media and Energy sectors also ended in the red, albeit with smaller losses.

Individual stock movements also played a role, with strong performances from names like Whirlpool of India and Elecon Engineering, while Prime Focus and Pine Labs saw significant declines. The overall market breadth remained positive, with advances outnumbering declines, suggesting underlying strength beyond the headline indices.

In contrast, DIIs have been net buyers every single day, consistently injecting capital into the Indian market. This sustained domestic buying has been a critical factor in cushioning the market from global headwinds and FII outflows, highlighting the increasing strength of domestic liquidity.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's session was primarily driven by a tug-of-war between global macroeconomic factors and strong domestic institutional support. The most significant global event was the continued surge in US Treasury yields, with the 10-year yield hitting a 19-year high above 5.05%. This development, coupled with a stronger dollar and concerns about further Federal Reserve rate hikes, led to FIIs pulling out ₹3,810 crore from the Indian equity market.

However, the market showed remarkable resilience, primarily due to the substantial buying by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), who invested ₹4,120 crore. This strong domestic liquidity helped the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty close in positive territory. Sectors like Metal, FMCG, and PSU Banks saw significant buying, while the IT sector, often sensitive to global economic outlooks and currency movements, faced selling pressure. There was no single defining domestic event today; rather, the session was a reflection of how Indian markets are navigating global monetary policy tightening with strong internal demand.

Setup for tomorrow

Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will remain crucial. The continued rise in US Treasury yields and a stronger Dollar Index (currently at 101.04, up +0.61%) could keep FIIs on the selling side. The Indian rupee's recent weakening to 95.74 per dollar will also be a factor to watch. Crude oil prices (Brent at $96.93, WTI at $91.23) have seen a decline, which could offer some relief on the inflation front for India if the trend continues. Gold and Silver also saw declines. Investors will monitor any further developments regarding US interest rate expectations and how global markets, particularly Wall Street (which slipped today), perform overnight. Domestically, the consistent DII buying will be key. Traders should watch for the 23500 level on Nifty 50 as a potential resistance and 23300 as support.
