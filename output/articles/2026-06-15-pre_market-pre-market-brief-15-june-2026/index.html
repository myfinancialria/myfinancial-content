Pre-Market Brief — 15 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets closed mostly positive, with Asian indices showing significant gains, driven by a preliminary US-Iran peace deal.
  • Crude oil prices plunged over 5%, a major positive for India's import bill and inflation outlook.
  • The Indian Rupee strengthened significantly against the US Dollar, benefiting from falling crude and anticipated foreign inflows.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained strong net buyers.
  • Regulatory scrutiny on retail derivatives trading is increasing, with calls for stricter qualification criteria.

Where global markets closed

Global markets ended yesterday on a largely positive note, buoyed by optimism surrounding a preliminary peace deal between the US and Iran. This development led to a significant drop in crude oil prices and boosted risk sentiment across equities. Asian markets, in particular, saw strong gains.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee saw a notable appreciation against the US Dollar, with USD/INR closing at ₹94.67, down 1.14%. This strengthening of the rupee is primarily attributed to the sharp decline in crude oil prices and expectations of increased foreign inflows. The Dollar Index also weakened by 0.24% to 99.51, reflecting broader dollar softness.

In the commodities market, crude oil prices witnessed a significant slump. Brent Crude fell by 4.83% to $83.11 per barrel, while WTI Crude dropped by 5.17% to $80.49 per barrel. This sharp retreat in oil prices is a substantial positive for India, as it helps alleviate concerns about inflation and reduces the country's import bill.

Gold prices advanced by 3.40% to $4358.10, and Silver also gained 3.92% to $70.52, suggesting some underlying safe-haven demand or inflation hedging despite the positive equity sentiment. The US 10-year Treasury Yield edged up slightly by 0.54% to 4.49%.

Recent FII/DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued to be net sellers in the Indian equity markets yesterday, offloading shares worth ₹-1,082 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained strong net buyers, pumping in ₹5,341 crore. This trend of consistent DII buying absorbing FII outflows has been observed over the last five trading sessions.

Overnight news that matters

  • Wall St. futures advance over 1% as markets cheer Iran deal

    • A preliminary deal between the US and Iran has significantly boosted global investor sentiment, leading to an advance in Wall Street futures. This positive global cue is expected to set a strong opening tone for Indian markets today.

  • Oil retreat hands RBI an assist in boosting rupee's near-term outlook

    • The recent U.S.-Iran peace deal has triggered a substantial slump in crude oil prices, which is a significant positive for the Indian rupee. This, coupled with the Reserve Bank of India's efforts to attract foreign inflows, is expected to further support the currency's appreciation, reducing India's import bill and potentially easing inflationary pressures.

  • Nilesh Shah bats for minimum qualifying criteria for F&O trading after Maharashtra man kills family, self over Rs 1.8 cr loss

    • Following a tragic incident linked to stock market losses, Kotak AMC's Nilesh Shah has advocated for mandatory qualification criteria for derivatives trading. This highlights growing concerns about retail participation in the F&O segment and could lead to regulatory discussions on stricter entry norms, potentially impacting trading volumes or participant demographics in the future.

  • Maruti Suzuki shares jump over 4%. How is the new E100 regulation triggering a surge?

    • Maruti Suzuki shares surged after the government approved legal recognition for 100% ethanol blend fuel (E100). This policy move aims to reduce India's import dependence on crude oil and provides a significant boost to the auto sector, particularly companies like Maruti Suzuki that are investing in ethanol-compatible vehicles.

  • Vedanta's demerger is finally here, but where will investors find the most value?

    • Vedanta's demerger has resulted in the creation of four new stocks, with aluminium drawing significant attention. However, analysis suggests that the residual Vedanta entity might offer the most compelling commodity exposure for investors. This development will be closely watched by shareholders for potential value unlocking across the newly formed entities.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Crude Oil Prices: The sharp fall in Brent and WTI crude will be a key monitorable. Any further sustained decline could provide continued tailwinds for the Indian economy and markets.
  2. Rupee Movement: Following yesterday's significant appreciation, the trajectory of the Indian Rupee against the US Dollar will be crucial. A stronger rupee aids in managing inflation and import costs.
  3. FII/DII Flows: While global cues are positive, the persistent FII selling trend needs close observation. DII buying has been robust, but sustained FII outflows could cap market upside.
  4. Auto Sector: Shares of auto companies, especially those with a focus on ethanol-blended fuels like Maruti Suzuki, could see continued interest following the E100 regulation news.
  5. Vedanta Group Stocks: With the demerger now in effect, investors will be closely watching the price discovery and performance of the newly listed entities and the residual Vedanta.

Bottom line

Indian markets are poised for a positive opening today, largely driven by favourable global cues stemming from the US-Iran preliminary peace deal and the subsequent sharp decline in crude oil prices. While the strengthening rupee and strong DII support provide a robust foundation, the continued selling by FIIs remains a factor to monitor for sustained upward momentum.
