Annual Financial Review: 6 Checks Beyond Mutual Funds

TL;DR

  • Financial planning is comprehensive and extends far beyond just investing in mutual funds.
  • Annually reviewing six key areas ensures your financial strategy remains robust and aligned with your goals.
  • These areas include assessing your income and expenses, managing your loans effectively, and optimising your investment portfolio.
  • It's crucial to review your insurance coverage for adequacy and update beneficiaries as needed.
  • Proactive tax planning and establishing a clear estate plan are vital for wealth preservation and smooth succession.

What this means in plain terms

Many of us focus heavily on our investments, particularly mutual funds, when we think about financial planning. While investing is undoubtedly a critical component of building wealth, it's just one piece of a much larger puzzle. A truly secure and effective financial plan requires a holistic approach, meaning you look at all aspects of your money life, not just where you're parking your savings.

Think of your finances as a complex machine. If you only focus on one part, like the engine (your investments), but neglect the brakes (your insurance), the fuel lines (your income and expenses), or the steering (your estate plan), the entire system can break down. An annual financial review means taking a step back to examine every major component of your financial life, ensuring everything is working together efficiently and is aligned with your long-term aspirations. It's about protecting what you've built, optimising what you have, and planning for the future, well beyond just checking your mutual fund NAVs.

Your Financial Foundation

A strong financial plan begins with a clear understanding of your current financial position and how you manage your liabilities. Regularly reviewing your income, expenses, and outstanding loans provides the bedrock for all other financial decisions.

Income and Expense Tracking

Understanding where your money comes from and where it goes is fundamental. An annual review of your income sources helps you identify potential growth areas or dependencies. Similarly, meticulously tracking your expenses allows you to distinguish between necessary outlays and discretionary spending. This exercise can reveal opportunities to save more, reduce unnecessary costs, and free up capital for investments or debt reduction. Many individuals find that small, consistent changes in spending habits can lead to significant savings over a year. For instance, automating savings transfers immediately after salary credit can be an effective way to "pay yourself first" before other expenses arise. Reviewing your bank statements and credit card bills for recurring subscriptions you no longer use is another quick win.

Debt Management and Loans

Loans can be powerful tools for wealth creation (like a home loan) or significant drains on your finances (like high-interest personal loans or credit card debt). An annual check-up of all your outstanding loans is crucial. This involves reviewing the interest rates on each loan – are you paying more than you need to? Could you refinance a home loan for a lower interest rate, potentially saving lakhs over the tenure? Consider the possibility of prepaying high-interest debt like credit card outstanding or personal loans, as the interest saved is often equivalent to a guaranteed, tax-free return on your money. Evaluate your debt-to-income ratio; ideally, your total EMI commitments (excluding home loan) should not exceed 30-40% of your net monthly income. If you have multiple loans, explore options for debt consolidation to simplify payments and potentially reduce interest costs.

Growing and Optimising Your Wealth

Beyond the basics, effective financial planning involves strategically growing your assets and ensuring you retain as much of that growth as possible through prudent tax management.

Investment Portfolio Review

While mutual funds are a popular investment vehicle, a comprehensive portfolio review encompasses all your assets: equities (direct stocks), bonds, real estate, gold, fixed deposits, provident fund (EPF), and the National Pension System (NPS). The goal is to ensure your asset allocation aligns with your risk tolerance and long-term financial goals. For example, if you're nearing retirement, you might want to gradually shift from aggressive equity funds to more conservative debt instruments. Reviewing your portfolio annually helps you rebalance to your target allocation, trim underperforming assets, and ensure diversification across asset classes. Don't just look at returns; consider the underlying risks, expense ratios of your funds, and the tax implications of any rebalancing. It's also important to check if your nominations are updated across all investment accounts.

Tax Planning and Compliance

Tax planning isn't a year-end scramble; it's an ongoing process. An annual review helps you optimise your tax liability legally and efficiently. This involves assessing your eligibility for various deductions under sections like 80C (EPF, PPF, ELSS, life insurance premiums, home loan principal), 80D (health insurance premiums), 80G (donations), and HRA exemptions. Review your capital gains and losses from investments, especially if you've sold any assets during the year. Understanding the difference between short-term and long-term capital gains tax implications can help you make informed selling decisions. Ensure you are aware of any changes in tax laws or new government schemes that could benefit you. For self-employed individuals, reviewing advance tax payments is critical to avoid penalties. Keeping accurate records of all income and expenses is paramount for smooth Income Tax Return (ITR) filing.

Safeguarding Your Legacy

Protecting your family and ensuring your wealth is passed on according to your wishes are crucial aspects of financial planning that are often overlooked until it's too late.

Insurance Coverage Assessment

Insurance is your financial safety net, protecting you and your dependents from unforeseen circumstances. An annual review of your life, health, and critical illness insurance policies is non-negotiable. For life insurance, check if the coverage amount is still adequate, especially if you've had major life changes like marriage, childbirth, or taking on a new loan. Term insurance is generally recommended for pure protection. For health insurance, assess if your sum insured is sufficient considering rising medical costs, and if your policy covers critical illnesses relevant to your family's medical history. Review the policy terms, deductibles, co-pays, and network hospitals. Ensure all nominees are correctly updated across all your policies, as this is vital for your family to receive the benefits seamlessly. Don't forget general insurance policies like home or motor insurance.

Estate Planning and Will Review

Estate planning is about deciding how your assets will be distributed after your lifetime and ensuring your loved ones are not burdened with legal complexities. Creating a Will is the cornerstone of estate planning, and it should be reviewed annually or after any significant life event (marriage, divorce, birth of a child, death in the family, acquisition of significant assets). A Will ensures your assets are distributed according to your wishes, not as per intestacy laws. Beyond a Will, ensure that nominations are updated across all your bank accounts, investment folios, and insurance policies. While nominations facilitate the transfer of assets, a Will ultimately determines the legal heir. Consider a Power of Attorney for managing affairs if you become incapacitated. For business owners, succession planning is an integral part of estate planning. Proper estate planning can prevent family disputes and ensure a smooth transfer of wealth.

A real example

Meet Priya, 38, earning ₹28 lakhs CTC, based in Bengaluru. She's married with one child, aged 5. Priya has been diligently investing in mutual funds but realised she needed a broader financial check-up.

This year, she decided to conduct a comprehensive review:

  1. Income & Expenses: Priya used a budgeting app for three months and found she was spending nearly ₹30,000 per month on dining out and subscriptions. By cutting down ₹15,000 from these discretionary expenses, she identified an additional ₹1.8 lakhs annually for savings.
  2. Loans: She had a personal loan of ₹5 lakhs at 14% interest and a home loan at 8.2%. She decided to use ₹1 lakh from her emergency fund (which was slightly overfunded) and ₹80,000 from her newly identified savings to prepay a significant portion of her personal loan, saving substantial interest over its remaining tenure.
  3. Investments: Her equity mutual fund portfolio had grown significantly, now forming 80% of her total investments, while her target was 65%. She decided to rebalance by redeeming ₹5 lakhs from an aggressive equity fund and investing it into a debt fund and her NPS account, bringing her allocation closer to her desired risk profile. She also checked all her fund nominations.
  4. Tax Planning: Priya realised she hadn't fully utilised her 80C limit beyond her EPF contribution. She invested an additional ₹50,000 into an ELSS fund and purchased a health insurance policy for her parents, claiming an additional ₹25,000 under 80D, saving approximately ₹15,000 in taxes.
  5. Insurance: Her existing term insurance coverage was ₹1.5 crores, which she took 7 years ago. With her higher income, new home loan, and a growing child, she realised it was insufficient. She opted for an additional term plan of ₹1 crore, increasing her total coverage to ₹2.5 crores. She also confirmed her husband was the nominee on all policies.
  6. Estate Planning: Priya had a basic Will but hadn't updated it since her child's birth. She revised it to include her child as a beneficiary and appointed a guardian. She also ensured her husband was nominated across all her bank accounts and investment folios.

By taking these steps, Priya not only optimised her financial situation but also gained immense peace of mind, knowing her family's future was better secured.

What to do this week

  1. Gather Financial Statements: Collect bank statements, credit card bills, loan statements, investment portfolio summaries, and insurance policy documents from the last 12 months.
  2. Track Your Spending: Use a simple spreadsheet or budgeting app to track all your expenses for at least one week to identify spending patterns.
  3. Review Loan Interest Rates: Check the interest rates on all your outstanding loans and research options for refinancing or prepayment.
  4. Verify Nominees: Confirm that nominees are correctly updated across all your bank accounts, investment folios, and insurance policies.
  5. Locate Your Will: Find your last Will (if you have one) and review its contents to ensure it reflects your current wishes and family situation.

FAQ

Why is an annual financial review important?

An annual review ensures your financial plan stays aligned with your evolving life goals, risk tolerance, and economic conditions. It helps identify gaps, optimise resources, and protect your wealth proactively.

How often should I review my insurance policies?

You should review your insurance policies annually, or immediately after any significant life event such as marriage, childbirth, taking a new loan, or a change in dependents.

What documents do I need for an estate plan?

Key documents for estate planning include a Will, nominations for all assets (bank accounts, investments, insurance), and potentially a Power of Attorney.

Can I do tax planning throughout the year?

Yes, tax planning is most effective when done throughout the year, rather than just at the financial year-end. This allows for better decision-making regarding investments and expenditures to optimise tax savings.

Should I only focus on returns during my investment review?

No, while returns are important, you should also consider asset allocation, risk levels, diversification, expense ratios, and the tax implications of your investments.

What if I have too many loans?

If you have multiple high-interest loans, consider strategies like debt consolidation or prioritising prepayment of the most expensive loans to reduce your overall interest burden.

Is a nominee the same as a legal heir?

No, a nominee is a custodian who receives the assets on behalf of the legal heirs. The legal heir, as per a Will or succession laws, is the ultimate owner of the assets.
