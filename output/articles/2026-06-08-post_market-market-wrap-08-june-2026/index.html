Market Wrap — 08 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed lower today.
  • Broader markets, Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100, saw even sharper declines.
  • Realty, Metal, and Auto sectors were the biggest laggards, while Pharma and FMCG showed relative resilience despite closing in the red.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support.
  • Volatility, as measured by India VIX, surged significantly, indicating increased market apprehension.

How the market closed

Indian equities witnessed a broad-based decline today, with the Nifty 50 shedding over 1% to close at 23,123.00, and the Bank Nifty also ending in the red at 54,063.75, down 0.79%. The broader markets experienced even steeper losses, reflecting a cautious sentiment across the board.

The India VIX, a key indicator of market volatility, surged by 7.85% to 17.03, suggesting an increase in investor anxiety and expected price swings in the near term.

Today's session saw most sectors closing in negative territory, with defensive sectors showing relatively less decline.

Top 3 Gainer Sectors (relative performance):

  • PHARMA: -0.41% - The sector showed resilience, declining the least among all sectors today.
  • FMCG: -0.42% - Similar to Pharma, FMCG stocks also saw a modest dip compared to the broader market.
  • PSU BANK: -0.72% - PSU Banks also outperformed the benchmark indices, closing with a relatively smaller loss.

Top 3 Loser Sectors:

  • REALTY: -2.56% - The Realty sector was the biggest laggard today, experiencing a significant downturn.
  • METAL: -2.33% - Metal stocks also faced considerable selling pressure, ending sharply lower.
  • AUTO: -1.85% - The Auto sector witnessed notable declines, contributing to the overall market weakness.

Market breadth

The market breadth was overwhelmingly negative today, with 433 declines against a mere 67 advances out of 500 stocks. This indicates a broad-based selling pressure and a lack of buying interest across a wide range of stocks, suggesting that the market's decline was not confined to just a few large-cap stocks.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued to be net sellers in the Indian equity market today, offloading shares worth ₹8,776 crore. This sustained selling pressure from FIIs was largely absorbed by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), who were net buyers to the tune of ₹9,134 crore, providing crucial support to the market.

| Investor Type | Net Position (₹ crore) | | :------------ | :--------------------- | | FII | -8,776 | | DII | +9,134 |

Over the past five trading sessions, FIIs have consistently been net sellers, with today's outflow being the largest in this period. Conversely, DIIs have consistently stepped in as net buyers, indicating strong domestic institutional support offsetting foreign outflows.

What moved markets today

Several factors contributed to today's market movements, both global and domestic:

  • Global Cues and Middle East Tensions: While US markets saw a rebound driven by chip stocks and easing Middle East tensions, the impact on Indian markets was mixed. The news of a potential immediate ceasefire between Iran and Israel, as stated by Trump, was seen as a positive for global sentiment. However, the rise in crude oil prices, despite easing tensions, likely weighed on Indian investor sentiment.
  • Elevated Crude Oil Prices and Rupee Slump: The rupee slumped today as elevated crude oil prices and Treasury yields supported the dollar. A weaker rupee and higher crude prices are generally negative for India, which is a major oil importer, potentially impacting inflation and corporate margins.
  • FII Selling Pressure: The consistent and significant selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) for the fifth consecutive session continued to exert downward pressure on the Indian market. DII buying provided some cushion but could not entirely negate the foreign outflows.
  • AI Bubble Concerns: Billionaire investor Ray Dalio's comments suggesting that the artificial intelligence market is displaying characteristics of a bubble, despite its transformative potential, might have added to global market caution and influenced sentiment in technology-related stocks.

Setup for tomorrow

Tomorrow's session will likely be influenced by the ongoing FII selling trend and global market cues. While US markets showed a rebound in chip stocks, the underlying concerns about crude oil prices and the rupee's depreciation could keep Indian investors on edge.

Globally, US futures are pointing higher, and Asian markets will react to the overnight developments. Key data releases overnight or early tomorrow could also provide direction. Investors will be watching for any further developments regarding global geopolitical situations and crude oil price movements. Sectors like IT, which saw significant selling today, and oil-sensitive sectors will be in focus.
