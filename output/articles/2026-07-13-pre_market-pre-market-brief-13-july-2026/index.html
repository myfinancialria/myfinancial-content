Pre-Market Brief — 13 July 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets presented a mixed picture, with US and European indices closing higher, while Asian markets showed a negative bias this morning.
  • GIFT Nifty indicates a slightly cautious to flat opening for the Indian benchmark indices.
  • Both FIIs and DIIs were net buyers in the cash segment yesterday, providing domestic support to the market.
  • A significant spike in crude oil prices and accelerating US inflation are key concerns, potentially impacting India's inflation outlook and bond yields.
  • Nifty 50's immediate support is seen around 24000-24050, with resistance at 24200-24250.
  • Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and upcoming US inflation data are the main risks to monitor this week.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Overnight, US markets closed with modest gains, with the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all inching higher. European markets also ended in positive territory. However, Asian markets this morning are largely trading with a negative bias, notably the Nikkei and Shanghai indices, reflecting some caution. This mixed global sentiment suggests a subdued start for Indian equities, with positive cues from the West being somewhat offset by weakness in parts of Asia.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee depreciated against the US Dollar, with USD/INR rising by 0.41% to 95.78. The Dollar Index also saw a slight uptick. Crude oil prices witnessed a significant surge, with Brent Crude jumping 4.47% to $79.41 and WTI Crude rising 4.64% to $74.72, likely due to escalating US-Iran tensions. Gold and Silver prices, typically safe-haven assets, declined. The US 10-year Treasury yield increased by 0.66% to 4.57%.

Interpretation: A rising USD/INR is generally positive for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters but puts pressure on importers. The sharp increase in crude oil prices is a significant concern for India, a major oil importer, as it could fuel domestic inflation, put pressure on oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors, and potentially lead to higher interest rate expectations. Rising US 10-year yields could make emerging markets like India less attractive for foreign institutional investors, posing a risk to FII flows.

GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling

The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 24160.00, down by 0.17%.

Interpretation: This indicates a flat to slightly negative opening for the Nifty 50 on the NSE today, suggesting a cautious start to the trading session.

FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers in the Indian equity cash segment yesterday, infusing ₹2,604 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) also remained net buyers, adding ₹2,020 crore. Over the last five sessions, FIIs have largely been net buyers, with only one session of net selling, while DIIs have consistently been net buyers.

Stock & sector news that matters

TCS shares jump 3%, extend gains to day two after Q1 earnings. TCS shares continued their upward trend after reporting a 5% YoY rise in Q1 FY27 net profit and announcing an interim dividend of ₹12 per share. Why it matters: This positive momentum for TCS, a bellwether IT stock, could provide a tailwind for the broader IT sector. Strong results from a major player often set the tone for sector sentiment, especially as other IT companies like HCL Tech and LTM are also in focus for their Q1 performance.

SK Hynix warns of historic memory chip shortage by 2027 as AI demand soars. SK Hynix projects a severe global memory chip shortage extending beyond 2030, driven by accelerating artificial intelligence infrastructure demand. Why it matters: This global development highlights the immense demand for AI-related hardware. While not directly impacting Indian chip manufacturers (which are few), it signals strong tailwinds for global technology companies involved in AI and could influence sentiment for Indian IT firms that cater to these global tech giants.

Goldman Sachs pegs Nifty target at 26,500 by June 2027. Goldman Sachs has turned more constructive on Indian equities, forecasting the Nifty 50 to rebound to 26,500 by June 2027, citing an improving macro backdrop and easing foreign selling. Why it matters: A positive long-term outlook from a major global investment bank like Goldman Sachs can boost investor confidence and attract further FII inflows into Indian markets, providing underlying support and potentially influencing long-term investment strategies.

India bonds fall as oil spike, Treasury rout weigh. Indian government bonds declined early Monday, mirroring U.S. Treasury selloffs, as escalating U.S.-Iran tensions boosted oil prices and raised inflation concerns for India. Why it matters: Rising bond yields in India indicate higher borrowing costs for the government and corporations. This can impact interest-rate sensitive sectors like banking and real estate, and signals increased inflation expectations, which could influence the RBI's monetary policy stance.

US Stock Market: Inflation gathers pace as tariffs, energy costs and AI investment lift price pressures, says Fed. U.S. inflation accelerated during spring due to tariffs and rising energy costs, with the Federal Reserve reporting inflation significantly above its target. Why it matters: US inflation data is crucial for global markets. Higher-than-expected inflation could lead the US Federal Reserve to maintain a hawkish stance, potentially strengthening the Dollar and increasing US bond yields, which could in turn trigger FII outflows from emerging markets like India.

Q1 surprise sends jewellery stocks shining 40% in a month. Indian jewellery stocks saw a 40% surge after strong June quarter business updates, driven by resilient demand and a shift to organized players. Why it matters: This highlights strong sectoral momentum for jewellery companies, indicating robust consumer demand and a potential shift towards branded players. Companies like Titan could see continued investor interest if this trend persists.

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  • US Inflation Data (this week): Markets typically react with high volatility to US inflation prints. Higher-than-expected inflation can lead to fears of tighter monetary policy from the Federal Reserve, potentially causing global equity markets to correct and the dollar to strengthen. Conversely, softer inflation could lead to a relief rally.
  • Middle East Tensions (ongoing): Geopolitical developments, especially those involving major oil-producing regions, can significantly impact crude oil prices. Escalating tensions typically lead to a surge in oil prices, which is a negative for net oil importers like India, often resulting in inflationary pressures and a cautious market sentiment.
  • Global Central Bank Commentary: Any statements or indications from major central banks (like the US Fed) regarding their monetary policy outlook can influence global liquidity and interest rate expectations. Markets tend to react with adjustments in bond yields and currency movements, impacting FII flows.
  • Q1 FY27 Earnings Season: While no Nifty 500 results are scheduled today, the ongoing earnings season for other companies (like HCL Tech, LTM, Avantel, Just Dial) will continue to drive stock-specific and sectoral movements. Strong results tend to be rewarded, while weak performance can lead to corrections.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Crude Oil Prices: The sharp jump in Brent and WTI crude will be closely monitored for its implications on inflation and sectors like OMCs, paints, and aviation.
  2. USD/INR Movement: Continued depreciation of the Rupee could provide tailwinds for IT and pharma exporters but pressure importers.
  3. Nifty 50's 24000-24200 Range: Watch if Nifty can hold the 24000 Put OI support and its 20-DMA, or if it challenges the 24200-24250 Call OI resistance.
  4. FII/DII Flows: Continued buying by both institutional categories would provide crucial support to the market.
  5. Global Tech Sentiment: The SK Hynix warning about chip shortages and the performance of global tech stocks could influence Indian IT sector sentiment.
  6. Sectoral Action: Keep an eye on the IT sector following TCS results and upcoming HCL Tech earnings, as well as jewellery stocks given their recent rally.

Bottom line

Indian markets are likely to open with a cautious tone, influenced by mixed global cues and a slightly negative signal from GIFT Nifty. The significant surge in crude oil prices and accelerating US inflation are key headwinds that could weigh on sentiment and raise domestic inflation concerns. While strong FII and DII buying provides a strong domestic cushion, traders should closely monitor the Nifty's ability to hold the 24000 support level and watch for any further developments in global crude prices and geopolitical tensions.
