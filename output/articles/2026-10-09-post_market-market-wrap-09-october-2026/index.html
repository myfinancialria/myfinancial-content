What moved the market — the factors

Today's market rally was primarily driven by positive global cues and strong domestic buying by DIIs. US markets opened on a positive note, with oil prices dipping, which eased concerns over Middle Eastern supply issues. This provided a favourable backdrop for Indian equities. Domestically, despite persistent selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) to the tune of ₹12,944 crore, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) stepped in with substantial buying of ₹10,703 crore, effectively absorbing the FII outflows and providing crucial support to the market.

Sectoral rotation played a significant role, with the Information Technology (IT) sector leading the charge, gaining over 3%. This strong performance in IT, along with robust gains in FMCG and PSU Banks, helped push the benchmark indices higher. Individual heavyweights within these sectors likely contributed to the overall positive sentiment. The broad-based participation, as indicated by a healthy advance/decline ratio, further underscored the positive momentum.

F&O build-up — where traders are positioning

OI build-up needs the previous session's OI — it will populate from the next run. Data for today's F&O build-up is unavailable.

Interpretation:

  • Long build-up (bullish — price up, OI up): Indicates fresh buying interest with conviction, often suggesting a continuation of the upward trend.
  • Short build-up (bearish — price down, OI up): Points to fresh selling pressure, implying traders are initiating new short positions, anticipating further price declines.
  • Short covering (price up, OI down): Suggests that traders who had previously sold short are now buying back to close their positions, leading to a bounce in prices. This move may not always indicate underlying strength.
  • Long unwinding (price down, OI down): Implies that traders who held long positions are exiting them, either due to profit-taking or concerns about future weakness.
