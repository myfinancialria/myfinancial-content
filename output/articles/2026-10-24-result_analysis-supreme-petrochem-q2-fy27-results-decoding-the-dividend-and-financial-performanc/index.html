Supreme Petrochem Q2 FY27 Results: Decoding the Dividend and Financial Performance

TL;DR

  • Supreme Petrochem has announced a first interim dividend of ₹2.50 per share for the financial year 2026-27.
  • The company's revenue for Q2 FY27 surged by 77.8% year-on-year, reaching ₹1,956.7 crore.
  • Profit After Tax (PAT) for the same quarter nearly doubled, amounting to ₹94.9 crore.
  • October 30 has been fixed as the record date for this interim dividend, impacting who receives the payout.
  • These results reflect a strong operational performance in the quarter for the petrochemical manufacturer.

What this means in plain terms

When a company like Supreme Petrochem announces its quarterly results, it's essentially opening its books to show how well it performed financially over the past three months. For Q2 of the financial year 2026-27, the company has reported significant growth in both its sales (revenue) and its profits. This indicates that the business is expanding and managing its operations effectively, leading to more money coming in and more remaining after expenses and taxes.

Alongside these financial numbers, the company has also declared an "interim dividend." A dividend is a portion of a company's profits that it distributes to its shareholders. An interim dividend means it's paid out before the full financial year's results are finalised, often reflecting confidence in the company's ongoing performance. The declared amount of ₹2.50 per share, coupled with a specific "record date," tells investors exactly how much they will receive per share they own, provided they hold the shares by that crucial date.

Q2 FY27 Performance: A Closer Look

Supreme Petrochem has demonstrated a robust financial performance in the second quarter of the financial year 2026-27. The company's revenue from operations saw a substantial increase, rising by 77.8% compared to the same quarter in the previous financial year. This translated into a top-line figure of ₹1,956.7 crore. Such a significant jump in revenue suggests strong demand for the company's products, effective pricing strategies, or an increase in sales volumes, or a combination of these factors.

Equally impressive is the growth in the company's profitability. The Profit After Tax (PAT) for Q2 FY27 nearly doubled, reaching ₹94.9 crore. This indicates that not only did the company generate more sales, but it also managed its costs efficiently, allowing a larger portion of its revenue to convert into net profit. This dual growth in both revenue and profitability is a healthy sign, reflecting operational leverage and potentially improved market conditions for the petrochemical sector.

Understanding the Revenue Surge

The 77.8% year-on-year revenue growth to ₹1,956.7 crore is a key highlight. In the petrochemical industry, revenue can be influenced by several factors including global crude oil prices (which impact raw material costs and product pricing), demand from end-user industries like automotive, construction, and packaging, and the company's own production capacities and market share. A growth of this magnitude suggests that Supreme Petrochem has either capitalised on favourable market dynamics or gained significant competitive ground.

Decoding Profitability

The near doubling of Profit After Tax to ₹94.9 crore is particularly noteworthy. This implies that the company's operating margins have either expanded or remained strong despite the revenue growth. Often, rapid revenue growth can sometimes come at the cost of squeezed margins if input costs rise disproportionately. However, in Supreme Petrochem's case, the strong PAT growth suggests that the company has maintained or improved its efficiency in converting sales into profits, which is a positive indicator for investors.

Dividend Announcement and Implications

For its shareholders, Supreme Petrochem has announced a first interim dividend of ₹2.50 per equity share for the financial year 2026-27. This is a direct return to shareholders from the company's profits and signals management's confidence in the company's ongoing financial health and future earnings potential.

What an Interim Dividend Means

An interim dividend is declared and paid by a company before the full year's financial results are finalised. It's distinct from a final dividend, which is typically declared after the annual results and approved at the Annual General Meeting (AGM). The decision to pay an interim dividend often reflects a strong performance in the initial quarters of the financial year, as seen with Supreme Petrochem's Q2 results.

The Significance of the Record Date

The company has set October 30 as the record date for this interim dividend. The record date is a crucial cut-off date used to determine which shareholders are eligible to receive the dividend. To be eligible, an investor must hold shares of Supreme Petrochem in their demat account by the end of this specific date. Shares bought on or after the ex-dividend date (which typically precedes the record date by one or two business days, depending on the settlement cycle) will not be eligible for this particular dividend payout.

Tax Implications for Dividend Income

In India, dividends received from domestic companies are taxable in the hands of the shareholder. For individuals, dividend income is added to their total income and taxed at their applicable income tax slab rates. It's important for investors to keep track of their dividend income for tax filing purposes.

Petrochemical Industry Context and Outlook

Supreme Petrochem operates within the broader petrochemical industry, which is a foundational sector providing raw materials to a vast array of downstream industries. These include plastics, packaging, automotive components, construction materials, textiles, and consumer goods. The performance of petrochemical companies is often closely linked to global economic growth, crude oil price trends, and the demand-supply dynamics of specific petrochemical products.

Global and Domestic Demand Drivers

The demand for petrochemical products in India continues to be robust, driven by the country's economic growth, increasing urbanisation, and rising consumption patterns. Sectors like packaging, which relies heavily on polymers, and the automotive industry, which uses plastics extensively, are significant consumers. Globally, while there can be fluctuations, the long-term trend for petrochemical demand generally remains positive, especially in developing economies.

Raw Material Volatility and Margins

A key challenge and opportunity in the petrochemical sector is the volatility of raw material prices, primarily crude oil and its derivatives. Fluctuations in these prices can significantly impact production costs and, consequently, profit margins. Companies that can efficiently manage their raw material procurement, possess integrated facilities, or have strong pricing power are better positioned to navigate such volatility. Supreme Petrochem's strong Q2 performance suggests it has managed these dynamics effectively during the reported period.

Future Outlook

Looking ahead, the outlook for the petrochemical industry will depend on several factors, including global economic stability, crude oil price trajectories, and environmental regulations promoting sustainability and recycling. Companies focusing on speciality chemicals, value-added products, and sustainable practices may find an edge. For Supreme Petrochem, continued strong demand from its end-user segments and efficient operational management will be key to sustaining its growth trajectory.

A real example

Let's consider Anjali, 42, ₹35L CTC, Mumbai, a salaried professional who has invested in Supreme Petrochem. Anjali owns 1,500 shares of Supreme Petrochem.

When Supreme Petrochem declared a first interim dividend of ₹2.50 per share for FY27, Anjali, being a shareholder, became eligible for this payout.

Here's how her dividend income would be calculated:

  • Number of shares held by Anjali: 1,500 shares
  • Dividend per share: ₹2.50
  • Total dividend income for Anjali: 1,500 shares * ₹2.50/share = ₹3,750

Since the record date for this dividend is October 30, Anjali needs to ensure her shares are in her demat account by this date to receive the dividend. The payment would typically be credited directly to her bank account linked to her demat account within a few weeks of the record date.

From a tax perspective, this ₹3,750 will be added to Anjali's total income for the financial year. Assuming Anjali falls into the 30% tax bracket (plus cess and surcharge, if applicable), a portion of this dividend income would be subject to income tax. For instance, if her effective tax rate is 31.2% (including cess), the tax payable on this dividend would be approximately ₹3,750 * 31.2% = ₹1,170. This highlights the importance of considering tax implications when evaluating dividend income.

FAQ

What is an interim dividend?

An interim dividend is a dividend paid by a company to its shareholders before the full financial year's results are finalised and before the Annual General Meeting. It usually reflects strong performance in the initial quarters.

What is a record date for a dividend?

The record date is a specific date set by a company to determine which shareholders are eligible to receive the declared dividend. Only those shareholders whose names appear in the company's register by the end of the record date will receive the dividend.

How is dividend income taxed in India?

In India, dividend income received from domestic companies is fully taxable in the hands of the shareholder. It is added to your total income and taxed at your applicable income tax slab rates.

What does "revenue rose 77.8% year-on-year" mean?

This means the company's total sales or income for the current quarter (Q2 FY27) increased by 77.8% compared to its total sales in the same quarter of the previous financial year (Q2 FY26). It indicates significant growth in business operations.

How can I find detailed corporate results?

Detailed corporate results, including financial statements and management commentary, are typically available on the company's investor relations section of its official website and on the websites of stock exchanges (BSE, NSE) where the company is listed.

What is the significance of "PAT nearly doubled"?

"PAT nearly doubled" means the company's Profit After Tax (net profit) for the quarter almost became twice as much as it was in the same quarter last year. This indicates strong profitability growth, often due to higher sales, better cost management, or both.
