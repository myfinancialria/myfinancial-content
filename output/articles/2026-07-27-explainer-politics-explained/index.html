TL;DR

  • The Haryana government is introducing a new policy to significantly boost Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) and exports.
  • A major highlight is the plan to triple the export freight subsidy limit from ₹10 lakh to ₹30 lakh per unit per year.
  • The policy aims for faster government approvals, including land feasibility certificates within 45 days.
  • It promises quicker release of incentives, with 50% in seven days and the rest within 45 days, plus interest for delays.
  • An expanded insurance cover under the 'Mukhyamantri Vyapari Kshatipurti Yojana' will protect small traders from fire, theft, and natural disasters.

What's this about?

The Haryana government is set to introduce a new "Progressive MSME and Export Promotion Policy 2026" aimed at strengthening its Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) and boosting the state's exports. MSMEs are small businesses that form the backbone of India's economy, employing many people and producing a wide range of goods and services. This new policy is designed to make it easier for these businesses to operate, grow, and sell their products internationally.

The policy includes several key measures, such as increasing financial support for exporters, speeding up administrative processes, and providing better protection against business risks. The goal is to help Haryana's industries become more competitive and reach new heights, both within India and globally.

Why it's in the news right now

Haryana Chief Minister Nayab Singh Saini announced the draft of this new MSME and Export Promotion Policy on Saturday, July 25, 2026. He shared these details while interacting with representatives from the plywood industry in Yamunanagar, a region known as India's plywood capital. The policy is currently awaiting cabinet approval, making it a current and significant development for businesses and the economy in Haryana.

How it actually works

  1. Tripled Export Freight Subsidy: The policy proposes to significantly increase the financial support for MSMEs that export their goods. The limit for export freight subsidy will rise from ₹10 lakh to ₹30 lakh per unit per year. This subsidy helps cover the costs of transporting goods to international markets, making exports more affordable for small businesses.
  2. Faster Land Approvals: To improve the 'ease of doing business', the government plans to issue land feasibility certificates for industrial plots within 45 working days. This speeds up the process for businesses looking to set up or expand their operations.
  3. Quicker Incentive Release: When MSMEs apply for government incentives, 50% of the eligible amount will be released within seven working days after an initial check. The remaining amount will be paid within 45 days after a detailed verification. If there's a delay from the industries department in releasing these incentives, entrepreneurs will receive 8% interest per year on the delayed payment.
  4. Expanded Insurance Cover: The 'Mukhyamantri Vyapari Kshatipurti Yojana' will be widened to offer more affordable insurance products. This expanded cover is designed to protect small traders and MSMEs against financial losses caused by events like fire, theft, and natural disasters.
  5. 'Udaan Dwar Portal' for Exporters: Industries that export more than 80% of their production will receive special recognition on a new online platform called the 'Udaan Dwar Portal'. This portal is expected to help these high-performing exporters connect more easily with international buyers.
  6. SAKSHAM Fund for Industrial Development: The state has created a special fund called SAKSHAM, with an initial allocation of ₹500 crore in the Budget 2026-27. This fund is dedicated to rejuvenating existing industrial areas and developing them into world-class industrial hubs.
  7. Land-on-Lease Policy: To address the high cost of industrial land, the government has introduced a "land-on-lease" policy. Under this, industrial plots will be provided on a long-term lease, with an option for businesses to convert them into freehold (full ownership) when needed.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine you run a small pickle business from your home in Haryana, and you want to sell your delicious pickles to a supermarket chain in Dubai. The Haryana government's new policy is like a helpful relative who offers to pay a big chunk of the shipping cost (tripled export freight subsidy). They also help you quickly get permission to build a small factory (faster land approvals) and give you some money upfront for new equipment, promising the rest soon, with interest if they're late (quicker incentive release). Plus, they offer insurance in case your factory catches fire or your pickles get stolen (expanded insurance cover). They even put your business on a special website to help you find more international buyers (Udaan Dwar Portal). All these steps make it much easier and less risky for your small pickle business to grow and reach customers far away.

Why it matters to you

  1. For MSME Owners and Entrepreneurs: If you run a small business in Haryana, especially one involved in exports, this policy offers direct financial relief and operational advantages. The increased freight subsidy can significantly reduce your export costs, making your products more competitive globally. Faster approvals mean less waiting time and quicker project implementation.
  2. For Employees of MSMEs: As MSMEs grow and become more profitable due to these incentives, there's a higher chance of job creation, better wages, and improved working conditions. This translates to more stable livelihoods for many families in Haryana.
  3. For Consumers in Haryana: A thriving MSME sector means more local production, potentially leading to a wider variety of goods and services available in the market. While not a direct price reduction, increased competition and efficiency can benefit consumers indirectly.
  4. For Local Economies: The SAKSHAM fund and the focus on developing industrial hubs can lead to overall economic growth in specific regions, attracting more investment and improving local infrastructure. Areas like Yamunanagar, with its plywood industry, are expected to see significant benefits.
  5. For Aspiring Business Owners: The 'ease of doing business' measures, such as faster land approvals and clearer incentive processes, can lower the entry barriers for new entrepreneurs, encouraging more people to start their own ventures.

FAQ

What is an MSME?

MSME stands for Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises. These are small businesses classified based on their investment in plant and machinery or equipment, and their annual turnover. They play a vital role in India's economy by creating jobs and contributing to production.

Which types of businesses will benefit most from this policy?

Businesses in Haryana that are involved in manufacturing and exporting goods are likely to benefit most, especially those looking to expand their international presence. The policy also supports small traders through expanded insurance.

When will this policy officially start?

The "Progressive MSME and Export Promotion Policy 2026" is currently in its draft stage and awaiting cabinet approval from the Haryana government. Once approved, it will be officially launched.

How can an MSME apply for these benefits?

Specific details on the application process for subsidies, incentives, and portal registration will be released once the policy is officially unveiled. It is expected to involve applications through relevant government departments or dedicated online portals.

Is this policy only for new businesses?

No, the policy aims to support both existing MSMEs looking to grow and new entrepreneurs setting up businesses in Haryana. Measures like the SAKSHAM fund for industrial area rejuvenation and the land-on-lease policy can benefit both established and new ventures.
