Pre-Market Brief — 03 July 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets closed mixed, with US indices split and Asian/European markets largely positive.
  • Indian Rupee strengthened slightly against the US Dollar, while crude oil and gold prices saw gains.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net buyers.
  • A significant number of companies, including Tech Mahindra, M&M, and Biocon, are trading ex-dividend today.
  • Power equipment stocks may face pressure following reports of a two-year exemption for Chinese firms in government tenders.

Where global markets closed

Global markets presented a mixed picture as they closed yesterday. In the US, the Dow Jones Industrial Average posted a gain of 1.14%, while the Nasdaq Composite saw a decline of 0.80%. The S&P 500 remained flat, closing with no change. Across the Atlantic, the FTSE 100 in the UK rose by 1.67%. Asian markets showed strength, with Japan's Nikkei 225 advancing by 1.33%, Hong Kong's Hang Seng gaining 1.23%, and China's Shanghai Composite up by 0.94%. The overall global tone appears to be cautiously optimistic, with strong performances in most Asian and European markets offsetting the mixed sentiment from the US.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee strengthened marginally against the US Dollar, with USD/INR trading at ₹95.26, down 0.16%. This move aligns with a broader weakening of the Dollar Index, which also declined by 0.14% to 100.72. In the commodities market, crude oil prices edged higher, with Brent Crude up 0.81% to $72.38 and WTI Crude gaining 0.67% to $69.15. Rising crude prices could pose an inflationary risk and impact India's import bill. Gold saw a significant jump of 1.72% to $4183.30, while Silver surged by 3.66% to $62.87, indicating increased safe-haven demand amidst global uncertainties. Recent FII/DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued to be net sellers in the Indian equity markets yesterday, offloading equities worth ₹312 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained strong buyers, injecting ₹1,784 crore into the market. Over the last five trading sessions, FIIs have been net sellers in four out of five sessions, while DIIs have consistently been net buyers, absorbing the FII outflows.

What to watch in today's session

  1. FII/DII Flows: Continue to monitor the trend of FII selling versus DII buying. Sustained DII support will be crucial in absorbing foreign outflows.
  2. Corporate Actions Impact: With a large number of companies going ex-dividend and two companies undergoing stock splits, expect stock-specific movements and potential adjustments in prices for these scrips.
  3. Power Equipment Sector Performance: The news regarding Chinese firms receiving tender exemptions could significantly impact domestic power equipment manufacturers. Watch for sharp reactions in stocks like Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy, and others.
  4. FMCG and IT Sector Sentiment: With the Q1 earnings season kicking off, expert views on FMCG outperformance and IT sector headwinds will guide investor sentiment and potentially lead to sectoral rotation.
  5. Crude Oil Prices: The uptick in Brent and WTI crude prices needs to be watched closely as sustained increases could impact inflation expectations and the profitability of oil marketing companies.

Bottom line

Indian markets are likely to open with mixed cues, influenced by a split performance in US markets and largely positive Asian and European closes. The persistent FII selling pressure continues to be offset by robust DII buying, providing a floor to the market. Today's session will see significant stock-specific action due to a high number of corporate actions, particularly dividends, and the negative news impacting the power equipment sector. Investors should remain cautious and focus on individual stock and sector-specific developments.
