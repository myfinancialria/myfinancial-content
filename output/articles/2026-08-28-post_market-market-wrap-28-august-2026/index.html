  • The Indian benchmark Nifty 50 ended the day with modest gains, while Bank Nifty remained largely flat, reflecting a cautious yet resilient market.

How the market behaved today

The Indian equity market saw a mixed performance today, with the Nifty 50 managing to close in positive territory, albeit with modest gains, while the Bank Nifty remained largely unchanged. The Nifty 50 opened cautiously but found support to trend upwards, closing near its day's high at 24175.65, up 0.35%. In contrast, the Bank Nifty saw some volatility but ultimately ended almost flat at 57496.30, down a marginal 0.02%. Broader markets, represented by the Nifty Midcap 100 and Nifty Smallcap 100, also posted slight gains.

What moved the market — the factors

Today's market action was a tug-of-war between cautious global sentiment and robust domestic institutional buying. The primary global driver was the hawkish stance reiterated by US Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh at the Jackson Hole symposium. Warsh's comments, emphasizing the central bank's commitment to bringing inflation down to 2%, fueled expectations of a potential September rate hike and led to a sell-off in US stocks and a rise in US Treasury yields. This created an initial cautious tone for Indian markets.

However, the Indian market showed resilience, largely due to strong buying by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), who pumped in nearly ₹4,977 crore. This countered the FII outflow of ₹298 crore. Sector-wise, the Information Technology (IT) sector was the standout performer, surging over 3.5%, likely benefiting from a weaker rupee (though USD/INR was down today, the broader dollar strength could be a factor) and renewed interest. Metals and Pharma also contributed positively. On the other hand, defensive sectors like FMCG, along with Energy and Auto, faced selling pressure. Individual stock movements, such as Ather Energy's jump following Hero MotoCorp's increased stake, also provided specific pockets of strength.

News that moved stocks

Global Central Bank Hawkishness Dominates Sentiment US Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh delivered hawkish comments at the Jackson Hole symposium, stating that elevated inflation was "concerning" and reaffirming the central bank's commitment to bringing it down to 2%. This led to a sell-off in US stocks, a rise in US Treasury yields, and a stronger dollar, raising expectations for a potential September rate hike and creating a cautious mood globally. The Nifty faces potential pressure on Monday following these comments.

Hero MotoCorp Deepens Stake in Ather Energy Hero MotoCorp acquired an additional 1.18 crore shares of Ather Energy for ₹1,758 crore in a block deal, significantly increasing its holding in the electric two-wheeler manufacturer. This news led to a strong surge in Ather Energy's share price.

SEBI Mulls Bigger Institutional Role in Small Firm IPOs India's market regulator, SEBI, is reportedly considering new rules for public offerings by small businesses, potentially including quotas for institutional investors ahead of share sales. These changes aim to enhance the robustness and investor confidence in SME IPOs.

Institutional Activity in Lenskart and Knowledge Realty Trust Lenskart Solutions witnessed institutional transactions totaling ₹1,857 crore, with major domestic and global investors like Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, ICICI Prudential, and SBI MF purchasing shares as Alpha Wave exited. Separately, Blackstone plans to sell up to a 25.03% stake, worth ₹11,988 crore, in Knowledge Realty Trust to meet regulatory public unitholding requirements.

SEBI Action Against Misleading IPO Disclosures SEBI has banned Trafiksol ITS and its promoters from the securities market for one year and imposed monetary penalties due to misleading financial disclosures during its IPO. This action underscores the regulator's vigilance against fraudulent practices in public offerings.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

The dominant event impacting market sentiment today was the ongoing Jackson Hole Economic Symposium, specifically the hawkish comments from US Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh. Warsh's firm stance on tackling inflation, indicating that further action might be necessary, led to a surge in US Treasury yields and a stronger dollar globally. This global development created an initial cautious undertone for the Indian market, as higher US rates typically make emerging markets less attractive for foreign capital.

While global cues suggested a negative opening and potential pressure, the Indian market showed resilience. This was largely attributed to the strong buying by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), who absorbed the FII selling and provided crucial support. The IT sector, in particular, reacted positively, possibly benefiting from the global dollar strength. However, the broader market breadth indicated that many stocks faced selling pressure, reflecting the underlying caution stemming from the global interest rate outlook.

Setup for tomorrow

The global landscape for tomorrow's session remains influenced by the hawkish tone from the US Federal Reserve. US markets ended lower, and US Treasury yields rose following Fed Chair Warsh's comments, which could weigh on Asian markets. The Dollar Index strengthened, while crude oil and gold prices saw declines. Investors will be closely watching for any further commentary from global central bankers and how these developments translate into FII flows. Key levels for Nifty 50 will be around today's high of 24188 and support at 24076.85. Sectors like IT might continue to see interest, while rate-sensitive sectors could remain under observation.
