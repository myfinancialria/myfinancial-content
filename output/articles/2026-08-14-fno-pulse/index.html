Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides insights into market sentiment and potential support or resistance levels.

The strike price with the highest Call Open Interest typically indicates a strong resistance level, as many option writers (sellers) expect the market to stay below this point. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put Open Interest often acts as a significant support level, where put writers believe the market will not fall below.

For the Nifty 50, today saw the heaviest fresh Call writing, meaning new call options were sold, concentrated near the 24,483 strike. This suggests that call sellers anticipate the index might find it challenging to move significantly above this level. On the other hand, the heaviest fresh Put writing was observed near 24,216, indicating that put sellers are comfortable providing support around this level, expecting the index to hold above it. The aggregate flow showed a significantly larger build-up in Put OI (17.6 million contracts) compared to Call OI (7.4 million contracts) today, which often signals underlying bullish sentiment as more participants are willing to sell puts, expecting prices to rise or remain stable.

In Sensex, the heaviest Put Open Interest is currently positioned at 78,000, which is right at the spot price, suggesting a strong immediate floor. Fresh Put writing was notably heavy near 76,833, reinforcing the expectation of support at lower levels.

For Bank Nifty, the highest Call Open Interest is concentrated at the 58,000 strike, which is also where the heaviest Put Open Interest resides. This creates a rather tight implied range, with 58,000 acting as both a potential ceiling and a floor. Today's aggregate flow showed a net build in Call OI of 1.2 million contracts, with negligible Put OI build, indicating a call-heavy bias in fresh positions.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value generally above 1.0 is often considered bullish, suggesting more puts are being written (expecting prices to rise), while a value below 1.0 is typically seen as bearish, with more calls being written (expecting prices to fall).

For Nifty 50, the overall PCR based on Open Interest stands at 0.86, which is a balanced book, not strongly bullish or bearish. However, the PCR based on today's fresh flow (ΔOI) is a significantly higher 2.39. This indicates that today's new option writing activity was heavily skewed towards puts, with many participants selling puts, which often suggests an expectation of stability or an upward move in the near term.

The Sensex saw its PCR(OI) at 1.04, indicating a fairly balanced options book. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR) was 1.27, suggesting a moderately balanced to slightly put-heavy fresh writing activity.

Bank Nifty's PCR(OI) is at 0.74, which is also a relatively balanced book. However, its PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR) was a very low 0.04. This extremely low figure suggests that today's fresh option writing was predominantly in calls, with very few new puts being written, which often points towards a cautious or bearish sentiment from option writers.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the underlying asset expires at that price. It's often considered a "magnet" because the underlying index or stock tends to gravitate towards this level as the expiry date approaches. The closer the expiry, the stronger this magnetic pull can become.

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain level is at 24,500, which is approximately 0.5% above its current spot price. This suggests that option writers are positioned for a potential slight upward movement or expiry around this higher level.

The Sensex Max Pain is precisely at 78,000, which is effectively at its current spot price. This indicates a balanced expectation from option writers, with no strong bias for a significant move up or down by expiry.

Bank Nifty's Max Pain is at 58,000, about 0.9% above its current spot price. Similar to Nifty, this suggests option writers might be anticipating the index to gravitate towards a slightly higher level by expiry.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to form your own informed perspective. You can start by visiting the NSE India website and looking for the "Option Chain" under the "Derivatives" section. Here, you'll find the Open Interest data for various strike prices.

Focus on identifying the strike prices with the highest Call Open Interest and highest Put Open Interest. These typically act as key resistance and support levels, respectively. Next, observe the "Change in Open Interest" (ΔOI) for the day. A significant increase in Call ΔOI at a particular strike suggests fresh call writing, indicating that sellers expect the price to stay below that level. Conversely, a large increase in Put ΔOI points to fresh put writing, implying sellers expect the price to hold above that level.

You can also calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI. Track the FII derivative positioning, which is usually available in a CSV file on the NSE archives, to see how institutional players are positioned in index and stock futures. By combining these data points, you can develop a more comprehensive understanding of the market's underlying sentiment and potential movements.
