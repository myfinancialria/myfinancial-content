How boAt Built a ₹4,000 Cr Audio Brand Using Cricketers Instead of TV Ads

The year was 2016. India’s audio accessories market was a chaotic landscape. On one side, you had global titans like Sony, JBL, and Bose, offering premium, often aspirational, but undeniably expensive products. On the other, a deluge of unbranded, flimsy Chinese imports flooded local markets and online platforms, breaking within weeks but costing a fraction. Caught between these two extremes were millions of young, aspirational Indians, armed with new smartphones but no affordable, stylish, and durable audio gear to truly "plug into nirvana." This glaring gap became the crucible for Aman Gupta and Sameer Mehta. They didn't have deep pockets or a legacy manufacturing setup. What they did have was an astute understanding of the Indian consumer psyche and a daring vision: to build a lifestyle audio brand that resonated with youth, not through costly TV commercials, but by tapping into the nation's twin obsessions – Bollywood and, more powerfully, cricket.

The World Before boAt

Before boAt entered the fray, the Indian audio market was a tale of two cities. Global players like Apple, JBL, Sony, and Sennheiser dominated the premium and sub-premium segments, with headphones often priced above ₹5,000, stretching to ₹20,000 or more for ultra-premium models. These brands commanded shelf space and advertising budgets, but their offerings often felt distant and inaccessible to the average Indian consumer who sought value and style. Conversely, the affordable segment, typically under ₹5,000, was a wild west of unorganized players and cheap imports. These products, while easy on the wallet, often compromised severely on quality, durability, and aesthetics, leading to frustratingly short lifespans.

There was no credible "middle ground"—a brand that offered quality sound, contemporary design, and robust build at prices that wouldn't break the bank for a college student or a young professional. Many believed that building a reputable hardware brand in India, especially against established international giants, was an impossible task. The prevailing wisdom dictated that you either competed on ultra-low prices with no-name products or invested heavily in traditional advertising to build trust, a costly affair that many startups couldn't afford. The direct-to-consumer (D2C) model, heavily reliant on online sales and digital marketing, was still nascent, and the power of influencer marketing was largely underestimated by incumbents.

The Crack

Aman Gupta, with his background in sales and marketing at Harman International (JBL's parent company), had a front-row seat to this market dynamic. He realised that Indian consumers wanted premium-looking products without the premium price tag. Along with co-founder Sameer Mehta, boAt was launched in 2016, initially with a modest capital of ₹30 lakhs from their own savings. Their very first products weren't even headphones, but tough, tangle-free charging cables, addressing a common pain point for smartphone users, especially those with iPhones. This initial success in a seemingly mundane category provided crucial insights into consumer electronics manufacturing and distribution.

The real game-changer came when boAt shifted its focus to audio accessories – earphones and headphones – with a clear value proposition: stylish, durable audio at an affordable price point, typically ranging from ₹1,000 to ₹5,000. They weren't trying to out-compete Sony on audiophile-grade sound; they focused on delivering "good-enough audio that looked cool" and resonated with the youth.

Their marketing strategy was revolutionary for its time, and a stark departure from the norms set by established players. Instead of splurging on expensive TV advertisements, boAt went "digital-first," allocating a significant portion of its marketing budget to online platforms like Instagram, YouTube, and TikTok. They understood that their target audience – Gen Z and millennials – lived online.

The true genius lay in their strategic use of celebrity endorsements, particularly with cricketers. India's fervent passion for cricket provided an unparalleled platform to connect with millions. Instead of signing a few A-list celebrities for astronomical sums, boAt opted for a wider net of popular cricketers like Hardik Pandya, K.L. Rahul, Shreyas Iyer, Shikhar Dhawan, Rishabh Pant, and Jasprit Bumrah. These endorsements were often "micro-priced" in comparison to mainstream TV ad deals, offering significant reach at a much lower customer acquisition cost (CAC). Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) refers to the cost associated with convincing a prospective customer to buy a product or service. By aligning with cricketers and social media influencers, boAt could achieve high engagement directly with their target demographic without the massive overhead of traditional media.

Aman Gupta himself articulated this philosophy: "We partner with iconic cricketers, Bollywood stars, and rising music artists to connect with our audience on a deeper level." They also roped in Bollywood stars like Kiara Advani, Kartik Aaryan, Jacqueline Fernandez, and Ranveer Singh, effectively blending sports and entertainment to create a lifestyle brand. This approach transformed boAt from a mere electronics seller into a "cool lifestyle" brand, fostering a loyal community they called "boAtheads." Their products were even showcased at Lakmé Fashion Week, positioning them as fashion accessories rather than just gadgets.

This disruptive strategy paid off handsomely. In its first full financial year (FY18), boAt's revenue reached ₹108 crore. By FY2020, revenue had soared to ₹701 crore. More importantly, by December 2020, boAt had overtaken major brands like Xiaomi, Realme, JBL, and Apple to become India's #1 audio wearables brand, according to Counterpoint Research. Globally, boAt also made its mark, becoming the fifth largest wearable brand worldwide in Q3 2020.

What Happened Next

boAt's meteoric rise continued, solidifying its position as a dominant player in the Indian audio and wearables market. The company saw hyper-growth between 2020 and 2022, fueled by increased demand for personal audio during the pandemic. Revenue quadrupled in just two years. By FY23, boAt's revenue touched ₹3,403 crore. While FY24 saw a slight dip to ₹3,122 crore, and FY25 recorded ₹3,097.81 crore, the brand maintained massive scale.

In terms of market share, boAt continued to dominate. In Q2 2024, boAt held a 26.7% market share in India's wearables space, specifically leading the "earwear" segment, ahead of both local and global competitors. As of early 2025, boAt commanded approximately 25-28% share in India's TWS (True Wireless Stereo) and neckband segments, ranking #1 nationally in earwear and among the top three in smartwatches with around 18% share. Globally, boAt made significant strides, becoming the second-largest wearable brand by shipment volume in Q3 2023, surpassing Xiaomi and Samsung, and ranking only behind Apple.

The company’s valuation reflected this growth. Its latest valuation stands at approximately ₹7,752 crore (around $955 million). boAt also set its sights on a public listing, initially filing for a ₹2,000 crore IPO in January 2022. This plan was later revised, and an updated draft red herring prospectus (UDRHP) was filed in May 2026 for a leaner ₹1,500 crore IPO, comprising a fresh issue of ₹500 crore and an offer for sale of ₹1,000 crore by existing investors. The decision to trim the IPO size reflected a more measured approach in a changed market environment and a focus on profitability.

After recording losses in FY23 (₹129.5 crore) and FY24 (₹79.7 crore), boAt made a significant comeback to profitability in FY25, achieving a consolidated net profit of ₹60 crore. This turnaround was attributed to disciplined cost management, product innovation, and a strong push towards localization, with over 70% of production volumes manufactured domestically by FY25.

Lessons to Take Away

  1. Understand the "Sweet Spot" in a Segmented Market: boAt didn't try to be the cheapest or the most premium. It identified a critical unmet need for affordable, stylish, and durable audio products for young, price-sensitive but aspirational Indian consumers. This "value-for-money" positioning carved out a massive segment that incumbents had overlooked.
  2. Digital-First & Hyper-Targeted Marketing Over Mass Media: By prioritizing digital platforms and influencer marketing, especially with cricketers and Bollywood personalities, boAt achieved high engagement with its target audience efficiently. This allowed them to bypass the prohibitive costs of traditional TV advertising, demonstrating that effective marketing isn't about the size of the budget, but the precision of its deployment.
  3. Build a Lifestyle, Not Just a Product: boAt successfully transformed functional audio devices into fashion statements and lifestyle accessories. By associating with youth culture, music, and sports, they created a brand identity that consumers wanted to be a part of, fostering a sense of community ("boAtheads") that drove loyalty and word-of-mouth.
  4. Agility and Responsiveness to Consumer Needs: From starting with charging cables to rapidly expanding into a wide range of audio and wearable products, boAt demonstrated an ability to quickly identify and fill market gaps. Their continuous product innovation, incorporating features demanded by Indian youth (like punchy bass and rugged designs), kept them relevant and ahead of the curve.
