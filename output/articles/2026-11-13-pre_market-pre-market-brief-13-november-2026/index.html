Pre-Market Brief — 13 November 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets closed mixed to negative, with US indices down and Asian markets showing a mixed trend.
  • GIFT Nifty is signalling a negative bias, pointing to a potential gap-down open for the Indian market.
  • FIIs continue their strong buying streak, while DIIs remain net sellers, supporting the market from the other side.
  • MSCI's index rejig is a significant event, potentially boosting India's representation and driving inflows into specific stocks.
  • Key Nifty technical levels are unavailable today, suggesting traders will need to rely on live price action.
  • Rising Indian bond yields and global interest rate concerns, reflected in the US 10Y yield, pose the main risk.

In Asia this morning, the picture is mixed, with Japan's Nikkei 225 showing gains, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng and China's Shanghai Composite are trading in negative territory.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) weakened against the US Dollar, trading at ₹94.25, a rise of 0.58%. The Dollar Index saw a marginal increase of 0.02% to 103.19. In commodities, Brent Crude was up slightly by 0.14% to $95.95, while WTI Crude dipped by 0.25% to $88.52. Gold prices edged higher by 0.47% to $3903.70, and Silver also saw a small gain of 0.09% to $50.01. A significant move was seen in the US 10-Year Yield, which climbed by 2.10% to 5.10%.

Interpretation: A weakening Rupee (USD/INR up) could provide a tailwind for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters, as their dollar earnings translate to more rupees. However, it also makes imports more expensive, potentially pressuring sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs) and those reliant on imported raw materials. The rise in Brent crude, even if slight, is a concern for India, a net oil importer, and could impact inflation and the current account deficit. The notable increase in the US 10-Year Yield indicates rising global interest rate expectations, which can make emerging markets like India less attractive for foreign capital, potentially leading to FII outflow risks.

Interpretation: This indicates a negative bias for the Indian market open, suggesting Nifty could see a gap-down opening today, aligning with the cautious global sentiment.

FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were strong net buyers yesterday, pumping in ₹7,831 crore into the Indian equity markets. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), on the other hand, were net sellers, offloading equities worth ₹6,331 crore. This trend of FII buying and DII selling has been consistent over the last five sessions.

Stock & sector news that matters

  • MSCI rejig: Which stocks may see the biggest inflows & outflows?

    • MSCI has announced changes to its indices, which will impact several Indian stocks starting November 30, potentially increasing India's weightage in the MSCI Emerging Markets Index. Why it matters: This is a significant event for Indian equities. A higher weightage in MSCI indices typically leads to passive inflows from global funds tracking these indices, providing a structural tailwind for the Indian market and specific stocks that see increased inclusion or weight. Investors should watch out for the list of specific stocks impacted.

  • Carlsberg said to start road show for $600 million India IPO

    • Carlsberg is reportedly preparing for a potential $600 million IPO of its Indian business, aiming for a $4 billion valuation, with a possible launch as early as January 2027. Why it matters: This news indicates a potential major new listing in the Indian market, reflecting global investor interest in India's consumption story. It could also bring focus to the broader consumer and beverage sector.

  • India bonds under pressure before debt supply; hot inflation prints pinch further

    • Indian government bonds faced selling pressure ahead of a major auction, with yields on the 6.94% 2036 bond rising due to increasing inflation in India. Why it matters: Rising bond yields in India signal concerns about inflation and government borrowing. Higher yields can make equity investments relatively less attractive and increase borrowing costs for companies, potentially impacting interest-rate sensitive sectors like banking and real estate.

  • Cisco shares rally after earnings beat: 6 key things investors need to know

    • Cisco shares surged after reporting strong Q1 FY2027 results, exceeding analyst expectations, driven by robust demand for AI data-centre infrastructure. Why it matters: While a US-specific stock, Cisco's strong performance, particularly driven by AI data-centre demand, reflects positive sentiment in the global technology sector. This could provide some positive cues for Indian IT services and technology stocks, especially those with exposure to cloud and AI infrastructure.

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  • Indian Bond Auction & Inflation Data: The pressure on Indian government bonds due to upcoming debt supply and rising inflation prints will be closely watched. Markets typically react to higher inflation and bond yields with caution, as it can signal potential interest rate hikes by the RBI, impacting growth and corporate earnings.
  • Global Interest Rate Environment: The significant jump in the US 10-Year Yield highlights global concerns about inflation and central bank policies. Markets tend to become volatile when there are shifts in global interest rate expectations, as it influences FII flows and currency movements.
  • F&O Expiry (next week): While not today, the upcoming F&O expiry often brings increased volatility and short covering/unwinding as traders adjust their positions.

  1. GIFT Nifty's lead: The negative indication from GIFT Nifty suggests a cautious open.
  2. FII vs DII flows: The continued FII buying will be key to counter any DII selling and support the market.
  3. Bond market movements: Keep an eye on Indian bond yields; any further rise could dampen sentiment.
  4. MSCI rejig related stocks: While effective November 30, news flow around which specific Indian stocks are impacted could generate early interest.
  5. Corporate actions: Stocks going ex-dividend or ex-bonus today might see price adjustments and specific trading interest.
  6. Global cues: Continued monitoring of US and Asian market performance throughout the day will be important.

Bottom line

The Indian market is likely to open with a negative bias today, influenced by mixed global cues and a weak GIFT Nifty. While strong FII buying provides a fundamental support, rising Indian bond yields and global interest rate concerns could keep investors cautious. Without clear technical levels available, traders will need to rely on live price action and global developments to navigate the day.
