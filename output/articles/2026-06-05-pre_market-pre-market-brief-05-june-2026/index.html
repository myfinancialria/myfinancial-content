Pre-Market Brief — 05 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets presented a mixed picture, with US indices largely positive while Asian markets closed in the red.
  • The Indian Rupee strengthened significantly against the US Dollar, supported by RBI's policy decisions and FPI norm liberalisation.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, offloading ₹4,447 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support with net buying of ₹4,360 crore.
  • Several prominent companies, including Reliance, Cipla, HDFCAMC, and Bank of Baroda, have their shares trading ex-dividend today.
  • The government is reportedly considering selling a stake in Hindustan Zinc, potentially raising around ₹5,000 crore.

Where global markets closed

Global markets closed with a mixed performance on Thursday. US indices showed strength, with the Dow Jones gaining significantly by 1.73% and the S&P 500 rising by 0.41%. However, the Nasdaq saw a slight dip of 0.09%. European markets, represented by the FTSE 100, closed marginally higher by 0.27%. In contrast, Asian markets ended the session in negative territory, with the Nikkei 225 down 1.31%, Hang Seng falling 1.00%, and the Shanghai Composite declining by 0.72%. The overall global tone appears cautious, with a divergence between Western and Eastern markets.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) showed significant strength, appreciating by 0.72% to 95.47 against the US Dollar. This move was largely influenced by the RBI's policy decisions and liberalisation of FPI investment norms in government securities. The Dollar Index, which measures the dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies, saw a marginal decline of 0.05% to 99.36, indicating a slight weakening of the dollar globally.

In the commodities market, Brent Crude was down marginally by 0.03% to $95.00, while WTI Crude also saw a slight dip of 0.38% to $92.69. This minor softening in crude oil prices could offer some relief on the import bill front for India. Gold prices were nearly flat, down 0.04% to $4473.90, while Silver saw a more notable decline of 1.77% to $72.47. A slight dip in precious metals could be attributed to a shift towards equities in some parts of the global market. The US 10-Year Yield decreased by 0.31% to 4.48%, suggesting a slight easing in bond market concerns, which can be positive for growth-oriented assets.

Overnight news that matters

Government said to weigh up to $525 million Hindustan Zinc share sale The Indian government is reportedly considering selling up to a 2% stake in Hindustan Zinc Ltd., which could raise approximately ₹5,000 crore. This potential divestment, likely to occur in June or July, is part of the government's broader divestment program and could bring a fresh supply of shares to the market, impacting the stock price of Hindustan Zinc.

Rupee rises 50 paise to 95.24 against US dollar post RBI policy decision The Indian Rupee significantly strengthened against the US dollar, gaining 50 paise to reach 95.24. This appreciation followed the RBI's decision to liberalize FPI investment norms in government securities and the government's removal of taxes on government bonds for FIIs, aimed at spurring foreign inflows. A stronger rupee can help curb imported inflation and make imports cheaper for Indian businesses.

Go Digit Insurance shares jump 9% after Rs 100 crore block deal attracts MFs, global investor Shares of Go Digit General Insurance saw a notable 9% increase following a ₹100 crore block deal. Aditya Birla Sun Life Mutual Fund and JPMorgan Taiwan Eastern Technology Fund were among the investors who acquired shares. This indicates institutional confidence in the company and could provide positive momentum for the stock.

Adani Power shares gain 3%, snap two-day losing streak. Why are Jefferies, Bernstein bullish on the stock? Adani Power shares climbed 3%, ending a two-day losing streak, as analysts from Jefferies and Bernstein expressed bullish views on the stock. Their optimism stems from strong prospects for India's power demand and the booming data centre sector, where the Adani Group is seen as well-positioned to capitalize on growth. This news could bring focus to the power sector and Adani Group stocks.

Wipro plunges 8% after shares turn ex-record date for Rs 15,000 crore buyback Wipro shares experienced a sharp decline of 8% after trading ex-record date for its ₹15,000 crore buyback offer. While buybacks are generally seen as positive, the immediate drop is a common occurrence as the share price adjusts to reflect the buyback entitlement. Investors who bought the stock yesterday will not be eligible for the buyback.

What to watch in today's session

  1. FII/DII Flows: Continue to monitor the FII selling trend and DII buying support. Sustained FII outflows could keep broader market sentiment cautious.
  2. Rupee Movement: The significant strengthening of the Rupee post-RBI policy will be closely watched. Further appreciation could positively impact import-heavy sectors and reduce inflationary pressures.
  3. Hindustan Zinc Divestment: Any official announcement or further details regarding the government's potential stake sale in Hindustan Zinc could impact the stock and the broader metals sector.
  4. Corporate Actions Impact: Shares of companies like Reliance, Cipla, HDFCAMC, and Bank of Baroda will trade ex-dividend today. Wipro's share price movement post-buyback ex-date will also be in focus.
  5. Inflationary Impact on Earnings: Experts suggest the full impact of inflation might reflect in the June quarter earnings. Investors should watch for any early indicators or commentary from companies.

Bottom line

Indian markets are likely to open with a cautious but potentially positive bias, influenced by mixed global cues and a stronger Rupee. While FII selling remains a concern, robust DII support and positive news flow around specific stocks and sectors could provide some resilience. Investors should remain vigilant about global developments and the ongoing FII selling pressure.
