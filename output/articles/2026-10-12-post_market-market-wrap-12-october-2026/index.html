Market Wrap — 12 October 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed lower today, reversing early gains.
  • Persistent heavy selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) was a significant drag on the market.
  • Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100 indices, however, showed resilience, closing in the green.
  • Gallantt Ispat Ltd. was the standout gainer, surging over 18%, while Birlasoft Ltd. was among the top losers.
  • FIIs were net sellers for a fifth consecutive session, offloading ₹12,944 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support with net buying of ₹10,703 crore.
  • India VIX surged over 6%, indicating an increase in market volatility.

How the market behaved today

Indian benchmark indices witnessed a volatile session today, reversing early gains to close in the red. The Nifty 50 ended below the 22,400 mark, while Bank Nifty also saw declines. Midcap and Smallcap indices, however, bucked the trend, showing strength throughout the day.

What moved the market — the factors

The Indian market's downturn today was largely influenced by a combination of persistent selling pressure from Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and cautious global cues. FIIs continued their selling spree, offloading a significant ₹12,944 crore from the cash segment, which put considerable pressure on frontline indices like the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty. Global sentiment remained subdued, with concerns over the US Federal Reserve's hawkish stance, as Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack advocated for a strict monetary policy to combat inflation. Additionally, US bank stocks are nearing correction territory ahead of earnings, adding to the global unease. Domestically, while large caps faced headwinds, the Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100 indices displayed resilience, closing with gains, suggesting a rotation of capital into broader markets. The rupee, however, saw a significant rebound of nearly 1% against the dollar after the RBI intervened with measures to curb speculative currency trades, providing some relief on the currency front. Sectorally, FMCG, PSU Bank, and IT were the biggest drags, while Media, Auto, and Realty sectors showed strength.

News that moved stocks

  • RBI Intervenes to Stabilise Rupee: The Indian rupee rebounded by nearly 1% against the dollar after the RBI announced measures to curb speculative currency trades, easing pressure on the local currency. This move provided some stability and confidence in the currency market.
  • Hawkish Stance from US Fed: Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack reiterated the need for a restrictive monetary policy to address inflation, which could impact global interest rate expectations and investor sentiment.
  • SBI Economists Propose Scrapping LTCG Tax: Economists at SBI have called for the elimination of long-term capital gains tax on equities to encourage sustained investment in Indian markets, a proposal that could significantly boost investor participation if implemented.
  • Polycab India's F&O Exit and NCLAT Stay: NSE has set December 30, 2026, as the deadline for Polycab India's exit from the equity derivatives segment. Separately, the NCLAT has stayed an NCLT order admitting insolvency proceedings against the company.
  • Indian Gas Exchange Gets Sebi Nod for IPO: Indian Gas Exchange Ltd. has received Sebi's approval to launch its initial public offering, which will be an offer for sale by promoter Indian Energy Exchange Ltd. This signals potential new listing activity in the market.
  • Jubilant Pharmova Block Deal: Quant Mutual Fund offloaded a significant stake worth ₹190 crore in Jubilant Pharmova through a block deal, while Abakkus Four2Eight Opportunities Fund acquired 11 lakh shares. This activity reflects institutional repositioning in the stock.

Market breadth

The market breadth was positive today, with 264 advances against 228 declines out of a total of 492 traded stocks. This indicates that despite the frontline indices closing lower, a larger number of stocks managed to end the day in the green, especially in the broader market.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree today, recording a net outflow of ₹12,944 crore from the Indian equity markets. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, provided strong counter-support, injecting ₹10,703 crore.

Looking at the 5-day history, FIIs have been consistent net sellers, with outflows ranging from ₹2,961 crore to ₹12,944 crore each day. Conversely, DIIs have consistently been net buyers, absorbing a significant portion of the FII selling pressure. This trend suggests that while foreign investors are pulling back, domestic institutions are actively supporting the market, cushioning the overall impact.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's session was primarily driven by a combination of persistent FII selling and a significant intervention by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). The heavy FII outflows of ₹12,944 crore put considerable pressure on the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, leading to their declines. However, a notable event was the RBI's measures to curb speculative currency trades, which led to the rupee rebounding by nearly 1% against the dollar. This move helped stabilise the currency market and prevented a further depreciation of the rupee, which could have otherwise exacerbated FII selling and broader market concerns. While no single major economic data release or corporate earnings report dominated the day, the ongoing concerns about global monetary policy, particularly the hawkish stance from the US Fed, also contributed to the cautious sentiment.

Setup for tomorrow

As we head into Tuesday, global cues will continue to be a key determinant for the Indian markets. US bank stocks nearing correction territory and the Cleveland Fed's hawkish comments suggest continued caution from global investors. The Dollar Index closed higher at 102.58, while crude oil prices (Brent at $106.19 and WTI at $93.33) rallied, potentially indicating inflationary pressures. The US 10-year yield saw a slight dip to 5.24. Domestically, the consistent FII selling remains a concern, though DII buying provides a crucial cushion. Investors will be watching for any further developments on global monetary policy and crude oil prices. Key levels for Nifty 50 will be around today's low of 22304.45 and high of 22651.10.
