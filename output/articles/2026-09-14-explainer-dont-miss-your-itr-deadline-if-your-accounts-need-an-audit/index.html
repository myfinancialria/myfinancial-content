Don't Miss Your ITR Deadline if Your Accounts Need an Audit!

TL;DR

  • The deadline to file your Income Tax Return (ITR) for Financial Year 2025-26 (Assessment Year 2026-27) is September 30, 2026, if your accounts need to be audited.
  • This deadline applies mainly to businesses and professionals whose income crosses certain limits.
  • Getting your accounts audited means a Chartered Accountant checks your financial records.
  • Missing this deadline can lead to penalties, loss of benefits like carrying forward losses, and delayed refunds.
  • Even if you miss it, you can file a 'belated return' but with consequences.

What's this about?

Every year, individuals and businesses in India need to tell the government about their income and the tax they owe. This is done by filing an Income Tax Return (ITR). For many people, especially those running businesses or practicing professions, their financial accounts also need to be checked by an independent expert. This checking process is called a "tax audit," and it's done by a Chartered Accountant (CA). The deadline to file your ITR after getting this audit done is coming up soon.

Why it's in the news right now

The calendar date is September 14, 2026, which means the September 30, 2026 deadline for filing Income Tax Returns for Financial Year 2025-26 (also called Assessment Year 2026-27) is just around the corner. This particular deadline is crucial for individuals and entities who are required to get their accounts audited under the Income Tax Act. As this date approaches, there are many reminders and discussions in financial circles and news about the importance of filing on time to avoid penalties and other issues.

How it actually works

  1. What is an Income Tax Return (ITR)? Think of an ITR as your annual report card to the government about your earnings. It's a form where you declare all your income from different sources (like salary, business profits, rent, interest) and claim any deductions or exemptions you are eligible for. Based on this, you calculate your tax liability or claim a refund.
  1. Who needs a Tax Audit? Not everyone needs an audit. Generally, a tax audit is required for:

    2. * Businesses: If your total sales, turnover, or gross receipts in the Financial Year 2025-26 exceeded ₹1 crore. This limit is increased to ₹10 crores if at least 95% of your business transactions (both receipts and payments) are done digitally. * Professionals: If your gross receipts from your profession (like doctors, lawyers, architects) exceeded ₹50 lakhs in FY 2025-26. * There are also specific rules for those declaring income under presumptive taxation schemes (like Section 44AD or 44ADA) if they declare lower profits than prescribed or have higher turnover/receipts.

  1. What is a Tax Audit? A tax audit is a detailed examination of your financial records (like ledgers, bank statements, bills) by a Chartered Accountant. The CA checks if your books of accounts are accurate, comply with accounting standards, and if you have correctly calculated your income and deductions as per the Income Tax Act. After the audit, the CA prepares and submits a 'Tax Audit Report' (Form 3CA/3CB and Form 3CD) online. This report certifies the correctness of your financial statements.
  1. The Filing Process:

    2. * First, you need to ensure all your books of accounts are ready and accurate for the CA to conduct the audit. * The CA will then perform the audit and generate the Tax Audit Report. This report is filed electronically by the CA on the income tax e-filing portal. * Once the audit report is filed, you, as the taxpayer, must then file your ITR (usually ITR-3 or ITR-4, depending on your income source) on the same portal. The ITR form will link to the audit report already filed. * Both the Tax Audit Report and your ITR must be filed by the September 30, 2026 deadline.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine you run a small, busy kirana shop. At the end of the year, you need to tell your landlord (the government) exactly how much money your shop made. Because your shop is quite busy (meaning your sales are above a certain limit), your landlord asks you to get an independent, trusted accountant (the Chartered Accountant) to check all your accounts – how much you bought, how much you sold, your expenses – and give a signed report saying everything is correct. Only after this report is submitted can you send your final income declaration to the landlord. The September 30th deadline is like the last day to submit both the accountant's report and your own declaration.

Why it matters to you

  1. Avoid Penalties: If you miss the September 30th deadline, you might have to pay a penalty. For instance, a late filing fee of up to ₹5,000 can be levied.
  2. Loss of Benefits: You might lose the ability to carry forward certain losses (like business losses or capital losses) to future years, which could otherwise reduce your tax bill later.
  3. Delayed Refunds: If you are eligible for a tax refund, filing late can significantly delay the processing and receipt of your money.
  4. Impact on Loan Applications: Banks and financial institutions often ask for recent ITRs when you apply for home loans, business loans, or even personal loans. A timely and properly filed ITR, especially with an audit report, shows financial discipline and makes your application stronger.
  5. Visa Applications: Some countries require proof of income and tax compliance through ITRs for visa applications. Late or non-filing can create issues.

Common confusions cleared up

  • Myth: Only big companies need tax audits.

    • * Fact: Many small businesses and self-employed professionals also need a tax audit if their turnover or gross receipts cross the specified limits (₹1 crore for businesses, ₹50 lakhs for professionals, with some exceptions).

  • Myth: If I miss the September 30th deadline, I cannot file my ITR at all.

    • * Fact: You can still file a 'belated return' (also called an updated return in some cases) for FY 2025-26, usually until December 31, 2026. However, this comes with a late filing fee, and you might lose some benefits like carrying forward losses.

  • Myth: Filing an ITR is only for paying tax.

    • * Fact: An ITR is primarily a declaration of your income. Even if you don't owe any tax, or are due a refund, filing your ITR is important to comply with the law, prove your income, and avail various financial benefits.

FAQ

Who exactly needs to get their accounts audited for ITR purposes?

Generally, businesses with turnover exceeding ₹1 crore (or ₹10 crore for 95% digital transactions) and professionals with gross receipts exceeding ₹50 lakhs in Financial Year 2025-26 need a tax audit. There are also specific rules for those opting out of presumptive taxation.

What happens if I miss the September 30th deadline for audited accounts?

If you miss the deadline, you will likely have to pay a late filing fee of up to ₹5,000. You may also lose the ability to carry forward certain losses to future years, and any tax refunds might be delayed.

Can I revise my ITR after filing if I discover a mistake?

Yes, if you file your original ITR by the September 30th deadline, you can generally revise it if you find any errors or omissions, typically until December 31, 2026.

What documents do I need for an audited ITR?

You'll need your financial statements (Profit & Loss Account, Balance Sheet), bank statements, invoices, bills, details of assets and liabilities, Form 26AS, AIS/TIS, and any other relevant financial records for the CA to conduct the audit.

What is the difference between ITR-3 and ITR-4 forms?

ITR-3 is for individuals and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) who have income from business or profession and do not opt for the presumptive taxation scheme. ITR-4 (Sugam) is for individuals, HUFs, and partnership firms whose total income includes business income under the presumptive taxation scheme (Sections 44AD, 44ADA, 44AE) and is below a certain limit.
