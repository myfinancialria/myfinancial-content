Your GST Just Got Simpler: Big Changes Expected from the Next Council Meeting

TL;DR

  • The 57th GST Council meeting, now on October 7, 2026, will focus on making tax rules easier for businesses.
  • Key discussions include allowing businesses to claim more Input Tax Credit (ITC) on common expenses like vehicles and office perks.
  • The Council aims to protect genuine buyers who lose ITC if their suppliers don't deposit the collected tax.
  • Expect simpler and faster ways to get GST registration, especially for larger businesses.
  • These "process reforms" are designed to reduce paperwork, cut down on legal disputes, and boost business confidence.

What's this about?

India's Goods and Services Tax (GST) system is always evolving. The GST Council, which is the main body that decides on GST rules, is set to hold its 57th meeting. This time, the focus isn't on changing tax rates, which were largely revamped last year under "GST 2.0." Instead, the Council will discuss "process reforms." This means they want to simplify how businesses comply with GST, reduce paperwork, and make the system smoother for everyone.

Why it's in the news right now

The 57th GST Council meeting was initially planned for September 12, 2026, but it has been rescheduled to October 7, 2026. This postponement was reportedly due to New Delhi hosting the BRICS Leaders' Summit around the same time. Despite the change in date, news reports from mid-September 2026 have been actively discussing the expected agenda items. Businesses and tax experts are keenly watching for these announcements, as they could significantly impact daily operations and financial planning.

How it actually works

The GST Council is a joint forum of the Centre and States, chaired by the Union Finance Minister, that makes recommendations on all important matters related to GST. Its decisions directly affect how GST is collected and how businesses operate across India. The upcoming meeting is expected to address several long-standing issues:

  1. Easing restrictions on Input Tax Credit (ITC):

    2. * What is ITC? Imagine you run a small business that makes furniture. When you buy wood, nails, and polish, you pay GST on these "inputs." When you sell your finished furniture, you collect GST from your customers. Input Tax Credit (ITC) allows you to reduce the GST you owe to the government by the amount of GST you've already paid on your inputs. It prevents "tax on tax" (cascading effect). * "Blocked Credit": Currently, there are certain business expenses on which you cannot claim ITC, even if they are for business purposes. These are called "blocked credits" under Section 17(5) of the CGST Act. Examples include GST paid on certain motor vehicles (with seating capacity of not more than 13 persons, unless used for specific purposes like transport or further supply), food and beverages, outdoor catering, beauty treatments, health services, club memberships, and goods/services used for constructing immovable property (like your office building, unless it's for further supply of works contract services). * Expected Change: The Council may consider easing some of these restrictions, particularly for employee-related expenses like rent-a-cab services or canteen facilities, and potentially for certain works contracts. The idea is to align GST rules more closely with income tax rules, where such expenses are often allowed as business deductions.

  1. Protecting buyers from supplier defaults:

    2. * The Problem: A major headache for many businesses is when they pay GST to their supplier, but the supplier fails to deposit that tax with the government. Under current rules (Section 16(2)(c) of the CGST Act), the buyer might then be denied their ITC, effectively paying the tax twice. This penalises the buyer for the supplier's non-compliance, even if the buyer acted in good faith. The Supreme Court has, in some rulings, upheld this provision, stating that ITC is a statutory concession, not a constitutional right, and can have conditions attached. * Expected Change: The Council is expected to examine mechanisms to protect "bona fide" (genuine) buyers. This means finding ways to ensure that buyers who have fulfilled all their obligations (paid the supplier, have valid invoices) do not lose their ITC solely because their supplier defaulted. This could involve new guidelines or procedural safeguards.

  1. Simplifying GST registration:

    2. * Current Situation: While the government introduced "GST 2.0" in November 2025 to simplify registration for small businesses (allowing automatic registration within three working days for low-risk applicants or those with monthly output tax liability not exceeding ₹2.5 lakh), there's a continuous push for more ease. * Expected Change: The Council may consider further reforms for faster GST registration, especially for larger businesses or those with higher Input Tax Credit (ITC) exceeding ₹2.5 lakh per month. Proposals include categorising registrations based on risk assessment, with faster approvals for "trusted businesses" and stricter checks for "emerging businesses."

  1. Reducing GST litigation and improving refunds:

    2. * The Council is also expected to discuss ways to reduce the number of legal disputes related to GST and streamline the process for obtaining refunds, particularly for businesses facing an "inverted duty structure" (where tax on inputs is higher than on outputs).

A simple way to think about it

Think of GST as a relay race where each runner (business) passes a baton (tax credit) to the next. If a runner drops the baton, the next runner might be penalised, even if it wasn't their fault. The GST Council is like the race organisers trying to make sure:

  1. More kinds of batons (expenses) can be used in the race (easing blocked ITC).
  2. If a runner drops the baton, the next runner isn't automatically out of the race, especially if they ran their part perfectly (protecting buyers from supplier defaults).
  3. It's easier and faster for new runners to join the race (simplifying registration).
  4. There are fewer arguments and clearer rules about who gets which baton and when (reducing litigation and improving refunds).

Why it matters to you

  1. For Businesses (especially MSMEs):

    2. * Lower Costs: If more expenses become eligible for ITC, businesses can reduce their overall tax burden, potentially leading to lower prices for consumers or higher profits. * Smoother Cash Flow: Getting ITC on time and avoiding its denial due to supplier issues means businesses have more working capital, which is crucial for growth. * Easier Start-up and Expansion: Faster and simpler GST registration can encourage new businesses to enter the formal economy and make it easier for existing ones to expand. * Reduced Legal Headaches: Clearer rules on ITC and supplier defaults can significantly cut down on tax notices, audits, and legal disputes, saving time and money.

  1. For Consumers:

    2. * Potentially Lower Prices: If businesses save money through easier compliance and more ITC, some of these savings could be passed on to you in the form of lower prices for goods and services. * Better Business Environment: A simpler tax regime fosters a healthier business environment, which can lead to more job creation and economic growth in the long run.

Common confusions cleared up

Myth: The GST Council meeting will increase or decrease GST rates. Fact: The 57th GST Council meeting is primarily focused on "process reforms" and simplifying compliance, not on changing GST rates. Major rate rationalisation was undertaken in the 56th meeting in September 2025.

Myth: Any proposed changes will take effect immediately after the meeting. Fact: The GST Council makes recommendations. These recommendations then need to be formally notified by the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC) through circulars or amendments to the GST Act and Rules. Until then, existing rules apply.

Myth: All blocked credits will be removed. Fact: While the Council is expected to ease restrictions on certain categories of blocked ITC, it is unlikely that all blocked credits will be removed. Specific items like those for personal consumption are likely to remain ineligible.

FAQ

What is the GST Council?

The GST Council is India's highest decision-making body for Goods and Services Tax, comprising the Union Finance Minister (as Chairperson) and state finance ministers. It recommends all major changes to GST laws, rates, and procedures.

When is the 57th GST Council meeting happening?

The 57th GST Council meeting is scheduled for October 7, 2026, in New Delhi.

What is Input Tax Credit (ITC)?

Input Tax Credit (ITC) allows businesses to reduce the tax they pay on their final sales by the amount of tax they have already paid on their raw materials, goods, or services used in their business.

How does a supplier default affect a buyer's ITC?

Currently, if your supplier collects GST from you but doesn't deposit it with the government, you as the buyer might be denied the Input Tax Credit, even if you paid the full amount. The upcoming meeting aims to address this issue to protect genuine buyers.

Will GST registration become fully automatic?

While "GST 2.0" introduced simplified and faster registration for small, low-risk businesses, the Council is expected to propose further streamlining. However, for larger or higher-risk businesses, some level of verification or security deposit might still be required.
