Micron Shares Surge on Blockbuster Earnings: How Indians Can Invest

TL;DR

  • Micron Technology reported record-breaking Q3 FY2026 results, with revenue of $41.46 billion and adjusted EPS of $25.11, significantly exceeding analyst expectations.
  • The surge is primarily driven by unprecedented demand for memory chips, especially High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) and DRAM, fueled by the booming Artificial Intelligence (AI) sector.
  • Micron's strong Q4 FY2026 guidance, projecting $50 billion in revenue and $31.00 adjusted EPS, further indicates a sustained AI-driven growth cycle and tight supply conditions.
  • Indian investors can gain exposure to Micron directly through international brokerage platforms or indirectly via Indian mutual funds and ETFs that invest in US equities.
  • Investing in US stocks involves understanding RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) limits, foreign exchange risks, and specific tax implications for capital gains and dividends in India.

What this means in plain terms

Micron Technology, a major player in the semiconductor industry, specifically in memory and storage chips, has delivered exceptional financial results for its third fiscal quarter of 2026. This means the company earned significantly more money and profit than market experts had predicted. The primary reason for this outstanding performance is the massive and growing demand for advanced memory chips, which are crucial components for Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems and data centres.

The company's positive outlook for the next quarter suggests that this high demand, coupled with a constrained supply of these specialised chips, is likely to continue for the foreseeable future. This "blockbuster" report has caused Micron's stock to surge, even briefly surpassing the market valuation of tech giants like Meta Platforms and Tesla, highlighting its increasing importance in the global technology landscape. For Indian investors looking to participate in this global AI-driven growth story, there are several avenues to consider for investing in companies like Micron.

Blockbuster Q3 FY2026 Performance and Strong Guidance

Micron Technology reported record-breaking results for its third fiscal quarter ended May 28, 2026. The company announced revenue of $41.46 billion, a remarkable increase from $23.86 billion in the previous quarter and $9.30 billion in the same period last year. This figure significantly surpassed Wall Street's average revenue estimate of $35.7 billion.

In terms of profitability, Micron posted a GAAP net income of $28.24 billion, or $24.67 per diluted share. The non-GAAP adjusted earnings per share (EPS) came in at an impressive $25.11, far exceeding analysts' expectations of $20.20 per share. The gross margin expanded dramatically to 85%, indicating strong pricing power and efficient operations. Operating cash flow for the quarter also reached a record $25.39 billion.

Looking ahead, Micron provided an equally robust forecast for its fiscal fourth quarter. The company expects revenue to be approximately $50 billion, plus or minus $1 billion, which is well above the consensus estimate of $43.2 billion. Adjusted EPS for Q4 is projected to be around $31.00, plus or minus $1.00, with gross margins expected to reach approximately 86%. This strong guidance underscores management's confidence in sustained demand and tight supply conditions.

Driving the Rally: The AI Memory Supercycle

The primary catalyst behind Micron's stellar performance and stock rally is the surging demand for memory chips, particularly High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) and Dynamic Random-Access Memory (DRAM), driven by the Artificial Intelligence (AI) revolution. AI chips, such as graphics processing units (GPUs) and custom AI processors, require substantial memory to efficiently process vast amounts of data for AI workloads in data centres.

Micron's CEO, Sanjay Mehrotra, highlighted that memory has become a "strategic asset" in the AI era, and the company is making record investments in technology and supply to meet this rapidly growing demand. The company reported that demand for high-bandwidth memory, enterprise SSDs, and data-centre DRAM continues to significantly outpace supply. Micron's HBM3E and HBM4 products are reportedly fully booked through calendar year 2027, with demand extending into 2028.

Furthermore, Micron has secured 16 Strategic Customer Agreements (SCAs), involving over $22 billion in commitments, including nearly $18 billion in cash deposits. These multi-year agreements, averaging three years in length, are expected to enhance the durability and predictability of Micron's financial performance and help mitigate the historical boom-and-bust cycles of the memory industry. The company's expansion plans, including significant capital expenditures guided for fiscal 2026 and 2027, are aimed at addressing the structural supply constraints in the memory market.

Investing in US Stocks from India: Pathways and Considerations

Indian investors keen on participating in the growth of global technology companies like Micron have several avenues available, primarily governed by the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS). Under the LRS, resident individuals can remit up to USD 250,000 (approximately ₹2 crore at current exchange rates) per financial year for various permissible purposes, including investments in overseas shares, securities, and mutual funds.

Direct Investment via International Brokerage Platforms

One popular method is to open an international trading account with a global brokerage firm or an Indian platform that partners with US brokers. Platforms like INDmoney, Vested Finance, Kuvera, Angel One, and Motilal Oswal facilitate direct investment in US stocks. This typically involves a KYC process, linking your Indian bank account, and remitting funds in US dollars under the LRS. Many platforms also allow fractional share investing, making it accessible for smaller investment amounts.

Indirect Investment through Mutual Funds and ETFs

For those who prefer a more diversified or hands-off approach, indirect investment options are available:

  • Indian Mutual Funds (Fund-of-Funds): Several Indian asset management companies (AMCs) offer "feeder funds" or Fund-of-Funds (FoFs) that invest in overseas equity mutual funds or ETFs. Examples include Kotak Nasdaq 100 FoF, Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 FoF, ICICI Prudential US Bluechip Equity Fund, and Franklin India Feeder US Opportunities Fund. These funds manage the foreign exchange and compliance aspects for investors.
  • Indian-listed ETFs tracking US Indices: There are also Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) listed on Indian exchanges (BSE and NSE) that track popular US indices like the Nasdaq 100 or S&P 500. These ETFs trade in rupees, eliminating the need for direct foreign currency remittance under LRS for the investor. Examples include Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 ETF and Mirae Asset NYSE FANG+ ETF.

Tax Implications for Indian Investors

Investing in US stocks or funds from India comes with specific tax considerations that investors must understand.

Capital Gains Tax

The taxation of capital gains depends on the holding period. For foreign stocks and ETFs held directly, if the investment is held for more than 24 months, it is considered a Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG) and taxed at 12.5% without indexation benefit. If held for up to 24 months, it is treated as a Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG) and added to your total income, taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate.

For Indian mutual funds or FoFs that invest primarily in foreign stocks, the tax rules have changed. For units bought after April 1, 2023, any capital gains are taxed at your income tax slab rate, regardless of the holding period, until March 31, 2025. From April 1, 2025, if units are held for 24 months or more, LTCG will be taxed at 12.5% without indexation. STCG (held for less than 24 months) will be taxed at the slab rate.

Dividend Income

Dividends received from US stocks are subject to a 25% withholding tax in the US. However, due to the Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) between India and the US, Indian investors can claim this tax paid in the US as a foreign tax credit when filing their income tax returns in India, avoiding double taxation. Dividends are taxable in India as "Income from Other Sources" at your applicable slab rate.

Tax Collected at Source (TCS)

As per current regulations, Tax Collected at Source (TCS) is applicable on remittances made under the LRS. For remittances exceeding ₹10 lakh in a financial year, a TCS of 20% is levied for all categories other than education and medical treatment. This TCS amount can be adjusted against your overall income tax liability when filing your Income Tax Return (ITR).

Mandatory Reporting

Indian residents investing in US stocks must disclose all foreign assets in Schedule FA (Foreign Assets) of their Income Tax Return (ITR), even if no gains or losses were made. This includes details like the name of the stock, number of shares, and their value in INR.

Net amount remitted for investment (after TCS) = ₹15,00,000 - ₹1,00,000 = ₹14,00,000 (this is incorrect, TCS is collected on the remittance, not deducted from the investment amount. Her bank collects ₹1,00,000 as TCS (20% on ₹5,00,000 above ₹10 lakhs). She then invests the USD equivalent of ₹15,00,000 (approx. $18,000) in Micron shares.

  1. Dividend Income (March 2027): Micron declares a dividend. Anjali receives $100 in dividends.

    2. * US withholding tax (25%): $25. * Net dividend received: $75. * When filing her ITR, Anjali converts $100 to INR (say, ₹8,500 at that time) and adds it to her income.   1. Selling Shares (August 2028): After 26 months, Anjali decides to sell her Micron shares for $25,000 (approx. ₹21,25,000 at ₹85/USD).

    2. * Investment cost: $18,000 (₹15,00,000). * Capital Gain: $25,000 - $18,000 = $7,000. * Capital Gain in INR: ₹21,25,000 - ₹15,00,000 = ₹6,25,000. * Since the shares were held for 26 months (more than 24 months), this is an LTCG. This example illustrates the process of remittance, dividend taxation with DTAA, and capital gains tax for direct investment in US stocks for an Indian resident.

FAQ

What is the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS)?

The LRS is an RBI scheme allowing resident Indians to remit up to USD 250,000 per financial year for permissible current or capital account transactions, including investing in foreign stocks and mutual funds.

How are capital gains from US stocks taxed in India?

For direct investments, gains on stocks held for over 24 months are taxed as LTCG at 12.5%. Gains on stocks held for up to 24 months are STCG, taxed at your income tax slab rate.

Are dividends from US stocks taxed in India?

Yes, dividends are taxable in India at your applicable income tax slab rate. A 25% withholding tax is levied in the US, which can be claimed as a foreign tax credit under the India-US DTAA.

Can NRIs invest in US stocks through LRS?

No, the LRS is exclusively for resident individuals. NRIs have different regulations for investing in foreign markets.

What is TCS on LRS remittances?

As of April 1, 2026, a 20% Tax Collected at Source (TCS) is applicable on LRS remittances exceeding ₹10 lakhs in a financial year for purposes other than education and medical treatment. This TCS can be adjusted against your income tax liability.

What documents are needed to invest in US stocks from India?

Typically, you will need a valid PAN card, Aadhaar card, and proof of address for KYC verification with your chosen brokerage platform.

Are there any limits on the number of remittances under LRS?

There is no restriction on the frequency or number of transactions, but the total amount remitted in a financial year must not exceed the USD 250,000 LRS limit.
