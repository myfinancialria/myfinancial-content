Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. When we talk about "highest Call OI strike" or "highest Put OI strike," we are identifying the price levels where the maximum number of Call or Put options, respectively, are currently open. These strikes are often seen as significant resistance (for Calls) or support (for Puts) levels, as option writers (sellers) at these strikes stand to gain if the price stays below (for Calls) or above (for Puts) their chosen level.

For Nifty 50, the aggregate flow today showed a net build-up of 24.8 million Put Open Interest against 5.5 million Call Open Interest. This significant fresh Put writing suggests that option sellers are betting the index will not fall below these newly written Put strikes, indicating a bullish outlook.

In Sensex, while the heaviest Call OI is at 73500, acting as a potential ceiling, the heaviest Put OI is also close by at 73500. This indicates a tight range being expected by option writers. Today's heaviest fresh Put writing was observed near 72166, suggesting that Put sellers believe this level will act as a floor.

For Bank Nifty, the heaviest Put OI is established at 55000, just below the current spot price, forming a close floor. The freshest Put writing today was concentrated near 54033, reinforcing the expectation among Put sellers that the index will find support at or above these levels.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total Open Interest (OI) of Put options to Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests more Put writing (often interpreted as bullish sentiment, as Put writers profit if the price stays above their strike), while a PCR below 1 suggests more Call writing (often interpreted as bearish sentiment, as Call writers profit if the price stays below their strike). We also look at PCR based on today's change in OI (ΔOI) to gauge fresh sentiment.

For Nifty 50, the PCR based on total OI stands at 0.82, suggesting a balanced book with a slight leaning towards Call writing. However, today's flow ΔPCR is a strong 4.53, indicating that the fresh writing activity was heavily skewed towards Puts. This combination suggests that while the overall book might be balanced, the immediate sentiment from today's new positions is bullish.

Sensex shows a PCR(OI) of 1.07, indicating a relatively balanced book with a slight Put-heavy bias. The PCR(today flow ΔOI) is 1.12, suggesting that today's fresh writing was also mixed to slightly Put-heavy, aligning with a neutral to mildly bullish sentiment.

Bank Nifty has a PCR(OI) of 0.95, indicating a balanced book with a slight lean towards Call writing. However, today's flow ΔPCR is -1.24. While this number itself might seem negative, it implies that the change in Call OI was significantly higher than Put OI, which can sometimes be interpreted as Call writers being active, but in the context of other bullish signals, it could also mean profit booking or hedging. Given the overall bias, the market is interpreting this as a mixed flow rather than a strong bearish signal.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is a concept in options trading that refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the least financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that specific level. It's often considered a magnetic point, suggesting that the index tends to gravitate towards this level as the expiry date approaches, as option writers may try to influence the price to expire there to minimise their losses.

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain strike is at 23000, which is approximately 1.4% above its current spot price of 22,687.65. This positioning suggests an upward pull for the index as expiry nears.

Sensex has its Max Pain at 73500, which is about 0.3% above its spot price of 73,249.39. This also indicates an upward magnetic pull, albeit a smaller one, suggesting a tighter expiry range.

Bank Nifty's Max Pain is located at 55400, approximately 0.9% above its spot price of 54,925.70. This further reinforces the bullish undertone, with option writers positioned for an upward move or expiry at a higher level.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding the daily F&O data can empower you to form your own perspectives on market sentiment. You can typically find Open Interest data on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and other indices. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in an easily digestible format.

To interpret it, first look at the "Open Interest" column for both Calls and Puts at various strike prices. The strikes with the highest OI often act as strong support (for Puts) and resistance (for Calls) levels. Next, pay attention to the "Change in OI" column (ΔOI) for today – this tells you where fresh positions were added or closed. A significant increase in Put OI at lower strikes, or Call OI at higher strikes, can indicate where option writers are building their positions. Finally, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI, and also for today's ΔOI, to get a sense of the overall and fresh market sentiment.
