Claiming Capital Gains Tax Exemption on Asset Sales up to ₹10 Crore

TL;DR

  • Selling long-term capital assets like shares, mutual funds, or gold can lead to capital gains tax.
  • Section 54F of the Income Tax Act allows you to exempt these long-term capital gains from tax.
  • To claim this exemption, you must reinvest the net sale consideration into a new residential property.
  • There are specific timelines for purchasing or constructing the new property to qualify.
  • A maximum investment of ₹10 crore in the new residential property is considered for this exemption.

What this means in plain terms

When you sell an asset like shares, mutual funds, gold, or even a commercial property that you've held for a long time, any profit you make is called a long-term capital gain. The government typically taxes these gains. However, the Income Tax Act provides certain ways to reduce or completely avoid this tax if you reinvest your profits.

One such powerful provision is Section 54F. It's designed to encourage investment in residential housing. Essentially, if you make a long-term capital gain from selling any asset (except for an existing residential house property, which falls under Section 54), you can save on tax by using the money to buy or construct a new home in India. This exemption can be substantial, allowing you to reinvest up to ₹10 crore into a new property to claim the benefit.

Understanding Section 54F Eligibility

Section 54F is a specific provision within the Income Tax Act, 1961, that allows individuals and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) to claim an exemption on long-term capital gains (LTCG) arising from the sale of any long-term capital asset, provided the gains are reinvested into a residential house property.

Who can claim this exemption?

Only individuals and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) are eligible to claim exemption under Section 54F. Companies, firms, or other entities cannot avail this benefit.

What assets are covered?

The exemption applies to LTCG from the sale of any long-term capital asset, other than a residential house property. This includes a wide range of assets such as:

  • Listed equity shares and equity-oriented mutual funds (if holding period qualifies as long-term).
  • Units of debt mutual funds.
  • Gold (physical or through Gold ETFs/Sovereign Gold Bonds).
  • Bonds and debentures.
  • Commercial property or land.
  • Any other capital asset that is not a residential house.

The asset sold must be a "long-term capital asset." The definition of long-term varies based on the asset type (e.g., generally 12 months for listed shares, 24 months for unlisted shares/immovable property, 36 months for others).

Conditions regarding existing residential properties

A crucial condition for claiming Section 54F exemption is that on the date of the sale of the original asset, you should not own more than one residential house property, other than the new house being purchased, in India. Additionally, you should not purchase any other residential house property (other than the new one) within one year before or two years after the date of sale, or construct any other residential house property within three years after the date of sale.

The Reinvestment Rule: Buying a New Home

The core of Section 54F lies in the reinvestment of the net sale consideration into a new residential house property in India. The exemption amount depends on how much of the net sale consideration is reinvested.

Investment in a single residential property

For sales made on or after April 1, 2023, the exemption under Section 54F is capped by limiting the cost of the new residential house property to ₹10 crore. This means that even if you invest more than ₹10 crore in a new property, the maximum investment considered for exemption will be ₹10 crore.

Timelines for reinvestment

To qualify for the exemption, the new residential property must be:

  • Purchased one year before the date of sale of the original asset.
  • Purchased within two years after the date of sale of the original asset.
  • Constructed within three years after the date of sale of the original asset.

It's important to note that the exemption is available only if the new house is acquired or constructed in India.

Calculating the exemption

The exemption under Section 54F is not always equal to the entire long-term capital gain. It is calculated proportionally. If the entire net sale consideration (not just the capital gain) is reinvested into the new house, the entire long-term capital gain is exempt. If only a part of the net sale consideration is reinvested, the exemption is calculated as: (Long-Term Capital Gain * Amount Invested in New House) / Net Sale Consideration.

Navigating the Capital Gains Account Scheme

What if you sell your asset but haven't found a new property to buy or start construction within the same financial year? The Income Tax Act provides a solution: the Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS), 1988.

Why CGAS is important

If the amount of net sale consideration that is to be reinvested in the new residential house is not utilised before the due date for filing your Income Tax Return (ITR) for the relevant assessment year, you must deposit the unutilised amount into a Capital Gains Account Scheme with a public sector bank. This deposit must be made before the ITR due date. If you fail to do so, the unutilised amount will be treated as capital gain of the year in which the original asset was sold and will be taxable.

How CGAS works

You can open a CGAS account in either a savings or term deposit format (Type A or Type B). The amount deposited in this account is considered as having been utilised for the purpose of claiming the Section 54F exemption. You can then withdraw funds from this account as and when needed for the purchase or construction of the new residential property within the specified timelines (two or three years, respectively).

Consequences of non-utilisation

If the amount deposited in the CGAS is not utilised for purchasing or constructing the new residential house within the specified period (two or three years from the date of sale), the unutilised amount will be treated as long-term capital gain of the previous year in which the two-year or three-year period expires. This amount will then become taxable in your hands.

Key Conditions and Limitations

While Section 54F offers significant tax relief, it comes with certain conditions and limitations that taxpayers must be aware of.

Lock-in period for the new property

The new residential house property acquired under Section 54F cannot be sold within three years from the date of its purchase or completion of construction. If you sell the new house before this three-year lock-in period, the capital gain that was originally exempted will become taxable in the year of sale. The cost of acquisition of the new house will be reduced by the amount of capital gain exempted earlier, for the purpose of computing capital gains on its subsequent sale.

Maximum exemption limit for investment

As mentioned, for capital gains arising from transfers made on or after April 1, 2023, the cost of the new residential house property purchased or constructed, which is considered for exemption, cannot exceed ₹10 crore. This means the maximum exemption you can claim under Section 54F is effectively capped by this ₹10 crore investment limit.

Investment in only one residential house

The exemption is available only if the investment is made in one residential house property in India. You cannot claim the exemption by investing in multiple residential properties.

A real example

Let's consider Meera, 42, a self-employed professional in Mumbai, with an annual income of ₹35 lakhs.

In July 2025, Meera sold her portfolio of unlisted equity shares that she had held for 5 years.

  • Sale Consideration: ₹1.20 crore
  • Indexed Cost of Acquisition: ₹40 lakhs
  • Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG): ₹1.20 crore - ₹40 lakhs = ₹80 lakhs

Meera wants to claim exemption under Section 54F. She does not own any other residential property in India.

She decides to purchase a new flat in Thane.

  • Net Sale Consideration (for Section 54F, this is the full sale amount as no expenses mentioned): ₹1.20 crore

Scenario 1: Meera invests the entire net sale consideration in a new property. In January 2026, Meera purchases a new flat for ₹1.20 crore. Since she invested the entire net sale consideration (₹1.20 crore) into the new residential property, her entire Long-Term Capital Gain of ₹80 lakhs will be exempt from tax under Section 54F.

Scenario 2: Meera invests only a part of the net sale consideration. Instead, Meera purchases a new flat for ₹90 lakhs in January 2026. Amount invested in new house: ₹90 lakhs Net Sale Consideration: ₹1.20 crore Long-Term Capital Gain: ₹80 lakhs

Exemption under Section 54F = (LTCG * Amount Invested) / Net Sale Consideration = (₹80 lakhs * ₹90 lakhs) / ₹1.20 crore = (₹80,00,000 * ₹90,00,000) / ₹1,20,00,000 = ₹60 lakhs

In this scenario, Meera can claim an exemption of ₹60 lakhs. The remaining capital gain of ₹20 lakhs (₹80 lakhs - ₹60 lakhs) will be taxable at the applicable long-term capital gains tax rate.

FAQ

What is the difference between Section 54 and Section 54F?

Section 54 applies when you sell an existing residential house property and reinvest the capital gains into another residential house. Section 54F applies when you sell any long-term capital asset (other than a residential house) and reinvest the net sale consideration into a residential house.

Can NRIs claim Section 54F exemption?

Yes, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are eligible to claim exemption under Section 54F, provided they meet all the specified conditions, including reinvesting in a residential house property in India.

What happens if I sell the new property within three years?

If the new residential property purchased or constructed using Section 54F exemption is sold within three years, the capital gain exempted earlier will be reversed and become taxable in the year of the new property's sale.

Is there a maximum limit on the capital gain I can exempt?

While there isn't a direct cap on the capital gain itself, for transfers made on or after April 1, 2023, the cost of the new residential house property considered for exemption under Section 54F cannot exceed ₹10 crore. This effectively limits the maximum exemption amount.

Can I invest in more than one residential property to claim this exemption?

No, the exemption under Section 54F is available only if the investment is made in one residential house property in India.

What if I can't buy a property before filing my ITR?

If you cannot utilise the full amount of the net sale consideration for purchasing or constructing the new residential property before the due date for filing your Income Tax Return, you must deposit the unutilised amount into a Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS) with a public sector bank.

Do I need to report this exemption in my ITR?

Yes, you must report the sale of the original asset, the long-term capital gain, and the claim for exemption under Section 54F in your Income Tax Return for the relevant assessment year.
