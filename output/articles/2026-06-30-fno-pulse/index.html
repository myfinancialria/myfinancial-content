Both Call and Put option writers have established strong positions very close to the current spot, indicating expectations of limited movement.

Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. In the options market, high Call OI at a particular strike price suggests that many Call options have been 'written' or sold at that level, indicating that sellers (often institutions or experienced traders) believe the price is unlikely to rise above that point. This effectively creates a resistance level. Conversely, high Put OI at a strike price means many Put options have been sold, implying that sellers expect the price to remain above that level, thus forming a support.

For NIFTY 50, the heaviest Call OI is observed at 23,900, which is just 0.1% above the spot price. This suggests that option writers are comfortable selling Calls at this level, potentially capping any immediate upward movement. On the Put side, the heaviest Put OI is at 23,850, a mere 0.1% below spot, indicating strong support building up. Today's fresh Call writing was also concentrated near 23,900, reinforcing the idea of a ceiling, while fresh Put writing around 23,850 further solidifies the perceived floor.

SENSEX shows its heaviest Call OI at 77,000, which is about 0.7% above the spot. This strike appears to be a significant resistance level where Call writers are actively positioning themselves. While specific fresh Call writing data wasn't detailed for Sensex, the fresh Put writing near 75,500 suggests that option sellers are confident the index will not fall below this level, establishing a potential base.

In BANK NIFTY, the heaviest Call OI is at 57,600, only 0.1% above the spot, and the heaviest Put OI is at 57,500, 0.1% below spot. This extremely close proximity of major Call and Put walls highlights the expectation of very limited movement. Today's fresh Call writing was heaviest near 57,700, suggesting writers anticipate a cap around this slightly higher level, while fresh Put writing near 57,500 reinforces the immediate support.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests a bullish sentiment as there are more Puts (often sold by those expecting prices to rise) than Calls. A value below 1, conversely, can indicate a bearish sentiment.

For NIFTY 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.8, which is generally considered a balanced book, leaning slightly towards more Call options outstanding. However, today's flow, measured by the change in Open Interest (PCR(today flow ΔOI)), was -1.23. This negative value indicates that today saw significantly more fresh Call writing than Put writing, suggesting a mixed to slightly cautious sentiment from new option sellers entering the market.

SENSEX also has a PCR(OI) of 0.78, similarly indicating a relatively balanced options book with a slight lean towards Call options. Today's flow (PCR(today flow ΔOI)) was 0.71. While still below 1, this value is less negative than Nifty's, suggesting a more balanced fresh writing activity today, though still with a slight tilt towards Call writing.

BANK NIFTY mirrors the other indices with a PCR(OI) of 0.78, again suggesting a balanced book. However, its PCR(today flow ΔOI) was 0.56. This lower value indicates that today's fresh option writing was notably Call-heavy, implying that more new Call options were sold compared to Put options, suggesting option writers are anticipating limited upside for the Bank Nifty.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (those who have sold options) would incur the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that level. It's often seen as a "magnet" because the underlying index or stock tends to gravitate towards this level as expiry approaches, as option writers try to minimise their collective losses.

For NIFTY 50, the Max Pain level is at 24,000. This is approximately 0.6% above the current spot price of 23,865.75. The data suggests that option writers are collectively positioned for the Nifty to potentially move higher towards this 24,000 mark by expiry.

SENSEX has its Max Pain level at 77,000, which is about 0.7% above its current spot of 76,478.67. Similar to Nifty, this indicates a potential upward pull for the Sensex towards this strike price as the expiry draws nearer.

BANK NIFTY's Max Pain is located at 57,800, approximately 0.4% above its spot price of 57,542.90. This suggests that option writers for Bank Nifty are collectively positioned for a slight upward movement, aiming for the index to settle around 57,800. The closer to expiry, the stronger this "magnet effect" tends to be.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment. Looking at yesterday's data (as today's NSE archive is not yet published), FIIs held a very low Index futures long ratio of 17.0%. This means that for every 100 Index futures contracts they hold, only 17 are long (bets on prices going up) while 83 are short (bets on prices going down). Historically, such a low long ratio in Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious to bearish outlook from FIIs on the broader market.

In contrast, their Stock futures long ratio stood at 54.6%, indicating a slightly bullish stance on individual stocks, with more long positions than short positions. This divergence between their index and stock futures positioning can sometimes suggest a selective approach, being cautious on the overall market while picking specific stocks for potential upside.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

The mildly bullish biases for Nifty and Sensex, as suggested by their Max Pain levels being above spot and the presence of fresh Put writing near current levels, indicate that option writers are positioning for a potential slight upward drift or at least a consolidation with a positive bias. The Call walls, however, are quite close for Nifty and Bank Nifty, suggesting that any significant upward movement might find immediate resistance.

Historically, when Max Pain is above the spot price and fresh Put writing is observed at lower strikes, it has tended to coincide with the market finding support and potentially attempting to move higher towards the Max Pain level. However, the relatively balanced PCR(OI) and the mixed to Call-heavy fresh writing flows, particularly for Bank Nifty, suggest that this potential upside might be capped. The FIIs' significantly low long ratio in Index futures presents a contrasting cautious signal from a major participant, which historically has tended to coincide with underlying market weakness or at least a lack of strong conviction for an upward rally. Therefore, while individual index options data points to a mild bullish lean for Nifty and Sensex, the overall environment, especially considering the FII positioning and the tight range for Bank Nifty, suggests that the market might attempt to hold its ground with a cautious upward bias, but with strong resistance levels nearby.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding the daily options and futures data can provide valuable insights into market sentiment and potential price movements. You can access this data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, specifically in the 'Derivatives' section, where you'll find option chain data and participant-wise Open Interest. Many brokerage platforms also offer intuitive interfaces to view this information.

When you look at the option chain, focus on the strike prices with the highest Open Interest (OI) for both Calls and Puts. These are often considered significant resistance and support levels, respectively. Also, observe the 'Change in OI' column to identify where fresh Call or Put writing (selling) has occurred today – this tells you where new positions are being built and what levels option writers are defending. Calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI to gauge overall market sentiment. Finally, locate the 'Max Pain' strike, which can act as a magnet for the underlying index as expiry approaches. By combining these data points, you can form a more comprehensive view of how market participants are positioned and what they collectively expect for the near term.
