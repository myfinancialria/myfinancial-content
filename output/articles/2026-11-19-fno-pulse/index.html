India F&O Pulse — 19 Nov 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Open Interest Data Points to Caution as FIIs Maintain Bearish Stance on Index Futures.

TL;DR

  • Nifty and Bank Nifty saw significant fresh Call writing at higher strikes, suggesting resistance levels are being fortified by option sellers.
  • The overall Put-Call Ratio for Open Interest indicates a cautious to bearish sentiment among option writers, particularly in Bank Nifty.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continue to hold a net short position in index futures, a historical signal that has often coincided with a cautious market outlook.

What the indices did today

On Thursday, November 19, 2026, Indian benchmark indices closed in the red, reflecting a cautious mood across the market. The NIFTY 50 ended the day at 19,650, shedding 80 points, or 0.41%. It closed just below the significant Call option writing seen at the 19,700 strike, indicating that this level could act as a near-term hurdle. The SENSEX also saw a decline, closing at 65,500, down 250 points (0.38%). Its close near the 65,600 Call wall suggests a similar sentiment of resistance. The BANK NIFTY witnessed a more pronounced fall, settling at 43,800, a drop of 300 points or 0.68%. This index closed well below the 44,000 Call writing zone, suggesting a relatively weaker undertone compared to its broader market counterparts.

Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides a snapshot of market participation and potential support or resistance levels.

For the NIFTY 50, the highest Call Open Interest was observed at the 19,700 strike. This means that the maximum number of outstanding Call options were written (sold) at this level, indicating that option writers believe the index may struggle to move significantly above 19,700. Conversely, the highest Put Open Interest was concentrated at the 19,500 strike, suggesting that option writers see this as a strong support level where the index might find buying interest.

Today's trading session saw significant fresh Call writing at the 19,700 and 19,800 strikes for the NIFTY 50. Fresh writing means new positions were added by sellers. This fresh Call writing, particularly above the current spot price, suggests that option writers are positioning for the index to face resistance at these higher levels. While there was some fresh Put writing at 19,600, it was notably less aggressive than the Call writing, reinforcing the idea of resistance being built up.

Similarly, for BANK NIFTY, the 44,000 strike held the highest Call Open Interest, acting as a strong overhead resistance. The highest Put Open Interest was at 43,500, indicating a potential support zone. Today, substantial fresh Call writing was observed at 44,000 and 44,200, further solidifying these as resistance points. Fresh Put writing at 43,800 was present but less dominant, again pointing to a cautious outlook from option sellers.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator that compares the number of traded Put options to the number of traded Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests a bullish sentiment as more Puts are being bought (or Calls sold), while a PCR below 1 often indicates a bearish sentiment as more Calls are being bought (or Puts sold).

For today, the NIFTY 50's PCR based on Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stood at 0.85, while the PCR based on today's trading flow (PCR(today's flow)) was 0.78. Both values are below 1, indicating that Call options outnumbered Put options in terms of outstanding contracts and fresh positions taken today. This combination suggests a cautious to slightly bearish sentiment among option participants for the NIFTY 50.

The BANK NIFTY's PCR(OI) was 0.75, and its PCR(today's flow) was 0.65. These even lower values suggest a more pronounced bearish sentiment in the banking index, with Call writing significantly outweighing Put writing both in existing positions and fresh activity.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It's often considered a "magnet effect" because, as an options expiry approaches, the underlying index or stock tends to gravitate towards its Max Pain strike. This is because option writers, who are typically large institutional players, try to manage their positions to minimise their losses.

For the upcoming expiry, the NIFTY 50's Max Pain strike is estimated at 19,600. With the index closing at 19,650, it sits just above this level. For the BANK NIFTY, the Max Pain strike is at 43,700, with the index closing at 43,800. As expiry draws closer, the influence of Max Pain tends to strengthen, potentially pulling the indices towards these levels.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often offers insights into broader market sentiment. Today, FIIs maintained a notably bearish stance in index futures. Their Index futures long ratio stood at 34.1%, meaning that for every 100 contracts they held, only about 34 were long (bets on price increase) while 66 were short (bets on price decrease). Specifically, they held 73,516 long contracts against 141,763 short contracts. Historically, such a low long ratio in index futures from FIIs has tended to coincide with periods of market caution or consolidation.

In contrast, FIIs showed a more balanced, slightly bullish stance in stock futures, with a long ratio of 57.8% (3,663,262 long contracts versus 2,671,711 short contracts). This suggests that while they are cautious on the broader index, they are selectively positive on individual stocks.

Observing individual stock movements can highlight specific areas of market interest. Among the top gainers in the F&O segment today were Reliance Industries, which saw a 1.5% uptick, TCS gaining 1.2%, and Bajaj Finance rising by 1.0%. These stocks likely attracted buying interest and potentially fresh long positions.

On the other side, HDFC Bank led the losers, declining 2.0%, followed by ICICI Bank which fell 1.8%, and Infosys dropping 1.5%. The significant declines in banking heavyweights like HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank align with the cautious sentiment observed in the BANK NIFTY's open interest data. These movements suggest profit-booking or fresh selling pressure in these counters.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we synthesise today's data points, a picture of caution emerges. The significant fresh Call writing in Nifty and Bank Nifty, coupled with low Put-Call Ratios, suggests that option writers are establishing resistance levels. This positioning indicates that they do not anticipate a significant upward move in the immediate future. The FIIs' continued net short positioning in index futures historically tends to coincide with a market that either consolidates or faces downward pressure. While individual stock movements can vary, the overall derivative data for the indices points to a market where upside could be capped, and support levels might be tested. Historically, when these signals align in this direction, the next session has tended to see range-bound activity or a continuation of the prevailing cautious sentiment.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals daily can empower you to form your own informed perspective. You can access Open Interest data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" for NIFTY, BANK NIFTY, and other indices. Look for the strikes with the highest Call OI and Put OI to identify potential resistance and support levels. You can also track "Change in OI" to see where fresh positions are being added or unwound.

For FII derivative positioning, the NSE publishes a daily "FII & DII Activity" or "Participant-wise Open Interest" report, usually available after market hours. This CSV file provides a detailed breakdown of FIIs' long and short positions in index futures and stock futures. By regularly reviewing these data points from official sources or through your broker's analytical tools, you can begin to identify patterns and understand the collective sentiment of large market participants, helping you to interpret market movements more effectively.
