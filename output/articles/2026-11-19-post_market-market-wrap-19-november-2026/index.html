What moved the market — the factors

Today's market sentiment in India appears to have been significantly buoyed by a major upgrade from Goldman Sachs, which turned bullish on the Indian stock market, upgrading its rating to 'Overweight' and setting a Nifty 50 target of 28,500 for December 2027. This positive outlook, citing expected earnings growth, resilient demand, and renewed foreign investment, likely fueled the substantial net buying by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) today, who pumped ₹5,867 crore into Indian equities. This FII enthusiasm was further echoed by other fund managers, who also turned overweight on India, marking the first such classification since January.

On the domestic front, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers, offloading ₹3,160 crore. Global cues presented a mixed picture. While some US tech stocks saw rallies driven by cybersecurity forecasts and initial AI IPO excitement, others like AMD faced headwinds due to reports of potential US scrutiny over China links. The offshore Chinese yuan dropped to its weakest level in over a month, indicating shrinking risk appetite in that region. Oil prices inched up due to tanker disruptions, despite an outlook for surplus supply.

Policy-wise, SEBI's roadmap focusing on faster IPOs and easier access for foreign investors also contributed to a positive long-term outlook for market efficiency and capital raising.

News that moved stocks

Goldman Sachs Turns Bullish on India, Fund Managers Follow Suit Goldman Sachs upgraded Indian equities to 'Overweight' with a Nifty 50 target of 28,500 by December 2027, citing strong earnings growth and renewed foreign investment. This positive sentiment was echoed by other fund managers who also turned overweight on India, marking a significant shift in global investment perspective towards the Indian market. This news is a major sentiment booster for the overall Indian market and could drive further FII inflows.

SEBI's Roadmap for Faster IPOs and Easier Foreign Investor Access The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) is working on streamlining IPO and rights issue timelines to enhance market efficiency and reduce friction in the capital-raising process. Additionally, the regulator aims to make it easier for foreign investors to access Indian markets. This initiative is expected to improve the ease of doing business and attract more foreign capital, positively impacting the broader market.

Real Estate Sector Sees Significant Activity India's six listed Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) distributed ₹3,234 crore to over 5.25 lakh unitholders in Q2, highlighting consistent returns for investors. Separately, Keystone Realtors announced plans to launch housing properties worth ₹5,000 crore in the Mumbai Metropolitan Region by March, while Signature Global acquired 10 acres of land in Gurugram for new realty projects. These developments suggest robust activity and growth potential within the Indian real estate sector.

Mixed Signals from Global Tech and AI Space Shares of AMD plunged by 8% following a report linking the company to Chinese firms, including those on the US government's blacklist, potentially inviting US scrutiny. In contrast, Palo Alto Networks' raised sales forecast powered a rally in cybersecurity stocks. The much-anticipated Anthropic IPO saw mixed reactions, with an initial surge in Nasdaq on hopes of reviving AI stocks, but later updates suggested it failed to reignite the AI trade, leading to a sharp fall in US stocks. These global tech developments could influence sentiment for Indian IT and tech-related stocks.

Offshore Chinese Yuan Weakens, Oil Prices Inch Up The offshore Chinese yuan dropped to its weakest level in over a month, as the dollar rallied and global risk appetite shrank. Meanwhile, oil prices saw a slight increase due to tanker disruptions, offsetting a broader outlook for surplus supply. These global macro factors can influence currency movements, import bills, and overall investor sentiment in India.
