Market Wrap — 28 May 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed marginally in the red.
  • Broader markets, represented by Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100, showed resilience with positive closes.
  • Media, Energy, and Metal sectors were the top performers, registering significant gains.
  • Banking, IT, and FMCG sectors faced selling pressure and ended the day as top losers.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued to be net sellers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong buying support.

How the market closed

The Indian equity markets witnessed a largely range-bound session today, with benchmark indices Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing slightly lower. The Nifty 50 ended the day at 23907.15, down by a marginal 0.03%, while the Bank Nifty declined by 0.43% to settle at 54853.85. In contrast, the broader market indices displayed strength, with the Nifty Midcap 100 gaining 0.42% and the Nifty Smallcap 100 rising 0.15%.

Market breadth

The market breadth leaned towards advances today, with 280 stocks advancing against 218 declines out of a total of 498 traded scrips. This indicates a moderately positive underlying sentiment, suggesting that while benchmark indices were subdued, a good number of individual stocks managed to gain ground.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, offloading Indian equities worth ₹1,043 crore today. However, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided robust support, pumping in ₹3,821 crore into the market.

| Investor Type | Net Cash (₹ crore) | | :------------ | :----------------- | | FII | -1,043 | | DII | +3,821 |

Looking at the 5-day history, FIIs have been net sellers for four out of the last five trading sessions, indicating a cautious stance. In contrast, DIIs have consistently been net buyers, absorbing the FII outflows and providing crucial stability to the Indian markets.

What moved markets today

Several factors contributed to the market's performance today, ranging from global economic data to domestic corporate developments and geopolitical tensions.

  • Global Inflation Concerns and Geopolitical Tensions: A key inflation gauge in the US worsened, with inflation jumping to 3.8% in April, the highest since May 2023. This, coupled with rising crude oil prices and fresh US-Iran strikes, sparked worries about global inflation and economic stability. Gold and silver prices plunged as these geopolitical uncertainties boosted the dollar and oil, triggering inflation fears.
  • US Market Performance and Jobless Claims: Despite rising crude oil prices and inflation concerns, US stocks held near record highs, bolstered by strong corporate profits. US jobless claims rose to 215,000, but layoffs remained low, suggesting a stable, albeit not rapidly growing, job market.
  • Real Estate Sector Developments: The real estate sector saw some significant news, with NorthWind Estates acquiring 5 acres of land in the Yamuna Expressway region for ₹189 crore. LIC is also reviewing its real estate portfolio to improve returns and explore a separate subsidiary. India emerged as a key logistics and data centre growth market in Asia Pacific, which saw a 19% rise in real estate investment in Q1.
  • ECB's Stance on Interest Rates: The European Central Bank's minutes revealed that holding rates in April was a "close call," signaling a potential readiness to raise borrowing costs in the near future. This could have implications for global liquidity and investor sentiment.

Setup for tomorrow

Tomorrow's session will likely be influenced by the lingering global inflation concerns and the ongoing geopolitical situation. Investors will also keep an eye on crude oil price movements, which have been volatile. The resilience shown by the broader market today, despite benchmark indices being flat, suggests underlying strength in mid and small-cap segments.

Globally, US stocks are holding near records despite inflation worries, while crude oil prices continue to rise. Asian markets will provide early cues tomorrow morning. Key data releases overnight and any further developments in the US-Iran situation will be closely watched. Sectors like Energy and Metals, which performed well today, might continue to see interest, while the banking sector could remain under pressure.
