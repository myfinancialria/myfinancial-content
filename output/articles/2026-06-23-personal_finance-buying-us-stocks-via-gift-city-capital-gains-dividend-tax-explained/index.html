Buying US Stocks via GIFT City: Capital Gains & Dividend Tax Explained

TL;DR

  • Investing in US stocks through an IFSC unit in GIFT City offers a structured route for Indian residents and NRIs.
  • Capital gains from transferring specified securities by an IFSC unit can be exempt under Section 47(viiab) if consideration is in foreign currency.
  • Dividend income from US stocks typically faces a 25% withholding tax in the US, which can be reduced to 15% under the India-US DTAA.
  • For Indian residents, dividends are taxable in India at slab rates, with a foreign tax credit available for US withholding tax.
  • NRIs investing via GIFT City generally face tax implications based on their residency and the DTAA between India and their country of residence.

What this means in plain terms

For many Indians, investing directly in US stocks has become an attractive option for portfolio diversification and exposure to global growth stories. While the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) allows individuals to send up to USD 250,000 abroad annually for investments, a newer route has emerged through the Gujarat International Finance Tec-City (GIFT City). GIFT City is India's first operational International Financial Services Centre (IFSC), designed to provide a global financial hub within India.

Investing in US stocks through an IFSC unit in GIFT City involves setting up an account with a broker or intermediary operating from this special economic zone. The primary appeal of this route, beyond ease of access, lies in its distinct tax treatment, which can differ significantly from direct LRS investments. Understanding these tax nuances, particularly regarding capital gains and dividend income, is crucial for both resident Indians and Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) looking to leverage this platform.

Understanding GIFT City and its Tax Framework

GIFT City, located in Gujarat, is India's ambitious project to create a global financial and IT services hub. It operates as a Special Economic Zone (SEZ) and hosts an International Financial Services Centre (IFSC), which provides a competitive tax regime and regulatory environment designed to attract international financial activities. The core idea is to bring offshore financial services onshore, providing Indian and global investors with an efficient platform.

Key Tax Benefits for IFSC Units

Units established within the IFSC at GIFT City enjoy several tax incentives under the Income Tax Act, 1961. These incentives are primarily aimed at promoting financial transactions and investments through this hub. One of the most significant benefits is the 100% tax exemption on income for ten consecutive years out of fifteen years, as per Section 80LA of the Income Tax Act. This applies to various incomes of an IFSC unit, including income from eligible financial services.

Furthermore, specific exemptions apply to transactions involving "specified securities" carried out by an IFSC unit. These include shares, debentures, units of mutual funds, and other instruments as notified. These tax benefits make GIFT City an attractive proposition for those looking to invest in international markets, including US stocks, with potentially lower tax liabilities compared to other routes.

Capital Gains Tax for US Stocks via GIFT City

The taxation of capital gains from US stocks purchased through an IFSC unit in GIFT City depends on several factors, including the investor's residency status and the specific provisions of the Income Tax Act.

Exemption under Section 47(viiab)

A crucial provision for investors routing their US stock investments through GIFT City is Section 47(viiab) of the Income Tax Act. This section provides that any transfer of a "specified security" by an IFSC unit, where the consideration for such transaction is paid in foreign currency, shall not be considered a "transfer" for the purpose of capital gains taxation. In simpler terms, if an IFSC unit sells US stocks (a specified security) and receives payment in foreign currency (like USD), the capital gains arising from this sale may be exempt from tax in India.

This exemption is a significant advantage for both resident Indians and NRIs investing through an IFSC unit. It effectively means that capital gains from the sale of US stocks, if routed and executed correctly through an IFSC entity, might not be subject to capital gains tax in India. However, it's essential to ensure that all conditions stipulated under Section 47(viiab) are met, including the transaction being carried out on a recognised stock exchange in the IFSC.

Short-Term vs. Long-Term Capital Gains

While Section 47(viiab) offers a broad exemption, it's still useful to understand the general capital gains framework. For assets not covered by such specific exemptions, US stocks would typically be considered "capital assets."

  • Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG): If US stocks are sold within 24 months of acquisition, the gains are classified as STCG and taxed at the investor's applicable income tax slab rates in India.
  • Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG): If held for more than 24 months, the gains are LTCG. These are generally taxed at 20% with indexation benefit, or 10% without indexation if the gains exceed ₹1 lakh for listed Indian equities, though the rules for foreign equities can differ.

However, with the applicability of Section 47(viiab) for transactions through an IFSC unit, these general STCG/LTCG rules might not apply, making the entire capital gain tax-free in India, provided the conditions are met.

Dividend Income Tax for US Stocks via GIFT City

Dividend income from US stocks, whether received directly or through an IFSC unit, is subject to taxation in both the source country (USA) and the investor's country of residence (India, for residents).

US Withholding Tax

When a US company pays dividends, the US government typically withholds a portion of the dividend as tax for non-resident investors. The standard withholding tax rate for non-residents in the US is 30%. However, due to the Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) between India and the USA, this rate is usually reduced to 15% for Indian residents. This means that if you receive a dividend of USD 100, USD 15 will be withheld by the US tax authorities, and you will receive USD 85.

Taxation in India for Resident Indians

For resident Indians, dividend income from foreign companies is fully taxable in India at their applicable income tax slab rates. This income needs to be declared in your Indian income tax return. To avoid double taxation (once in the US and once in India), the India-US DTAA allows for a Foreign Tax Credit (FTC). The 15% tax already withheld in the US can be claimed as a credit against your Indian tax liability on that dividend income, as per Section 90/91 of the Income Tax Act.

For example, if your marginal tax rate in India is 30% and you received a dividend on which 15% US tax was withheld, you would pay the remaining 15% tax in India after claiming the FTC. The process for claiming FTC involves reporting the foreign income and the tax paid on it in Form 67 and your Income Tax Return (ITR).

Taxation for Non-Resident Indians (NRIs)

For NRIs, the tax treatment of dividends from US stocks via GIFT City can be more complex and depends on their tax residency.

  • US Withholding Tax: Similar to resident Indians, NRIs will also face a 15% withholding tax in the US on dividends, thanks to the India-US DTAA.
  • Taxation in India: If an NRI is investing through an IFSC unit, the dividend income might not be taxable in India if they are not considered a resident for tax purposes in India and the income does not accrue or arise in India. However, the exact implications depend on the NRI's specific tax residency status (e.g., Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident - RNOR, or Non-Resident - NR) and the DTAA between India and their country of residence. Generally, if the NRI is a tax resident of another country (e.g., a Gulf country), they would typically report this income and claim credit for US tax withheld in their country of residence, based on that country's DTAA with the US.

A real example

Meet Anjali, 42, a software architect from Hyderabad with a CTC of ₹35 lakhs. She decided to invest in US stocks through an IFSC unit in GIFT City to diversify her portfolio.

Scenario:

  • Anjali invested ₹10 lakhs (approx. USD 12,000 at an exchange rate of ₹83.33/USD) in US stocks through an IFSC unit in May 2023.
  • In August 2025, she sold these stocks for ₹15 lakhs (approx. USD 18,000), resulting in a capital gain of ₹5 lakhs. The consideration was received in USD.
  • During her holding period, she also received USD 200 (approx. ₹16,666) in dividends from these US stocks.

Capital Gains Tax Calculation: Since Anjali invested through an IFSC unit and the transaction involved "specified securities" with consideration paid in foreign currency, the transfer is exempt under Section 47(viiab) of the Income Tax Act.

  • Capital Gain: ₹15,00,000 - ₹10,00,000 = ₹5,00,000
  • Indian Capital Gains Tax: ₹0 (due to Section 47(viiab) exemption).

Dividend Income Tax Calculation:

  • Total Dividend Received: USD 200 (approx. ₹16,666)
  • US Withholding Tax (15% under DTAA): 15% of USD 200 = USD 30 (approx. ₹2,500)
  • Net Dividend Received by Anjali (after US tax): USD 170 (approx. ₹14,166)

Anjali's Indian income tax slab is 30% (since her CTC is ₹35 lakhs, placing her in a higher bracket).

  • Indian tax on dividend income (at slab rate): 30% of ₹16,666 = ₹5,000
  • Foreign Tax Credit (for US withholding tax): ₹2,500
  • Net Indian Tax Payable on Dividends: ₹5,000 - ₹2,500 = ₹2,500

So, Anjali pays ₹0 in capital gains tax and ₹2,500 in Indian income tax on her dividends, in addition to the ₹2,500 already withheld in the US.

FAQ

What is GIFT City?

GIFT City is India's first operational International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) located in Gujarat, designed as a global financial and IT services hub with a competitive tax and regulatory environment.

How does Section 47(viiab) benefit me?

Section 47(viiab) of the Income Tax Act exempts capital gains from the transfer of specified securities by an IFSC unit if the consideration is paid in foreign currency, making such gains tax-free in India.

Will I pay tax in the US on my dividends?

Yes, the US typically withholds tax on dividends paid to non-resident investors. Under the India-US DTAA, this rate is usually reduced from 30% to 15%.

Can I claim a credit for US taxes paid?

Yes, resident Indians can claim a Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) for the tax withheld in the US against their Indian income tax liability on the same dividend income, as per the DTAA and Indian tax laws.

Is this route only for high-net-worth individuals?

While GIFT City offers sophisticated investment avenues, it is becoming increasingly accessible to retail investors through various platforms. However, understanding the tax complexities is crucial, regardless of investment size.

What are "specified securities" under Section 47(viiab)?

Specified securities typically include shares, debentures, units of mutual funds, and other instruments as notified by the government, traded on a recognised stock exchange in the IFSC.

Do NRIs investing via GIFT City pay tax in India?

For NRIs, the tax implications depend on their tax residency status and the DTAA between India and their country of residence. Generally, if they are not tax residents of India, income not accruing or arising in India might not be taxable in India.
