Your Insurance Just Got a Digital Upgrade: What IRDAI Chief Ajay Seth is Planning

TL;DR

  • IRDAI Chairman Ajay Seth recently discussed big changes coming to how insurance is sold and managed in India.
  • The regulator plans to cap commissions and expenses for insurance companies, aiming for lower policy costs.
  • A new Public Insurance Registry (PIR) is being built to make all your policy details easily accessible digitally.
  • These changes are meant to make insurance more affordable, transparent, and easier to manage for you.
  • IRDAI is listening to feedback from everyone before finalising these important reforms.

What's this about?

Ajay Seth, the Chairman of the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI), has been discussing significant upcoming reforms in the Indian insurance sector. These reforms focus on two main areas: changing how insurance products are sold, particularly by capping commissions and expenses, and introducing a new digital platform called the Public Insurance Registry (PIR) to centralise policy information. The overall goal is to make insurance more accessible, affordable, and transparent for ordinary Indians.

Why it's in the news right now

Mr. Ajay Seth was recently (on October 15, 2026) speaking at the Samvaad 2026 India Open Finance Summit in Mumbai. During his address, he provided updates on the proposed insurance distribution reforms and the development of the Public Insurance Registry (PIR). He assured stakeholders that IRDAI is open to suggestions, including a phased rollout of the commission changes, and that the aim is an "orderly transition" without disrupting existing business models or agents' livelihoods. The deadline for public feedback on the distribution reforms is October 25, 2026, and for the PIR consultation paper, it was October 17, 2026, making these topics very current.

How it actually works

1. Insurance Distribution Reforms:

  • The Proposal: IRDAI had previously released a consultation paper proposing to cap the commissions that insurance agents and brokers earn on various insurance products. It also suggested tightening the limits on how much insurers can spend on expenses related to their business.
  • The Goal: These caps are intended to reduce the overall cost of insurance policies for customers and to prevent 'mis-selling' – where an agent might push a product that earns them a higher commission, rather than one that is best for the customer.
  • Current Status: Mr. Seth has indicated that IRDAI is actively considering feedback from industry players and is open to changes, including a gradual, phased implementation of these new rules. The final implementation date could be January 1 or April 1 next year, depending on this feedback.

2. Public Insurance Registry (PIR):

  • What it is: The PIR will be a central digital platform where all your insurance policy details can be stored and accessed easily. Think of it as a single, secure online locker for all your insurance documents, regardless of which company issued them.
  • How it will be built: The existing Insurance Information Bureau (IIB) will be transformed into a not-for-profit company, which will then host and manage the PIR.
  • Timeline: IRDAI is targeting January 2028 for the beta launch (an early test version) of the Public Insurance Registry.
  • Benefits: This registry aims to standardise how insurance data is collected and stored across different insurers. It will make it easier for you, other financial institutions, and even insurers themselves to access and share necessary insurance information, with your consent. This could help with things like getting loans based on your insurance history or assessing your overall insurance needs more accurately.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine your local kirana shop. Earlier, the shopkeeper might have kept different types of goods (like pulses, spices, biscuits) in separate, unorganised boxes. It was hard to compare prices, know what was in stock, or even find what you needed quickly.

Now, imagine the government asking all kirana shops to:

  1. Standardise their pricing and profit margins on certain goods (like pulses) so that no shop charges unfairly high prices, and customers get a better deal. (This is like the insurance distribution reforms aiming to cap commissions and expenses for fairer policy costs).
  2. Create a central digital inventory where all shops list exactly what they have, how much it costs, and who bought what, all in one place. You, as a customer, could then easily check online what you bought from which shop, and even other services (like a delivery service) could quickly see your past orders (with your permission) to offer better services. (This is like the Public Insurance Registry making all your insurance details easily accessible and shareable digitally).

This makes the whole process more organised, fairer, and easier for everyone involved.

Why it matters to you

  1. Potentially Lower Premiums: By capping commissions and expenses, insurance companies might be able to offer policies at more competitive and affordable prices. This could save you money on your life, health, or general insurance.
  2. Reduced Mis-selling: With less incentive for agents to push high-commission products, you are more likely to be offered insurance plans that genuinely suit your needs, leading to better financial protection.
  3. Easier Access to Policy Information: The Public Insurance Registry means all your insurance policies, from different companies, will be in one digital place. No more hunting for physical documents or logging into multiple portals – just one login for all your insurance details.
  4. Faster Claims and Services: With standardised and easily accessible data, processes like claims settlement, policy renewals, and even switching insurers could become much faster and smoother.
  5. Better Financial Planning: Having a consolidated view of all your insurance coverage can help you and your financial advisor make more informed decisions about your future financial planning, including how insurance integrates with other financial products like loans.

Common confusions cleared up

Myth: The new rules will stop insurance agents from earning money, making it harder to buy policies. Fact: IRDAI Chairman Ajay Seth has clarified that the intent is not to disrupt agents' livelihoods or existing business models. The reforms aim for an "orderly transition" and may even create new business opportunities within the sector by making insurance more attractive and accessible to a wider audience.

Myth: The Public Insurance Registry means the government will have access to all my private financial details without my consent. Fact: The PIR is designed to improve data sharing and accessibility with your consent. It's about centralising your own policy information for your convenience and for authorised institutions to use with your permission, not about unauthorised access. Data privacy and security will be key considerations.

Myth: All these changes will happen overnight. Fact: Mr. Seth mentioned that IRDAI is open to a phased implementation of the commission reforms, possibly from January 1 or April 1 next year. The Public Insurance Registry is planned for a beta launch in January 2028. This indicates a gradual approach to allow the industry and public to adapt.

FAQ

Who is Ajay Seth?

Ajay Seth is the current Chairman of the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI), which is the main regulatory body for the insurance sector in India.

What is the Public Insurance Registry (PIR)?

The Public Insurance Registry (PIR) is a proposed digital platform that will centralise and standardise all insurance policy information, making it easier for policyholders to access their details and for authorised entities to share data.

When will these new insurance rules come into effect?

The final timeline for the insurance distribution reforms (like commission caps) depends on industry feedback, but IRDAI is considering January 1 or April 1 next year. The beta version of the Public Insurance Registry is targeted for launch in January 2028.

Will these changes affect all types of insurance?

The proposed commission caps apply across more than 30 product and distribution-channel categories, suggesting a broad impact across various types of insurance products. The Public Insurance Registry aims to cover all insurance policy details.

Where can I provide feedback on these proposed changes?

IRDAI has an ongoing consultation process. The deadline for stakeholder feedback on the distribution reforms is October 25, 2026, and for the Public Insurance Registry consultation paper, it was October 17, 2026. You can usually find details on how to submit feedback on the official IRDAI website.
