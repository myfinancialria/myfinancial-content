ITR-1 (Sahaj) AY 2026-27: Eligibility & Simple Filing Guide

TL;DR

  • ITR-1, or Sahaj, is designed for resident individuals with a total income up to ₹50 lakhs.
  • It simplifies tax filing for those primarily earning from salary, pension, one house property, and other specified sources.
  • Individuals with capital gains, business income, or who own more than one house property are not eligible for ITR-1.
  • The online filing process is streamlined with pre-filled data, making it quicker and less prone to errors.
  • Ensure all relevant documents like Form 16 and interest certificates are ready before you begin filing.

What this means in plain terms

The Income Tax Return (ITR) Form 1, commonly known as Sahaj, is the simplest of all ITR forms. It's specifically tailored for the average Indian taxpayer – typically salaried individuals or pensioners – to make their annual tax filing process straightforward. The government designed Sahaj to reduce the complexity of tax compliance for a large segment of the population.

If your income primarily comes from your job, a pension, or you have just one house property (which could be self-occupied or rented out), and your total income doesn't cross the ₹50 lakh mark, then ITR-1 is likely the form for you. It's a "sahaj" (easy) way to declare your income and pay your taxes without getting bogged down in intricate details meant for more complex financial situations.

Eligibility for ITR-1 (Sahaj) AY 2026-27

Understanding who can and cannot file ITR-1 is crucial to ensure you choose the correct form, thereby avoiding potential issues with the Income Tax Department.

Who Can File ITR-1 (Sahaj)?

ITR-1 is applicable for a Resident Individual (not a Resident Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) or a Non-Resident Indian (NRI)) whose total income for Assessment Year 2026-27 does not exceed ₹50 lakhs. The income sources must strictly fall into one or more of the following categories:

  • Income from Salary or Pension: This includes your regular employment income, allowances, perquisites, and any pension you receive.
  • Income from One House Property: This refers to income derived from a single house property, which can be self-occupied or let out. If the property is self-occupied, the annual value is typically considered nil, though interest paid on a home loan can be claimed as a deduction.
  • Income from Other Sources: This category typically includes interest income from savings accounts, fixed deposits, post office schemes, family pension, and dividend income up to ₹10 lakhs.

Who Cannot File ITR-1 (Sahaj)?

Several criteria make an individual ineligible for ITR-1, requiring them to file other ITR forms like ITR-2, ITR-3, or ITR-4. You cannot file ITR-1 if you are:

  • A Non-Resident Indian (NRI) or a Resident Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR).
  • An individual whose total income exceeds ₹50 lakhs.
  • Someone with income from more than one house property. Even if both properties are self-occupied, owning more than one house property makes you ineligible for ITR-1. You would typically need to file ITR-2 in such a scenario.
  • An individual with income from Capital Gains (e.g., from selling shares, mutual funds, or property).
  • Someone with income from business or profession.
  • A Director in a company.
  • An individual who has invested in unlisted equity shares at any time during the financial year.
  • Someone with agricultural income exceeding ₹5,000.
  • An individual with foreign assets or foreign income.
  • Someone claiming relief under Section 90/90A/91.
  • An individual who has paid tax under Section 194N (TDS on cash withdrawals).
  • An individual whose income includes winnings from lottery or horse races.

Understanding Income Sources for Sahaj

ITR-1 is specifically designed for straightforward income profiles. Let's delve into the types of income it accommodates.

Income from Salary/Pension

This is the most common income source for ITR-1 filers. It includes your basic salary, dearness allowance, house rent allowance, transport allowance, special allowances, perquisites, and any retirement or family pension received. Your employer typically provides Form 16, which summarises this income and the Tax Deducted at Source (TDS).

Income from One House Property

As established, ITR-1 is strictly for individuals with income from one house property. This can be:

  • Self-occupied property: If you live in the property, its annual value is considered nil. However, you can claim a deduction for interest paid on a home loan, up to ₹2 lakhs per financial year, for a self-occupied property.
  • Let-out property: If you have rented out your property, the rent received (or the fair rental value, whichever is higher) is considered income. Deductions for municipal taxes paid and a standard deduction of 30% of the Net Annual Value are allowed. Interest paid on a home loan for a let-out property can also be claimed as a deduction without a monetary limit, though the total loss from house property that can be set off against other income in a given year is capped at ₹2 lakhs.

It is critical to reiterate: if you own more than one house property, regardless of whether they are self-occupied or rented out, you are not eligible to file ITR-1. This is a common point of confusion, especially with discussions around claiming interest deductions for two self-occupied properties. While the Income Tax Act does allow treating up to two properties as self-occupied for income calculation purposes (i.e., their annual value is nil), the eligibility for ITR-1 still restricts you to having income from one house property source. If you own multiple properties, you must file ITR-2.

Income from Other Sources

This category covers various other types of income that are not from salary, pension, or house property. For ITR-1, this typically includes:

  • Interest income: From savings bank accounts, fixed deposits, recurring deposits, and post office schemes.
  • Family pension: Pension received by family members after the death of the employee.
  • Dividend income: Dividends received from Indian companies are taxable in the hands of the recipient. For ITR-1, the total dividend income must not exceed ₹10 lakhs.

Capital Gains Limit & ITR-1

A crucial point to remember for ITR-1 eligibility is the complete exclusion of capital gains. If you have any income from capital gains, you are automatically ineligible to file ITR-1.

What are Capital Gains?

Capital gains arise from the sale of a capital asset. A capital asset can be anything from shares, mutual funds, gold, jewellery, land, or buildings. These gains are categorised into:

  • Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG): Arise from assets held for a shorter period, as defined by tax laws (e.g., equity shares held for less than 12 months, real estate for less than 24 months).
  • Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG): Arise from assets held for a longer period.

Examples of situations that would make you ineligible for ITR-1 due to capital gains include:

  • Selling equity shares or equity-oriented mutual funds.
  • Selling debt mutual funds.
  • Selling a house property (unless it's your primary residence and certain exemptions apply, but even then, the gain itself typically requires ITR-2).
  • Selling gold or other precious metals.

Even if the capital gain is exempt from tax (e.g., LTCG from equity up to ₹1 lakh under Section 112A), the very fact that you have capital gains income means you cannot use ITR-1. In such cases, you would need to file ITR-2.

Online Filing Process for ITR-1

Filing ITR-1 online is the most popular and recommended method, thanks to its simplicity and the pre-filled data facility. The Income Tax Department's e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in) is the official platform.

Prerequisites for Online Filing

Before you begin, ensure you have the following documents and information handy:

  1. PAN and Aadhaar Number: Mandatory for filing.
  2. Bank Account Details: Ensure your pre-validated bank account details are updated on the e-filing portal for receiving refunds.
  3. Form 16: Issued by your employer, detailing salary income and TDS.
  4. Form 26AS/Annual Information Statement (AIS)/Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS): These statements provide a comprehensive view of all your financial transactions and TDS/TCS details. It's crucial to reconcile these with your own records.
  5. Interest Certificates: From banks for savings accounts and fixed deposits.
  6. Investment Proofs: For deductions claimed under Section 80C, 80D, 80G, etc. (e.g., life insurance premium receipts, PPF passbook, ELSS statements, health insurance premium receipts).
  7. Rental Receipts (if applicable): For HRA claims.

Step-by-Step Online Filing Guide

  1. Log in to the e-filing portal: Visit incometax.gov.in and log in using your PAN/Aadhaar and password. If you're a new user, register first.
  2. Select 'e-File' -> 'Income Tax Returns' -> 'File Income Tax Return'.
  3. Choose the Assessment Year: Select '2026-27'.
  4. Select 'Online' mode: For easier filing directly on the portal.
  5. Select 'Individual' as the Status.
  6. Choose ITR Form 1 (Sahaj).
  7. Proceed with Pre-filled Data: The portal will often show pre-filled data based on information available with the Income Tax Department (e.g., salary details from Form 26AS, interest income). It is critical to verify this pre-filled data with your own documents (Form 16, bank statements, etc.). Make any necessary corrections.
  8. Fill in Details:

    9. * Personal Information: Verify your name, address, PAN, Aadhaar, and bank account details. * Gross Total Income: This section will have pre-filled salary details. Add income from one house property and other sources. * Deductions: Enter eligible deductions under Chapter VI-A (e.g., 80C, 80D, 80CCD(1B)). * Tax Computed & Tax Paid: The system will automatically calculate your tax liability. Verify TDS/TCS details. If any additional tax is payable, you can pay it online.

  9. Preview and Validate: Carefully review all the information entered. The system will run validations and highlight any errors. Correct them before proceeding.
  10. Submit and E-Verify: Once validated, click 'Proceed to Verification'. You can e-verify your return immediately using Aadhaar OTP, Net Banking, Demat Account, or by sending a physical ITR-V form to CPC, Bengaluru. E-verification is the quickest and preferred method.

A real example

Let's consider Anjali, 35, earning ₹22 lakhs CTC, living in Hyderabad. She is a salaried employee, receives ₹1.5 lakhs as HRA, and has invested in ELSS and PPF. She also has a savings bank account with ₹15,000 interest income and owns one self-occupied house property for which she pays ₹2.5 lakhs as home loan interest annually.

Here's how Anjali would approach her ITR-1 filing:

  1. Eligibility Check:

    2. * Resident Individual: Yes. * Total Income < ₹50 lakhs: Yes (₹22 lakhs salary + ₹15,000 interest). * Income sources: Salary, one self-occupied house property (where interest deduction is claimed), and interest from other sources. No capital gains, no business income, no foreign assets. * Conclusion: Anjali is eligible to file ITR-1.

  1. Gathering Documents:

    2. * Form 16 from her employer. * Bank passbook/statement for interest income. * Home loan interest certificate. * Investment proofs for ELSS and PPF. * Aadhaar and PAN.

  1. Filling ITR-1 (Simplified Steps):

    2. * Personal Information: She verifies her pre-filled details. * Gross Salary: From Form 16, say ₹22,00,000. * Exemptions: Standard Deduction of ₹50,000. HRA exemption as per rules (let's assume ₹70,000 is exempt after calculation). * Income from House Property: She would declare 'Self-occupied' and claim the home loan interest deduction of ₹2,00,000 (capped for self-occupied property). This would result in a loss of ₹2,00,000 under 'Income from House Property'. * Income from Other Sources: ₹15,000 (bank interest). * Gross Total Income (before Chapter VI-A deductions): * (₹22,00,000 - ₹50,000 - ₹70,000) + (₹15,000) - ₹2,00,000 = ₹18,95,000. * Deductions (Chapter VI-A): * Section 80C: PPF (₹1,00,000) + ELSS (₹50,000) = ₹1,50,000 (full limit). * Section 80TTA: Deduction for savings bank interest up to ₹10,000. * Total Deductions: ₹1,50,000 + ₹10,000 = ₹1,60,000. * Taxable Income: ₹18,95,000 - ₹1,60,000 = ₹17,35,000. * The system will then calculate her tax liability based on the applicable tax regime (old or new, as chosen by her), deduct any TDS from Form 16, and show if any tax is payable or refundable.

Anjali would carefully review all sections, especially the pre-filled data, before submitting and e-verifying her return.

What to do this week

To prepare for your ITR-1 filing for Assessment Year 2026-27, here are a few concrete steps you can take in the next 30 minutes:

  1. Locate Form 16 and other key documents: Dig out your Form 16 (for FY 2025-26), bank statements for interest income, and any home loan interest certificates. Having these readily accessible will save time later.
  2. Check Form 26AS/AIS/TIS: Log in to the Income Tax e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in) and download your Form 26AS, Annual Information Statement (AIS), and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS) for FY 2025-26. Cross-verify the TDS details and other financial transactions against your own records.
  3. Validate Bank Account: Ensure your primary bank account is pre-validated on the e-filing portal. This is essential for receiving any tax refunds promptly. If not, update and validate it.

FAQ

Can NRIs file ITR-1?

No, ITR-1 (Sahaj) is exclusively for Resident Individuals. Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are not eligible to file ITR-1 and typically need to file ITR-2.

What if my income exceeds ₹50 lakhs?

If your total income for the financial year exceeds ₹50 lakhs, you are not eligible to file ITR-1. You would generally need to file ITR-2 or ITR-3, depending on your income sources.

Can I file ITR-1 if I have a capital gain?

No, individuals with any income from capital gains (short-term or long-term, from any asset) are not eligible to file ITR-1. They must file ITR-2.

Is it mandatory to e-verify ITR-1?

Yes, it is mandatory to verify your ITR-1 after submission. E-verification is the quickest and most preferred method, using Aadhaar OTP, Net Banking, or Demat account. Without verification, your ITR is considered invalid.

What is the due date for filing ITR-1 for AY 2026-27?

For most individual taxpayers not requiring an audit, the due date for filing ITR for Assessment Year 2026-27 (Financial Year 2025-26) is typically July 31, 2026. Please always refer to the official Income Tax Department notifications for any specific changes or extensions.

Can I file ITR-1 if I own two self-occupied properties?

No, if you own more than one house property, even if both are self-occupied, you are not eligible to file ITR-1. ITR-1 is specifically for individuals with income from one house property. In such a scenario, you would generally need to file ITR-2.
