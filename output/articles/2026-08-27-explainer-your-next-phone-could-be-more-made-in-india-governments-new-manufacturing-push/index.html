Your Next Phone Could Be More 'Made in India': Government's New Manufacturing Push

TL;DR

  • The government has launched a new ₹62,500 crore scheme to boost mobile phone manufacturing in India.
  • Called the Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme (MPMS), it offers cash incentives to companies that increase their production and sales of phones made in India.
  • It has two parts: one for big manufacturers and another with higher support for Indian-owned smartphone brands.
  • The goal is to make India a bigger global hub for mobile phone production and to grow local brands.
  • This could mean more affordable phones, more jobs, and less reliance on imports for you.

What's this about?

The Indian government has recently introduced a significant new policy called the Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme (MPMS). This scheme is designed to encourage companies to make more mobile phones right here in India. It's a special kind of incentive program, where the government offers money back to manufacturers based on how much their sales grow from products made within the country. The aim is to make India a strong global player in mobile phone manufacturing, create more jobs, and help Indian brands grow and compete with international ones.

Why it's in the news right now

This new Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme (MPMS) was approved in July 2026 and officially notified in August 2026, making it a very current topic. It is set to run for five years, from the financial year 2026-27 to 2030-31. Just recently, on August 21, 2026, Prime Minister Narendra Modi spoke at the Economic Times World Leaders Forum, highlighting the overall success of Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes in India. He mentioned that these schemes have attracted over ₹2.5 lakh crore in actual investments, generated ₹22 lakh crore in production and sales, and created over 14 lakh jobs across various sectors. The new MPMS builds on this successful model, specifically targeting the mobile phone sector to further its growth and impact.

How it actually works

The Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme (MPMS) is a type of Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme. Think of it as a reward system for companies that achieve specific manufacturing goals. Here’s how it works:

  1. Performance-Based Incentives: The government doesn't just give money upfront. Instead, it offers financial incentives (cashbacks) to companies. These incentives are "production-linked," meaning they are paid out only after a company manufactures and sells more mobile phones from its Indian factories compared to a "base year." The more a company increases its production and sales in India, the more incentive it can receive.
  1. Two Target Segments: The MPMS is smartly designed with two main groups of companies in mind:

    2. * Segment 1: Large Manufacturers and Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) Companies: This part of the scheme focuses on big players, both Indian and international, who already make phones or provide manufacturing services. To qualify, a mobile phone manufacturer in India needs to have had a minimum turnover of ₹10,000 crore in the financial year 2025-26. For Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) companies, which build components or assemble devices for other brands, the minimum turnover requirement is ₹1,000 crore in FY 2025-26. These companies receive incentives based on their incremental (additional) sales over their previous year's figures. * Segment 2: Indian Smartphone Brands: This segment is specifically designed to boost homegrown mobile phone companies. It offers higher incentives to promote the growth of genuinely Indian brands. To qualify as an Indian brand under this scheme, a company must be registered in India, its brand name and intellectual property (like patents and designs) must be held in India, and Indian citizens must have control over its management (owning more than 51% of the company). It also needs to have its own design and research & development (R&D) capabilities within the country. These Indian brands can get a 5% incentive on their incremental sales. They can also receive an additional 3% for designing and developing products in India and up to 1.5% for sourcing components locally.

  1. Scheme Duration and Outlay: The MPMS has a total budget, or "outlay," of ₹62,500 crore. This money will be distributed over the five-year period from FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine your local cricket club wants to encourage more young players to join and improve their game. So, the club announces a special "Performance Bonus" scheme. If a young player practices extra hard and scores more runs or takes more wickets than they did last season, the club gives them a bonus. They even have a special, higher bonus for players who not only score well but also help coach younger kids and bring new Indian-made equipment to the club.

The Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme is similar. The government (the cricket club) wants more mobile phones to be 'Made in India'. So, it offers incentives (performance bonuses) to companies (players) that increase their phone production and sales (score more runs/wickets). And just like the club offers a special bonus for players who also develop local talent and use Indian equipment, the government gives higher incentives to Indian brands that design, develop, and source components locally.

Why it matters to you

This new scheme can impact your everyday life and finances in several ways:

  1. More Affordable "Made in India" Phones: When companies manufacture more phones locally, they can save on import duties and logistics costs. These savings can sometimes be passed on to you, potentially making smartphones and feature phones more affordable.
  2. More Job Opportunities: Increased manufacturing means factories need more workers. This can create thousands of new direct and indirect jobs in areas like assembly, testing, quality control, and logistics across India.
  3. Growth of Indian Phone Brands: With special incentives for Indian brands, you might see more innovative and competitive mobile phones from homegrown companies. This gives you more choices and fosters local entrepreneurship.
  4. Reduced Reliance on Imports: As more phones are made in India, our country becomes less dependent on importing them from other nations. This strengthens India's economy and can help stabilize prices during global supply chain disruptions.
  5. Boost to Related Industries: Mobile phone manufacturing isn't just about assembling phones. It also boosts demand for components like batteries, chargers, screens, and other electronic parts. This can lead to growth in these supporting industries too.

Common confusions cleared up

Myth: The government is just giving free money to phone companies. Fact: This is a "Production Linked Incentive" scheme. Companies only receive money after they have successfully increased their manufacturing and sales in India beyond a certain baseline. It's a reward for actual performance and growth.

Myth: This scheme only benefits big foreign companies. Fact: While large manufacturers, including international ones, can benefit, a significant part of the scheme is specifically designed to support and encourage Indian-owned smartphone brands. These Indian brands get higher incentives if they also focus on local design, research, and component sourcing.

Myth: All phones will now be 100% Indian-made overnight. Fact: Building a complete manufacturing ecosystem takes time. The scheme aims to increase "domestic value addition," meaning more parts and processes will gradually be done in India. It's a journey towards greater self-reliance, not an instant switch.

FAQ

What is a Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme?

A PLI scheme is a government program that offers financial rewards to companies for increasing their production and sales of specific goods manufactured within the country. The incentives are "linked" to their "production" performance.

What kind of companies can benefit from the MPMS?

Both large mobile phone manufacturers and Electronics Manufacturing Services (EMS) companies, as well as eligible Indian smartphone brands, can benefit if they meet the specified turnover and incremental sales criteria.

Will mobile phones become cheaper because of this scheme?

The scheme aims to boost local manufacturing, which can reduce costs for companies. While there's no guarantee, these cost savings can potentially lead to more competitively priced mobile phones for consumers in India.

How does this scheme help "Make in India"?

By offering incentives for local production, the scheme directly encourages companies to set up or expand manufacturing units in India, use more local components, and even develop products domestically, thereby strengthening the "Make in India" initiative.

What does "incremental sales" mean in this context?

"Incremental sales" refers to the increase in the value of goods sold by a company in a given year, compared to a pre-defined base year. The incentives are calculated on this additional sales value.
