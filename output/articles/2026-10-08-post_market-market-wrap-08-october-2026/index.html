Market Wrap — 08 October 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian markets witnessed a sharp decline today, with Nifty 50 falling over 1.6%.
  • Persistent FII selling, a hawkish RBI policy stance, and rising global crude oil prices were the primary drivers of the downturn.
  • LIC Housing Finance Ltd. emerged as the top Nifty 500 gainer, while Adani Green Energy Ltd. was the biggest loser.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree, offloading ₹6,121 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support with net buying of ₹4,597 crore.
  • India VIX surged over 10%, indicating a significant increase in market volatility.
  • F&O positioning data is unavailable for today's wrap and will be populated from the next session.

How the market behaved today

Indian markets experienced a significant downturn today, with the benchmark Nifty 50 plunging to an 18-month low of 22,231.80, marking a 1.64% decline. The session saw a gap down opening followed by a continuous downward trend, hitting a 52-week low during the day. The Bank Nifty also closed lower at 54,515.05, down 0.98%. Broader markets mirrored the weakness, with Midcap and Smallcap indices shedding over 2% each.

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, surged by 10.01% to 15.28, reflecting heightened investor anxiety and uncertainty in the market.

What moved the market — the factors

The Indian market witnessed a sharp decline today, primarily driven by a confluence of negative factors. Persistent selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) for the fifth consecutive session, coupled with a hawkish stance from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on policy tightening, significantly battered investor sentiment. Rising bond yields and a weakening rupee further added to the market's woes.

Global cues also played a crucial role, with US markets falling as surging crude oil prices and elevated Treasury yields stoked inflation fears. Brent crude oil surged over 3.6%, crossing the $103 mark, which threatens to keep inflation elevated and potentially trigger more rate hikes globally.

Specific sector-related news also impacted the market. The IT sector, despite being among the 'gaining' sectors, saw its major players like Infosys and Wipro ADRs fall nearly 3% in US trading due to concerns over TCS's quarterly results and a US crackdown on the H-1B-linked green card program. A significant block deal in ITC, where a GQG-linked fund sold shares worth nearly ₹9,437 crore, also contributed to the overall cautious mood. The broad-based selling was evident across most sectors, with Metal, Realty, and Energy being the biggest laggards.

News that moved stocks

IT Sector Under Pressure from US Policies & Earnings

  • Infosys, Wipro ADRs fall nearly 3% after TCS earnings, US crackdown on H-1B-linked green card programme: Infosys and Wipro ADRs declined in US trading following TCS's quarterly results and new US immigration restrictions, raising concerns over technology spending and tighter H-1B policies. This news contributed to the slight negative performance of the IT sector despite it being among the top 'gainer' sectors today.
  • Infosys, Wipro ADRs drop 3% as Cognizant, Infosys, TCS, Wipro, HCL among IT firms suspended from US green card programme: The Trump administration's suspension of major technology outsourcing firms from a green card program for H-1B workers led to a 3% drop in Infosys and Wipro ADRs, impacting investor sentiment towards the Indian IT sector.

Macroeconomic Headwinds & Market Sentiment

  • Indian markets plunged on Thursday as RBI policy tightening, persistent FII selling, rising bond yields and rupee weakness battered sentiment: The combination of a hawkish RBI, continuous FII outflows, increasing bond yields, and a depreciating rupee created significant downside pressure on the overall Indian market.
  • US stocks fall as higher yields and oil prices flag inflation risk: US markets experienced a decline as surging crude oil prices and elevated Treasury yields fueled inflation concerns, contributing to negative global cues for Indian equities.
  • Nifty sinks to an 18-month low as rate fears bite: The Nifty's continued slide, now in its ninth straight week, deepened concerns over further market losses, with surging oil prices threatening to keep inflation elevated and trigger more rate hikes.

Corporate Specific Developments

  • Rs 9,395 crore block deal! GQG exits ITC shares after a sharp 30% crash this year: A significant block deal where a GQG-linked fund sold 36.51 crore shares of ITC, valued at nearly ₹9,437 crore, while Fidelity and major Indian mutual funds bought stakes, brought ITC into focus and highlighted institutional activity.

  • RBI's 29-day VRRR auction receives tepid response despite high surplus liquidity: The Reserve Bank of India's 29-day variable rate reverse repo auction, which attracted ₹79,655 crore in bids, received a tepid response despite high surplus liquidity, indicating cautiousness in the banking system.

Market breadth

The market breadth was overwhelmingly negative today, with 461 declines against only 33 advances out of a total of 494 traded stocks. This indicates a broad-based selling pressure across the market, suggesting that the downturn was not limited to a few specific stocks or sectors.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their aggressive selling for the fifth consecutive session, offloading a substantial ₹6,121 crore from the Indian equity market today. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, provided some counter-balance by net buying ₹4,597 crore.

Over the past five trading sessions, FIIs have been consistent net sellers, with outflows ranging from ₹2,961 crore to ₹10,148 crore daily. In contrast, DIIs have consistently been net buyers, providing crucial support to the market, with daily inflows between ₹4,597 crore and ₹11,272 crore. This persistent FII selling trend has been a significant drag on market sentiment and a key factor behind the recent market weakness.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's market session was predominantly driven by a combination of a hawkish stance from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and persistent selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs). The news reports highlighted that RBI's policy tightening, coupled with rising bond yields and a weakening rupee, significantly dampened investor sentiment. This macro environment, along with surging global crude oil prices, which threaten to keep inflation elevated and prolong the period of higher interest rates, led to a broad-based market decline. The Nifty 50, in particular, reacted sharply, plunging to an 18-month low. While no single major event like a policy decision or earnings release dominated the day, the cumulative effect of these macroeconomic factors and continuous FII outflows created a strong bearish sentiment across all sectors, as evidenced by the widespread declines and the surge in India VIX.

Setup for tomorrow

Looking ahead to tomorrow, Indian markets are likely to remain under pressure given the continued FII selling and the prevailing global macroeconomic concerns. Global cues suggest caution, with Brent Crude holding above $103 and WTI Crude above $91, indicating persistent inflation risks. The USD/INR closed higher at 96.77, reflecting rupee weakness. US 10-year yields are still elevated despite a slight dip today. Investors will be watching global crude oil prices, bond yields, and the trajectory of FII flows closely. Key levels for Nifty 50 will be crucial to watch, with the current chart pattern indicating increasing downside pressure.
