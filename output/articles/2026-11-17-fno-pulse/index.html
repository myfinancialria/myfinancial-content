India F&O Pulse — 17 Nov 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Mixed Signals Emerge Across Indian Indices as Nifty Stays Range-bound, Sensex Leans Bullish, and Bank Nifty Shows Bearish Undertones.

TL;DR

  • Nifty 50's option data suggests a range-bound movement, with significant open interest at both higher and lower levels.
  • Sensex positioning indicates a mildly bullish sentiment, primarily driven by fresh put writing at lower strikes.
  • Bank Nifty shows a mildly bearish bias, influenced by call writing at higher levels and its max pain point sitting below the current spot.

What the indices did today

Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, like options or futures, that have not yet been settled. For options, the strike price with the highest Call OI often acts as a significant resistance level, as many traders have sold (written) Calls at that strike, expecting the index not to rise above it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI typically serves as a strong support level, as many traders have sold (written) Puts there, anticipating the index will not fall below it.

Today, for the Nifty 50, we observed the heaviest fresh Call writing near the 25,633 level. This suggests that Call sellers (also known as Call writers) believe the Nifty might struggle to move significantly above this point, potentially capping its upside. On the Put side, fresh writing was concentrated near 24,966, indicating that Put sellers view this level as a potential floor, expecting the index to find support there.

For the Sensex, the most notable fresh Put writing was seen around 81,166. This positioning by Put sellers suggests an expectation of support at or above this level.

In Bank Nifty, fresh Call writing was prominent near 62,766, implying that Call sellers anticipate this level to act as a ceiling. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing around 61,433 suggests that Put sellers expect this to be a supportive level.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator derived from Open Interest. It is calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 generally suggests a bullish sentiment, as more puts are being written (indicating expectations of support), while a value below 1 typically points to a bearish sentiment, with more calls being written (suggesting expectations of resistance).

For the Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stood at 0.93, indicating a relatively balanced book with a slight lean towards Call writing. The PCR based on today's flow of new Open Interest (ΔPCR) was 1.13, suggesting a mixed to slightly bullish flow during the day. This combination points towards a balanced to range-bound outlook.

The Sensex recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.95, also indicating a fairly balanced options book. Its ΔPCR for today's flow was 1.19, suggesting a slightly more bullish sentiment from the day's fresh option activity.

Bank Nifty's PCR(OI) was 0.96, similar to the other indices in indicating a balanced options book overall. However, its ΔPCR for today's flow was -475.91, which is an unusually high negative number, typically indicative of significant Call writing or Put unwinding, suggesting a strong bearish bias from the day's fresh activity.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying index were to expire at that level. It's often considered a "magnetic" point, as the index tends to gravitate towards it, especially as the expiry date approaches.

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain point is at 25,350, which is very close to its current spot of 25,387.40. This proximity suggests a strong pull towards this level by expiry.

The Sensex's Max Pain is at 82,600, also very near its spot of 82,532.19, indicating a similar magnetic pull.

However, for Bank Nifty, the Max Pain point is at 61,900, which is approximately 0.6% below its current spot of 62,273.25. When Max Pain sits below the current spot, it can suggest a potential downward pull towards that level as expiry nears.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant players in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning is closely watched. Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 33.4%, meaning that out of their total Index futures positions, about one-third were long contracts, and two-thirds were short. Historically, a lower FII Index futures long ratio, where short positions outweigh long positions significantly, has tended to coincide with periods of caution or consolidation in the broader market.

In stock futures, FIIs had a long ratio of 57.8%, indicating a net long position in individual stock futures.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we combine these various signals, a nuanced picture emerges for the upcoming session. The Nifty 50's option chain, with balanced PCRs and Max Pain near spot, historically tends to coincide with continued range-bound activity, where strong moves in either direction might find resistance or support at the observed OI walls. The Sensex, with its mildly bullish bias from fresh Put writing, has historically tended to see some underlying strength, although overall market balance suggests caution. Bank Nifty's mildly bearish undertones, stemming from significant Call writing and Max Pain below spot, have historically tended to coincide with potential downward pressure or consolidation, even after a strong positive day. The FIIs' net short positioning in Index futures has historically tended to coincide with a cautious sentiment among institutional players, which can influence overall market direction.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements more effectively. You can start by visiting the NSE India website (archives.nseindia.com) to download the daily Bhavcopy and the F&O Bhavcopy. The Option Chain data for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty is also readily available on the NSE website or through your broker's trading platform.

Once you have the data, look for the strike prices with the highest Call Open Interest and Put Open Interest to identify potential resistance and support levels. Calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI to gauge overall sentiment. Pay attention to "Max Pain" as it often indicates a gravitational pull for the index. Finally, review the FII derivative positioning from the NSE participant-wise Open Interest file to understand how institutional investors are positioned. Combining these data points daily can help you form a more informed perspective on market dynamics.
