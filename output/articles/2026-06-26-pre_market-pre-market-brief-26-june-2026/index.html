Pre-Market Brief — 26 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets presented a mixed picture, with Asian indices, particularly Japan and South Korea, experiencing significant declines.
  • Crude oil prices eased, with both Brent and WTI falling, which could offer some relief on the inflation front for India.
  • Indian Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong support yesterday, recording substantial net buying of ₹5,748 crore.
  • The Indian residential real estate market showed robust growth in Q2 2026, with sales up 19% and new launches surging 43%.
  • FMCG stocks in India have faced considerable pressure year-to-date, with the sector index declining 10%.

Where global markets closed

Global markets closed with a mixed sentiment, leaning negative, especially across Asia. In the US, the Dow Jones managed a modest gain of 0.14%, while the Nasdaq and S&P 500 saw marginal declines of 0.46% and 0.01% respectively. European markets, represented by the FTSE 100, closed higher by 0.65%. However, Asian markets witnessed significant selling pressure, with Japan's Nikkei 225 plunging 4.47%, the Hang Seng down 1.80%, and the Shanghai Composite falling 1.91%. This indicates a cautious to negative overall global tone, heavily influenced by the sharp downturns in key Asian economies.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee showed a slight strengthening against the US Dollar, with USD/INR closing at 94.39, down 0.06%. This marginal appreciation could provide some stability. The Dollar Index also saw a minor dip of 0.05% to 101.38, indicating a slight weakening of the greenback globally.

In the commodities market, crude oil prices eased, which is generally a positive for India, a major oil importer. Brent Crude fell by 1.18% to $74.37 per barrel, while WTI Crude dropped 1.75% to $70.66 per barrel. This decline is attributed to easing fears of supply disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz, despite ongoing Middle East tensions. Gold prices saw a slight uptick of 0.18% to $4037.70, while Silver declined by 1.78% to $57.31. Data for the US 10-year yield is unavailable.

Recent FII/DII flows

Yesterday, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were significant net buyers in the Indian equity markets, injecting ₹5,748 crore. Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) also turned net buyers, albeit with a smaller figure of ₹384 crore.

Over the last five trading sessions, DIIs have consistently been net buyers, demonstrating strong domestic support for the markets. FII flows, however, have been more volatile, with net selling observed on three out of the last five days, though they ended yesterday as net buyers.

Overnight news that matters

Crude oil prices fall up to 2%, head for weekly losses as Hormuz supply fears ease Global crude oil prices declined sharply, with Brent falling below $75 a barrel, as concerns about supply disruptions through the Strait of Hormuz diminished. This matters to Indian markets as lower crude oil prices can help reduce India's import bill, ease inflationary pressures, and potentially improve the current account deficit, benefiting sectors reliant on crude as a raw material or those impacted by fuel costs.

Housing sales in top 9 cities rise 19% in Q2 2026 as supply jumps 43%; Bengaluru leads demand: PropEquity India's residential real estate market showed strong performance in Q2 2026, with housing sales increasing 19% year-on-year across major cities, and new launches surging by 43%. This positive trend in real estate is significant for Indian markets as it indicates robust consumer demand and economic activity, potentially boosting sectors like cement, steel, home finance, and other allied industries.

US Stock Market: Sticky inflation keeps September Fed rate hike on table despite July pause expectations Despite expectations of a pause in rate hikes at the US Federal Reserve's July meeting, sticky inflation, which hit a three-year high, suggests a September rate hike remains a possibility. This news is crucial for Indian markets as the US Fed's monetary policy decisions significantly influence global liquidity and capital flows, impacting FII sentiment and the attractiveness of emerging markets like India.

South Korean stocks in tailspin as Kospi crashes 8%, head for worst week since March South Korean stocks experienced a dramatic plunge of over 8%, triggering a trading halt, primarily driven by a significant selloff in major semiconductor companies. This sharp decline in a major Asian economy, particularly in the technology sector, signals broader regional market weakness and could contribute to a cautious sentiment in Indian markets, especially for IT and tech-related stocks.

ITC, HUL among 10 FMCG stocks that have tumbled up to 31% in 2026. FMCG stocks in India have been under considerable pressure in 2026, with the sector index declining 10% year-to-date due to geopolitical disruptions and inflationary pressures. This highlights a challenging environment for consumer staples and discretionary spending, which could impact the performance of major FMCG players like ITC and HUL in today's session.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Global Market Cues: Investors should closely monitor the performance of other Asian markets, especially given the sharp declines in Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's Kospi yesterday, which could influence sentiment on the Indian bourses.
  2. Crude Oil Prices: Continued movements in crude oil prices will be important. A sustained fall could provide further relief for India's economy and specific sectors.
  3. Real Estate Sector: Stocks in the real estate, housing finance, and allied sectors (like cement, building materials) could see increased activity following the strong Q2 2026 housing sales data.
  4. FMCG Stocks: Watch for continued pressure or any signs of reversal in FMCG stocks, given their year-to-date underperformance and the recent news highlighting challenges in the sector.
  5. FII/DII Flows: The trend of FII and DII flows will remain a key indicator. Sustained DII buying could continue to provide a floor for the market, while FII sentiment will be crucial for broader market direction.

Bottom line

Indian markets are likely to open with a cautious bias today, influenced by the mixed global cues and significant weakness observed in key Asian markets. While the strong support from Domestic Institutional Investors yesterday and easing crude oil prices offer some positives, the broader global sentiment regarding inflation and interest rates, particularly from the US Fed, remains a critical factor. Investors should brace for potential volatility and focus on sector-specific developments.
