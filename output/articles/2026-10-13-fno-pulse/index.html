Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts (options or futures) that have not yet been settled or closed. It gives us a sense of how many positions are currently active in the market.

For options, a Call Option gives the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy an underlying asset at a specified price (strike price) on or before a certain date. Call writers (sellers) profit if the price stays below the strike. Conversely, a Put Option gives the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to sell an underlying asset at a specified price (strike price) on or before a certain date. Put writers (sellers) profit if the price stays above the strike.

Today's data shows significant activity from option writers. For the NIFTY 50, the heaviest Put OI, acting as a potential floor, is observed at the 22,500 strike, approximately 1.0% below the spot price. On the Call side, the 23,000 strike holds the highest Call OI, potentially acting as a ceiling. Today's fresh writing, indicated by the change in Open Interest (ΔOI), saw a much larger build-up in Put OI (52.2 million contracts) compared to Call OI (25.5 million contracts), particularly around the 22,700 and 22,600 strikes, suggesting writers are comfortable selling puts at these levels, expecting the index to stay above them.

For the SENSEX, the option chain shows the highest Call OI at 74,000 and the highest Put OI at 70,000, defining a broad implied range. Today's fresh writing was heavily skewed towards puts, with 18.4 million contracts of Put OI added against only 1.8 million contracts of Call OI. This indicates strong conviction from put writers that the index will hold its ground or move higher.

BANK NIFTY's option chain indicates its heaviest Put OI at 55,000, about 0.9% below the spot price, acting as a strong support. The heaviest Call OI is further out at 58,000. Interestingly, today's heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near 54,833, suggesting some call sellers expect the index to be capped around or below this level. However, this was counterbalanced by significant fresh Put writing near 55,300, indicating put sellers are building a floor close to the current spot.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR above 1 generally suggests that more puts are being written than calls, which can be interpreted as option writers expecting the underlying asset's price to rise or remain stable, thus providing a "floor." Conversely, a PCR below 1 suggests more calls are being written, potentially indicating expectations of a price decline or a "ceiling."

For the NIFTY 50, the overall PCR(OI) stands at 1.13, which is a balanced book, leaning slightly towards puts. However, the PCR based on today's fresh flow (ΔOI) is much higher at 2.05. This combination suggests that while the existing open interest is relatively balanced, today's fresh activity was strongly dominated by put writing, indicating a bullish sentiment from option sellers for the immediate future.

The SENSEX shows a PCR(OI) of 1.41, which is significantly above 1, indicating that puts dominate the open interest and suggesting strong downside protection. This bullish sentiment was further amplified by today's flow, with a remarkable ΔPCR of 10.36, signifying an overwhelming amount of fresh put writing compared to call writing.

BANK NIFTY's PCR(OI) is at 0.98, indicating a relatively balanced book between puts and calls, slightly favouring calls. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR) was -0.55, which suggests a mixed or balanced flow of fresh writing today, not strongly favouring either side.

Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It is often considered a "magnet" for the expiry price, as option writers, who typically hold larger positions, may try to steer the market towards this level to minimise their losses. This magnetic pull tends to become stronger as the expiry date approaches.

For the NIFTY 50, the max pain strike is at 22,600, which is about 0.5% below the current spot price of 22,721.95. This suggests a potential downward pull towards this level by expiry.

The SENSEX has its max pain strike at 72,200, approximately 1.1% below its current spot of 73,033.78. Similar to Nifty, this indicates a potential gravitational pull towards a lower level.

For BANK NIFTY, the max pain strike is at 55,500, which is almost exactly at its current spot price of 55,481.05. When max pain is at or very near the spot, it often suggests that the market may consolidate around current levels as expiry approaches.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held a very low index futures long ratio of 9.8%. This means that out of their total index futures positions, only a small percentage were long (35,416 contracts), while a much larger proportion were short (324,911 contracts). Historically, such a heavily net short position in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or hedging stance from FIIs.

In stock futures, FIIs had a long ratio of 56.0%, with 3,608,422 long contracts against 2,834,402 short contracts. This indicates a more balanced, slightly net long position in individual stock futures, suggesting a selective positive outlook on specific companies.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When option data, particularly a strong lean towards put writing and a high PCR based on fresh activity, aligns with positive spot performance and declining volatility, the next session has historically tended to see continued positive momentum or consolidation at higher levels. The significant put writing across Nifty and Sensex suggests that option sellers are building strong support bases, indicating an expectation that the indices will not fall significantly below these levels. While max pain for Nifty and Sensex points slightly lower, its influence is often balanced by strong fresh put writing, especially when expiry is not immediate. Bank Nifty's balanced max pain and strong put base also contribute to a mildly constructive outlook. However, the heavily net short FII positioning in index futures often serves as a cautionary signal, suggesting that larger institutional players may be hedging or anticipating some downside, which could temper significant upward moves.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can empower you to form your own informed perspective on market sentiment. You can typically find the raw option chain data on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, where daily Open Interest and Change in Open Interest for various strikes are published. Your broker's trading platform may also provide a simplified view of this data.

To interpret it, first look at the highest Call OI and Put OI strikes to identify potential resistance and support levels. Then, observe where the fresh writing (Change in OI) is concentrated – significant fresh put writing at lower strikes indicates support, while fresh call writing at higher strikes suggests resistance. Calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) for both total OI and today's flow to gauge the overall bullish or bearish bias among option writers. Finally, identify the max pain strike and consider its proximity to the current spot price, remembering its magnetic effect, particularly closer to expiry. Combining these insights with FII positioning and individual stock movements can help you build a comprehensive picture of the market's underlying dynamics.
