Decoding Big Trades: What Are Block Deals and Bulk Deals?

TL;DR

  • Block Deals are very large, pre-arranged stock trades (at least ₹25 crore or 5 lakh shares) done in a special, separate window to avoid sudden price changes.
  • Bulk Deals are also large trades (over 0.5% of a company's shares) but happen during regular market hours, visible to everyone.
  • Both types of deals are usually done by big investors like mutual funds or institutions.
  • Details of both block and bulk deals are made public by stock exchanges after market hours on the same day.
  • They offer insights into what big players are doing, but are not direct signals to buy or sell.

What's this about?

In the stock market, most of us buy or sell shares in small quantities. But what happens when big investors, like large mutual funds or insurance companies, want to buy or sell shares worth many crores? They can't just place a huge order in the regular market, as it could dramatically change the share price for everyone. This is where "Block Deals" and "Bulk Deals" come in. These are special ways for large quantities of shares to change hands, designed to manage the market impact and ensure transparency.

Why it's in the news right now

You might have seen headlines recently about specific companies where large institutions bought or sold significant stakes. For example, on October 30, 2026, there were reports of major bulk deals in companies like Hubtown Ltd. and block deals involving institutional buyers and sellers in other firms. These big transactions are a regular feature of our stock markets, and their details are published daily by exchanges like NSE and BSE. These reports often spark curiosity about who is buying or selling big, and what it might mean for the stock.

How it actually works

Let's break down how these two types of big trades operate:

Block Deals

  1. Big Size: A block deal involves a single trade of a very large number of shares. According to SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India) rules, to qualify as a block deal, the transaction must be for a minimum value of ₹25 crore or involve at least 5,00,000 shares. This threshold was updated by SEBI, effective December 7, 2025, from earlier limits.
  2. Special Window: Unlike regular trades, block deals don't happen in the main trading window where you place your orders. Instead, they are executed in dedicated, separate 15-minute trading windows. There are two such windows each trading day: one in the morning (typically 8:45 AM to 9:00 AM) and another in the afternoon (typically 2:05 PM to 2:20 PM).
  3. Pre-arranged & Private: These deals are usually pre-arranged between a buyer and a seller, often through a broker. During the execution in the special window, the trade is not visible in the live market order book. This helps prevent the large transaction from causing immediate, sharp price movements.
  4. Price Limit: The price at which a block deal can be executed is usually restricted to a narrow band, often within 3% of a reference price (like the previous day's closing price or a volume-weighted average price).
  5. Mandatory Delivery: All block deals must result in actual delivery of shares, meaning they cannot be squared off (bought and sold within the same day) for speculative purposes.
  6. Disclosure: Once the block deal window closes, the stock exchange publishes the details of the trade, including the names of the buyer and seller, the quantity of shares, and the price, usually after market hours on the same day.

Bulk Deals

  1. Significant Volume: A bulk deal happens when the total quantity of shares bought or sold by a single investor (under one client code) in a particular company, during a single trading day, is more than 0.5% of the company's total listed equity shares.
  2. Regular Market Hours: Unlike block deals, bulk deals are executed through the normal trading window, just like any other buy or sell order you place.
  3. Visible & Impactful: Because they happen in the open market, bulk deals are visible to all market participants in real-time. A very large bulk deal can sometimes cause immediate price movements in the stock due to the sudden change in demand or supply.
  4. Disclosure: Brokers are required to report bulk deals to the stock exchange. The exchange then makes this information public, usually after market hours on the same day.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine your local kirana store.

Bulk Deal: This is like a big caterer coming into the store during peak hours and buying 100 kg of rice. Everyone in the shop sees it happening. The shelves for rice become noticeably emptier, and the shopkeeper might even slightly increase the price for the remaining rice because of the sudden demand. The transaction is public and impacts the immediate price.

Block Deal: This is like the same caterer calling the shopkeeper before the shop opens. They agree to buy 500 kg of rice at a fixed price, and the shopkeeper arranges for it to be delivered directly from the godown. No one in the shop sees this transaction happen during regular hours. Only later, perhaps at the end of the day, does the shopkeeper put up a notice saying, "We sold 500 kg of rice to a caterer today." The deal is large but designed not to disrupt the daily flow or prices for other customers.

Why it matters to you

  1. Market Transparency: Both types of deals ensure that large transactions, which could otherwise be hidden, are disclosed to the public. This helps maintain fairness in the market.
  2. Insight into "Smart Money": When big institutional investors (often called "smart money") are buying or selling a stock in large quantities, it can signal their conviction or concerns about a company.
  3. Understanding Price Movements: If a stock you follow suddenly sees a big price jump or fall, checking the bulk and block deal reports for that day might help you understand if a large institutional transaction was behind it.
  4. Informed Research: While these deals are not direct "buy" or "sell" signals, they can be a starting point for your own research. If a respected fund is buying a lot of a particular stock, you might want to investigate that company further.
  5. Regulatory Oversight: SEBI's rules for these deals help prevent market manipulation and ensure that even the largest trades happen in a structured, accountable way.

Common confusions cleared up

  • Myth: Block deals are completely secret.

    • * Fact: While block deals are executed in a separate window and not visible in real-time, their full details (buyer, seller, quantity, price) are publicly disclosed by the stock exchanges after market hours on the same day.

  • Myth: If a big institution buys a stock in a bulk or block deal, it's a guaranteed sign that the stock price will go up.

    • * Fact: Not at all. These deals show institutional activity, but they are not a direct signal for future price movements. Institutions buy and sell for many reasons – portfolio rebalancing, exiting a position, or taking a new view. Always do your own research before making any investment decisions.

  • Myth: Only institutional investors can do bulk or block deals.

    • Fact: While block deals are almost exclusively for institutional investors due to their high value threshold, a retail investor could* technically trigger a bulk deal if their single-day transaction in a stock exceeds 0.5% of the company's total listed shares. However, this is rare for most individual investors.

FAQ

Where can I see details of bulk and block deals?

You can find these details on the official websites of the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) after market hours each day. They usually have dedicated sections for "Bulk Deals" and "Block Deals".

Do block deals affect the stock price?

Block deals are specifically designed to minimize immediate price impact because they happen in a separate, pre-arranged window outside the live market. However, the public disclosure of a large block deal after market hours can influence market sentiment and the stock's price on subsequent trading days.

What kind of investors typically participate in these deals?

Mainly institutional investors like mutual funds, foreign portfolio investors (FPIs), insurance companies, pension funds, and high-net-worth individuals (HNIs) participate in both bulk and block deals.

Is there a minimum value for a bulk deal?

While some older references might mention a value, the primary definition for a bulk deal by SEBI is based on quantity: buying or selling more than 0.5% of a company's total listed equity shares in a day.
