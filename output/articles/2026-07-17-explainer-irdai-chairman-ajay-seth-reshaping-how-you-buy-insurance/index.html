IRDAI Chairman Ajay Seth: Reshaping How You Buy Insurance

TL;DR

  • IRDAI Chairman Ajay Seth is leading major reforms in how insurance is sold in India.
  • The regulator plans to change how insurance agents and distributors are paid, moving from large upfront commissions to payments spread over the policy term.
  • This aims to curb "mis-selling," where unsuitable policies might be pushed for higher immediate payouts.
  • Another key proposal is to digitally link every insurance policy to the individual who sold it, boosting accountability.
  • These changes are designed to build greater trust and transparency for policyholders across India.

What's this about?

The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI), led by its Chairman Ajay Seth, is bringing in significant changes to the way insurance products are sold in the country. The main goal is to protect you, the customer, from being sold policies that aren't right for you, often called "mis-selling." This involves reforming how agents and distributors are paid and making them more accountable for the policies they sell.

Why it's in the news right now

IRDAI Chairman Ajay Seth recently announced that the regulator is preparing a detailed consultation paper on these "distribution reforms," which is expected to be released by the end of July 2026. This follows earlier reports from early July 2026 about the specific proposals, including overhauling commission structures and digitally tagging policies to individual sellers. These moves are part of a broader effort to boost transparency and trust in India's fast-growing insurance market.

How it actually works

  1. Current Commission Structure: Traditionally, insurance agents and distributors often receive a large portion of their commission (sometimes up to 40% of the premium) right when a policy is sold, especially for life and health insurance.
  2. The Problem with Upfront Payouts: This system can sometimes encourage intermediaries (agents, brokers, bank employees) to focus on selling as many policies as possible, or even encouraging customers to switch policies frequently ("churn"), to get higher immediate commissions, rather than finding the best long-term fit for the customer. This leads to mis-selling.
  3. Proposed Commission Overhaul: IRDAI's new plan suggests replacing these large upfront payments with "staggered payments." This means the commission would be spread out over the entire duration of the insurance policy. The idea is to link the agent's earnings more directly to the service they provide throughout the policy's life, not just at the point of sale.
  4. Linking Policy to Seller: Another crucial proposal is to digitally tag every insurance policy to the specific individual who sold it. This could be a bank employee, an insurance agent, a broker, or any other salesperson. Currently, the responsibility often rests with the organisation (like a bank or corporate agent).
  5. Increased Accountability: By linking the policy to an individual, it becomes much easier to trace who was responsible if a customer claims they were mis-sold a product or received incorrect advice. This aims to make the sales process more transparent and hold individual sellers accountable.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine you hire a carpenter to build a new cupboard for your home.

  • Old way: You pay the carpenter a large chunk of money upfront. After that, they might rush the job or not care much about the finishing, because most of their payment is already done. If something breaks later, it's hard to trace who did what.
  • New way (IRDAI's proposal): You pay a smaller amount upfront, and then pay the rest in instalments over several months, as long as the cupboard is in good shape and they provide any necessary follow-up service. Also, the carpenter puts their unique ID on the cupboard. This way, they have a reason to do a good job and fix any issues, because their future payments depend on your satisfaction, and everyone knows exactly who built it.

Similarly, IRDAI wants insurance sellers to be invested in your policy's long-term success, not just the initial sale, and to be clearly identifiable.

Why it matters to you

  1. Less Mis-selling: You are less likely to be pressured into buying an insurance policy that doesn't fit your needs or financial situation, as agents will have less incentive for quick, high-commission sales.
  2. Better Advice: Agents might focus more on understanding your actual requirements and offering suitable products, knowing their earnings are tied to the policy's longevity and good service.
  3. Clearer Accountability: If you ever face an issue with your policy or feel you were misinformed, it will be easier to identify the specific individual who sold it to you. This makes the complaint and redressal process simpler.
  4. Increased Trust: These reforms aim to build greater trust in the insurance sector, making you feel more confident when buying a policy.
  5. Fairer Market: The changes could lead to a more ethical and customer-centric insurance market, where the focus shifts from sales targets to genuine customer welfare.

Common confusions cleared up

  • Myth: All insurance agents are bad and only care about commissions.

    • * Fact: Many insurance agents are ethical and provide valuable service. The IRDAI's reforms aim to create a system that supports and rewards good practices, while discouraging the few who might exploit loopholes.

  • Myth: These changes mean my insurance premiums will become more expensive.

    • Fact: The reforms primarily target the distribution cost* within the premium, not necessarily increasing the overall premium. In fact, by reducing mis-selling and churn, it could lead to better value for policyholders over the long term.

  • Myth: I won't be able to buy insurance easily anymore.

    • Fact: The goal is to make buying insurance safer and more transparent*, not harder. The consultation paper will gather feedback to ensure the new framework is effective and balanced.

FAQ

What is "mis-selling" in insurance?

Mis-selling happens when an insurance policy is sold to a customer by providing incorrect information, hiding important details, or recommending a product that is unsuitable for their needs, often driven by the seller's desire for higher commissions.

Will these changes affect my existing insurance policies?

The proposals are primarily for new policies and the future conduct of agents. While the exact impact on existing policies isn't detailed yet, the overall aim is to improve the industry's standards, which could indirectly benefit all policyholders through better service and accountability.

When will these new rules come into effect?

IRDAI is expected to release a consultation paper by the end of July 2026. After receiving feedback from various stakeholders (like insurance companies, agents, and the public), the final regulations will be drafted and notified, which could take some time.

How much compensation can I get if I'm mis-sold a policy?

The IRDAI's Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, which came into effect on July 1, 2026, allows for compensation up to ₹30 lakh for consequential losses and up to ₹3 lakh for loss of time, expenses, harassment, and mental anguish in cases of deficiency in service.
