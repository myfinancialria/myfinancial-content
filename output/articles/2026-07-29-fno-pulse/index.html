Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides a good indication of market participation and potential support or resistance levels.

The strike with the highest Call Open Interest typically acts as a significant resistance level. This is because a large number of participants have sold (written) Call options at this strike, expecting the underlying price not to rise above it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put Open Interest often serves as a strong support level, as many participants have sold (written) Put options here, anticipating the underlying price will not fall below this point.

For Nifty 50, today's heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near the 24,600 strike. This suggests that Call sellers anticipate the index might face resistance around this level, potentially capping further upside in the immediate term. On the Put side, the heaviest fresh writing was seen near 23,800, indicating that Put sellers are establishing a potential floor, expecting the index to hold above this level.

In the Sensex options, fresh Call writing was concentrated near 78,100, which could act as a ceiling for the index. Meanwhile, substantial fresh Put writing was evident near 77,366, suggesting a robust support base being formed by Put sellers. Notably, the aggregate flow for Sensex showed a significant build in Put OI (22.4 million contracts) compared to Call OI (5.6 million contracts) today, indicating a strong preference for selling Puts.

For Bank Nifty, fresh Call writing was most prominent near 57,600, potentially limiting upward movement. On the other hand, fresh Put writing was concentrated around 57,400, implying immediate support levels are being established by option writers.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that compares the total Open Interest (OI) of Put options to the total OI of Call options. A PCR value above 1 generally suggests a bullish sentiment, as it indicates more Put options have been written (sold) than Call options. Put writers typically expect the market to remain stable or rise, as they profit if the price stays above their strike. Conversely, a PCR below 1 often points to a bearish sentiment, with more Call writing.

For Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stood at 1.02, suggesting a relatively balanced options book overall. However, today's flow, measured by the change in Open Interest (PCR(today flow ΔOI)), was significantly higher at 1.73. This indicates that fresh writing today was heavily skewed towards Puts, suggesting a bullish sentiment from option writers during the session.

Sensex displayed a strong PCR(OI) of 1.56, implying that Put options significantly outnumber Call options in terms of Open Interest, suggesting good downside protection. The PCR(today flow ΔOI) was even more pronounced at 4.01, indicating a very strong put-heavy fresh writing activity today, which is typically interpreted as a highly bullish signal from option writers.

Bank Nifty had a PCR(OI) of 0.81, which suggests a slightly call-heavy or balanced book. The PCR(today flow ΔOI) was 1.01, indicating a mixed or balanced flow of fresh writing activity for the day, without a strong directional bias from today's new positions.

Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that particular price. It often acts like a magnetic force, tending to pull the index towards it as the options expiry date approaches.

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain strike is at 24,200, which is very close to its spot closing price of 24,250.20. This suggests that option writers are comfortable with the index expiring around current levels, with little incentive for a significant move in either direction based on their collective positioning.

The Sensex Max Pain is observed at 76,800. This level is approximately -1.1% below its spot closing price of 77,654.60. This positioning could indicate a potential downward pull from option writers as expiry nears, aiming to minimise their collective losses.

For Bank Nifty, the Max Pain strike is at 58,000. This is approximately +1.4% above its spot closing price of 57,205.90. This suggests a potential upward pull, with option writers positioned to benefit if the index moves higher towards this level by expiry.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market dynamics can be a powerful tool for your daily analysis. You can access the raw data yourself from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website or through your broker's platform.

To begin, navigate to the "Option Chain" section on the NSE website for Nifty, Bank Nifty, or Sensex. Here, you'll find the Open Interest data for various Call and Put strikes. Look for the strikes with the highest Call OI and highest Put OI to identify potential resistance and support levels. To gauge fresh activity, observe the "Change in OI" column for both Calls and Puts; significant increases indicate new positions being built. You can also calculate the Put-Call Ratio by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI. For FII positioning, the NSE archives often provide daily participant-wise Open Interest data in futures and options. By consistently tracking these data points and observing how they change day-to-day, you can develop your own understanding of market sentiment and positioning, empowering you to form your own informed perspectives.
