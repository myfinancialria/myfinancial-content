Your Big Stock Trades Just Got Bigger: Understanding Block Deals

TL;DR

  • Block deals are special, large share transactions done by big investors.
  • They happen in separate, short trading windows on stock exchanges.
  • India's market regulator, SEBI, recently raised their minimum value to ₹25 crore.
  • This change ensures shares are actually delivered, preventing quick reversals.
  • Block deals help big money move without disturbing regular market prices.

What's this about?

Imagine a situation where a very large investor, like a big mutual fund or an insurance company, wants to buy or sell shares worth hundreds of crores in a company. If they tried to do this in the regular stock market, it could cause the share price to jump or fall sharply, affecting many small investors. To avoid such big swings, stock exchanges have a special mechanism called a "block deal."

A block deal is essentially a large, pre-arranged transaction where a buyer and a seller agree to trade a significant quantity of shares at a specific price. These deals are executed through a separate trading window provided by stock exchanges like NSE and BSE, away from the normal market activity.

Why it's in the news right now

Block deals are in the news because the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), our market regulator, recently made some important changes to how they work. Around May 2026, SEBI announced a major overhaul of the framework for block deals.

The biggest change is that SEBI increased the minimum transaction size for a single block deal from the earlier ₹10 crore to ₹25 crore. This means only truly massive trades will now qualify as block deals. Additionally, SEBI mandated that all block deals must be settled on a 100% delivery basis. This effectively stops these large trades from being quickly reversed or "squared off" within the same day. These new regulations were set to come into effect in the weeks following the May announcement, making them a current topic of discussion among market participants.

How it actually works

  1. Large Transaction: A block deal involves a single transaction for a very large quantity of shares or a high value.
  2. Minimum Value: As per the latest SEBI guidelines, a transaction must be for a minimum value of ₹25 crore to qualify as a block deal. Previously, this limit was ₹10 crore.
  3. Institutional Players: These deals are almost always carried out by large institutional investors such as mutual funds, insurance companies, foreign portfolio investors (FPIs), or domestic institutional investors (DIIs). High-net-worth individuals (HNIs) with significant capital might also participate.
  4. Special Trading Windows: Block deals do not happen during regular market hours. Instead, stock exchanges provide specific, short time slots, known as "block deal windows." For instance, on the NSE and BSE, there's typically a morning window (e.g., 8:45 AM to 9:00 AM) and an afternoon window (e.g., 2:05 PM to 2:20 PM).
  5. Pre-agreed Price: The buyer and seller negotiate and agree on a price before the deal is executed. This price must usually be within a small range (e.g., +/- 1% or 3%) of the previous day's closing price or the current market price, depending on the window.
  6. Mandatory Delivery: A key recent change is that every block deal must now result in "100% delivery." This means the shares bought must actually be transferred to the buyer's account, and the trade cannot be cancelled or reversed (squared off) on the same day.
  7. Disclosure: To maintain transparency, stock exchanges publicly disclose the details of block deals, including the company's name, the quantity of shares, and the traded price. This information is usually made available after market hours on the same day.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine you run a large catering business, and you need to buy 1,000 kilograms of potatoes. If you go to your local kirana shop and try to buy such a huge quantity, it would empty their stock, cause a huge rush, and probably make the price of potatoes shoot up for everyone else.

Instead, you call a big potato wholesaler directly. You agree on a price and quantity beforehand, and they deliver the 1,000 kg of potatoes to your kitchen at a specific time, without disturbing the local kirana shop's daily sales or prices.

A block deal is similar. Large investors are like the catering business, buying or selling huge quantities of shares (potatoes) directly from another large investor (wholesaler) in a special, pre-arranged way, without causing chaos in the regular stock market (kirana shop).

Why it matters to you

  1. Market Stability: Block deals help prevent large buy or sell orders from causing sudden, drastic price changes in the regular market. This creates a more stable trading environment for everyone, including small investors.
  2. Transparency: Even though they are pre-arranged, the details of block deals are disclosed to the public after market hours. This allows you to see how big institutional investors are moving their money, offering insights into their sentiment towards certain stocks.
  3. Market Liquidity: These deals provide an efficient way for large investors to enter or exit significant positions. This contributes to the overall "liquidity" (ease of buying and selling) of the market, which indirectly benefits all investors by ensuring smoother trading conditions.
  4. Indirect Influence on Sentiment: While block deals aim to minimise direct price impact, a very large block deal in a particular stock can signal strong institutional confidence (if buying) or concern (if selling). This sentiment can sometimes influence the stock's price in the days following the disclosure.
  5. No Direct Participation: As an ordinary retail investor, you cannot directly participate in block deals due as they require a minimum transaction value of ₹25 crore, which is typically beyond the scope of individual investors.

Common confusions cleared up

  • Myth: Block deals are secret transactions that manipulate stock prices.

    • Fact: While the negotiation is private, SEBI mandates that details of block deals (scrip name, quantity, price) are publicly disclosed by the exchanges after market hours on the same day. They are designed to prevent price manipulation in the regular market, not cause it.

  • Myth: Block deals are the same as bulk deals.

    • Fact: They are different. A block deal is a single, pre-arranged transaction of at least ₹25 crore, executed in a separate trading window, and now requires 100% delivery. A bulk deal involves buying or selling a total quantity of shares exceeding 0.5% of a company's total equity during regular market hours, which can be done through multiple orders. Bulk deal disclosures happen at the end of the trading day.

  • Myth: When a block deal happens, the stock price immediately shoots up or crashes.

    • Fact: Block deals are specifically designed to happen in separate windows to minimise their immediate impact on the ongoing market price. Any subsequent price movement is usually due to the market's interpretation of the large institutional activity, rather than the direct execution of the block deal itself.

FAQ

Who regulates block deals in India?

Block deals in India are regulated by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) to ensure transparency, fairness, and market integrity.

Can I, as a small investor, participate in a block deal?

No, small retail investors cannot directly participate in block deals because they have a high minimum transaction value, currently set at ₹25 crore, making them exclusive to large institutional investors.

What are the specific timings for block deals on stock exchanges?

Block deals are executed during two specific windows: a morning window, typically from 8:45 AM to 9:00 AM, and an afternoon window, usually from 2:05 PM to 2:20 PM.

What does the "100% delivery" mandate for block deals mean?

It means that for every block deal, the shares bought must be physically delivered and cannot be squared off (sold back) within the same trading day, ensuring genuine change of ownership.

Why did SEBI increase the minimum value for block deals?

SEBI increased the minimum value to ensure that the block deal window is used for genuine, high-conviction, large-scale investment and divestment decisions, preventing its misuse for smaller, speculative trades and enhancing market integrity.
