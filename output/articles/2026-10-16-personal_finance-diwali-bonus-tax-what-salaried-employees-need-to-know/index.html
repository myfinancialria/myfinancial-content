Diwali Bonus Tax: What Salaried Employees Need to Know

TL;DR

  • A Diwali bonus received from your employer is fully taxable as part of your salary income.
  • This bonus is added to your total income and taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate.
  • Your employer typically accounts for this bonus when calculating Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) throughout the financial year.
  • Cash gifts from employers are generally not considered tax-exempt gifts but are treated as salary.
  • You must report your Diwali bonus under "Salaries" when filing your Income Tax Return (ITR).

What this means in plain terms

As the festive season approaches, many salaried individuals look forward to a Diwali bonus from their employer. While this extra payout brings cheer, it's crucial to understand its tax implications. Simply put, any cash bonus you receive from your employer, whether it's called a Diwali bonus, performance bonus, or festive payout, is considered part of your salary.

This means it's not a tax-free windfall. The entire bonus amount will be added to your gross salary income for the financial year. Consequently, it will be taxed according to your individual income tax slab rate, just like your regular monthly salary. Your employer is usually obligated to deduct tax at source (TDS) on this amount, adjusting it against your overall tax liability for the year.

How Your Diwali Bonus is Taxed

When your employer disburses a Diwali bonus, it falls under the ambit of "Salaries" as defined by the Income Tax Act, 1961. This category includes wages, annuities, gratuities, perquisites, and any profits in lieu of or in addition to salary, including bonuses. Therefore, a festive bonus is treated no differently than your basic pay or other allowances for taxation purposes.

Applicable Tax Rates

The bonus amount is simply added to your total taxable income for the financial year. Your total income then determines which income tax slab you fall into. For instance, if your annual salary is ₹12 lakhs and you receive a Diwali bonus of ₹1 lakh, your total taxable salary income becomes ₹13 lakhs. This entire amount will be subject to income tax as per the prevailing slab rates applicable to individuals, under either the old tax regime or the new tax regime, depending on your choice.

Understanding TDS and Tax Slabs

Your employer is responsible for deducting Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on your salary, including any bonuses. When a bonus is paid, your employer typically recalculates your estimated annual income, factoring in the bonus. They then adjust the TDS deduction for the remaining months of the financial year to ensure that the correct amount of tax is withheld.

Impact on Your Overall Tax Liability

Receiving a substantial Diwali bonus can sometimes push you into a higher income tax bracket, or increase the portion of your income taxed at a higher rate. It's important to be aware of this, as it means a larger chunk of your bonus might go towards taxes. For example, if your salary places you in the 20% tax slab, but a large bonus pushes a part of your income into the 30% slab, that portion of your bonus will be taxed at 30%. This is why financial planning around bonuses is crucial to avoid any surprises at the end of the financial year. You can refer to the income tax slab rates on the Income Tax Department's official website.

Gifts vs. Bonuses: What's the Difference for Tax?

It's common to confuse a "bonus" with a "gift," especially during festive seasons. However, for income tax purposes, there's a clear distinction, particularly when the payment comes from an employer.

Taxability of Gifts

Under Section 56(2)(x) of the Income Tax Act, gifts received by an individual are taxable if their aggregate value exceeds ₹50,000 in a financial year. However, there are specific exemptions for gifts received from relatives, on the occasion of marriage, under a will or inheritance, or in contemplation of death. Crucially, this section primarily applies to gifts from non-employers. While an employer can give a gift, if it's a cash payment or a transferable asset, it's almost always treated as a perquisite or salary income, regardless of the ₹50,000 limit, unless it's a non-monetary gift of a trivial nature (e.g., a small memento).

Employer's Cash Bonus is Salary

A Diwali bonus, being a cash payment directly from your employer as part of your employment terms or as a performance incentive, is unequivocally considered "salary" or "profits in lieu of salary". It does not qualify for the gift exemption under Section 56(2)(x). Therefore, the entire amount of a cash Diwali bonus from your employer is taxable, irrespective of its value. This is a critical point to remember, as many mistakenly believe smaller bonuses might be tax-free.

Reporting Your Festive Payout in Your ITR

When you file your Income Tax Return (ITR), your Diwali bonus will already be included in the "Salaries" section of your Form 16, which is issued by your employer. Form 16 provides a consolidated summary of your salary income, TDS deducted, and other relevant details for the financial year.

How to Verify and Declare

You should cross-verify the bonus amount shown in your Form 16 with your payslips or bank statements. The total taxable salary reported in Part B of your Form 16 will already incorporate your bonus. When filling out your ITR, you simply declare the consolidated salary figure as provided in your Form 16. There is no separate head or section specifically for "Diwali bonus"; it's all part of your gross salary income. Ensuring that your Form 16 accurately reflects all your income from the employer, including bonuses, is essential for a smooth tax filing process.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 35, ₹22L CTC, Gurugram. Priya works for a multinational IT firm. Her annual Cost to Company (CTC) is ₹22 lakhs. Her take-home salary is approximately ₹1.5 lakhs per month. In October, she receives a Diwali bonus of ₹1,20,000.

Here's how her bonus impacts her taxes:

  1. Annual Income before bonus: Priya's taxable salary income (after standard deductions, but before other exemptions/deductions) is around ₹20 lakhs.
  2. Bonus Addition: The ₹1,20,000 Diwali bonus is added directly to her taxable salary income.

    3. New Taxable Salary Income = ₹20,00,000 + ₹1,20,000 = ₹21,20,000.

  3. Tax Slab Impact (assuming Old Regime for simplicity, without specific deductions):

    4. * For income between ₹10 lakhs and ₹21.20 lakhs, the tax rate is 30% (plus cess). * Her employer will re-calculate her estimated annual income to ₹21,20,000. * The additional ₹1,20,000 bonus will be taxed at 30% (plus 4% health and education cess). * Tax on bonus = 30% of ₹1,20,000 = ₹36,000. * Cess on bonus tax = 4% of ₹36,000 = ₹1,440. * Total tax on bonus = ₹36,000 + ₹1,440 = ₹37,440.

  4. TDS Adjustment: Her employer will adjust the TDS deducted from her remaining monthly salaries to account for this additional tax liability of ₹37,440. For example, if she has 5 months left in the financial year, an additional ₹7,488 (₹37,440 / 5) might be deducted from her salary each month.

So, while Priya receives ₹1,20,000 as a bonus, a significant portion, approximately ₹37,440, will go towards income tax.

What to do this week

  1. Review your payslip: Check your latest payslip after receiving the bonus to see how it's reflected and if any additional TDS has been deducted.
  2. Understand your Form 16: Familiarise yourself with how your bonus will appear in your annual Form 16, typically under the "Salaries" head.
  3. Estimate your tax liability: If you receive a large bonus, use an online income tax calculator to estimate your total tax liability for the year, considering the bonus, to avoid surprises.
  4. Plan for tax-saving investments: If the bonus pushes you into a higher tax bracket or increases your overall tax, consider utilising tax-saving instruments under Sections 80C, 80D, etc., before the financial year ends.

FAQ

Is a Diwali bonus always taxable?

Yes, a cash Diwali bonus received from your employer is always considered part of your salary income and is fully taxable as per your applicable income tax slab rates.

Does my employer deduct TDS on the Diwali bonus?

Yes, your employer is legally obligated to deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on your Diwali bonus, just like your regular salary. They will adjust the TDS for the remaining months of the financial year.

Can I claim any exemptions on my Diwali bonus?

No, a cash Diwali bonus from an employer is fully taxable as salary and does not qualify for any specific exemptions under the "gifts" section of the Income Tax Act.

<h3>Will a Diwali bonus affect my tax slab?</h3> A Diwali bonus is added to your total annual income. If this increased income pushes you into a higher tax bracket, or increases the portion of your income taxed at a higher rate, then yes, it will affect your overall tax liability.

How do I report my Diwali bonus in my Income Tax Return (ITR)?

You don't report it separately. Your employer will include the bonus in your consolidated salary income in Form 16. You simply declare the total salary figure as provided in your Form 16 when filing your ITR.
