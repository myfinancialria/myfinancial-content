PhonePe Inactivity Fee: Avoid the ₹100 Quarterly Charge

TL;DR

  • PhonePe has introduced a ₹100 quarterly inactivity fee for wallets that have not recorded any financial transaction for 365 consecutive days.
  • This fee will be deducted directly from the existing wallet balance, though the balance will not go into negative territory.
  • Merely logging into the app or performing UPI transactions will not count as wallet activity to reset the inactivity clock.
  • Users will receive a 15-day notice before the fee is deducted, providing an opportunity to reactivate the wallet.
  • To avoid the charge, users should perform at least one wallet-based transaction or transfer their balance out if the wallet is no longer needed.

What this means in plain terms

Digital payment platforms have become an indispensable part of our daily financial lives, simplifying transactions from bill payments to online shopping. However, a recent policy update from PhonePe, a prominent player in the Indian fintech space, requires attention from its users. The company has announced a new quarterly inactivity fee of ₹100 for wallets that remain unused for an entire year. This means if you have funds in your PhonePe wallet but haven't used them for any financial transaction for 365 days, you could start seeing deductions from your balance.

This move is not entirely unprecedented in the digital payments industry, with other platforms having introduced similar charges in the past to manage dormant accounts and cover operational costs. The key takeaway for PhonePe users is that "inactivity" specifically refers to a lack of wallet-based financial transactions. Simply opening the app or making UPI payments directly from your bank account will not reset the inactivity period for your PhonePe wallet. Therefore, it's crucial for users to understand these new terms to avoid unexpected deductions from their wallet balances.

PhonePe has stated that this fee is intended to support continuous platform updates, fixes, and improvements, acknowledging the costs associated with maintaining these accounts. While the policy aims to encourage active usage or closure of dormant wallets, it places the onus on the user to monitor their wallet activity, especially if they primarily use PhonePe for UPI transactions and have forgotten about small balances in their wallet.

Understanding the New PhonePe Wallet Policy

Defining 'Inactive'

A PhonePe wallet is classified as 'inactive' if no financial transaction has been made through it for 365 consecutive days. It's important to distinguish between general app usage and specific wallet activity. Actions like logging into the PhonePe application, making UPI payments, or using other non-wallet services (like bill payments directly from a linked bank account) do not count as wallet activity and will not prevent the wallet from being marked inactive. Only transactions that involve the actual PhonePe wallet balance — such as sending money from the wallet, making a payment using the wallet balance, or adding money to the wallet — will reset the inactivity period.

The Quarterly Inactivity Fee

Once a wallet is deemed inactive for a year, PhonePe will begin charging a ₹100 quarterly maintenance fee. This fee includes GST and will be deducted from the existing wallet balance. PhonePe has clarified that the wallet balance will never go into negative territory; if the balance is less than ₹100, the entire remaining amount will be debited, bringing the wallet balance to zero. Before any deduction, users will receive a 15-day notice period, during which they can reactivate their wallet by performing a financial transaction to avoid the charge.

Why this Policy Now?

PhonePe states that the fee is levied to support ongoing platform updates, maintenance, and improvements, which incur operational and compliance costs for maintaining inactive prepaid wallet accounts. This aligns with broader industry practices where maintaining dormant accounts, even with small balances, requires infrastructure and regulatory compliance efforts. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) also has guidelines for Prepaid Payment Instruments (PPIs) regarding disclosure of charges, expiry, closure, and refunds, which PPI issuers must adhere to.

Strategies to Avoid the Inactivity Fee

Regular Small Transactions

The simplest way to keep your PhonePe wallet active and avoid the quarterly fee is to perform at least one financial transaction using the wallet within a 365-day period. This could be a small mobile recharge, a bill payment, sending money to another PhonePe user from your wallet balance, or making a purchase at a merchant that accepts PhonePe wallet payments. Even a transaction as small as ₹10-₹50 is sufficient to reset the inactivity clock. Setting a calendar reminder to perform such a transaction annually can be an effective proactive measure.

Transferring Funds to Your Bank Account

If you rarely use your PhonePe wallet and prefer to keep your funds in your bank account, consider transferring any existing balance out of your wallet. Since the inactivity fee is only deducted from the wallet balance and cannot result in a negative balance or be recovered from your linked bank account, maintaining a zero balance in your wallet effectively prevents any charges. This is a practical solution for users who primarily rely on UPI for transactions and find their wallet balances accumulating unintentionally.

Closing Unused Wallets

For wallets that you are certain you will not use in the future, and after ensuring the balance is zero, you might consider closing them. While the process for closing a wallet might require a few steps within the app or through customer support, it ensures that no future inactivity fees can be levied. This option provides a definitive solution for users who wish to entirely disengage from the PhonePe wallet service.

Broader Implications for Digital Payments

The introduction of an inactivity fee by a major player like PhonePe reflects a maturing digital payments ecosystem in India. While UPI has significantly streamlined person-to-person and merchant payments, often bypassing the need for wallet balances, prepaid wallets still serve specific use cases. This policy change highlights the operational realities for fintech companies in managing a vast number of accounts, including those that become dormant.

This isn't an isolated incident; other fintech players like MobiKwik and Airtel Payments Bank have also implemented similar inactivity or maintenance charges for dormant wallets in the past. This trend underscores the increasing focus on managing costs associated with compliance, infrastructure, and security for inactive accounts. For consumers, it serves as a reminder to periodically review their digital payment accounts and balances across various platforms. The RBI's Master Direction on Prepaid Payment Instruments (PPIs) also mandates PPI issuers to disclose all features, charges, and terms in clear language to holders, emphasizing consumer awareness and protection.

A real example

Meet Rahul, 38, a Senior Software Engineer in Bengaluru, with a CTC of ₹28 lakhs. Rahul primarily uses PhonePe for UPI payments, directly linked to his bank account. He occasionally loaded small amounts into his PhonePe wallet for quick recharges or small merchant payments, but hasn't done so in over 15 months. Currently, his PhonePe wallet has a balance of ₹450.

Here's how the inactivity fee could affect Rahul:

  1. Initial Inactivity: Rahul's wallet became inactive after 365 days (12 months) of no wallet-based transactions. Let's say this milestone was reached in January 2026.
  2. First Fee Deduction: In April 2026 (the end of the first quarter after inactivity), PhonePe would have sent Rahul a 15-day notice. If he took no action, ₹100 would be deducted.

    3. * Wallet balance: ₹450 - ₹100 = ₹350.

  3. Second Fee Deduction: In July 2026 (the end of the second quarter), another ₹100 would be deducted after a similar notice period.

    4. * Wallet balance: ₹350 - ₹100 = ₹250.

  4. Third Fee Deduction: In October 2026, another ₹100 would be deducted.

    5. * Wallet balance: ₹250 - ₹100 = ₹150.

  5. Fourth Fee Deduction: In January 2027, another ₹100 would be deducted.

    6. * Wallet balance: ₹150 - ₹100 = ₹50.

If Rahul had noticed the 15-day alert before the first deduction in April 2026, he could have simply done a ₹10 mobile recharge using his wallet balance. This single transaction would have reactivated his wallet, resetting the 365-day inactivity clock and saving him ₹400 in potential fees over the year. Alternatively, he could have transferred the ₹450 to his bank account, leaving the wallet at zero and incurring no charges.

What to do this week

  1. Check Your PhonePe Wallet Activity: Open your PhonePe app and navigate to your wallet section. Review your transaction history to see when you last made a wallet-based payment or added funds.
  2. Perform a Small Wallet Transaction: If your wallet has been inactive or is approaching the 365-day mark, make a small transaction. A ₹10 mobile recharge, a bill payment using wallet balance, or sending a small amount to a friend's PhonePe wallet are quick ways to reactivate it.
  3. Transfer Wallet Balance to Bank Account: If you have a significant balance in your PhonePe wallet that you don't intend to use for wallet-specific transactions, transfer it to your linked bank account. This ensures your funds are secure and avoids any potential inactivity fees.
  4. Consider Wallet Closure: If you are certain you will not use your PhonePe wallet in the future and have zeroed out its balance, explore the option to close the wallet through the app's help section or customer support.
  5. Set a Calendar Reminder: To stay proactive, set an annual reminder in your calendar (e.g., "Check PhonePe Wallet Activity") to perform a small transaction or review your wallet status, preventing future inactivity charges.

FAQ

When does the PhonePe inactivity fee come into effect?

The PhonePe inactivity fee is applicable from June 2026. If your wallet has been inactive for 365 days as of this month, you may start receiving notices for deductions.

What constitutes an "inactive" PhonePe wallet?

A PhonePe wallet is considered inactive if there have been no financial transactions made from the wallet for 365 consecutive days. Logging in, checking balance, or making UPI payments (not using wallet balance) do not count as activity.

Can the inactivity fee lead to a negative wallet balance?

No, PhonePe has clarified that the wallet balance will never go into negative territory. If your wallet balance is less than ₹100, the entire remaining amount will be debited, bringing the wallet balance to zero.

How can I check my PhonePe wallet activity?

You can check your PhonePe wallet activity by opening the PhonePe app, going to the 'My Money' or 'Wallets' section, and then viewing your transaction history specifically for the PhonePe wallet.

Is there a way to close my PhonePe wallet?

Yes, you can typically close your PhonePe wallet through the app's help and support section or by contacting customer service. Ensure your wallet balance is zero before initiating the closure process.

Do other digital wallets charge inactivity fees?

Yes, PhonePe is not the first to introduce such a fee. Other platforms like MobiKwik and Airtel Payments Bank have also had similar inactivity or maintenance charges for dormant wallets.

Does this fee apply to UPI transactions via PhonePe?

No, this inactivity fee applies only to the PhonePe wallet balance. UPI transactions made directly from your linked bank account through the PhonePe app are not affected by this policy.
