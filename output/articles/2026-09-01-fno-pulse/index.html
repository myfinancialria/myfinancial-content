Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding Call or Put contracts that have not yet been settled. It provides a snapshot of market sentiment and potential support and resistance levels. The strike price with the highest Call OI is often considered a significant resistance level, meaning many option sellers (writers) believe the price will not rise above this point. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI typically acts as a strong support level, where writers expect the price to not fall below.

For the NIFTY 50, the heaviest Call OI was observed at the 24100 strike, suggesting that option writers anticipate the index may struggle to move significantly above this level. The strongest Put OI, on the other hand, was concentrated at the 24050 strike, indicating a robust support base around current levels. Today's fresh writing activity saw heavy Call writing near 24100, reinforcing the idea of a cap, while fresh Put writing near 24000 suggests conviction among put sellers that the index will hold above this floor.

In the SENSEX, the 78000 strike emerged as the most significant Call OI wall, implying a strong resistance zone. The heaviest Put OI was noted at 76000, which could serve as a foundational support. Today's fresh Call writing was most prominent near 78000, with overall Call OI build significantly outweighing Put OI build (14.7 million versus 5.8 million), pointing towards a cautious sentiment from option sellers.

For BANK NIFTY, both the heaviest Call OI and Put OI were seen around the 57500 strike, creating a tight implied range. Fresh Call writing was concentrated near 57733, suggesting that option sellers are positioning for a cap around these slightly higher levels. Conversely, fresh Put writing was more active near 56566, indicating potential support much lower than the current spot. The aggregate flow also showed a higher net Call OI build (0.7 million) compared to Put OI build (0.1 million), reinforcing the bearish tilt from fresh writing.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used derivative indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR(OI) value above 1 generally indicates that more Put options are being written (sold) than Call options, which can be interpreted as a bullish signal, as Put writers expect prices to rise or remain stable. Conversely, a PCR(OI) below 1 suggests more Call writing, often seen as a bearish signal, as Call writers expect prices to fall or remain stable. We also look at PCR(today's flow ΔOI), which focuses on the fresh writing activity for the day.

For NIFTY 50, the PCR(OI) stood at 0.86, suggesting a relatively balanced book with a slight inclination towards Call writing. However, today's flow ΔPCR was significantly higher at 1.82, indicating that fresh writing activity was heavily skewed towards Puts. This combination suggests that while the overall book might be slightly balanced, today's fresh participants are showing a mildly bullish conviction by selling more Puts, anticipating support.

The SENSEX recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.68, which is below 0.7, typically indicating a dominance of Call options and a bearish sentiment where upside may be capped. This was further corroborated by today's flow ΔPCR of 0.4, signifying that fresh writing was heavily concentrated in Calls, reinforcing the expectation of limited upside.

BANK NIFTY had a PCR(OI) of 1.05, suggesting a balanced options book overall. However, today's flow ΔPCR was a low 0.15, indicating that fresh writing was overwhelmingly Call-heavy. This strong influx of new Call writing despite a balanced overall PCR suggests a fresh bearish sentiment emerging from today's participants, expecting the recent decline to continue or consolidate.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate financial loss if the underlying asset expires at that particular price. It often acts like a magnet, drawing the underlying index towards it as expiry approaches, as option writers try to minimise their collective losses.

For NIFTY 50, the Max Pain point was at 24100, which is very close to the spot price, indicating a potential magnetic pull towards this level as expiry nears.

The SENSEX Max Pain was positioned at 77500, which is approximately 0.7% above its current spot price. This suggests a potential upward pull towards this level if option writers manage to steer the expiry in their favour.

BANK NIFTY also had its Max Pain at 57500, very near its closing spot price. This indicates that option writers are currently positioned for the index to expire around these levels, potentially leading to range-bound movement around this strike.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held a notably low Index futures long ratio of 9.1%. This means that out of their total index futures positions, only 9.1% were long contracts, while the vast majority (90.9%) were short. Specifically, FIIs held 24,718 long contracts against 246,750 short contracts. Historically, such a pronounced net short position in index futures from FIIs has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from these large institutional players, often preceding periods of market consolidation or downward pressure.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of 54.3%, holding 3,449,700 long contracts versus 2,898,377 short contracts. This indicates a slight net long position in individual stock futures, suggesting a more balanced or selectively positive view on specific companies, even while expressing caution on the broader indices.

The strong net short FII stance in index futures, in particular, has historically tended to coincide with a cautious sentiment from institutional players, which can lead to selling pressure or limited upside potential. Therefore, the next session has historically tended to see participants navigating conflicting signals, potentially leading to range-bound movements as the market attempts to find clear direction.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals daily can empower you to form your own informed perspective. You can access the necessary data points primarily from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website and your broker's platform.

To start, visit the NSE website's "Option Chain" section. Here, you can view the Open Interest (OI) for various Call and Put options across different strike prices. Look for the strike prices with the highest Call OI and Put OI; these represent potential resistance and support levels, respectively. Also, pay attention to the "Change in OI" column to identify where fresh writing (new positions) occurred for the day. A significant increase in Call OI at a particular strike suggests new sellers believe the price won't go above it, while a rise in Put OI indicates new sellers expect the price to stay above that level.

Next, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI. You can also calculate the PCR for today's fresh writing (ΔOI) by dividing the change in Put OI by the change in Call OI. A PCR value above 1 suggests bullishness, while below 1 points to bearishness. Finally, look for the "Max Pain" strike, which some broker platforms or financial data websites provide; this is the strike where option writers face minimum loss. By combining these insights with FII positioning data (available in the "F&O Participant-wise Open Interest" section on NSE) and the India VIX, you can build a comprehensive understanding of the market's underlying sentiment and potential movements.
