What moved the market — the factors

The market's behaviour today was primarily shaped by a mix of global and domestic factors. Positive global cues, particularly from US markets where the S&P 500 and Nasdaq rose on strong CoreWeave results easing AI spending concerns, provided an initial uplift to Indian equities. The Nasdaq surged 2% as rebounding AI stocks lifted Wall Street.

However, this optimism was tempered by rising bond yields and hawkish statements from the US Federal Reserve, which led to the market surrendering its early gains. The US dollar firmed against other currencies, including the Sterling, due to expectations of further rate hikes following hot inflation data and hawkish Fed talk. This global macro environment created headwinds for emerging markets like India.

On the domestic front, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their buying spree, injecting ₹7,212 crore into the market, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers, offloading ₹7,173 crore. This FII inflow provided some support, but DII selling pressure countered it.

Specific news also played a role. Yes Bank bondholders pressed for compliance with the Supreme Court's AT-1 order, keeping the banking sector in focus. Fairfax Financial's potential exit from IIFL Finance also garnered attention. The Reserve Bank of India's announcement of a ₹250 billion bond switch auction next week also set the stage for future bond market activity.

News that moved stocks

Here's a look at the key news items that influenced market sentiment and specific stocks today:

Global AI Stocks Rebound Amid CoreWeave Results, Nvidia Volatility US stocks, including the S&P 500 and Nasdaq, saw positive growth as CoreWeave posted impressive financial results, easing concerns about AI expenditure. This positive sentiment helped Nvidia shares recover nearly 4.5% after a steep sell-off, though Baird downgraded Nvidia due to concerns over US restrictions on chip sales to China and broader AI demand worries. Separately, Anthropic’s IPO is reportedly multiple times oversubscribed, reflecting strong bullish sentiment in the AI space.

Indian Financials in Focus Yes Bank bondholders filed a contempt petition in the Supreme Court, pressing for compliance with the court's AT-1 order that set aside the lender's write-off of ₹84 billion in AT-1 bonds. In other news, Fairfax Financial Holdings is reportedly looking to divest its nearly 11% stake in IIFL Finance to raise funds for acquiring a significant stake in IDBI Bank.

Macroeconomic Headwinds and Policy Moves The US dollar firmed against multiple currencies, including the Sterling, due to expectations of Federal Reserve interest rate hikes, prompted by an increase in consumer prices and hawkish Fed commentary. This global macro trend contributed to Indian equities surrendering early gains. Domestically, the Reserve Bank of India announced a significant switch auction worth ₹250 billion on November 16, involving replacing six bonds maturing between 2027 and 2029 with longer-dated securities.

Specific Stock Movements Indo Thai Securities shares experienced a sharp decline of nearly 90% in 2026, reaching a more-than-two-year low of ₹31.70, following significantly weaker quarterly results. Meanwhile, MRF was in the spotlight after Citi maintained its 'Outperform' rating and set a target price of ₹1,65,238, despite the tyre maker reporting declines in quarterly margins and profits due to higher raw material costs.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's session was not dominated by a single defining event but rather by a confluence of broad market flows and global cues. The initial positive sentiment stemmed from global markets, particularly the strong performance of US tech stocks following impressive earnings from AI firm CoreWeave, which eased concerns about AI spending. This contributed to a rebound in stocks like Nvidia.

However, the market's inability to hold onto its gains was largely attributed to a hawkish stance from the US Federal Reserve and the subsequent rise in global bond yields. Strong US inflation data further fuelled expectations of interest rate hikes, leading to a stronger US dollar and creating a cautious environment for risk assets.

Domestically, the consistent buying by FIIs provided underlying support, but DII selling pressure acted as a counterweight. News regarding Yes Bank's AT-1 bonds and Fairfax's potential exit from IIFL Finance also kept specific financial stocks in focus.
