How the market behaved today

In contrast, the broader markets, represented by the Nifty Midcap 100 and Nifty Smallcap 100, remained subdued, closing marginally in the red.

What moved the market — the factors

Today's market movements were primarily influenced by a confluence of global and domestic factors. A significant driver was the easing of crude oil prices, which offered some relief to investors. This, coupled with softer Japanese bond yields and the widespread expectation that the upcoming US Federal Reserve interest rate hike was largely priced into the market, contributed to improved sentiment across Indian equities. US stocks also traded cautiously higher ahead of the much-anticipated Fed decision, providing a positive global backdrop.

Domestically, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling trend, offloading shares worth ₹2,978 crore. However, this outflow was significantly cushioned by robust buying from Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), who injected ₹2,686 crore into the market. This consistent DII support has been a crucial factor in absorbing FII selling pressure over recent sessions.

Sector-wise, banking and other heavyweight stocks attracted value buying, pushing the Nifty and Sensex higher. The FMCG and PSU Bank sectors emerged as strong performers, while the IT and Pharma sectors faced selling pressure. The decline in India VIX also suggested a decrease in immediate market anxiety. The overall anticipation of the US Fed's policy decision later in the day kept traders on their toes, with Indian government bonds, for instance, seeing gains due to short covering ahead of the announcement.

News that moved stocks

Several key news items influenced market sentiment and specific stock movements today:

  • Anticipation of US Federal Reserve Rate Decision: Global markets, including India, were keenly awaiting the US Federal Reserve's interest-rate decision. US stocks edged higher as oil prices eased, with investors positioning themselves ahead of the Fed's announcement. This overarching global event created a cautious yet optimistic sentiment, influencing broad market movements and leading to short covering in Indian bonds.
  • NSE IPO Anchor Investor Allocation: The National Stock Exchange (NSE) allocated shares worth ₹6,745 crore (over $700 million) to more than 100 anchor investors ahead of its Initial Public Offering (IPO). Prominent participants included India's Life Insurance Corporation (LIC) and Norway's Norges Bank. This significant anchor book allocation signals strong institutional confidence in the upcoming IPO.
  • Easing Crude Oil Prices: Crude oil prices retreated from recent highs, offering a crucial relief to markets. This decline in oil prices was cited as a factor for Indian equities gaining and for the rise in Indian government bonds, as it alleviates concerns about inflation and current account deficit.
  • Major Real Estate Leasing Deal: ASF Group announced a significant leasing agreement, providing Genpact with four lakh square feet of office space at ASF Insignia in Gurugram. This substantial deal is a positive indicator for the commercial real estate market and likely contributed to the Realty sector's strong performance today.
  • Large-Cap Stocks Hit 52-Week Lows: Despite the broader market recovery, several prominent large-cap stocks hit fresh 52-week lows. These included Maruti Suzuki, Shree Cement, IRFC, Britannia, Ambuja Cements, Godrej Consumer, Bank of Baroda, and HDFC Life. This highlights that while frontline indices gained, underlying weakness persists in certain segments of the large-cap space.

Interpretation: For future reference, here's what different F&O build-ups typically signify:

  • Long build-up (bullish — price up, OI up): Indicates fresh buying interest with conviction, suggesting a potential continuation of the upward trend.
  • Short build-up (bearish — price down, OI up): Points to fresh selling pressure, often signaling a potential downtrend.
  • Short covering (price up, OI down): Suggests that traders who had taken short positions are now buying back to close them, leading to a price bounce. This may not always indicate genuine underlying strength.
  • Long unwinding (price down, OI down): Implies that traders who held long positions are exiting them, either due to profit-taking or concerns about weakness, contributing to a price decline.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

The dominant event influencing today's market was the anticipation of the US Federal Reserve's interest-rate decision. Global markets, including the US, were trading cautiously higher as investors awaited the outcome of the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting. Indian equities gained as expectations of a largely priced-in US Fed hike improved overall sentiment.

Adding to this, easing crude oil prices provided significant relief, as lower oil prices generally bode well for import-dependent economies like India. This combination of global cues led to a positive bias in the Indian market, particularly in frontline indices. Indian government bonds also saw gains, driven by short covering activities ahead of the Fed's policy announcement.

The market's reaction was broad, with banking and heavyweight stocks attracting value buying, contributing to the Nifty and Bank Nifty's upward movement. However, the IT sector, sensitive to global economic shifts and interest rate environments, experienced declines, while the Pharma sector also saw some weakness. The Realty sector, on the other hand, performed well, potentially benefiting from domestic news like the large leasing deal by ASF Group.

Setup for tomorrow

As we head into tomorrow's session, the outcome of the US Federal Reserve's interest-rate decision, expected overnight, will be the most critical global cue. US markets edged higher today in anticipation, and their reaction to the Fed's announcement will set the tone for Asian markets, including India. The significant drop in Brent and WTI crude oil prices overnight could provide further tailwinds for the Indian market, potentially easing inflationary concerns. Investors will also monitor the Dollar Index and US 10-year Treasury yields for directional cues. Domestically, sectors like banking and FMCG, which showed resilience today, along with the Realty sector, will be in focus. Key levels on the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty will be crucial to watch for further direction.
