Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides insight into the total number of positions held by market participants.

For the NIFTY 50, the highest Call OI, often considered a resistance level or a "ceiling" where many sellers (Call writers) expect the price to stay below, is concentrated at the 23,000 strike. Conversely, the highest Put OI, which typically acts as a support level or a "floor" where Put writers expect the price to stay above, is seen at 22,000. Today's fresh Call writing was heaviest near 22,866, suggesting that Call sellers anticipate the index to face resistance around these levels. Fresh Put writing was most significant near 22,200, indicating that Put sellers expect the index to find support there.

On the SENSEX, the heaviest Call OI is observed at the 73,000 strike, which is quite close to the current spot price, potentially acting as an immediate ceiling. Today's heaviest fresh Call writing was near 73,833, reinforcing the idea that Call sellers are positioning for an upside cap.

For the BANK NIFTY, the highest Put OI is at 55,000, which is relatively close to the current spot price and could act as a strong support base. The heaviest fresh Put writing today was near 53,900, suggesting Put sellers are confidently building positions expecting the index to remain above this level.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests more Put options are written than Call options, which can be interpreted as a bullish signal by option writers expecting prices to rise. A PCR below 1, where Call options dominate, often points to a bearish sentiment from option writers, expecting prices to fall or stay capped.

For the NIFTY 50, the PCR(OI) stands at 0.66, indicating that Call options significantly outnumber Put options in existing positions. This suggests that option writers are broadly positioned for the upside to be capped. The PCR for today's fresh flow (ΔOI) is even lower at 0.4, meaning that new positions added today were heavily skewed towards Call writing, reinforcing a bearish sentiment from the perspective of option sellers.

The SENSEX has a PCR(OI) of 0.77, suggesting a relatively balanced book but still leaning towards Call dominance. Today's flow ΔPCR at 0.52 indicates that fresh writing for the day was predominantly in Calls, pointing towards a mildly bearish sentiment from new positions.

In contrast, the BANK NIFTY's PCR(OI) is 0.88, which is closer to a balanced book. More notably, its PCR(today flow ΔOI) is 1.38. This strong reading above 1 signifies that today's fresh option writing was heavily concentrated in Put options, suggesting that Put writers are expecting the index to hold its ground or move higher.

Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the least aggregate loss if the underlying index were to expire at that specific level. It's often considered a "magnet" because the index tends to gravitate towards this point as expiry approaches, as option writers try to minimise their losses.

For the NIFTY 50, the Max Pain point is at 23,000, which is approximately 1.7% above the current spot price of 22,620.45. This suggests that option writers, on aggregate, would prefer the index to expire higher than its current level.

The SENSEX's Max Pain is at 73,000, about 0.7% above its spot price of 72,480.29. Similar to Nifty, this indicates a slight upward pull from the perspective of option writers.

The BANK NIFTY shows its Max Pain at 55,800, which is a significant 2.1% above its current spot price of 54,633.05. This substantial difference suggests a strong upward pull, implying that option writers are positioned for a considerable rally or for the index to hold well above current levels by expiry. The closer we get to expiry, the stronger this "magnet effect" tends to be.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment. Today, FIIs held a long ratio of 8.6% in Index futures, with 29,394 long contracts against 312,264 short contracts. This very low long ratio indicates a substantial net short position in index futures by FIIs. Historically, such a pronounced net short positioning by FIIs in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from these large institutional players.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of 53.9%, holding 3,360,170 long contracts versus 2,870,621 short contracts. This indicates a net long position in stock futures, suggesting a more balanced or slightly bullish stance on individual stocks compared to the broader indices.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When the NIFTY 50 and SENSEX show a mildly bearish bias from option data, particularly with strong Call writing and low PCR readings, it has historically tended to coincide with periods where these indices face resistance on the upside. The significant net short positioning by FIIs in index futures further reinforces a cautious sentiment for the broader market.

However, the bullish signals emanating from the BANK NIFTY, characterised by strong Put writing and a higher Max Pain, historically tend to suggest that the banking sector might show relative strength or act as a support to prevent a sharper decline in the broader market. When these divergent signals appear, the market has historically tended to exhibit sector-specific movements, with some indices showing resilience while others might consolidate or face downward pressure. The overall picture suggests a market that could see some underlying strength in specific sectors, even if the broader indices remain under a cautious cloud.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can empower you to form your own daily market perspective. You can access options Open Interest data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" for NIFTY, BANK NIFTY, and SENSEX. Look for the "Highest OI" for both Calls and Puts to identify potential resistance and support levels.

To gauge the sentiment from fresh positions, observe the "Change in OI" column. A significant increase in Call OI at a particular strike suggests new Call writing, indicating expected resistance. Similarly, a rise in Put OI points to new Put writing, suggesting expected support. The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) can be calculated by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI. For FII positioning, the "FII Derivatives Data" CSV file is available on the NSE website, typically updated at the end of the trading day. By combining these observations, you can build a more informed understanding of the market's underlying structure and the positioning of various participants.
