Indian Shares Poised for Muted Open Amidst Oil Price Concerns and Foreign Outflows

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices are set for a cautious start, despite positive cues from the IT sector.
  • HCLTech's Q2 FY27 earnings surpassed expectations, reporting a 21.2% year-on-year rise in net profit to ₹5,132 crore.
  • Elevated crude oil prices, which have recently been around $107 a barrel, are a significant inflationary and external balance risk for India.
  • Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) have continued their selling spree, with outflows exceeding ₹3.08 lakh crore in 2026.
  • The market's near-term outlook remains cautious, balancing strong corporate performance in IT with broader macroeconomic headwinds.

What this means in plain terms

Indian stock markets are facing a mixed bag of signals, leading to an anticipated muted opening. On one hand, the IT sector, particularly bellwethers like HCLTech, has delivered strong quarterly results, providing a much-needed boost to investor sentiment and leading to a significant rally in IT stocks on Tuesday. This suggests that corporate fundamentals in certain sectors remain robust.

However, this positive momentum is being heavily countered by two major macroeconomic concerns: persistently high crude oil prices and continued outflows by foreign investors. India is a large importer of crude oil, so rising prices directly impact inflation, the current account deficit, and the value of the rupee, creating a challenging environment for the broader economy. Simultaneously, foreign institutional investors have been consistently pulling money out of Indian equities, driven by global factors like rising US bond yields and the allure of AI-related stocks in developed markets. This dual pressure creates a cautious outlook for Indian shares, preventing a strong upward trajectory despite individual company successes.

HCLTech's Q2 FY27 Performance: Beating Expectations

HCLTech, a leading Indian IT services company, announced a robust performance for the second quarter of Financial Year 2027 (Q2 FY27), ending September 30, 2026. These results largely surpassed market expectations, providing a significant positive impulse to the IT sector.

Headline Numbers

For Q2 FY27, HCLTech reported a consolidated net profit of ₹5,132 crore, marking an impressive 21.2% increase year-on-year (YoY) and an 11% rise quarter-on-quarter (QoQ). The company's revenue from operations stood at ₹35,620 crore, reflecting an 11.5% YoY growth and a 3% QoQ increase. In constant currency (CC) terms, revenue grew 2.3% sequentially and 2.6% year-on-year.

The company's Earnings Before Interest and Tax (EBIT) margin expanded to 18.2%, a significant improvement of 131 basis points QoQ and 71 basis points YoY. This margin expansion included an 84 basis point impact from restructuring costs, implying an underlying operational margin of 19.0% when excluding these one-time expenses. New deal bookings were strong at $3.3 billion, up 27% YoY, contributing to a record high for first-half bookings. A noteworthy highlight was the advanced Artificial Intelligence (AI) revenue, which reached $219 million, surging 78% YoY and 28.4% QoQ in constant currency terms, underscoring the growing importance of AI in the company's portfolio.

Segmental and Geographic Breakdown

HCLTech's business is diversified across three primary segments. IT & Business Services, which constitutes 75.4% of the revenue mix, grew 4.4% YoY in constant currency with an EBIT margin of 18.6%. The Engineering & R&D Services segment, accounting for 16.7% of revenue, saw a 2.1% YoY constant currency growth. However, HCLSoftware revenue experienced a decline.

Geographically, India demonstrated the fastest growth at 21.8% YoY in constant currency, while the US market grew by 3.5% YoY in constant currency.

Management Commentary and Future Outlook

C Vijayakumar, CEO & Managing Director of HCLTech, highlighted the strong quarter, record bookings, and significant margin expansion, attributing much of the success to the rapidly scaling advanced AI business. The company also announced a Global Premier Partnership with Anthropic to accelerate enterprise adoption of Agentic AI, focusing on safety, cybersecurity, and governance.

For the full Financial Year 2027, HCLTech narrowed its revenue growth guidance to 2.5-4% YoY in constant currency terms, an upward revision from its earlier guidance of 1-4%. The EBIT margin guidance was retained at 17.5-18.5%. Despite the strong Q2 performance, management expressed caution regarding the external environment, citing volatile demand conditions and uneven spending patterns among some clients.

Macroeconomic Headwinds: Oil Prices and FPI Outflows

While HCLTech's results offered a glimmer of optimism, the broader Indian market faces significant headwinds from global macroeconomic factors, primarily rising crude oil prices and sustained foreign institutional investor (FII) outflows.

The Crude Oil Challenge

Crude oil prices have been on an upward trend, hovering around $107 a barrel, largely driven by escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. For India, this is a critical concern, as the country imports approximately 85% of its crude oil requirements.

The direct implications of elevated crude prices for the Indian economy are severe:

  • Inflation: Higher oil prices translate to increased fuel and transportation costs, which then feed into the prices of goods and services across the board, pushing up retail inflation. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) closely monitors oil prices due to their impact on the Consumer Price Index (CPI).
  • Current Account Deficit (CAD): A larger import bill for crude oil widens India's current account deficit, putting pressure on the nation's external balance.
  • Rupee Depreciation: A widening CAD and increased demand for US dollars to pay for oil imports can lead to a depreciation of the Indian Rupee against the US dollar, making imports even more expensive.

Analysts suggest that every $1 increase in crude oil price can add $1.5–2 billion to India's annual import bill, and a $10 rise could widen the CAD by 0.35–0.5% of GDP.

Persistent Foreign Outflows

Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) have been consistent net sellers in the Indian equity markets. On Tuesday alone, FPIs recorded outflows of ₹13.05 billion ($136.96 million), marking their 13th consecutive session of net selling. Over the first nine months of calendar year 2026, FPIs have withdrawn a record ₹3.08 lakh crore ($32.73 billion) from Indian equities, nearly double the outflows recorded in the entirety of 2025.

Several factors are contributing to this sustained selling pressure:

  • Rising US Bond Yields: US bond yields recently crossed the 5.2% mark, making dollar-denominated assets more attractive to global investors seeking "risk-free" returns.
  • Global AI Stock Rally: The significant rally in artificial intelligence-related stocks in global markets has drawn capital towards other investment destinations, diverting funds that might otherwise come to emerging markets like India.
  • India's Valuations: Some market participants also point to India's relatively high valuations compared to other emerging markets as a reason for FPIs to reduce exposure.

While Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) have been providing some counter-balancing support, buying shares worth net ₹24.39 billion on Tuesday, the sheer volume of FPI outflows remains a significant concern for market stability.

Broader IT Sector Context and AI's Role

The IT sector's performance, exemplified by HCLTech's strong results, stands out amidst the general market caution. Both the Nifty and Sensex climbed about 1.6% on Tuesday, largely led by information technology stocks.

Industry Trends

The Indian IT services industry is projected to see a strong growth recovery from FY27, driven by a global shift in technology spending from building AI infrastructure to deploying AI across enterprises. While AI offers immense opportunities, it also presents a challenge, with some industry reports suggesting that AI could cause an estimated 2-3% annual deflation across traditional IT services for the next couple of years due to increased automation and productivity.

However, this disruption is also expected to unlock a massive new market. Analysts estimate an incremental AI-led addressable market of $300-400 billion for Indian IT services by 2030, a significant expansion compared to the current industry size of approximately $280-315 billion.

HCLTech's AI Strategy

HCLTech is actively positioning itself to capitalize on this AI-driven transformation. The substantial growth in its advanced AI revenue (78% YoY in constant currency) indicates successful early adoption and execution of its AI strategy. The management's commentary emphasizes that AI is becoming a key growth driver, and the company plans to reinvest gains from operational efficiency into expanding its AI capabilities, intellectual property, and talent. This proactive approach, including strategic partnerships like the one with Anthropic, aims to build a full-stack AI offering and gain market share in this evolving landscape.

A real example

Priya, 42, ₹32L CTC, Gurugram

Priya works as a Senior Project Manager in a non-IT manufacturing firm and has been investing regularly in a diversified portfolio, including a significant allocation to large-cap Indian equities, with a fair share in the IT sector. She's been pleased with the recent bounce in IT stocks following HCLTech's strong results, as her IT mutual fund units saw a good uptick.

Let's say Priya holds ₹10 lakhs in an IT-sector focused mutual fund. On Tuesday, with the IT sector leading the market's 1.6% climb, her ₹10 lakh investment might have provisionally gained around ₹16,000. This is a welcome sight after a period of volatility.

However, Priya also frequently checks global news. The rising crude oil prices and consistent FPI outflows are a concern for her broader portfolio. If oil prices remain high, the increased import bill could weaken the rupee. For instance, if the rupee depreciates by 1% due to oil prices, her overall portfolio, especially any international investments or even domestic companies with significant import exposure, could see a marginal value erosion. Furthermore, the FPI outflows signal a lack of confidence from foreign investors, which can cap overall market upside, even if individual sectors perform well.

While HCLTech's individual performance is strong, the macroeconomic environment acts as a dampener. If FPIs continue to sell, the broader market might struggle to sustain rallies. Priya understands that while her IT fund benefits from strong earnings, the overall market's direction is influenced by these larger forces. She might see her IT fund gains partially offset by a subdued performance in other sectors, or even a slight correction if the macro headwinds intensify. For example, if the broader market falls by 0.5% due to FII selling and oil concerns, her entire ₹30 lakh equity portfolio (including the IT fund) could see a ₹15,000 notional loss, effectively negating her IT sector gains. This highlights the importance of not just looking at individual company results, but also the broader market context.

What to do this week

  1. Review your IT Sector Exposure: Given the strong performance and positive outlook for AI-driven growth in IT, assess if your portfolio's allocation to the IT sector aligns with your long-term goals. Consider rebalancing if it has become overweight or underweight relative to your strategy.
  2. Monitor Crude Oil Trends: Keep an eye on global crude oil prices and geopolitical developments. Understand that sustained high prices can impact your daily expenses (fuel, goods) and indirectly affect company profitability and the broader market.
  3. Check FPI/DII Activity: While you shouldn't react to daily flows, being aware of the trend in foreign and domestic institutional investments can offer insights into market sentiment. Resources from SEBI or AMFI India can provide aggregated data.
  4. Evaluate Export-Oriented vs. Domestic-Focused Stocks: Companies with significant export revenues might benefit from a weaker rupee (if it depreciates due to oil), while those heavily reliant on domestic consumption might face pressure from inflation. Review your holdings from this perspective.
  5. Revisit Your Emergency Fund: In an environment of macroeconomic uncertainty (inflation, potential interest rate hikes), ensuring your emergency fund is adequately stocked (6-12 months of expenses) becomes even more crucial.

FAQ

What are FPI outflows and why do they matter?

FPI outflows refer to foreign portfolio investors selling their investments in a country's financial markets and repatriating the money. They matter because large outflows can depress stock prices, weaken the local currency, and signal a lack of confidence in the economy.

How do rising crude oil prices affect the Indian economy?

Rising crude oil prices negatively impact India by increasing the import bill, widening the current account deficit, potentially depreciating the rupee, and fueling inflation through higher transportation and production costs.

Did HCLTech's earnings truly beat expectations?

Yes, HCLTech's Q2 FY27 net profit of ₹5,132 crore and revenue of ₹35,620 crore both surpassed analyst estimates, contributing to a positive sentiment for the IT sector.

What is HCLTech's outlook for the rest of FY27?

HCLTech has narrowed its FY27 revenue growth guidance to 2.5-4% in constant currency and retained its EBIT margin guidance at 17.5-18.5%, indicating cautious optimism despite a volatile global environment.

Is the Indian IT sector resilient to global slowdowns?

While the Indian IT sector has shown resilience, particularly with growth in advanced AI services, it is not entirely immune to global macroeconomic slowdowns and client spending uncertainties.

What role is AI playing in the IT sector's growth?

AI is emerging as a significant growth driver, with companies like HCLTech reporting substantial increases in advanced AI revenue. It is also expected to create new market opportunities, though it may cause some deflation in traditional IT services.
