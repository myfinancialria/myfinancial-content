Section 80C Deductions: Full List & The ₹1.5 Lakh Limit Explained (FY 2025-26)

TL;DR

  • Section 80C allows individuals and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) to reduce their taxable income by investing in or spending on specific instruments.
  • The maximum deduction available under Section 80C is ₹1.5 lakh in a financial year, which is a combined limit for all eligible investments and expenses.
  • Popular options include Public Provident Fund (PPF), Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS), life insurance premiums, and Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) contributions.
  • Other eligible deductions cover home loan principal repayments, children's tuition fees, and stamp duty/registration charges for a new house.
  • Utilising Section 80C effectively is a fundamental part of tax planning, helping to lower your overall tax liability.

What this means in plain terms

Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961, is arguably the most popular tax-saving provision for salaried individuals and self-employed professionals in India. In simple language, it allows you to reduce the amount of income on which you pay tax by investing in certain specified instruments or incurring specific expenses. Think of it as a government-approved way to save money and get a tax break simultaneously.

The key thing to remember is the overall limit: you can claim a maximum deduction of ₹1.5 lakh under Section 80C in any given financial year (FY 2025-26, for example). This limit is a combined cap for all eligible investments and expenditures you make throughout the year. Whether you invest ₹50,000 in a Public Provident Fund (PPF), pay ₹70,000 in life insurance premiums, and repay ₹30,000 of your home loan principal, the total of these (₹1.5 lakh) is the maximum you can claim. It's a powerful tool for reducing your tax outgo, provided you plan your investments wisely.

Understanding the ₹1.5 Lakh Combined Limit

The ₹1.5 lakh limit under Section 80C is a crucial aspect of tax planning. It's not ₹1.5 lakh for each investment, but a cumulative ceiling for all eligible deductions claimed under this section. This means that if you invest in multiple instruments that qualify for 80C, the total deduction you can claim from all of them combined cannot exceed ₹1.5 lakh in a financial year.

For instance, if you contribute ₹70,000 to your Employees' Provident Fund (EPF), pay ₹40,000 in life insurance premiums, and invest ₹60,000 in an ELSS fund, your total eligible amount would be ₹1,70,000. However, due to the ₹1.5 lakh limit, you can only claim a deduction of ₹1.5 lakh, not the full ₹1,70,000. This makes it essential to diversify your investments strategically to meet your financial goals while maximising your tax savings up to the prescribed limit. It also encourages taxpayers to look beyond just one investment avenue and explore various options that align with their risk appetite and liquidity needs.

Who can claim 80C deductions?

Section 80C deductions are available to two categories of taxpayers:

  • Individuals: This includes salaried employees, self-employed professionals, and business owners.
  • Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs): HUFs can also claim deductions under Section 80C for investments made in the name of any member of the HUF.

It's important to note that companies, firms, and other corporate entities are not eligible to claim deductions under Section 80C.

Popular Investment Avenues for 80C

Several investment options qualify for deductions under Section 80C, offering different risk-return profiles. Understanding these can help you choose what best fits your financial plan.

Public Provident Fund (PPF)

PPF is a long-term savings scheme backed by the government, offering attractive interest rates and tax benefits. Contributions to PPF are eligible for deduction under Section 80C. The interest earned is tax-exempt, and the maturity amount is also tax-free, making it an EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) instrument. It has a lock-in period of 15 years, with partial withdrawals allowed after 7 years.

Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS)

ELSS are diversified equity mutual funds that come with a tax benefit under Section 80C. They have a mandatory lock-in period of 3 years, which is the shortest among all 80C options. While they offer the potential for higher returns due to equity market exposure, they also carry market risk. Returns from ELSS are subject to Long Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax if they exceed ₹1 lakh in a financial year.

Life Insurance Premiums

Premiums paid for a life insurance policy for yourself, your spouse, or your children are eligible for deduction under Section 80C. For HUFs, premiums paid for the life of any member of the HUF are eligible. The deduction is subject to certain conditions, primarily that the premium payable for any year does not exceed 10% of the actual capital sum assured for policies issued on or after April 1, 2012. For policies issued before April 1, 2012, this limit was 20%.

Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)

For salaried individuals, the mandatory contributions made to the Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) by the employee are eligible for deduction under Section 80C. The employer's contribution is not considered for the employee's 80C deduction. EPF offers a fixed interest rate and is a retirement savings scheme.

National Savings Certificate (NSC)

NSC is a fixed-income investment scheme offered by the Indian Post Office. Investments in NSCs are eligible for deduction under Section 80C. The interest earned on NSC is taxable, but the accrued interest is also deemed reinvested and thus qualifies for a deduction under Section 80C in subsequent years, up to the overall limit. The maturity amount is fully taxable.

Other Eligible Expenses under Section 80C

Beyond traditional investments, several common expenses also qualify for tax deductions under Section 80C, providing additional avenues for saving tax.

Home Loan Principal Repayment

The principal component of your Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI) paid towards a housing loan is eligible for deduction under Section 80C. This is a significant benefit for homeowners. It's important to distinguish this from the interest component, which can be claimed under Section 24(b) of the Income Tax Act.

Stamp Duty and Registration Charges

If you have purchased a new house, the stamp duty and registration charges paid at the time of purchase are eligible for deduction under Section 80C. This benefit can be claimed in the year the payment is made.

Children's Tuition Fees

Tuition fees paid for the education of up to two children in any university, college, school, or other educational institution situated in India are eligible for deduction under Section 80C. This includes full-time education fees and excludes any development fees or donation payments.

Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)

This is a small savings scheme aimed at encouraging parents to build a fund for their girl child's education and marriage. Deposits made into an SSY account are eligible for deduction under Section 80C. The interest earned and the maturity amount are also tax-exempt, making it an EEE instrument.

Fixed Deposit (FD) for Tax Saving

Certain fixed deposits offered by banks with a lock-in period of 5 years also qualify for deduction under Section 80C. The interest earned on these FDs is taxable as per your income tax slab.

A real example

Meet Ankita, 32, ₹18L CTC, Mumbai. Ankita is a marketing manager and wants to maximise her tax savings for FY 2025-26.

Here's a breakdown of her eligible 80C contributions:

  1. EPF Contribution: Her mandatory employee contribution to EPF for the year totals ₹90,000.
  2. Life Insurance Premium: She pays an annual premium of ₹25,000 for a term life insurance policy.
  3. Home Loan Principal Repayment: Ankita recently bought an apartment, and the principal component of her home loan EMI for the year amounts to ₹40,000.
  4. ELSS Investment: To gain equity exposure and tax benefits, she invested ₹30,000 in an ELSS fund.

Let's calculate her total 80C eligibility:

  • EPF: ₹90,000
  • Life Insurance Premium: ₹25,000
  • Home Loan Principal: ₹40,000
  • ELSS Investment: ₹30,000

Total Eligible 80C Amount = ₹90,000 + ₹25,000 + ₹40,000 + ₹30,000 = ₹1,85,000

However, the maximum deduction allowed under Section 80C is ₹1.5 lakh.

Therefore, despite her total eligible investments and expenses being ₹1,85,000, Ankita can only claim a deduction of ₹1,50,000 under Section 80C. This ₹1.5 lakh will be reduced from her gross taxable income, thereby lowering her overall tax liability. The additional ₹35,000 (₹1,85,000 - ₹1,50,000) will not provide any further tax benefit under this section.

What to do this week

  1. Review your current 80C contributions: Gather statements for your EPF, life insurance, home loan principal, and any other 80C-eligible investments you've made so far for FY 2025-26.
  2. Calculate your current 80C utilisation: Sum up all your existing contributions to see how close you are to the ₹1.5 lakh limit.
  3. Identify any shortfall: If you are below the ₹1.5 lakh mark, research suitable 80C investment options like PPF, ELSS, or tax-saving FDs that align with your financial goals and risk profile.
  4. Set up new investments (if needed): If there's a shortfall, consider making a fresh investment in an ELSS fund via SIP, a lump sum into PPF, or a tax-saving FD.
  5. Organise your documents: Ensure you have all necessary proofs of investment (receipts, statements) ready for tax filing.

FAQ

Can NRIs claim Section 80C deductions?

Yes, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) can claim deductions under Section 80C, provided the income is taxable in India and the investments are made in India.

What happens if I invest more than ₹1.5 lakh under Section 80C?

If your total eligible investments and expenses exceed ₹1.5 lakh, you can only claim a deduction up to the maximum limit of ₹1.5 lakh. The excess amount does not provide any further tax benefit under Section 80C.

Is interest earned on PPF eligible for 80C deduction?

No, the interest earned on PPF contributions is exempt from tax and does not qualify for a deduction under Section 80C. Only the contributions made to PPF are eligible for the deduction.

Are contributions to NPS covered under Section 80C?

Contributions to the National Pension System (NPS) Tier I account are covered under Section 80CCD(1) which is part of the overall Section 80C limit. Additionally, an exclusive deduction of up to ₹50,000 is available under Section 80CCD(1B) over and above the ₹1.5 lakh limit.

Is the principal repayment of a second home loan also eligible for 80C?

Yes, the principal repayment component of a home loan for a second house is also eligible for deduction under Section 80C, subject to the overall ₹1.5 lakh limit.

Can I claim tuition fees for my spouse's education under 80C?

No, Section 80C allows deduction for tuition fees paid for the education of up to two children. Tuition fees for your spouse's or your own education are not eligible for this deduction.

What is the lock-in period for ELSS investments?

Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS) have a mandatory lock-in period of 3 years from the date of investment. This is the shortest lock-in period among all Section 80C investments.
