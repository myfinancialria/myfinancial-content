Pre-Market Brief — 10 September 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets closed mixed to negative, with US indices down and Asian markets showing caution amidst geopolitical tensions.
  • GIFT Nifty indicates a positive bias for the Indian market open, trading up by 0.39%.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers yesterday, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong buying support.
  • Rising US 10-year Treasury yields, hitting their highest since 2023, and crude oil prices above $100/barrel are key global headwinds.
  • Nifty 50's CPR pivot is at 23447.98, with 23500 and 23400 being crucial levels to watch from option chain data.
  • The day's main risk stems from global macro concerns, particularly elevated US bond yields and crude oil prices, alongside Middle East tensions.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Overnight, US markets closed lower, with the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all registering declines, reflecting investor caution. European markets, however, saw a slight positive close with the FTSE 100 gaining. This morning, Asian markets are largely trading with a negative bias, with the Hang Seng and Shanghai falling, while the Nikkei 225 saw a modest gain. The overall global tone suggests a cautious sentiment, influenced by geopolitical developments and rising US bond yields.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) depreciated against the US Dollar, rising by 0.50% to 95.29. The Dollar Index remained largely stable, down by a marginal 0.03% at 98.74. Crude oil prices saw slight declines, with Brent Crude at $100.87 (-0.34%) and WTI Crude at $95.88 (-0.18%), though both remain above the critical $100/barrel mark. Gold prices rose by 0.93% to $4457.00, indicating safe-haven demand. The US 10-year Treasury yield increased by 0.65% to 4.84%, reaching its highest level since November 2023.

Interpretation: A rising USD/INR is generally a tailwind for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters, as their dollar earnings translate to more rupees. However, it puts pressure on importers, including those buying crude oil. While crude prices saw a minor dip, they remain elevated above $100, which could pose inflation concerns and pressure for oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors in India. The significant rise in US 10-year yields is a major concern, as higher global yields can make emerging markets like India less attractive, potentially leading to FII outflows and increasing borrowing costs for Indian companies. The rise in gold prices suggests increasing risk aversion among global investors.

GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling

The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 23507.50, up by 0.39%. This suggests a positive opening for the Nifty 50 in today's session, potentially indicating a gap-up.

FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the Indian cash market yesterday, offloading equities worth ₹-583 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide strong support, with net purchases of ₹1,509 crore.

Option-chain signals (OI) — where support & resistance sit

NIFTY: The highest Call Open Interest (OI) is observed at the 24000 strike (13,396,240 contracts), followed by 23500 (12,522,705) and 23700 (11,251,760). These strikes are likely to act as immediate resistance levels. On the Put side, the highest OI is at 23400 (13,731,705 contracts), followed by 23500 (12,500,345) and 23000 (10,710,960), indicating strong support at these levels. The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) for Nifty is 1.01.

BANK NIFTY: For Bank Nifty, 57500 (2,209,080 contracts) holds the highest Call OI, followed by 57000 (912,690) and 56500 (390,990). Key Put OI is concentrated at 57500 (1,744,290 contracts), 57000 (883,710), and 56000 (778,560). The PCR is 1.17.

SENSEX: Sensex sees the highest Call OI at 75000 (9,307,860 contracts), then 75500 (5,492,260) and 74900 (5,268,540). Key Put OI is at 74500 (8,651,720 contracts), 74800 (7,905,480), and 74700 (7,134,420). The PCR is 1.08.

Interpretation: For Nifty, the PCR of 1.01 is neutral-to-slightly bullish. The 23500 strike shows significant Call and Put OI, suggesting it could be a battleground today. A break above 23500 could see Nifty move towards 23700, while a break below 23400 could lead to further downside. Bank Nifty and Sensex PCRs above 1.0 also lean towards a supportive or bullish-to-neutral bias. High Call OI indicates that sellers expect the market to find a ceiling at those strikes, while high Put OI suggests buyers are defending those levels as a floor. Combining with technicals, Nifty's range today could broadly be between 23400 (strong Put support, S1) and 23500 (Call resistance, close to R1).

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  • US Consumer Price Index (CPI) data (Friday): This is a crucial inflation indicator. Higher-than-expected inflation could strengthen expectations of further interest rate hikes by the US Federal Reserve, typically leading to global market volatility and a cautious stance from investors. Lower-than-expected inflation could ease rate hike concerns and be positive for markets.
  • US Federal Reserve's policy decision (next Wednesday): The Fed's stance on interest rates and its forward guidance on monetary policy are closely watched globally. Any hawkish surprises (e.g., unexpected rate hikes or aggressive guidance) typically lead to increased market volatility and a risk-off sentiment. Dovish signals could boost market confidence.
  • Bank of Japan (BOJ) monetary policy decision (next week): The BOJ's policy decisions, particularly any shifts in its ultra-loose monetary policy, can impact global currency markets and investor sentiment, especially for Asian equities.
  • Middle East Tensions: Escalating geopolitical tensions, particularly around critical shipping lanes, can lead to spikes in crude oil prices and increased global risk aversion, typically causing market declines.

  2. FII Flows: Keep an eye on FII activity in the cash market; continued selling could put pressure on the indices.
  3. Crude Oil Prices: Any significant movement in Brent crude prices will be crucial given its impact on India's import bill and inflation.
  4. Nifty 50 Levels: Watch the 23500 level as a key resistance and 23400 as a crucial support, as indicated by option chain data and technical levels.

Bottom line

The Indian market is poised for a potentially mixed to slightly positive open, as suggested by GIFT Nifty, despite cautious global cues and negative FII flows. The key will be how the market reacts to the rising US 10-year Treasury yields and elevated crude oil prices, which pose significant macro headwinds. The 23500 level for Nifty will be critical to watch; a sustained move above it could signal strength, while a slip below 23400 could invite further selling. Investors should remain vigilant about global developments and FII activity.
