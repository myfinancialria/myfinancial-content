Pre-Market Brief — 29 September 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets ended mixed, with US indices closing lower and Asian markets showing a cautious tone this morning.
  • GIFT Nifty indicates a negative opening bias for the Indian benchmark Nifty 50 today.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support with net buying.
  • Indian equity markets are showing signs of a deepening sell-off, with key indices like Nifty Bank breaking significant support levels.
  • Nifty 50's immediate support from Option Chain data is at 22600, while the CPR pivot is at 22660.77.
  • The primary risk for today's session remains persistent FII selling and global inflation concerns impacting sentiment.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight. US indices, the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500, all closed in negative territory, reflecting a cautious sentiment. European markets, represented by the FTSE 100, managed a slight gain. In Asia this morning, the Nikkei 225 saw a decline, while the Hang Seng also dipped, though the Shanghai Composite posted a modest gain. This overall mixed to negative global tone suggests a lack of strong positive cues for the Indian market open.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) depreciated slightly against the US Dollar, closing at ₹96.00, a 0.22% increase. The Dollar Index also saw a minor uptick of 0.19% to 101.39. In commodities, Brent Crude experienced a significant drop of 6.83% to $98.09, while WTI Crude saw a marginal rise of 0.29% to $92.87. Gold prices declined by 3.47% to $4171.40, and Silver also fell by 0.40% to $60.97. The US 10-Year Yield climbed by 1.08% to 5.24%.

Interpretation: A depreciating Rupee (USD/INR up) can be a tailwind for IT and Pharma exporters but could increase import costs and inflationary pressures for the broader economy. The sharp fall in Brent Crude is a positive for India, potentially easing concerns around inflation and current account deficit, benefiting sectors like OMCs, paints, and aviation. However, rising US 10-Year Yields typically make emerging markets less attractive, posing a risk to FII flows into India.

The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 22685.00, down by 0.60%.

Interpretation: This indicates a negative opening for the Indian Nifty 50, suggesting a potential gap-down at the market open today.

Stock & sector news that matters

D-Street stocks are breaking long-held supports as selloff deepens Indian equity markets are experiencing a significant decline, with the NSE Nifty 50 Index falling for seven consecutive weeks. The collective market capitalization has seen a substantial reduction. Why it matters: This news highlights a broad-based weakness in the Indian market, indicating a bearish sentiment. It suggests that the current downturn is more than a minor correction and could lead to further price adjustments across various stocks and sectors. Investors should be cautious about potential further downside.

Nifty Bank crashes 1,800 points in 2 days, slips below 54K for first time in 4 months. What’s next? Banking stocks have faced intense selling pressure, leading the Nifty Bank index to plunge nearly 1,800 points in just two days, breaching the 54,000 mark for the first time since early June. Why it matters: The banking sector is a significant component of the Indian market. A sharp fall in Nifty Bank indicates deep concerns within the financial space, potentially due to rising interest rates, asset quality worries, or FII selling. This weakness could drag down the broader market and impact investor confidence in financial stocks.

Global Market: ECB's Lagarde says eurozone inflation has yet to trigger second-round effects European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde noted rising Eurozone inflation, primarily due to increased energy prices, which is expected to approach 4%. She stated that second-round effects on wages have not yet been triggered. Why it matters: While this pertains to the Eurozone, global inflation trends, especially in major economies, influence central bank policies worldwide. If inflation persists globally, it could lead to continued hawkish stances from central banks, impacting global liquidity and FII flows into emerging markets like India.

Global Market: Japanese bond yields hover near multi-decade highs as inflation concerns persist Japanese government bond yields are near multi-decade highs, driven by persistent inflation, elevated oil prices, and expectations of further Bank of Japan rate hikes. Why it matters: Rising bond yields globally, including in Japan, reflect tightening monetary conditions and inflation concerns. Higher yields in developed markets can divert capital from emerging markets, impacting FII inflows into Indian equities and bonds. This adds to the global risk-off sentiment.

Adani Airport Holdings raises $120 million through local bond issue Adani Airport Holdings successfully raised ₹10 billion (approximately $120 million) through a local bond sale with a coupon rate of 8.96% per annum for three years. Why it matters: This news is specific to Adani Group companies. A successful bond issue indicates market confidence in the company's ability to raise funds, which can be positive for Adani Airport Holdings and potentially other Adani Group stocks. The coupon rate also provides insight into borrowing costs for large Indian corporates.

Azad Engineering shares jump 10% as company expands GE Vernova's manufacturing footprint Azad Engineering shares surged after inaugurating two dedicated lean manufacturing facilities for GE Vernova’s Gas Power business in Hyderabad. Why it matters: This is positive news for Azad Engineering, indicating business expansion and strengthening its partnership with a major global player like GE Vernova. This could lead to increased order books and revenue, making the stock a focus for investors.

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  • Global Inflationary Pressures: Statements from central bank officials, like ECB's Lagarde, on inflation and interest rate outlook will continue to be closely watched. Markets typically react with volatility to any hints of prolonged high inflation or further monetary tightening, which can impact global liquidity and FII flows.
  • FII Flow Trends: The consistent FII selling trend is a key concern. Any reversal or continuation of this trend will significantly influence market sentiment. Strong FII selling typically leads to market weakness, while a shift to buying can provide a boost.
  • Crude Oil Price Movement: While Brent Crude saw a sharp decline yesterday, sustained volatility in crude oil prices can impact India's inflation outlook and current account deficit. A significant rise in crude prices usually puts pressure on sectors like OMCs, paints, and aviation, while a fall is generally positive.
  • Indian Market Sentiment & Technical Levels: Given the news of Indian markets breaking long-held supports and the Nifty Bank's recent crash, the overall market sentiment remains fragile. Traders will closely monitor key technical support levels for Nifty and Bank Nifty, as a breach could trigger further selling.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Nifty 50's reaction to 22600: Will the Option Chain support at 22600 hold, or will a break below this level trigger further downside towards 22500?
  2. Bank Nifty's recovery or further slide: After a significant two-day crash, watch if Bank Nifty finds support around 54000 or if the selling pressure continues, potentially dragging the broader market.
  3. FII vs. DII battle: The ongoing tug-of-war between FII selling and DII buying will be crucial in determining market direction and stability.
  4. Global cues and crude oil prices: Any significant shifts in global market sentiment or a rebound in Brent crude prices could impact domestic trading.
  5. Specific stock movements: Keep an eye on Azad Engineering following its expansion news and Adani Group stocks after Adani Airport Holdings' bond issue.

Bottom line

The Indian market is poised for a cautious to negative open, largely influenced by negative global cues and a significant gap-down signal from GIFT Nifty. The persistent FII selling, despite strong DII support, indicates underlying weakness. The key will be Nifty's ability to hold critical support levels, particularly around 22600, as a breach could deepen the current sell-off. Investors should remain vigilant, focusing on risk management amidst the prevailing market fragility and global inflation concerns.
