Bajaj Auto Buyback: Understanding Your Capital Gains Tax

TL;DR

  • Bajaj Auto's ₹5,632 crore share buyback will be taxed under a new capital gains framework effective April 1, 2026.
  • Under the new rules, buyback proceeds are now treated as capital gains for shareholders, not as a dividend distribution tax for the company.
  • Shareholders will need to calculate short-term or long-term capital gains based on their holding period and original acquisition cost.
  • The new framework aims to simplify taxation for shareholders by aligning buyback proceeds with other capital asset sales.
  • Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) tendering shares in the buyback will also be subject to these capital gains provisions.

What this means in plain terms

When a company like Bajaj Auto buys back its own shares from the market, it's essentially returning capital to its shareholders. Until recently, the tax on such buybacks for listed companies was primarily borne by the company itself through a Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) on the distributed income, making the proceeds tax-free for shareholders.

However, a significant change has come into effect from April 1, 2026. Under this new framework, the tax liability shifts from the company to the individual shareholder. This means that if you tender your Bajaj Auto shares in this buyback, the money you receive will be treated as a capital gain in your hands, much like selling shares on the stock exchange. You will need to calculate your profit (or loss) and pay tax accordingly, based on how long you held the shares.

The Evolving Landscape of Buyback Taxation

Historically, the taxation of share buybacks for listed companies in India has seen various shifts. Prior to April 1, 2026, Section 115QA of the Income Tax Act, 1961, made buybacks by unlisted companies subject to a specific tax on distributed income at the company level. For listed companies, however, buyback proceeds were generally exempt from tax in the hands of the shareholders, as the company paid a Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) on the distributed income. This often made buybacks an attractive, tax-efficient way for companies to return surplus cash to shareholders.

The new framework, effective from April 1, 2026, has brought a significant change. It now treats the income from buybacks of listed shares as capital gains for the shareholder, aligning it with the taxation of other share sales. This move aims to streamline the tax treatment across different forms of capital returns and eliminate any arbitrage opportunities that may have existed. This means that individual shareholders, including Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), tendering their shares in the Bajaj Auto buyback will now be directly responsible for calculating and paying tax on the capital gains arising from the transaction.

Calculating Capital Gains on Buyback Proceeds

To determine your tax liability from the Bajaj Auto buyback, you need to calculate the capital gain. This is essentially the difference between the buyback price you receive and your original cost of acquiring those shares.

Determining the Cost of Acquisition

The "cost of acquisition" is typically the price at which you originally purchased the shares. If you acquired shares through multiple purchases, you might need to use the First-In, First-Out (FIFO) method, especially if you are tendering only a portion of your holdings. For instance, if you bought shares at ₹4,000 and later at ₹5,000, and the buyback offer is for shares, you would consider the ₹4,000 shares as being tendered first for calculation purposes.

Ascertaining the Period of Holding

The period you held the shares is crucial as it determines whether your gain is considered short-term or long-term.

  • Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG): If you held the shares for 12 months or less from the date of acquisition to the date of tendering in the buyback, any profit is considered a short-term capital gain.
  • Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG): If you held the shares for more than 12 months, the profit is treated as a long-term capital gain.

The buyback price offered by Bajaj Auto will be considered your "full value of consideration" for calculating this gain.

Tax Rates for Short-Term and Long-Term Capital Gains

Once you've calculated your capital gain and determined if it's short-term or long-term, you apply the relevant tax rates.

Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG)

For listed equity shares, STCG arising from transactions on which Securities Transaction Tax (STT) has been paid is taxed at a flat rate of 15% under Section 111A of the Income Tax Act, 1961. This rate applies irrespective of your total income slab.

Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG)

For listed equity shares, LTCG exceeding ₹1 lakh in a financial year, where STT has been paid, is taxed at a rate of 10% without indexation benefit under Section 112A of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The first ₹1 lakh of LTCG from such shares in a financial year is exempt from tax. This means if your total LTCG from listed equity shares in a year is ₹1.5 lakhs, you pay 10% tax only on ₹50,000 (₹1.5 lakhs - ₹1 lakh exemption).

It's important to note that these rates are applicable to resident individuals. For Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), the tax rates for STCG and LTCG on listed shares are generally the same, but specific provisions regarding withholding tax (TDS) might apply.

A real example

Let's consider Meera, 42, a salaried professional in Gurugram, earning ₹35L per annum.

Meera had purchased 500 shares of Bajaj Auto on June 15, 2024, at an average price of ₹4,500 per share. Her total investment was ₹22,50,000. Bajaj Auto announces a buyback offer at ₹8,000 per share, and Meera decides to tender all her 500 shares, which are accepted by the company on July 10, 2026.

  1. Date of Acquisition: June 15, 2024
  2. Date of Tendering in Buyback: July 10, 2026
  3. Period of Holding: More than 12 months. This means the gain will be Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG).
  4. Cost of Acquisition: 500 shares * ₹4,500/share = ₹22,50,000
  5. Buyback Proceeds: 500 shares * ₹8,000/share = ₹40,00,000
  6. Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG): ₹40,00,000 (Proceeds) - ₹22,50,000 (Cost) = ₹17,50,000

Now, let's calculate the tax:

  • Exemption for LTCG on listed shares under Section 112A: First ₹1,00,000 is exempt.
  • Taxable LTCG: ₹17,50,000 - ₹1,00,000 = ₹16,50,000
  • Tax Rate: 10% on taxable LTCG.
  • LTCG Tax Payable: 10% of ₹16,50,000 = ₹1,65,000

Meera would need to pay ₹1,65,000 as long-term capital gains tax on her buyback proceeds, assuming no other LTCG from listed equity shares in that financial year.

FAQ

What is a share buyback?

A share buyback is when a company repurchases its own shares from the open market or from its shareholders. It's a way for companies to return cash to shareholders, reduce the number of outstanding shares, and potentially boost earnings per share.

How has the taxation of buybacks changed recently?

Effective April 1, 2026, the tax liability for buyback proceeds from listed shares shifts from the company (which previously paid DDT) to the individual shareholder. Shareholders now pay capital gains tax on the profit.

Is the ₹1 lakh LTCG exemption still applicable to buyback gains?

Yes, the exemption of the first ₹1 lakh of Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) from the sale of listed equity shares (where STT is paid) under Section 112A of the Income Tax Act, 1961, remains applicable to gains from buybacks.

Do NRIs pay tax on buyback proceeds?

Yes, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) tendering shares in a buyback are also subject to capital gains tax in India. The rates for STCG and LTCG are generally the same as for residents, but specific TDS provisions may apply.

What is the significance of the holding period for buyback shares?

The holding period determines whether your gain is short-term (held for 12 months or less, taxed at 15%) or long-term (held for more than 12 months, taxed at 10% above ₹1 lakh exemption).

What if I incur a loss in the buyback?

If your buyback proceeds are less than your cost of acquisition, you incur a capital loss. This loss can be set off against other capital gains in the same financial year or carried forward to be set off in future years as per income tax rules.

How do I report buyback gains in my income tax return?

You will need to report the capital gains from the buyback under the head "Capital Gains" in your Income Tax Return (ITR) form for the relevant assessment year. It's advisable to consult a tax professional for accurate filing.
