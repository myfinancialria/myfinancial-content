Market Wrap — 26 October 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian markets staged a notable recovery today, with Nifty 50 closing higher by 0.68% after early losses.
  • Strong domestic institutional investor (DII) buying and a broad-based rally in mid and smallcap stocks were key drivers.
  • Ather Energy Ltd. was the standout gainer, surging over 14% in the Nifty 500 pack.
  • Five-Star Business Finance Ltd. emerged as the top loser, declining nearly 7.5%.
  • DIIs were significant net buyers at ₹3,235 crore, while FIIs also turned net buyers with ₹888 crore.

How the market behaved today

Indian equities witnessed a strong rebound today, shaking off early losses to close firmly in the green. The Nifty 50 ended the session at 22753.20, marking a gain of 0.68%, after touching its day's high at the close. The Bank Nifty also closed positively at 54366.70, up 0.15%, though its gains were more modest compared to the broader market. The day saw a recovery trend, with indices climbing steadily after opening lower. Midcap and Smallcap indices significantly outperformed, indicating a broad-based participation in the rally.

What moved the market — the factors

Today's market action was primarily driven by a robust recovery from early session lows, fueled by strong buying interest from Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs). The Nifty 50 and other key indices saw a steady upward trajectory throughout the day, indicating underlying strength and positive sentiment.

A significant factor was the broad-based participation, particularly visible in the Nifty Midcap 100 and Nifty Smallcap 100 indices, which surged by 1.19% and 1.38% respectively. This suggests that the rally was not confined to large-cap stocks but extended across the broader market.

Sectoral performance also played a crucial role. The Metal, Pharma, and Energy sectors were the top gainers, contributing significantly to the overall market uplift. The Metal sector, in particular, climbed by 2.21%, indicating renewed investor interest. On the flip side, the Financial Services sector, despite some positive Q2 earnings reports from individual banks, saw some of its constituents among the top losers, suggesting selective profit-taking or concerns within specific segments.

The consistent buying by DIIs, who infused ₹3,235 crore into the market today, provided a strong floor and propelled the indices higher. FIIs also turned net buyers, adding ₹888 crore, which further bolstered market sentiment. This combined institutional support helped the market overcome initial jitters and sustain the positive momentum.

  • FMCG: +0.28%. The Fast-Moving Consumer Goods sector posted modest gains, suggesting a relatively stable but subdued performance.

News that moved stocks

  • Paytm Q2 Results: Profit rockets 976% YoY to Rs 226 crore, revenue jumps 28%

    • Fintech major Paytm reported a massive 976% year-on-year growth in consolidated net profit to ₹226 crore, with revenue jumping 28%. This strong earnings report could positively influence investor sentiment towards the fintech sector.

  • Indian Overseas Bank to raise $500 million as Q2 profit jumps 56%

    • Indian Overseas Bank announced plans to raise $500 million through external commercial borrowings after reporting a 56% rise in Q2 net profit to ₹1,912 crore. This news highlights the improving health of public sector banks and their expansion plans.

  • Bank of Maharashtra Q2 profit jumps 41.5% as bad loans decline

    • Bank of Maharashtra posted a 41.5% year-on-year rise in Q2 net profit to ₹2,310 crore, driven by stronger income and improved asset quality. This result, along with other public sector banks, indicates a positive trend for the banking sector.

  • Tamilnad Mercantile Bank Q2 Results: Net profit rises 42% to record Rs 451 crore on high net interest income

    • Tamilnad Mercantile Bank achieved a record net profit of ₹451 crore for Q2, a 42% rise, buoyed by a substantial increase in interest income. This further reinforces the positive earnings trend seen in the banking space.

  • The Rs 1.5 lakh crore MTF problem: How shrinking leveraged positions are deepening India's smallcap sell-off

    • A report highlighted concerns over the intensifying smallcap sell-off due to declining margin trading facility (MTF) positions, with nearly ₹72,000 crore exposed to smallcaps. This news provides context to the volatility in the broader market, especially for smallcap stocks, despite today's positive close for the Nifty Smallcap 100.

  • Anthropic's filing for blockbuster US IPO shows key executives and board

    • AI frontrunner Anthropic has filed for a US initial public offering, with a potential mid-November listing. While a US-specific event, the buzz around a potential $1.5 trillion valuation for an AI company could influence global tech sentiment, including in India.

Market breadth

The market breadth was overwhelmingly positive today, indicating a broad-based rally. Out of 495 stocks traded on the NSE, 334 advanced while only 161 declined. This advance/decline ratio of approximately 2:1 suggests strong buying interest across various segments of the market.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's session was largely characterized by a strong recovery from initial weakness, primarily driven by robust domestic institutional buying and positive Q2 earnings reports from several key companies. The market's ability to rebound after early losses suggests underlying resilience and investor confidence.

Several public sector banks, including Indian Overseas Bank and Bank of Maharashtra, reported strong Q2 profit growth and improving asset quality, which generally bodes well for the banking sector. Tamilnad Mercantile Bank also posted record profits. However, despite these positive results, the broader Bank Nifty saw only modest gains, and some financial services stocks like Five-Star Business Finance and AU Small Finance Bank were among the top losers, indicating selective profit-taking or specific concerns within the non-banking financial sector.

The fintech space also saw positive news with Paytm reporting a massive 976% year-on-year profit growth in Q2. This could contribute to positive sentiment in the digital payments and technology-driven financial services segment.

The broader market, especially mid and small caps, showed significant strength, with the Nifty Midcap 100 and Nifty Smallcap 100 outperforming the frontline indices. This broad participation, coupled with strong DII flows, suggests that investors are finding value beyond the large-cap space, although concerns about the "₹1.5 lakh crore MTF problem" and its potential impact on smallcap sell-off were also highlighted in the news.

Overall, the session was driven by a combination of positive corporate earnings, strong domestic institutional support, and a broad-based recovery across market segments, overcoming initial cautiousness.

Setup for tomorrow

Tomorrow, investors will likely keep an eye on global cues, especially the US markets, following the news of Anthropic's planned IPO which could set a benchmark for AI valuations. The consistent DII buying trend will be crucial for sustaining momentum. Key levels to watch for Nifty 50 will be today's high at 22753.20 as immediate resistance, and 22387.35 as support.

Globally, the USD/INR closed higher at 95.18, and the Dollar Index also saw a slight uptick at 101.96. Brent Crude and WTI Crude both closed lower, which could be a positive for crude oil importing nations like India. Gold and Silver saw modest gains, while the US 10-Year Yield dipped slightly to 4.98. These global factors will set the tone for tomorrow's opening.
