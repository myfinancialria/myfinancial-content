Story 1: Don't Let Your Old PF Account Go Inoperative: What You Need to Know

Topic signal: Your old PF account isn’t dead money: You may be missing out on this important EPFO rule

Many salaried individuals believe that if their Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) account receives no contributions for three years, it becomes inoperative and stops earning interest. This is a common misconception. Your EPF account can continue to earn interest even if it's inactive, depending on your age and circumstances.

An EPF account is generally classified as inoperative if no contributions have been received for 36 months after you retire at 55 or above, or permanently move abroad, or in case of the member's death and no claim is made. If you leave a job before age 55, your PF balance will continue to earn interest until you turn 58, even without new contributions.

If your account does become inoperative, your accumulated balance is still claimable. You can use Form 19 for a final withdrawal or Form 13 to transfer your old PF balance to a new employer's account, after ensuring your Know Your Customer (KYC) details are updated.

Story 2: Multi-Asset Funds: A Diversified Approach to Investing

Topic signal: Multi-Asset Funds: Can one fund do the job of five investments – equity, debt, gold, silver and real estate?

Multi-asset allocation funds offer a way to diversify your investments across different asset classes within a single fund. As per SEBI guidelines, these funds must invest at least 10% in each of three distinct asset classes, typically including equity, debt, and commodities like gold.

The main benefit of multi-asset funds is diversification, which can help manage risk and potentially provide more stable returns across various market conditions. Instead of managing separate investments in stocks, bonds, and gold, a multi-asset fund handles the allocation and rebalancing for you.

While these funds offer convenience and professional management, it's important to remember that their risk profile can vary significantly depending on the fund manager's allocation strategy. Always review the fund's specific asset mix and objectives to ensure it aligns with your financial goals and risk tolerance.
