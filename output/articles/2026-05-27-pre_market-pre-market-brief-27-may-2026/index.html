Pre-Market Brief — 27 May 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets closed mixed, with US tech indices showing strength while Asian markets largely ended lower.
  • Crude oil prices saw a significant decline, which is a positive development for India, a major oil importer.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support by buying Indian equities.
  • Several prominent companies, including ITC and Page Industries, are trading ex-dividend today.
  • HDFC Bank is reportedly under an internal probe, and Coal India's Offer For Sale (OFS) could impact its stock performance.

Where global markets closed

Global markets presented a mixed picture in yesterday's session. US technology stocks led gains, pushing the Nasdaq and S&P 500 higher, reflecting continued optimism around AI infrastructure and semiconductor demand. In contrast, the Dow Jones Industrial Average saw a slight dip. Across Asia, markets largely closed in negative territory, with Hang Seng and Shanghai recording notable declines, while the Nikkei 225 managed a marginal gain. European markets, represented by the FTSE 100, closed slightly positive.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) depreciated against the US Dollar, closing at ₹95.79, marking a 0.56% increase for the dollar. This weakening of the rupee could make imports more expensive and potentially impact companies with significant foreign currency debt. The Dollar Index, which measures the dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies, saw a marginal decline of 0.06% to 99.09.

In the commodities space, crude oil prices experienced a significant drop. Brent Crude fell by 4.57% to $95.03 per barrel, and WTI Crude was down 2.11% to $91.91 per barrel. This substantial decline in oil prices is a positive development for India, as lower crude import bills can help manage inflation and improve the country's current account deficit.

Gold prices dipped by 0.42% to $4481.40, while Silver also declined by 1.38% to $75.25. A fall in precious metal prices often indicates a shift in investor sentiment towards riskier assets or reduced safe-haven demand.

The US 10-Year Treasury Yield decreased by 1.43% to 4.49%. A falling US bond yield can sometimes signal easing inflation concerns or a potential shift in monetary policy outlook, which could influence global capital flows.

Recent FII/DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling trend in the Indian equity market yesterday, offloading shares worth ₹2,408 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, provided crucial support by net buying equities worth ₹1,361 crore.

Over the last five trading sessions, FIIs have been net sellers in four out of five sessions, indicating a consistent outflow of foreign capital. In contrast, DIIs have been net buyers in all five sessions, absorbing the FII selling pressure.

Overnight news that matters

Here are some of the most market-relevant news items from overnight:

Coal India shares drop 6% after OFS launch Coal India's stock fell over 6% after the government announced an Offer for Sale (OFS) worth ₹5,000 crore, priced at a 10% discount to the previous closing price. This matters to Indian markets as it's a significant government divestment, potentially increasing supply of the stock in the short term and impacting sentiment in public sector undertakings (PSUs).

HDFC Bank shares fall 2% on reports of internal probe over Rs 45 cr interest payments HDFC Bank shares declined following reports of an internal investigation into ₹45 crore payments to the Maharashtra State Road Development Corporation. This news is significant for the banking sector and HDFC Bank specifically, as any probe into financial irregularities can impact investor confidence and the stock's performance.

Oil shock fears ease, but $80-$90 crude may be the new normal: Arvind Sanger Market expert Arvind Sanger suggests that while extreme oil price surges might be less likely, consumers should prepare for elevated energy costs, with crude potentially settling in the $80-$90 range. This matters to Indian markets because sustained high oil prices, even if not at extreme levels, can still exert inflationary pressure and impact the profitability of energy-intensive sectors. However, the recent sharp drop in crude is a near-term positive.

The aluminium boom is here: Why Morgan Stanley says Hindalco is set to explode Morgan Stanley initiated coverage on Hindalco Industries with an 'Overweight' rating and a target price of ₹1,325, citing a favourable aluminium demand-supply outlook and strong free cash flow generation. This is important for Indian markets as it could drive positive sentiment in the metals sector, particularly for aluminium producers like Hindalco.

Stock Market Holiday 2026: Are NSE, BSE closed tomorrow on May 28 for Bakri Id? Check market holidays here Indian stock markets (NSE and BSE) will be closed tomorrow, May 28, for the Bakri Id holiday. This is crucial information for traders and investors to note, as it means a shortened trading week and impacts liquidity and trading strategies for the remaining sessions.

What to watch in today's session

  1. FII/DII Flows: Continue to monitor the trend of foreign and domestic institutional flows. Persistent FII selling could cap market upside, while DII buying provides a floor.
  2. Crude Oil Prices: After yesterday's significant fall, further movements in Brent crude will be key. Sustained lower oil prices are beneficial for India's economy and could support market sentiment.
  3. Banking Sector Performance: Watch how HDFC Bank reacts to the internal probe news. The broader Bank Nifty, which has underperformed recently, will also be in focus, with analysts looking for selective opportunities, especially in PSU banks.
  4. Metal Stocks: Following Morgan Stanley's positive outlook on Hindalco, keep an eye on aluminium and other metal stocks for potential upward momentum.
  5. Coal India: The stock's reaction to the government's OFS will be important. Any significant price action could influence other PSU stocks undergoing divestment.
  6. Market Holiday: Remember that tomorrow, May 28, is a market holiday for Bakri Id, leading to a shortened trading week.

Bottom line

Indian markets are likely to open with mixed cues today. While the significant drop in crude oil prices and positive sentiment around select sectors like metals offer some tailwinds, continued FII selling and specific stock-related news like the HDFC Bank probe could induce caution. Investors should remain vigilant, especially given the upcoming market holiday tomorrow.
