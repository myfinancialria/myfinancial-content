Your Small Savings Scheme Interest Rates for October-December 2026 are Out!

TL;DR

  • The government has kept interest rates for most small savings schemes unchanged for October-December 2026.
  • This is the tenth consecutive quarter with stable rates for many popular schemes.
  • Schemes like PPF, NSC, and Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana will continue to offer the same returns.
  • These schemes are popular for their safety and assured returns, backed by the government.
  • Check the latest rates to plan your savings for the upcoming festive season and beyond.

What's this about?

The Indian government regularly reviews and sets the interest rates for various "small savings schemes" (like PPF, NSC, Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana, etc.). These are popular savings options, especially for those who prefer safe, government-backed investments. The rates are typically announced every three months.

Why it's in the news right now

The Ministry of Finance announced on September 30, 2026, that the interest rates for small savings schemes for the third quarter of the financial year 2026-27, which runs from October 1 to December 31, 2026, will remain unchanged. This means that for the tenth consecutive quarter, many of these rates have stayed the same, providing stability for millions of savers.

How it actually works

Small savings schemes are investment products offered by the government, primarily through post offices and some banks. They are designed to encourage savings among the general public, offering fixed and assured returns. The interest rates for these schemes are reviewed and adjusted quarterly by the Ministry of Finance.

Here are the interest rates for the October-December 2026 quarter:

  • Public Provident Fund (PPF): 7.1% per annum
  • Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY): 8.2% per annum
  • Senior Citizen Savings Scheme (SCSS): 8.2% per annum, paid quarterly
  • National Savings Certificate (NSC): 7.7% per annum, compounded annually but paid at maturity
  • Kisan Vikas Patra (KVP): 7.5% per annum, matures in 115 months (9 years and 7 months)
  • Post Office Monthly Income Scheme (POMIS): 7.4% per annum, paid monthly
  • Post Office Time Deposits (TD):

    • * 1-year TD: 6.9% per annum * 2-year TD: 7.0% per annum * 3-year TD: 7.1% per annum * 5-year TD: 7.5% per annum

  • 5-Year Post Office Recurring Deposit (RD): 6.7% per annum
  • Post Office Savings Account (POSA): 4.0% per annum

These rates apply to new investments made during this quarter and, for some schemes like PPF and SCSS, also to existing balances.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine you have a fixed deposit in your local bank or post office. Every three months, the government acts like the bank manager, checking how the economy is doing. Based on this, they decide if the interest rate on your deposit will go up, down, or stay the same for the next three months. For this quarter, the "bank manager" has decided to keep most rates exactly as they were, providing a steady return for your savings.

Why it matters to you

  1. Predictable Returns: If you rely on these schemes for your savings, like many retirees or parents saving for their child's future, these stable rates mean you know exactly how much your money will grow.
  2. Financial Planning: Knowing the rates in advance helps you plan your investments for the next three months. For instance, if you were considering a PPF or NSC, you know the return you'll get.
  3. Safety First: These schemes are backed by the Government of India, making them very safe options, especially for those who prefer guaranteed returns over market-linked risks.
  4. Tax Benefits: Several of these schemes, like PPF, NSC, and Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana, offer tax benefits under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961, making them attractive for tax-saving purposes under the old tax regime.

Common confusions cleared up

  • Myth: Small savings rates change every quarter, so my existing investment rate will also change.

    • * Fact: While rates are reviewed quarterly, for schemes like PPF, the interest rate applicable for the quarter applies to the entire outstanding balance. For instruments like NSC and KVP, the interest rate is fixed at the time of investment for the entire tenure.

  • Myth: These schemes are only for rural areas or specific groups.

    • * Fact: While some schemes like Kisan Vikas Patra were initially designed with specific groups in mind, most small savings schemes are open to all resident Indians. Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana is specifically for a girl child, and SCSS is for senior citizens.

  • Myth: Small savings schemes offer the highest returns available.

    • * Fact: Small savings schemes offer assured and safe returns. Depending on market conditions, other investment avenues like mutual funds or certain bank fixed deposits might offer higher returns, but they often come with different risk profiles. The government balances returns with safety and other economic factors.

FAQ

How often are these rates revised?

The interest rates for small savings schemes are reviewed and revised by the Ministry of Finance on a quarterly basis.

Can I open these accounts online?

While some banks offer online facilities for certain small savings schemes, most are traditionally opened and operated through post offices. It's best to check with your nearest post office or specific bank for their current procedures.

Is the interest earned from these schemes taxable?

The taxability varies by scheme. For example, PPF offers an "Exempt-Exempt-Exempt" (EEE) status, meaning contributions, interest, and maturity amounts are tax-free. NSC interest is compounded annually but paid at maturity, and the interest is taxable, though the investment qualifies for Section 80C deduction. SCSS interest is taxable if it exceeds a certain limit. Always check the specific tax rules for each scheme.

What is the minimum investment required for these schemes?

The minimum investment varies by scheme. For instance, PPF requires a minimum of ₹500 per financial year, while NSC and KVP can be opened with ₹1,000.
