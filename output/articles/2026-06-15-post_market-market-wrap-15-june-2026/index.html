Market Wrap — 15 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian equities witnessed a strong rally today, with both Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing significantly higher.
  • A preliminary US-Iran peace framework eased geopolitical tensions, leading to a sharp drop in crude oil prices globally.
  • Realty, Auto, and Metal sectors led the charge, while Pharma and Media saw some selling pressure.
  • Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their strong buying spree, offsetting selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs).
  • The Indian Rupee strengthened considerably against the US Dollar, hitting a five-week high.

How the market closed

Indian benchmark indices surged on Monday, buoyed by positive global cues following an interim US-Iran peace framework. The Nifty 50 closed above the 23,850 mark, gaining nearly 1%, while the Bank Nifty also ended in the green, crossing 57,190.

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, cooled off by 2.51% to settle at 14.35, indicating a reduction in market uncertainty.

Sectors — winners and losers

The market rally was broad-based, with several sectors witnessing robust buying interest.

Top 3 Gainer Sectors:

  • REALTY: +3.96% - The sector saw significant gains, likely benefiting from improved overall market sentiment and positive economic outlook.
  • AUTO: +2.60% - Automobile stocks revved up, possibly on hopes of sustained demand and easing input costs due to falling crude oil prices.
  • METAL: +1.78% - Metal counters shined, potentially driven by global growth optimism and a weaker dollar.

Top 3 Loser Sectors:

  • PHARMA: -0.66% - The pharmaceutical sector experienced some profit booking, making it the top laggard of the day.
  • MEDIA: -0.14% - Media stocks also faced selling pressure, closing marginally lower.
  • FMCG: +0.44% - Despite being listed among the top losers, the FMCG sector managed to close in positive territory, albeit with minimal gains.

Market breadth

The market breadth was overwhelmingly positive today, with 377 advances against just 123 declines out of 500 stocks. This indicates a broad-based rally where buying interest was spread across a significant number of stocks, rather than being concentrated in just a few large-caps.

FII / DII flows

Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide strong support to the Indian markets, injecting a substantial ₹5,341 crore today. This robust buying by DIIs helped absorb the selling pressure from Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs), who were net sellers to the tune of ₹1,082 crore.

| Investor Type | Net Position (₹ crore) | | :------------ | :--------------------- | | FII | -1,082 | | DII | 5,341 |

Over the past five trading sessions, FIIs have consistently been net sellers, with their net outflow for the period amounting to ₹15,316 crore. Conversely, DIIs have been persistent net buyers, pumping in a cumulative ₹24,009 crore into the Indian equity markets during the same period, providing a crucial cushion against foreign outflows.

What moved markets today

1. US-Iran Peace Framework Eases Geopolitical Tensions A preliminary agreement between Washington and Tehran to resolve the Middle East conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz significantly boosted global market sentiment. This development eased geopolitical concerns and led to a sharp decline in crude oil prices, which fell below $85 a barrel. Market Impact: The easing tensions and lower oil prices improved inflation and interest-rate expectations, lifting overall market sentiment and driving Indian equities higher. The Indian Rupee also strengthened significantly against the US Dollar, hitting a five-week high.

2. Government to Divest Stake in GIC The Indian government announced its plan to sell up to a 5% stake in General Insurance Corporation (GIC) through an Offer For Sale (OFS). The OFS will open for non-retail investors on June 16 and for retail investors on June 17. Market Impact: This divestment move is part of the government's strategy to raise funds and could attract institutional interest in the insurance sector.

3. SEBI Revamps ETF Trading Framework SEBI has introduced a revamped framework for ETF trading, including dynamic price bands. Previously, equity, debt, and commodity ETFs had a fixed 20% price limit, while overnight ETFs operated with a 5% band. Market Impact: This regulatory change aims to enhance market efficiency and investor protection in the rapidly growing ETF segment, potentially leading to more robust trading mechanisms.

4. NSE Launches 11 New Sectoral Indices NSE Indices has introduced 11 new sectoral benchmarks, expanding its total sectoral index count to 34. These new indices, including Nifty Power and Nifty Hospitals, aim to deepen sector-specific market coverage. Market Impact: This move will support the growing passive investment ecosystem and provide more granular options for investors and fund managers looking for sector-specific exposure.

Setup for tomorrow

Indian markets are likely to open on a positive note tomorrow, carrying forward the momentum from today's global optimism. The interim US-Iran peace deal and the subsequent fall in crude oil prices are significant tailwinds. Investors will closely watch the government's OFS for GIC, which opens for non-retail investors tomorrow.

Globally, US futures are pointing higher, and Asian markets are expected to react positively to the easing geopolitical tensions. Overnight, Brent crude fell over 5%, and WTI crude dropped more than 5.5%, which is a positive for India's import bill. The Dollar Index was down, and the Rupee strengthened, indicating a favourable currency environment.
