Market Wrap — 28 July 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, ended marginally lower today after a day of range-bound trading.
  • Weakness in FMCG, Energy, and PSU Bank sectors weighed on the market, offsetting gains in IT and Realty.
  • Coforge Ltd. (IT) was the standout gainer, surging over 10%, while Suzlon Energy Ltd. (Capital Goods) was the biggest loser, falling nearly 10% after its Q1 results.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, offloading ₹1,688 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support with net buying of ₹2,329 crore.
  • The India VIX saw a slight dip, indicating a marginal easing of market volatility.
  • F&O positioning data is unavailable for today's wrap and will be included from the next session.

How the market behaved today

Indian equity benchmarks closed marginally lower today, surrendering early gains to end a largely range-bound session. The Nifty 50 closed at 23985.35, down 0.04%, while the Bank Nifty ended at 56755.60, declining by 0.58%. Midcap and Smallcap indices saw mixed performance.

What moved the market — the factors

Today's market movement was a tug-of-war between strong sectoral performance and broader selling pressure. The Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty struggled to hold onto early gains, ultimately closing in the red. A significant driver was the continued selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs), who net sold ₹1,688 crore in the cash market. This selling was largely absorbed by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), who net bought ₹2,329 crore, providing crucial support.

Sectorally, the Information Technology (IT) sector was a clear outperformer, surging over 3%, driven by positive sentiment and specific stock movements like Coforge and Affle. The Realty sector also showed robust gains. However, this strength was countered by considerable weakness in heavyweights like Fast Moving Consumer Goods (FMCG), Energy, and PSU Banks. Key FMCG stocks like Hindustan Unilever and Godfrey Phillips India witnessed significant declines, contributing to the overall market drag. Global cues also played a role, with US stock futures indicating a weaker open and Brent crude oil prices slipping below $85, which helped the Indian Rupee strengthen but also reflected broader global economic concerns.

News that moved stocks

  • L&T Q1 Results Impress: Larsen & Toubro (L&T) reported a consolidated net profit increase of nearly 14% year-on-year to ₹4,123 crore for Q1 FY27, with revenue up 7%. This positive earnings report could provide support to the Capital Goods sector.
  • Suzlon Energy Shares Crash Post Q1 Results: Suzlon Energy's shares fell nearly 10% after its Q1 FY27 net profit slipped 6% YoY to ₹305 crore and EBITDA margins compressed to 15.6%, despite a 22.5% growth in revenue.
  • Tata Capital's Strong Q1 Performance: Tata Capital announced a substantial net profit jump of over 56% YoY to ₹1,547 crore, with revenue from operations rising over 15%. This strong performance could positively impact the Financial Services sector.
  • Birlasoft Q1 Net Profit Jumps: Birlasoft reported a 51% YoY increase in net profit to ₹161 crore for Q1 FY27, with total contract value (TCV) rising 20%. This contributed to the positive sentiment in the IT sector.
  • Rupee Strengthens on Oil Slide and Intervention: The Indian rupee intensified to its strongest position in two weeks, aided by a decline in oil prices and potential central bank intervention. A stronger rupee is generally positive for import-dependent sectors.
  • US Markets Face Sell-off Concerns: US stock futures indicated a weaker open, with chip stocks facing a fresh sell-off amid concerns about AI spending returns. This global sentiment could influence Indian markets tomorrow.

Market breadth

The market breadth today leaned towards declines, with 314 stocks falling against 185 advances out of a total of 499 traded stocks. This indicates that despite the Nifty's marginal dip, more stocks across the broader market experienced negative movement.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree today, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net buyers, providing crucial support to the market.

This trend suggests that domestic liquidity is actively absorbing the foreign outflows, preventing a sharper market correction.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's session was primarily driven by a combination of broad market flows, specific earnings announcements, and global cues rather than a single defining event. The continued selling by FIIs, largely counteracted by DII buying, set the overall tone.

Several companies released their Q1 FY27 results, which significantly impacted their respective stocks and sectors:

  • Larsen & Toubro (L&T) reported a 14% YoY rise in net profit, which could support sentiment in the Capital Goods sector.
  • Tata Capital saw its net profit jump 56% YoY, potentially boosting the Financial Services sector.
  • Birlasoft posted a 51% YoY increase in net profit, contributing to the strong performance of the IT sector.
  • Conversely, Suzlon Energy witnessed a nearly 10% decline in its share price after its Q1 net profit slipped 6% YoY, highlighting the market's reaction to weaker-than-expected earnings.

Globally, the decline in Brent crude prices below $85 and concerns over US chip stocks facing a sell-off also influenced investor sentiment, contributing to the cautious approach seen in the Indian market.

Setup for tomorrow

Tomorrow, global cues will continue to be a key factor. US stock futures pointing to a weaker open and concerns around AI spending could keep investors cautious. The decline in Brent crude oil prices and the strengthening Rupee might offer some relief. Investors will be watching for any further FII activity and how DIIs continue to counter it. Key sectors to watch include IT, which showed strong resilience today, and FMCG, which experienced significant declines. Individual stock reactions to ongoing Q1 earnings season will also be crucial.
