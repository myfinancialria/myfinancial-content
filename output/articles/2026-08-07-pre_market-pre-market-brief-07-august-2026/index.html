Pre-Market Brief — 07 August 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets presented a mixed picture, with US indices closing lower while some Asian markets showed resilience.
  • GIFT Nifty indicates a slightly positive opening bias for the Indian benchmark Nifty.
  • Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net buyers yesterday, providing support, while Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned net sellers.
  • Rising crude oil prices, fueled by Strait of Hormuz tensions, are a significant concern.
  • Nifty's key level to watch is around 24600, which acts as both a pivot and a significant Call/Put OI strike.
  • The day's main risks include geopolitical developments impacting crude and the ongoing adjustments to India's new closing auction system.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Global markets exhibited a mixed performance overnight. Major US indices closed lower, with the Dow Jones shedding 0.85%, the Nasdaq down 0.06%, and the S&P 500 declining by 0.18%, as investors booked profits after a recent rally. In Asia this morning, the picture was varied; Japan's Nikkei 225 was down 0.60%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng gained 0.15% and China's Shanghai Composite rose 0.49%. South Korean stocks fluctuated amidst concerns over AI spending.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) traded at 95.26, depreciating by 0.19% against the US Dollar. The Dollar Index (DXY) remained largely stable at 99.96, with a marginal decline of 0.01%. In the energy markets, WTI Crude oil saw a significant rise of 1.72% to 78.62, while Brent Crude data was unavailable. Gold prices surged by 1.92% to 4323.50, and Silver also gained 1.69% to 62.47. The US 10-Year Treasury Yield increased by 1.15% to 4.67.

Interpretation: A rising USD/INR is generally positive for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters as their dollar earnings translate to more rupees, but it puts pressure on importers and can contribute to inflationary concerns. The significant jump in WTI Crude oil prices, especially amid Strait of Hormuz tensions, is a major headwind for India, a net oil importer. This could negatively impact sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation due to higher input costs. The rise in gold and silver suggests increased safe-haven demand amidst global uncertainties. An uptick in US 10-Year Yields could potentially make emerging markets like India less attractive for FIIs, posing a risk to capital flows.

GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling

The GIFT Nifty is trading at 24699.50, showing a gain of 0.24%.

Interpretation: This indicates a positive bias for the Indian Nifty 50, suggesting a potential gap-up open for the benchmark index today. This signal is somewhat divergent from the negative close seen in US markets.

FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape

Yesterday, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the cash market, offloading equities worth ₹-943 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide support, emerging as net buyers with an inflow of ₹2,883 crore.

Over the last five sessions, FIIs have been net buyers on three days but turned sellers yesterday, while DIIs have largely maintained their buying momentum, consistently supporting the market.

Interpretation: While FIIs turned net sellers yesterday after a few sessions of buying, DIIs continue to be strong buyers, absorbing the FII selling pressure. This DII support has been a crucial factor in preventing sharper market corrections and indicates domestic conviction in the Indian equity story. The market direction today will depend on whether FII selling intensifies or DII buying continues to outweigh it.

Combining technical and OI levels, Nifty's 24600-24700 zone appears to be a critical range to respect today, with 24600 acting as a pivot and a battleground for bulls and bears. For Bank Nifty, 58000 is a crucial level for immediate direction.

Stock & sector news that matters

Sensex drops over 200 points, Nifty tests 23,600 as Strait of Hormuz tensions rattle oil markets Indian stock markets opened lower yesterday due to increased oil prices and concerns over Iran potentially closing the Strait of Hormuz, impacting investor sentiment. Why it matters: This is a critical geopolitical development. Any escalation in the Strait of Hormuz, a key global oil transit choke point, could lead to further spikes in crude oil prices. For India, a major oil importer, this translates to higher import bills, potential inflationary pressures, and increased costs for sectors like aviation, logistics, and manufacturing. This news sets a cautious tone for the broader market.

Dalal Street's biggest stock reform in years endures rocky first week India's newly introduced closing auction system for stock prices is facing early challenges, with traders reporting erratic price fluctuations and confusion. Why it matters: This news highlights operational challenges in a significant market reform. Erratic price movements during the closing auction can create uncertainty and impact trading strategies, especially for active traders and institutional participants. Market participants will be closely watching how these issues are addressed and if volatility persists during the closing hours.

This could provide a tailwind for Britannia's stock and potentially other FMCG players, especially those with strong brand power and pricing ability.

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  • Geopolitical Tensions (Strait of Hormuz): Any further news or escalation regarding the Strait of Hormuz could directly impact crude oil prices and global risk sentiment. Markets typically react with increased volatility and a flight to safety (like gold) during such geopolitical uncertainties, with oil-sensitive sectors facing pressure.
  • Performance of the New Closing Auction System: The ongoing challenges with India's new closing auction system will be closely watched by traders. Continued erratic price fluctuations could lead to increased intra-day volatility and caution during the market close.
  • US Jobs Report (Awaiting insights): While the US jobs report is not due today, market watchers are awaiting its release for insights into the US Federal Reserve's monetary policy stance, as mentioned in the context of the Rupee's movement. Strong job data could signal continued hawkishness, potentially impacting global liquidity and FII flows.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Crude Oil Prices: Monitor WTI Crude's movement closely, given the geopolitical tensions. Any further surge could weigh heavily on Indian markets.
  2. Nifty's 24600-24700 Range: This zone is critical for Nifty, acting as both technical pivot and strong OI resistance/support. A sustained move above or below this range could dictate the day's trend.
  3. FII/DII Flow Dynamics: Observe if FIIs continue their selling streak or if DIIs maintain their strong buying support.
  4. Impact of Corporate Actions: With many companies going ex-dividend, monitor the price adjustments in these stocks and their potential impact on broader indices if they are large-cap names.
  5. New Closing Auction System: Traders should be mindful of potential volatility and erratic price movements during the market close due to the ongoing issues with the new system.
  6. Sectoral Performance: Watch oil-sensitive sectors (OMCs, aviation, paints) for impact from crude prices, and FMCG (Britannia) for potential positive momentum.

Bottom line

The Indian market is likely to open with a slightly positive bias, as indicated by GIFT Nifty, despite cautious global cues from the US. However, the significant rise in crude oil prices due to geopolitical tensions in the Strait of Hormuz presents a notable headwind. The Nifty's ability to sustain above the 24600-24700 range will be crucial, while ongoing DII support against FII selling will be key to market stability. Traders should remain vigilant regarding crude oil movements and the performance of the new closing auction system.
