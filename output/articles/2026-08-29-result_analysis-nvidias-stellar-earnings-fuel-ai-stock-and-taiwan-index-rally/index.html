Nvidia's Stellar Earnings Fuel AI Stock and Taiwan Index Rally

TL;DR

  • Nvidia reported blockbuster quarterly results, with revenue more than doubling to $96.2 billion, significantly exceeding market expectations.
  • The strong performance was primarily driven by its Data Centre segment, which saw a 117% year-on-year jump in revenue to $89 billion, powered by AI demand.
  • Nvidia's optimistic revenue guidance of $108 billion for the next quarter further boosted investor confidence in the sustained AI boom.
  • This ignited a rally in global AI-linked stocks and the Taiwan Stock Exchange (TAIEX), which rose 0.8% to 46,349.
  • Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC), a crucial Nvidia supplier, saw its shares rise, underscoring its indispensable role in the AI supply chain.

What this means in plain terms

When a major company like Nvidia announces its financial results, it provides a snapshot of its health and future prospects. For a company at the forefront of Artificial Intelligence (AI) technology, these results become a bellwether for the entire sector. Nvidia's recent earnings report was exceptionally strong, indicating that the demand for chips essential for AI development and deployment is booming.

This positive news had a ripple effect across global markets. Investors, seeing Nvidia's robust performance and confident outlook, became more optimistic about other companies involved in the AI ecosystem. This sentiment was particularly strong in Taiwan, home to TSMC, the world's largest contract chipmaker and a key supplier to Nvidia. The rally in Taiwan's stock index and AI-related stocks signifies that the market believes the AI growth story has significant momentum.

Nvidia's Blockbuster Performance Outshines Estimates

Nvidia, a global leader in graphics processing units (GPUs) and AI computing, delivered a stellar financial performance for its July fiscal quarter. The company reported revenue that more than doubled to an impressive $96.2 billion, significantly surpassing analyst expectations. This robust top-line growth was accompanied by an adjusted profit of $2.22 per share, further underscoring the company's profitability amidst surging demand.

The positive momentum is expected to continue, with Nvidia issuing an upbeat revenue guidance of approximately $108.0 billion for the subsequent quarter. This forecast comfortably exceeded market consensus, signalling management's strong confidence in sustained demand for its cutting-edge AI technologies. The strong earnings and optimistic outlook helped alleviate some concerns that had recently emerged regarding the sustainability of the AI stock rally.

The Data Centre: Powering the AI Revolution

The primary engine behind Nvidia's exceptional growth was its Data Centre segment. This crucial division recorded an astonishing $89 billion in revenue, marking a substantial 117% increase year-on-year. This surge highlights the massive investment by cloud service providers, enterprises, and consumer internet companies in building and expanding infrastructure for AI training, inferencing, and large language models.

Nvidia's advanced GPUs are indispensable for these complex computational tasks, making the company a foundational pillar of the ongoing AI revolution. Beyond data centres, the edge computing segment also contributed positively, with revenue rising 27% year-on-year to $7.2 billion. This indicates a broader adoption of AI capabilities beyond centralised cloud environments, extending to devices and localised systems.

Taiwan's Indispensable Role in the AI Supply Chain

Nvidia's strong results had an immediate and significant impact on global markets, particularly in Taiwan. The TAIEX, Taiwan's benchmark stock index, closed 0.8% higher at 46,349, reaching its highest level since early July. This rally was largely driven by the performance of companies within the AI supply chain, with Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) being a prominent beneficiary.

TSMC, the world's largest contract chipmaker, saw its shares rise by 0.4%. TSMC is a critical partner for Nvidia, manufacturing its advanced AI chips and accounting for nearly 40% of the TAIEX's total market value. Nvidia is estimated to contribute over 20% of TSMC's total annual revenue in 2026, making it TSMC's largest customer. This symbiotic relationship underscores Taiwan's pivotal position in the global semiconductor industry and its indispensable role in powering the AI boom.

Margin Outlook and Broader Industry Implications

While Nvidia's revenue figures were impressive, the company's guidance on gross margins provided a nuanced view for different parts of its extensive supply chain. Nvidia indicated that Q4 gross margins are expected to be slightly lower, in the 71-72% range, before recovering to 72-73% in fiscal 2028. This temporary compression is attributed to higher memory prices.

This detail had a varied impact across Asian suppliers. Memory manufacturers, such as SK Hynix and Samsung, saw their shares surge, as Nvidia's willingness to absorb higher memory costs validated their strong pricing power. Conversely, Japanese equipment makers like Advantest experienced a sell-off. The market interpreted potential margin pressure at the top of the chain as a signal that hyperscalers might push back on pricing, potentially leading to caution in capital expenditure for new equipment further down the line. This divergence highlights the complex interplay of costs and demand across the sophisticated global semiconductor ecosystem.

A real example

Consider Anjali, a 42-year-old software architect in Bengaluru, earning ₹35 lakhs per annum. She has a diversified investment portfolio, including a global technology-focused mutual fund that invests in leading international tech companies. Let's assume her fund has a 5% allocation to Nvidia and a 3% allocation to TSMC, reflecting their prominence in the tech sector.

Her global technology fund's Net Asset Value (NAV) is ₹150 per unit, and she holds 5,000 units, making her investment ₹7.5 lakhs. The fund's underlying holdings in Nvidia and TSMC, due to Nvidia's strong earnings and the resulting market rally, see an average increase of 1.5% in their stock prices within the fund's portfolio.

Calculation of impact:

  • Total value of Nvidia and TSMC holdings in the fund for Anjali's portion:

    • * Nvidia exposure: 5% of ₹7.5 lakhs = ₹37,500 * TSMC exposure: 3% of ₹7.5 lakhs = ₹22,500 * Total direct exposure to these two companies: ₹37,500 + ₹22,500 = ₹60,000

  • Increase in value due to rally (assuming 1.5% average gain for these specific holdings within the fund):

    • ₹60,000 1.5% = ₹900

While ₹900 might seem small, this is just the direct impact from two companies. The overall positive sentiment from Nvidia's results would likely lift other tech holdings in her fund, leading to a broader increase in the fund's NAV. If the entire fund's NAV rises by, say, 0.5% due to the overall market sentiment, her investment of ₹7.5 lakhs would increase by ₹3,750 (₹7,50,000 * 0.5%). This illustrates how strong corporate earnings from bellwether companies can positively influence diversified portfolios, even for indirect investors.

  2. Stay informed on sector trends: Dedicate 30 minutes to read up on the latest developments in AI and semiconductors. Understanding the underlying drivers can help you make informed decisions.
  3. Assess portfolio diversification: Ensure your portfolio isn't overly concentrated in a single sector, even a high-growth one like AI. Diversification remains key to managing risk.

FAQ

What are corporate earnings reports?

Corporate earnings reports are quarterly or annual financial statements released by public companies. They detail the company's revenues, profits, and expenses, providing investors with insight into its financial health and performance.

Why did Nvidia's earnings impact the Taiwan Index?

Nvidia is a major customer of Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC), which is the world's largest contract chipmaker and a significant component of Taiwan's stock index (TAIEX). Strong Nvidia earnings imply robust demand for TSMC's chips, boosting investor confidence in the Taiwanese market.

What does "AI stocks rally" mean?

An "AI stocks rally" refers to a period where the share prices of companies involved in Artificial Intelligence (AI) technology experience significant and widespread increases, driven by positive news or investor optimism about the sector's growth prospects.

Is it too late to invest in AI stocks?

Investing decisions should always align with your financial goals and risk tolerance. While AI stocks have seen significant gains, market analysts often caution against blindly chasing hot thematic stocks. Consider a long-term approach, possibly through diversified funds.

How does Nvidia's gross margin guidance affect its suppliers?

Nvidia's gross margin guidance, especially when influenced by rising input costs like memory, can signal varying impacts across its supply chain. It can be positive for memory suppliers (as Nvidia absorbs costs) but potentially cautious for equipment makers if it suggests a slowdown in future capital expenditure.
