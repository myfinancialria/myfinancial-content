Madhabi Puri Buch: Making Your Online Financial Advice Safer

TL;DR

  • SEBI Chairperson Madhabi Puri Buch is leading efforts to make online financial advice trustworthy.
  • The market regulator is tightening rules for "finfluencers" – financial influencers on social media.
  • Only SEBI-registered professionals can now give specific investment advice or stock recommendations.
  • Regulated financial firms are barred from partnering with unregistered finfluencers.
  • These steps aim to protect ordinary investors from misleading tips and market manipulation, especially in small and mid-cap stocks.

What's this about?

Madhabi Puri Buch is the Chairperson of the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), which is India's main regulator for the securities market. Think of SEBI as the referee for all stock market activities, ensuring fair play and protecting investors. Lately, Ms. Buch and SEBI have been actively working to bring more transparency and accountability to the world of online financial advice, particularly concerning "finfluencers" – individuals who share financial tips and recommendations on social media platforms like YouTube, Instagram, and X (formerly Twitter).

Why it's in the news right now

The regulation of finfluencers and the safety of investments, especially in smaller company stocks, have been major topics for SEBI under Ms. Buch's leadership. Earlier in March 2024, Ms. Buch publicly warned about "pockets of froth" and "irrational exuberance" in small and mid-cap stocks, indicating potential price manipulation in the SME (Small and Medium Enterprises) segment. This highlighted the risks for ordinary investors. More recently, SEBI has been implementing and proposing clearer rules for financial content creators. An important framework from October 2024 already barred regulated financial entities from associating with unregistered finfluencers. Furthermore, effective July 1, 2026, new rules for educational content require market price data to have a 30-day lag to prevent disguised real-time tips. SEBI is also discussing a "Common Advertisement Code" to standardise how financial content is advertised across the board. These ongoing efforts, driven by Ms. Buch's vision, are constantly in the news as they reshape how Indians receive and trust financial information online.

How it actually works

SEBI's new approach focuses on clear rules for who can give financial advice and how it should be presented:

  1. Who can give advice? The core principle is that only a SEBI-registered Investment Adviser (IA) or Research Analyst (RA) is legally allowed to give specific investment advice or make recommendations on buying or selling securities (like stocks or mutual funds).
  2. What are "Finfluencers"? This term refers to "financial influencers" – people who create content about money, investing, and finance on social media. While many provide genuine education, some offer specific "buy/sell" calls or investment tips without proper registration.
  3. No Association with Unregistered Finfluencers: Since October 2024, SEBI has prohibited regulated entities (like stockbrokers, mutual funds, and registered investment advisors) from partnering with or paying unregistered finfluencers. This means no sponsorships, client referrals, or promotional activities with those who give advice without a license.
  4. Stricter Disclosure Rules: Finfluencers are now expected to make clear disclosures about their registration status, any conflicts of interest (e.g., if they own the stock they're discussing), and whether their content is sponsored.
  5. Limits on "Educational" Content: Even for content labelled as "educational," SEBI has set boundaries. For example, as of July 1, 2026, market data used in educational content must be at least 30 days old. This stops creators from using fresh data to indirectly suggest trades.
  6. Proposed Common Advertisement Code: SEBI has also proposed a Common Advertisement Code that would treat finfluencers with over 5 lakh followers like "celebrities." They could promote a regulated entity's brand but not endorse specific financial products or make performance claims. Advertisements would need to be reported to SEBI-recognised bodies within 24 hours of publication.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine you need medical advice. You wouldn't trust a random person on social media claiming to be a doctor without a proper medical degree and license, right? You'd go to a qualified, registered doctor. Similarly, Madhabi Puri Buch and SEBI are saying that financial advice is serious business, just like health advice. Only those with proper "licenses" (SEBI registration) should be giving specific investment recommendations, and everyone else needs to be very clear about whether they are just educating or actually advising.

Why it matters to you

  1. Protects Your Savings: These rules are designed to shield you from misleading or biased financial advice that could lead to significant losses, especially in volatile segments like small-cap stocks.
  2. Clearer Information: It helps you easily distinguish between genuine financial education and potentially risky, unregistered investment advice. You'll know who is qualified to advise and who isn't.
  3. Accountability: If you take advice from a SEBI-registered professional, there's a clear regulatory framework for accountability. This is not the case with unregistered finfluencers.
  4. Safer Market Environment: By curbing manipulative practices, SEBI aims to create a fairer and more trustworthy capital market for everyone.
  5. Empowers Informed Decisions: With better disclosures and less misleading information, you are better equipped to make your own informed financial decisions.

Common confusions cleared up

  • Myth: SEBI is banning all finfluencers and financial content online.

    • Fact: SEBI is not banning financial content or education. It is regulating unregistered investment advice* and promoting transparency. Genuine educational content, without specific stock tips or performance claims, is still allowed.

  • Myth: I can't learn about finance from social media anymore.

    • * Fact: You absolutely can. Many creators offer valuable financial literacy and educational content. The key is to differentiate between general education and specific, personalised investment advice, which should only come from registered professionals.

  • Myth: If a finfluencer has many followers, their advice must be good.

    • * Fact: Popularity does not equal credibility or expertise. SEBI's rules aim to ensure that advice comes from qualified and registered individuals, regardless of their follower count.

FAQ

Who is Madhabi Puri Buch?

Madhabi Puri Buch is the current Chairperson of the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), the regulatory body for India's stock and securities market. She is the first woman to hold this position.

What is a "finfluencer"?

A "finfluencer" is a portmanteau of "financial influencer," referring to individuals who create and share financial content, advice, or recommendations on social media platforms.

Can I still follow finfluencers for financial tips?

You can follow finfluencers for general financial education and awareness. However, for specific investment advice or recommendations on buying/selling stocks, it is crucial to consult only SEBI-registered Investment Advisers or Research Analysts.

What if a finfluencer gives me a "sure shot" stock tip?

Be very cautious. Registered professionals are bound by strict rules and cannot guarantee returns. Unregistered individuals giving "sure shot" tips might be involved in manipulative practices, and following such advice can lead to significant financial losses.

What are SEBI's future plans regarding finfluencers?

SEBI is continuing to evolve its regulatory framework, including discussions around a "Common Advertisement Code" and potentially introducing measures like a "Verified Finfluencer Badge" to further enhance transparency and investor protection.
