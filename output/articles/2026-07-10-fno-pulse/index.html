India F&O Pulse — 10 Jul 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Indices Close Higher, But FIIs Maintain a Cautious Stance in Futures

TL;DR

  • The Nifty 50 appears to be positioned for a range-bound or mixed session, with fresh option writing suggesting both a ceiling and a floor close to current levels.
  • Sensex shows a mildly bullish undertone from today's option flow, though significant Call and Put Open Interest walls are very close to the spot price, indicating potential consolidation.
  • Bank Nifty exhibits a bullish bias, supported by the Max Pain point being comfortably above the spot price and strong Put writing activity.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continue to hold a significantly low long ratio in index futures, historically coinciding with a cautious or bearish outlook from this segment.

What the indices did today

The Nifty 50 closed the day at 24,206.90, marking a gain of 1.02%. The index ended within its option-chain implied range of 23,000 to 24,500, with significant Call and Put Open Interest walls suggesting these levels could act as immediate resistance and support respectively. This positioning points towards a potentially consolidating market in the near term.

The Sensex saw a robust gain of 1.08%, settling at 77,569.39. Its option-chain implied range, indicated by the highest Open Interest, was tightly clustered around 77,500. This suggests that while the index moved up, option writers are anticipating it to remain within a narrow band.

Bank Nifty was the strongest performer, advancing 1.39% to close at 58,045.90. The index closed just above its heaviest Put Open Interest at 58,000, and well below the heaviest Call Open Interest at 59,000, providing a comfortable upward potential within its implied range. This indicates a relatively positive sentiment among option participants for the banking sector.

Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. In options, the strike price with the highest Call OI is often considered a significant resistance level, as many call option sellers (writers) do not expect the price to rise above this point. Conversely, the strike price with the highest Put OI is typically seen as a strong support level, as put option sellers do not anticipate the price falling below it.

For the Nifty 50, today's heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near 24,333. This suggests that call sellers believe the index might find it challenging to move significantly above this level. On the Put side, the heaviest fresh Put writing occurred near 24,150, indicating that put sellers are comfortable with the index finding support around this mark.

Sensex saw fresh Call writing concentrated near 77,633, implying that option writers expect this level to act as a cap. Simultaneously, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 76,633, suggesting a strong base is being formed at this lower strike.

In Bank Nifty, the most significant fresh Put writing was seen near 57,900. This indicates that put sellers are actively building support at this level, expecting the index to hold above it. No specific heavy fresh Call writing was highlighted, which can be seen as a less restrictive ceiling from the perspective of new call sellers.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the trading volume or open interest of put options to call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests a bullish sentiment, as more puts are being written (sold), implying that sellers expect prices to rise or remain stable. A PCR below 1 often indicates a bearish sentiment, as more calls are being written, suggesting sellers expect prices to fall or remain stable.

For Nifty 50, the PCR based on Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stood at 1.13, indicating a relatively balanced book with a slight bullish tilt. However, the PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) was -5.91, which suggests a mixed to balanced flow of fresh writing during the day, with no strong directional bias emerging from new positions.

Sensex recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.1, also pointing to a balanced options book. More notably, its PCR(today flow ΔOI) was 1.58, which is a bullish signal, indicating that today's fresh option writing was predominantly in put options, suggesting confidence among put sellers that the index will not fall significantly.

Bank Nifty's PCR(OI) was 0.85, suggesting a slightly bearish leaning in the overall open interest, as more call options are outstanding. However, its PCR(today flow ΔOI) was -3.22, indicating a mixed or balanced fresh flow for the day, similar to Nifty, and not providing a strong directional signal from new positions alone.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option buyers would suffer the maximum financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. Conversely, it is often the point where option sellers (writers) would realise the maximum profit. As expiry approaches, the underlying index price tends to gravitate towards this "max pain" strike, acting like a magnet.

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain point is at 24,150, which is very close to today's spot close of 24,206.90. This suggests that option writers are positioned for the index to consolidate around current levels by expiry.

Sensex's Max Pain is at 77,500, also very near its spot close of 77,569.39. This proximity implies that option writers are expecting the Sensex to hover around this level as expiry draws closer.

Bank Nifty's Max Pain is at 58,500, which is approximately 0.8% above its spot close of 58,045.90. This positioning of Max Pain above the current spot price can be interpreted as a mildly bullish signal, as the "magnet effect" would theoretically pull the index upwards towards this level as expiry approaches.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment. Today, FIIs held a very low Index futures long ratio of 10.6%. This means that out of their total open positions in index futures, only a small fraction were long (betting on a rise), while the vast majority were short (betting on a fall). Historically, such a low long ratio in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or outright bearish sentiment from these large institutional players.

In contrast, FIIs maintained a more balanced stance in Stock futures, with a long ratio of 54.5%. This indicates that while they are significantly bearish on the broader index, their positioning in individual stocks is relatively neutral to mildly bullish.

Stocks where money moved today

Among the F&O watchlist, several stocks saw notable activity. Top gainers included ADANIENT, up 2.39% with a volume of over 21.45 lakh shares, and RELIANCE, which rose 2.19% on a significant volume of over 84.12 lakh shares. AXISBANK also performed well, gaining 2.01%.

On the losing side, BHARTIARTL declined 0.55% with a volume of over 64.20 lakh shares, while ITC saw a marginal dip of 0.11% on a high volume of over 1.17 crore shares. These movements reflect specific interest and positioning in individual F&O counters.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we look at the confluence of signals for tomorrow's session, we observe a mixed picture. The Nifty 50's range-bound option positioning, coupled with a balanced PCR flow, historically tends to coincide with a day of consolidation or sideways movement. Sensex, despite today's gains, has tight option walls and Max Pain near spot, suggesting a limited upside in the immediate term, though today's put-heavy flow indicates some underlying strength. Bank Nifty, with its bullish Max Pain and strong Put writing, historically tends to show continued strength or resilience.

However, the significantly low FII Index futures long ratio (10.6%) introduces a strong note of caution. Historically, such a low FII long ratio has often coincided with periods where the broader market struggles to sustain significant upward momentum, or experiences bouts of selling pressure. While individual indices might show internal strength, the overall institutional sentiment suggests a need for prudence. Therefore, while Bank Nifty appears bullish, and Sensex mildly so, the overarching FII positioning suggests that any upward moves might face headwinds, potentially leading to a day of selective strength within a broader cautious environment.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to form your own informed perspective. You can access options Open Interest data, including the highest Call and Put OI strikes, directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the 'Derivatives' section. Look for the 'Option Chain' for Nifty, Bank Nifty, or Sensex. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in a user-friendly format.

To calculate the PCR(OI), simply divide the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. For PCR(today's flow ΔOI), you'd need to compare the change in Put OI with the change in Call OI for the day. FII positioning data is also available on the NSE website under 'Participant-wise Open Interest'. By tracking these data points daily and observing how the market reacts, you can gradually build your understanding of how option writers and large institutions are positioning themselves, and what that has historically tended to coincide with in terms of market direction.
