Your UPI Payments Just Got a Small Update: What the New Rules Mean

TL;DR

  • Sending money to friends or family (Person-to-Person or P2P) via UPI remains completely free, no matter the amount.
  • When you pay a shop or business (Person-to-Merchant or P2M) using UPI, transactions up to ₹2,000 are still free for the merchant.
  • For P2M payments above ₹2,000, a small fee called Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) will apply to the merchant.
  • This MDR is a nominal 0.4% of the transaction value, capped at ₹300 for very large transactions (₹75,000 and above).
  • The government's aim is to ensure the UPI system remains strong and sustainable for everyone.

What's this about?

The Indian government has clarified how charges apply to Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions. UPI is the popular digital payment system that lets you send and receive money instantly using your phone. The big news is that sending money to another person (like a friend or family member) will continue to be free. However, when you pay a business or shop using UPI for amounts over ₹2,000, a small fee, called the Merchant Discount Rate (MDR), will now be applicable to the merchant.

This move aims to balance the ease of digital payments for users with the need to keep the payment system financially healthy for the banks and payment service providers who run it.

Why it's in the news right now

The Central Government issued a notification on September 14, 2026, under an amended Payment and Settlement Systems (PSS) Act, 2007. This notification empowers the government to specify electronic payment modes that will not attract charges. Following this, the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) — the organisation that developed and manages UPI — issued a detailed circular on September 15, 2026. This circular outlined the operational details, fee distribution, and category caps for the new MDR structure, making it a current and widely discussed topic.

How it actually works

Here's a breakdown of how the new UPI transaction rules function:

  1. Person-to-Person (P2P) Transactions Remain Free: If you send money to another individual's UPI ID or phone number (e.g., paying back a friend for dinner), there will be no charges at all. This applies regardless of the transaction amount.
  2. Person-to-Merchant (P2M) Transactions: These are payments made to businesses, shops, or service providers.

    3. * Up to ₹2,000: For P2M transactions where the amount is ₹2,000 or less, no Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) will be levied. This means small payments to your local kirana store or chaiwala will continue to be free for them. * Above ₹2,000: For P2M transactions exceeding ₹2,000, a nominal MDR of 0.4% of the transaction value will be applied. This fee is paid by the merchant to their bank or payment service provider. * MDR Cap for High-Value Transactions: To ensure large transactions aren't overly burdened, the MDR for P2M payments of ₹75,000 and above will be capped at ₹300 per transaction. * Flat MDR for Essential Sectors: Certain essential sectors like railways, telecom, insurance, fuel, and agriculture inputs will have a flat MDR of ₹5 per transaction for amounts above ₹2,000. This provides cost stability for critical public services.

  3. What is MDR? Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) is a small commission that a merchant pays to their bank or payment service provider for processing a digital transaction. This fee is then shared among different partners in the payment ecosystem, including the banks and UPI app providers, to cover their costs and help maintain the payment infrastructure.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine UPI as a super-fast digital road network.

Sending money to a friend is like driving your own scooter on a public road – it's completely free.

Now, if a shop uses this road network to receive payments from many customers, it's like they're running a small delivery service. For small deliveries (payments up to ₹2,000), the road is still free for them. But for bigger deliveries (payments over ₹2,000), the shop pays a small fee to the road maintenance crew (the banks and payment providers) to keep the road in good condition and ensure smooth, fast service for everyone. This way, the road stays well-maintained for all users.

Why it matters to you

  1. Your personal transfers remain free: You can continue to send money to friends, family, or pay rent to your landlord via UPI without any extra cost. This is a big relief for everyday personal transactions.
  2. Impact on purchases from businesses: While the MDR is paid by the merchant, some businesses might choose to adjust their pricing slightly to cover this cost, especially for larger transactions. However, for most everyday purchases under ₹2,000, there should be no direct impact.
  3. Clearer pricing for essential services: If you pay for fuel, train tickets, or your phone bill using UPI for amounts over ₹2,000, the flat ₹5 MDR for merchants in these sectors means more predictable costs for them, which could help keep your prices stable.
  4. Sustainable digital ecosystem: By ensuring that payment service providers receive some revenue for larger transactions, this move helps them invest in technology, security, and infrastructure. This means UPI can continue to be reliable and innovative in the long run.
  5. Increased transparency: The new rules bring more clarity on how charges are applied within the UPI ecosystem, reducing confusion and making the system more transparent for both users and merchants.

Common confusions cleared up

Myth: All UPI transactions will now have charges. Fact: No, this is incorrect. Only Person-to-Merchant (P2M) transactions above ₹2,000 will attract a nominal Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) for the merchant. All Person-to-Person (P2P) transfers remain free.

Myth: I, as a customer, will directly pay a fee when I use UPI to pay a shop. Fact: The Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) is paid by the merchant (the business or shop) to their bank or payment service provider. It is not directly charged to you, the customer, at the time of your UPI payment.

Myth: Small shopkeepers will be heavily impacted. Fact: For transactions up to ₹2,000, which cover a large number of daily purchases at small shops, no MDR is applicable. This protects small merchants from additional costs for smaller-value digital payments.

FAQ

What is the Payment and Settlement Systems (PSS) Act, 2007?

The PSS Act, 2007, is a law in India that provides for the regulation and supervision of payment and settlement systems. The recent amendment to this Act empowers the government to notify specific electronic payment modes where no charges shall apply, providing the legal basis for the new UPI rules.

Who benefits from the Merchant Discount Rate (MDR)?

The MDR is shared among the payment ecosystem partners, including the banks that enable UPI transactions and the app providers (like Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm) that facilitate them. This revenue helps cover their operational costs and allows for further investment in the UPI infrastructure.

Does this apply to all UPI apps?

Yes, the rules are set by the government and NPCI, which govern the entire UPI ecosystem. Therefore, these rules apply universally to all banks and payment applications that offer UPI services in India.

Why has the government introduced this change now?

The government aims to ensure the long-term sustainability and growth of the UPI ecosystem. While UPI has seen massive adoption due to its free nature, maintaining such a vast digital infrastructure requires resources. The nominal MDR on higher-value merchant transactions helps provide revenue to the service providers, encouraging continued innovation and robust service.
