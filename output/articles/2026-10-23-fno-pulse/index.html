Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It gives us a sense of how much money is committed to a particular strike price or contract.

When we look at the highest Call Open Interest (Call OI) strike, it typically indicates a level where many Call option sellers (writers) believe the underlying index is unlikely to rise above. These writers are essentially betting on the price staying below that strike. Conversely, the highest Put Open Interest (Put OI) strike signifies a level where many Put option sellers believe the index is unlikely to fall below, acting as a potential support.

For Nifty 50, today's heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near the 22900 strike. This suggests that Call sellers are actively taking positions, expecting the Nifty to face a cap around this level in the near future. On the other hand, aggregate flow showed a significantly higher build in Call OI (42.0M) compared to Put OI (7.9M), reinforcing a cautious outlook.

In Sensex, the heaviest Call OI wall is positioned at 73500, which is quite close to today's closing spot price, suggesting a nearby ceiling. However, there was also heavy fresh Put writing near 71766, indicating that Put sellers are establishing a floor at lower levels.

For Bank Nifty, the heaviest fresh Call writing was seen near the 55066 strike. This indicates that Call sellers are actively building positions, anticipating that Bank Nifty may find it challenging to move significantly above this level. The aggregate flow also showed a much larger build in Call OI (2.8M) compared to Put OI (0.7M), further pointing to a bearish bias.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that compares the volume or open interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests more Put activity, which can sometimes be seen as bullish (as Put writers expect prices to rise), while a PCR below 1 indicates more Call activity, often interpreted as bearish (as Call writers expect prices to fall).

For Nifty 50, the overall PCR (OI) stands at 0.73, indicating a Call-heavy book, which is generally considered neutral to mildly bearish. More significantly, today's flow ΔPCR (change in PCR based on today's fresh writing) was a low 0.19. This very low figure suggests that today's fresh option writing was heavily skewed towards Calls, reinforcing a bearish sentiment for the day's activity.

Sensex has an overall PCR (OI) of 0.81, also indicating a Call-heavy book but still within a range often considered balanced. Today's flow ΔPCR was 0.59, which, while still Call-heavy, is less extreme than Nifty's, aligning with a more mixed or range-bound outlook for the day's fresh positions.

Bank Nifty recorded an overall PCR (OI) of 0.93, which is quite close to 1, suggesting a relatively balanced book in terms of total open interest. However, today's flow ΔPCR was 0.27, indicating that fresh writing during the day was heavily dominated by Call options, pointing to a bearish bias from today's new positions.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is a concept in options theory that refers to the strike price at which option buyers (both Calls and Puts) would experience the maximum financial loss at expiry. Conversely, option sellers (writers) would experience the maximum profit at this strike. As expiry approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this "max pain" level, as option writers, who typically hold larger positions, may try to influence the price towards this point to maximise their profits.

For Nifty 50, the max pain level is currently at 23000. This is approximately +1.8% above today's spot price of 22600.00. Historically, a max pain level significantly above the spot price can act as an upward "pin pull" or a magnet, especially as expiry draws closer.

Sensex has its max pain level at 73500, which is about +0.5% above its spot price of 73123.83. This relatively close proximity and slight upward pull could suggest a tendency for the index to drift higher towards this level by expiry, though the pull is less pronounced than Nifty's.

Bank Nifty shows a max pain level of 55000, which is approximately +1.3% above its spot price of 54287.35. Similar to Nifty, this upward pull from max pain could suggest a potential for the index to move higher, but it is important to consider this alongside other signals.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are often considered "big money" players, and their positioning in derivatives can offer insights into their market outlook.

Today, FIIs held a very low Index futures long ratio of 10.4%. This means that for every 100 contracts they held, only about 10 were long positions, while the remaining 90 were short. Specifically, they held 36,986 long contracts against 317,169 short contracts. Historically, such a significantly low long ratio in Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or outright bearish stance from FIIs on the broader market.

In contrast, their Stock futures long ratio stood at 56.2%, with 3,644,206 long contracts against 2,843,116 short contracts. This indicates a more balanced to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks, suggesting selective buying interest despite a cautious view on the broader index.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding F&O data can seem complex at first, but with a few key data points, you can start to form your own interpretations. The first step is to identify the expiry series you're interested in (weekly or monthly).

Then, visit the NSE website (nseindia.com) and navigate to the 'Derivatives' section, specifically the 'Option Chain' for your chosen index (Nifty, Bank Nifty, Sensex). Here, you'll find the Open Interest (OI) for various Call and Put strikes. Look for the strikes with the highest OI on both the Call and Put sides – these are your potential resistance and support walls. Also, pay attention to the 'Change in OI' column for the day; significant positive changes indicate fresh writing or buying at those strikes. To calculate the PCR, simply divide the total Put OI by the total Call OI. For FII data, look for the 'Participant-wise Open Interest' file usually found in the archives section of the NSE website, which is published at the end of each trading day. By consistently tracking these metrics and observing how they change daily, you can begin to identify patterns and understand the market's underlying positioning.
