Pre-Market Brief — 25 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets closed mixed, with US indices largely flat to negative, while Asian markets showed divergence, led by a strong Nikkei.
  • Crude oil prices continued their downward trend, falling below $73 for Brent, which is a positive for India's import bill and inflation outlook.
  • The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, supported by falling crude prices and positive sentiment.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers yesterday, pulling out ₹1,843 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net buyers.
  • Several prominent companies, including LICI, IndusInd Bank, and Tata Chemicals, have their shares trading ex-dividend today.

Where global markets closed

Global markets presented a mixed picture in yesterday's session. In the US, the Dow Jones managed a modest gain, rising by 0.35% to close at 51,848.90. However, the tech-heavy Nasdaq saw a slight dip of 0.43% to 25,476.64, and the broader S&P 500 also edged down by 0.10% to 7,358.22. Across the Atlantic, London's FTSE 100 posted a gain of 0.31%, closing at 10,461.60. Asian markets showed more significant divergence, with Japan's Nikkei 225 surging by an impressive 4.91% to 72,574.69. In contrast, Hong Kong's Hang Seng index declined by 1.65% to 23,026.28, while China's Shanghai Composite registered a marginal gain of 0.05% to 4,112.89. The overall global tone appears cautious, with some regional strength offsetting broader market indecision.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, with USD/INR trading at 94.50, a decline of 0.72%. This appreciation of the Rupee is a positive sign for India's import costs, particularly crude oil. The Dollar Index, which measures the US Dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies, also saw a slight dip of 0.07% to 101.54, indicating a marginal softening of the dollar globally.

In the commodities market, crude oil prices continued their downward trajectory. Brent Crude fell by 1.27% to $72.80 per barrel, and WTI Crude was down 0.97% at $69.66 per barrel. This significant decline in crude oil prices, which have crashed 42% from their April peak, is a major positive for India, as it reduces the country's import bill and eases inflationary pressures.

Gold prices saw a marginal uptick of 0.12% to $3995.00, while Silver declined by 1.58% to $57.13. The US 10-year Treasury Yield also moved lower, falling by 0.27% to 4.45%. A lower US yield can sometimes make emerging markets like India more attractive to foreign investors.

Overnight news that matters

Crude oil prices crash 42% from April’s $126 peak. More downside coming? Global crude oil prices have plummeted significantly, with Brent falling below $73 a barrel, a 42% drop from its peak in April. This decline is attributed to easing supply concerns and stabilization in global oil markets. Why it matters to Indian markets: As a major oil importer, India directly benefits from lower crude prices. This reduces the country's import bill, helps control inflation, and improves the current account deficit, which is broadly positive for the economy and corporate earnings, especially for sectors like aviation, paints, and logistics.

Aviation stocks fly high: IndiGo, SpiceJet shares jump up to 6% as crude oil prices slip to below pre-US-Iran war levels Shares of Indian airlines, including IndiGo and SpiceJet, surged yesterday following the sharp decline in crude oil prices. Lower fuel costs directly improve the profitability margins for aviation companies. Why it matters to Indian markets: This news highlights a direct beneficiary sector of falling crude prices. Investors will likely keep a close watch on aviation stocks today, as lower fuel expenses can significantly boost their financial performance.

India bonds jump ahead of auction as oil erases war-led gains Indian government bonds rallied for the fourth consecutive day, driven by falling oil prices, positive foreign inflows, and the Reserve Bank of India's cautious stance. Why it matters to Indian markets: A rally in government bonds indicates lower borrowing costs for the government and potentially for corporations. This positive sentiment in the bond market can also spill over into equities, as lower interest rates generally support economic growth and corporate investments.

Tata Chemicals shares rise 4% on hopes of Tata Sons listing after RBI’s new norms Shares of Tata Chemicals saw a 4% increase amid renewed speculation about a potential listing of Tata Sons. This comes after the RBI tightened norms for upper-layer NBFCs, which could pressure the conglomerate to list if its assets exceed ₹1 lakh crore. Why it matters to Indian markets: The potential listing of a major conglomerate like Tata Sons could unlock significant value for its shareholders and create a large new listing on the bourses. This news could generate interest in other Tata Group companies as well, as investors might look for indirect ways to participate.

Manappuram Finance, Muthoot Finance, other gold financier stocks drop up to 3%. Here's why Shares of gold finance companies declined as a sharp fall in gold prices and a stronger US dollar weighed on sentiment. Expectations of further US Federal Reserve rate hikes also pressured bullion. Why it matters to Indian markets: This indicates a potential headwind for gold finance companies. Investors in these stocks should monitor gold price movements and global interest rate expectations, as these factors directly impact their business model and asset valuations.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Crude Oil Prices: The continued decline in Brent and WTI crude is a significant positive for India. Investors should monitor global oil price movements as they directly impact inflation, import bills, and the profitability of various sectors.
  2. FII/DII Activity: While DIIs have been consistent buyers, FIIs showed net selling yesterday. The direction of foreign institutional flows will be crucial for market sentiment and overall liquidity.
  3. Aviation and Oil-Sensitive Stocks: With crude oil prices falling, aviation stocks (like IndiGo, SpiceJet) and other oil-sensitive sectors (e.g., paints, logistics, tyres) could see continued interest.
  4. Corporate Actions: Today is ex-dividend for a large number of companies, including LICI, IndusInd Bank, and Tata Chemicals. Shareholders should be aware of these dates and their implications.
  5. Global Cues: Despite a strong Nikkei, global markets were mixed. Investors should watch the opening of European markets and US futures for further directional cues.

Bottom line

The Indian market is likely to open with mixed to positive cues, primarily supported by the significant decline in crude oil prices and continued DII buying. However, FII selling yesterday and mixed global signals, particularly from the US and some Asian markets, might induce some caution. Investors should closely monitor crude oil trends and FII activity for market direction.
