Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been settled. It provides insights into where market participants, especially option writers (sellers), are expecting price resistance and support. The highest Call OI strike typically acts as a resistance level, as many call options have been sold at that price, indicating sellers do not expect the price to rise above it significantly. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike often serves as a support level, as numerous put options have been sold there, suggesting sellers anticipate the price will not fall below it.

For the NIFTY 50, today's fresh writing activity showed a balanced flow, with new Call and Put sellers appearing to offset each other. The heaviest Put OI is at 22500, while the heaviest Call OI is at 23000, forming the immediate boundaries for the index.

On the SENSEX, today's flow saw heavier Put writing, particularly near 71833, indicating that put sellers expect this level to act as a floor. However, there was also significant fresh Call writing near 74566, suggesting call sellers anticipate the index might find a cap around this level. The highest Put OI is at 72500, and the highest Call OI is at 74000.

For BANK NIFTY, there was notable fresh Put writing near 53600, with put sellers expecting this level to hold as a floor. The heaviest Put OI is at 55000, which is currently above the spot price, indicating a strong base has been built at a higher level. The heaviest Call OI is at 56000.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator derived from options data. It is calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest (PCR(OI)), or by dividing the fresh Put writing by fresh Call writing for the day (PCR(today flow ΔOI)). A PCR above 1 generally suggests a bullish sentiment (more puts written than calls, implying writers expect prices to rise or stay stable), while a PCR below 1 often indicates a bearish sentiment (more calls written than puts, implying writers expect prices to fall or stay stable). A PCR near 1 suggests a balanced or neutral outlook.

For NIFTY 50, the PCR(OI) stands at 0.9, suggesting a relatively balanced options book. Today's flow ΔPCR was 1.03, indicating a mixed to slightly put-heavy fresh writing, which aligns with a balanced or mildly positive flow for the day.

The SENSEX recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.18, pointing to a balanced book with a slight lean towards put writing. Its PCR(today flow ΔOI) was 1.35, indicating that today's fresh writing was significantly put-heavy, suggesting put sellers are more active and expecting support.

BANK NIFTY has a PCR(OI) of 0.91, suggesting a balanced book. Today's flow ΔPCR was 0.82, indicating a mixed to slightly call-heavy fresh writing, but this needs to be viewed in conjunction with other bullish signals.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that specific price. It's often considered a "magnet" for the underlying index as expiry approaches, as option writers, who typically hold large positions, might try to influence the price towards this level to minimise their losses.

For NIFTY 50, the Max Pain is at 22800, which is approximately +0.4% above today's spot price. This suggests a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry nears.

The SENSEX has its Max Pain at 72900, which is about -0.5% below today's spot price. This indicates a potential downward pull towards this level.

BANK NIFTY shows a Max Pain of 55000, a significant +1.3% above its current spot price. This suggests a strong upward magnetic pull for the index towards this strike.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can be a powerful tool for any informed investor. You can access much of this data yourself daily. For Open Interest (OI) data, including the option chain, you can visit the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section. Look for "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex. Here, you'll see the total Call OI and Put OI at various strike prices, which helps identify potential support and resistance levels.

To track the day's fresh writing (ΔOI or Change in OI), you can also find this on the NSE option chain, usually displayed alongside the total OI. This shows where new positions were added or closed during the day. Many brokerage platforms also offer intuitive interfaces to view option chain data, PCR, and Max Pain calculations. By regularly observing these data points – the highest Call and Put OI, the daily change in OI, the Put-Call Ratio, and the Max Pain level – you can begin to form your own informed perspective on the market's underlying sentiment and potential short-term movements.
