India F&O Pulse — 17 Sep 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Nifty shows a mild bullish tilt, while Sensex appears range-bound, and Bank Nifty leans mildly bearish, with FIIs maintaining a cautious stance in index futures.

TL;DR

  • Nifty 50: The options data suggests a mild bullish bias for tomorrow's session, with a potential upward pull towards the max pain level.
  • Sensex: The index appears set for a range-bound session, with strong Call and Put walls close to the current spot, indicating a balanced tug-of-war.
  • Bank Nifty: Options flow points to a mildly bearish outlook for the banking index, primarily due to significant fresh call writing today.

What the indices did today

The Nifty 50 closed the day at 23,270.60, marking a modest gain of 0.23%. The index settled just below its Max Pain level of 23,350, suggesting a potential upward magnetic pull. The option chain indicates broad walls between 22,000 and 24,500, with today's close well within this expected range.

The Sensex experienced a marginal dip, closing at 74,314.59, down 0.03%. It ended the day very close to its ATM (At The Money) strike of 74,300. The option chain shows tight implied walls around 74,500, indicating that option writers are positioning for the index to stay within a narrow band.

The Bank Nifty saw a decline of 0.42%, settling at 56,055.75. This close was significantly below its Max Pain level of 57,500. The option chain also highlights a tight implied range, with both heavy Call and Put OI walls at 57,500, suggesting a ceiling for upward movement in the near term.

Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts that have not yet been settled. When we look at the highest Call OI strike, it indicates a level where a significant number of Call options have been sold. These Call writers (sellers) profit if the index stays below this strike, effectively acting as a potential resistance or ceiling. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike represents a level where many Put options have been sold. Put writers profit if the index stays above this strike, thus forming a potential support or floor.

For Nifty 50, the highest Call OI is notably higher than the spot, while the highest Put OI is significantly lower, contributing to the mildly bullish bias. Today's fresh writing saw a balanced flow, with no overwhelming concentration at any specific strike, which suggests a lack of aggressive new bets on either side for immediate direction.

In Sensex, the heaviest Call OI and Put OI are both concentrated around 74,500. This indicates that option writers are expecting the index to largely consolidate around this level. Today's fresh Call writing was heaviest near 75,300, suggesting some call sellers anticipate a cap around this higher level. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was strongest near 73,000, indicating put sellers are confident of a floor being maintained there.

For Bank Nifty, the heaviest Call OI and Put OI are both positioned at 57,500. This strike is considerably above the current spot, suggesting option writers are anticipating the index to remain below this level for calls, and above for puts, creating a wide range. Today's fresh Call writing was particularly heavy near 57,166, implying that call sellers are expecting resistance around this mark. The aggregate flow also showed a significant build-up in Call OI (0.5 million contracts) compared to Put OI (0.0 million contracts), reinforcing a cautious sentiment.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to that of Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests a bullish sentiment among option writers, as more Put options are being written (sold), implying expectations of the price staying above those strikes. A PCR below 1 often indicates a bearish sentiment, with more Call options being written, suggesting expectations of the price staying below those strikes.

For Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.97, which is quite balanced, indicating no strong directional bias from the overall book. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) is 0.93, also suggesting a mixed to slightly cautious fresh writing activity.

The Sensex has a PCR(OI) of 1.11, which leans slightly bullish, implying that overall, there are more put writers in the system. The PCR based on today's flow is 1.09, also indicating a slightly higher proportion of put writing today, suggesting some confidence in underlying support.

Bank Nifty shows a PCR(OI) of 0.86, which is below 1 and suggests a slightly bearish inclination from the overall option book. More notably, the PCR based on today's flow is a very low 0.06, indicating that today's fresh option writing was overwhelmingly dominated by Call selling. This points to a strong bearish sentiment from new positions taken today.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that level. As expiry approaches, the index often tends to gravitate towards this "Max Pain" strike, acting like a magnetic pull.

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain level is at 23,350, which is approximately 0.3% above the current spot of 23,270.60. This suggests a potential upward pull towards this level as we approach expiry.

The Sensex Max Pain is at 74,500, which is very close to the current spot of 74,314.59. This proximity indicates that option writers are positioning for the index to expire around this level, reinforcing the idea of a range-bound movement.

Bank Nifty has its Max Pain at 57,500, which is a significant 2.6% above its current spot of 56,055.75. While Max Pain can act as a magnet, the substantial distance from spot, combined with today's heavy call writing, suggests that reaching this level might face considerable resistance.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment. Today, the FII Index futures long ratio stands at 12.3%. This means that only 12.3% of their total index futures positions are long, while the vast majority are short. Historically, such a significantly low long ratio in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or net bearish outlook from FIIs on the broader market. Their stock futures long ratio, however, is at a more balanced 54.2%, suggesting a more neutral to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks.

Stocks where money moved today

In the F&O segment, we observed some notable movements in individual stocks. Among the top gainers, MARUTI saw a rise of 1.41% to ₹12,338.00, with a volume of 249,064. BAJFINANCE gained 0.87% to ₹1,015.00, experiencing significant volume of over 48 lakh shares. ITC also moved up by 0.78% to ₹266.20, with a substantial volume of over 1.3 crore shares.

On the losing side, TITAN declined by 1.38% to ₹4,841.00. HDFCBANK fell by 1.18% to ₹713.00, recording the highest volume among the losers at over 2.1 crore shares. HINDUNILVR was down 1.01% to ₹1,942.20. These movements reflect shifts in sentiment and positioning within specific scrips.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we synthesise the various signals from today's F&O data, a nuanced picture emerges for the upcoming session. Nifty's mild bullish bias, supported by its Max Pain level, suggests a potential for some upward momentum. However, Sensex's tight range-bound indications, with strong Call and Put walls close to spot, point towards consolidation. Bank Nifty's distinctly bearish fresh option flow and lower PCR suggest that the banking sector might face headwinds. The overall cautious stance from FIIs in index futures, as indicated by their low long ratio, historically tends to coincide with a market that may struggle for sustained upward moves. Therefore, when these signals align in this direction, the next session has historically tended to see a market that is mixed, potentially with Nifty attempting to inch higher while Bank Nifty faces pressure, and Sensex remains confined to a narrow trading range.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals yourself can be a powerful tool. You can start by looking at the Open Interest (OI) data for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty options, which is freely available on the NSE website under the "Derivatives" section. Look for the option chain for the nearest expiry. Identify the strike prices with the highest Call OI and highest Put OI – these are your potential resistance and support levels. Also, observe the "Change in OI" column to see where fresh positions are being added or closed, which can give clues about the immediate sentiment of option writers.

Next, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI. Do this for both the overall OI and for the change in OI for the day. A PCR above 1 suggests more put writing (bullish), while below 1 suggests more call writing (bearish). Finally, check the FII derivative statistics, also available on the NSE site, to gauge the positioning of institutional players in index futures. Combining these data points daily can help you form your own informed perspective on the market's underlying dynamics.
