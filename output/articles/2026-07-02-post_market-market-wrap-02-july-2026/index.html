Market Wrap — 02 July 2026

TL;DR

  • Nifty 50 closed higher, gaining over 0.7%, while Bank Nifty remained flat.
  • IT and Auto sectors were the top performers, driving the market's upward momentum.
  • PSU Banks saw some profit booking, emerging as the top losing sector.
  • Broader markets, represented by Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100, also posted healthy gains.
  • Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their buying spree, offsetting selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs).

How the market closed

The Indian equity benchmarks concluded the trading session on a positive note, with the Nifty 50 advancing by 0.71% to close at 24175.70. The Bank Nifty, however, ended flat at 58031.65.

| Index | Close | Change | High | Low | | :---------------- | :-------- | :------- | :--------- | :--------- | | Nifty 50 | 24175.70 | +0.71% | 24194.55 | 24058.80 | | Bank Nifty | 58031.65 | +0.00% | 58396.10 | 57884.60 | | Midcap 100 | 62307.90 | +0.48% | 62421.00 | 62127.50 | | Smallcap 100 | 19167.80 | +1.25% | 19179.20 | 18984.10 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, dropped significantly by 7.18% to 12.29, indicating a reduction in market uncertainty.

Sectors — winners and losers

Top 3 Gainer Sectors:

  • IT (+4.64%): The Information Technology sector witnessed a strong rally today, emerging as the top gainer.
  • REALTY (+1.45%): Real estate stocks also performed well, adding to the market's positive sentiment.
  • AUTO (+1.21%): The Automobile sector saw notable buying interest, contributing positively to the indices.

Top 3 Loser Sectors:

  • PSU BANK (-0.43%): Public Sector Banks faced some selling pressure, ending the day in the red.
  • ENERGY (-0.15%): The Energy sector experienced a slight decline.
  • BANK (+0.00%): The broader banking sector remained flat, mirroring the Bank Nifty's performance.

Market breadth

The market breadth was overwhelmingly positive today, with 347 advances against 151 declines out of a total of 498 traded stocks. This indicates that the upward movement was broad-based, with more stocks participating in the rally than those that fell.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers today, offloading Indian equities worth ₹-1,140 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to be strong buyers, injecting ₹3,159 crore into the market.

| Investor Type | Net Position (₹ crore) | | :------------ | :--------------------- | | FII | -1,140 | | DII | 3,159 |

Looking at the 5-day history, FIIs have been net sellers for four out of the last five sessions, with a cumulative net outflow. DIIs, on the other hand, have consistently been net buyers, providing crucial support to the Indian markets and absorbing the FII selling pressure.

What moved markets today

1. Rupee Weakens Despite Weaker Dollar The Indian rupee extended its losing streak for the fourth consecutive session against the dollar, despite a weaker dollar globally and likely intervention from the central bank. Merchant and arbitrage flows were cited as factors contributing to the rupee's depreciation. This ongoing weakness in the rupee could be a point of concern for import-heavy sectors.

2. India's Semiconductor Ambitions and IT Sector Optimism India is accelerating its semiconductor ambitions with a proposed second-phase incentive programme, positioning the country to evolve from a global chip design hub into a full-stack player. This long-term positive outlook for the tech sector, coupled with immediate tailwinds, likely contributed to the strong performance of IT stocks today.

3. Banks Raising FCNR Deposit Rates to Attract Inflows Indian banks are increasing returns on FCNR(B) deposits following eased norms by the RBI, aimed at attracting foreign currency inflows. Ujjivan Small Finance Bank is offering 7.5% on 3-5 year USD FCNR deposits, while DBS Bank offers up to 5.6%. This move could help shore up foreign exchange reserves and provide liquidity.

4. Major IPOs on the Horizon SBI Mutual Fund is reportedly planning to launch its IPO on July 13, aiming to raise $1.2 billion, which could be one of the largest in India's financial sector. Additionally, Swara Baby has filed its DRHP with Sebi for a ₹1,000 crore IPO. These upcoming public issues indicate a robust primary market and potential for significant capital mobilization.

Setup for tomorrow

The Indian market is expected to remain cautiously optimistic, with Nifty eyeing the 24,500-24,800 range in July, supported by cooling crude prices and a potential slowdown in FII selling. Investors might look to "buy the dip" towards the 23,600-23,800 levels. The broader market is predicted to remain range-bound in the medium term, with opportunities in deep tech and data infrastructure.

Globally, US stock futures traded mixed as the AI-fuelled tech rally showed signs of losing steam, with investors awaiting crucial US employment data. Asian markets, particularly South Korea's Kospi, saw a sharp selloff for a second day, with Samsung and SK Hynix shares plunging amid renewed concerns over AI chip demand. Brent crude prices also cooled, which is generally positive for India. The USD/INR closed higher at 95.39, indicating continued pressure on the rupee.
