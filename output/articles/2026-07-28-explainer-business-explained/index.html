Let's start writing.# Flipkart's New Food Delivery Plan: What ONDC Means for Your Meals

TL;DR

  • E-commerce giant Flipkart is entering the food delivery market using the government-backed ONDC network.
  • This means you might soon order food from a new app connected to Flipkart, offering more choices.
  • ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce) is an open system aiming to democratise online buying and selling.
  • The move will increase competition in food delivery, potentially leading to better prices and service for you.
  • It also empowers more local restaurants to reach customers without relying on just one or two big platforms.

What's this about?

India's e-commerce giant, Flipkart, is getting into the food delivery business. But they are doing it in a new way – by joining the Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC). ONDC is a government-backed initiative designed to create an open platform for online buying and selling, breaking away from the traditional model where a few big companies control most of the market. Flipkart plans to launch its food delivery service in select cities soon, using a dedicated app that connects to the ONDC network.

Why it's in the news right now

This is big news because Flipkart officially announced its plan to launch an online food delivery service in partnership with ONDC on July 24, 2026. This move is set to shake up the existing food delivery market, which is currently dominated by a couple of major players. Flipkart's entry through ONDC signals a significant shift in how large companies are approaching digital commerce in India, leveraging the open network to expand their services.

How it actually works

ONDC isn't a single app like Amazon or Zomato. Instead, it's a set of open rules and specifications that allow different online platforms to talk to each other. Think of it like a common language for online businesses. Here’s how it works:

  1. Buyer Apps: These are the apps you use to search for and order products or services. Examples include Paytm, PhonePe's Pincode, and now, a new app from Flipkart for food delivery.
  2. Seller Apps: These are used by businesses (like your local restaurant or kirana store) to list their products and manage orders. When a seller registers on one of these apps, their products become visible across all buyer apps connected to ONDC.
  3. Logistics Providers: These are the delivery partners who pick up orders from sellers and deliver them to buyers. ONDC allows for various logistics providers to be part of the network, giving sellers and buyers more options.

When you use a buyer app connected to ONDC (like Flipkart's upcoming food app), you'll see food listings from many restaurants that have joined the ONDC network, regardless of which "seller app" they originally used to list their menu. ONDC acts as the bridge, connecting buyers, sellers, and logistics partners seamlessly.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine you live in a neighbourhood with many small shops and a few big supermarkets. Traditionally, if you wanted to order online, you'd have to use each supermarket's own app, or a specific app that only lists certain shops.

ONDC is like a new, common "digital marketplace" for your entire neighbourhood. Now, all the small shops AND the big supermarkets can list their products on this one common digital marketplace. You, as a customer, can use any compatible "shopping app" (like Flipkart's new food app) that connects to this marketplace, and see products from all participating shops. You can compare prices and choose easily, without needing a separate app for every single shop. It's like UPI for payments, but for buying and selling goods and services online.

Why it matters to you

  1. More Choices and Better Prices: With more restaurants and food outlets joining ONDC and being accessible through various buyer apps, you'll have a much wider selection of food options. Increased competition among sellers on the network can also lead to more competitive pricing and better deals.
  2. Support for Local Businesses: ONDC makes it easier for smaller, local restaurants and eateries to get online and reach a larger customer base without paying high commissions to individual large platforms. This means you can discover and support your favourite neighbourhood spots more easily.
  3. Potential for Faster Deliveries: ONDC's design encourages hyperlocal (local area) deliveries and integrates various logistics partners. This could mean quicker delivery times, especially when ordering from nearby restaurants.
  4. Reduced Dependency on Single Platforms: You won't be locked into one or two dominant food delivery apps. ONDC gives you the flexibility to choose which buyer app you prefer, while still accessing a vast network of sellers.
  5. Innovation and New Services: As more companies like Flipkart join ONDC, it encourages innovation in how services are offered. This could lead to new features, better customer service, and more integrated digital experiences.

Common confusions cleared up

Myth: ONDC is a new app that I need to download to order food. Fact: ONDC is not an app itself. It's an open network or a set of rules. You will use existing or new "buyer apps" (like Flipkart's upcoming food app, or Paytm, PhonePe's Pincode) that are connected to the ONDC network to place orders.

Myth: ONDC will replace Zomato and Swiggy. Fact: ONDC aims to create a level playing field and increase competition, but it doesn't necessarily replace existing platforms. Zomato and Swiggy can also choose to join the ONDC network as buyer or seller participants, or continue operating independently. It offers an alternative and expands the ecosystem.

Myth: ONDC is only for food delivery. Fact: While food delivery is a growing segment, ONDC is designed for all kinds of digital commerce – from groceries and fashion to mobility services (like metro and bus ticketing) and even financial services. It's a broad network for various goods and services.

FAQ

How can I start using ONDC for food delivery?

Once Flipkart launches its dedicated food delivery app, you can download it and use it like any other food ordering app. You can also use other ONDC-enabled buyer apps like Paytm or PhonePe's Pincode to find restaurants on the network.

Do restaurants need to join a specific app to sell on ONDC?

Restaurants need to register with a "seller app" that is part of the ONDC network. Once they do, their menu becomes visible to customers using any "buyer app" connected to ONDC.

Is ONDC a government company?

ONDC is an initiative by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Government of India. It is led by a private non-profit Section 8 company.

How many businesses are currently on ONDC?

As of April 2026, over 7 lakh (700,000) sellers and service providers have onboarded ONDC, operating in more than 1,200 cities. The network has facilitated over 450 million cumulative transactions as of June 2026.
