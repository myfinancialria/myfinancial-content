Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. In the options market, the strike price with the highest Call OI is often seen as a significant resistance level, indicating where many traders believe the upward movement might be capped. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI is typically viewed as a strong support level, suggesting where the market might find a floor.

For Nifty 50, today's fresh Call writing was most concentrated near the 22,800 strike. This suggests that option sellers, often referred to as "writers," are positioning themselves for the index to potentially stay below this level. On the Put side, fresh writing was observed near 22,266, indicating that some writers anticipate the index will find support around this point. The aggregate flow showed a net build-up of 59.6 million in Call OI compared to 21.6 million in Put OI, suggesting a stronger conviction among Call sellers today.

In the Sensex, the heaviest fresh Call writing today was seen near the 73,666 strike, with the overall heaviest Call OI wall already at 73,000. This indicates a strong belief among Call writers that the index may struggle to move significantly higher. Fresh Put writing, on the other hand, was notable near 71,500, implying expected support at that level. The overall flow saw a net Call OI build of 19.7 million against 9.8 million in Put OI, reinforcing the Call-heavy sentiment.

For Bank Nifty, fresh Put writing was most prominent near 55,333. This positioning suggests that Put writers expect the index to remain above this level, potentially acting as a floor. The heaviest Put OI base for the index is already quite close to the spot at 55,000, further strengthening this potential support zone.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that helps gauge market sentiment. It is calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 generally suggests a bullish sentiment (more Puts written than Calls), while a value below 1 often indicates a bearish sentiment (more Calls written than Puts).

For Nifty 50, the overall PCR based on Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.7, which typically suggests a relatively balanced options book, leaning slightly towards more Call writing. However, today's flow, reflected in the change in Open Interest (ΔOI), shows a PCR of 0.36. This significantly lower value indicates that fresh writing activity today was heavily dominated by Call options, signaling a bearish undertone for the session.

The Sensex also has an overall PCR(OI) of 0.89, indicating a balanced book with a slight lean towards Call writing. Its PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) is 0.5, again pointing to a day where Call writing outpaced Put writing, suggesting bearish sentiment from today's fresh positioning.

For Bank Nifty, the PCR(OI) is 0.89, similar to Sensex, suggesting a relatively balanced overall options structure. However, its PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) is 1.22. This value, above 1, suggests that today's fresh writing activity saw more Put options being written than Call options, which can be interpreted as a neutral to mildly bullish signal for the day's flow.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is a concept in options trading that refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. As expiry approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this Max Pain strike, acting like a magnetic pull.

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain level is identified at 22,900. This is approximately 1.3% above the current spot price of 22,603.05, suggesting that the "pin pull" or magnetic effect, as expiry nears, could be towards the upside.

The Sensex has its Max Pain level at 72,800, which is very close to its current spot price of 72,638.70. When Max Pain is near the spot, it often indicates that the market might consolidate around current levels as expiry approaches.

Bank Nifty shows a Max Pain level of 56,000. This is about 1.7% above its current spot price of 55,055.55. This higher Max Pain level suggests a potential upward pull for the index as it heads towards expiry.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are often considered significant players in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held a long ratio of 9.2% in Index futures. This means that out of their total Index futures positions, only 9.2% were long, while the remaining 90.8% were short. Historically, such a low long ratio in Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from these large institutional participants.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of 54.6%, indicating a more balanced to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks compared to their index-level positioning.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to form your own informed perspectives. You can access the raw data yourself daily from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, specifically the "Option Chain" for indices and the "FII Derivatives" section. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in a more user-friendly format.

To interpret the data, first look at the Option Chain to identify the strikes with the highest Call and Put Open Interest, as these form your immediate resistance and support levels. Then, observe the "Change in Open Interest" column to see where fresh positions (writing) are being added – heavy fresh Call writing at a strike above spot indicates expected resistance, while heavy fresh Put writing below spot suggests expected support. Calculate the PCR by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI, and also look at the PCR of the change in OI for a clearer picture of today's fresh sentiment. Finally, locate the Max Pain strike to understand the "magnetic" pull for option writers. Combining these data points can help you build a comprehensive view of market positioning.
