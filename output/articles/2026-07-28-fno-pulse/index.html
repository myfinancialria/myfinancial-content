Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, like Calls and Puts, that have not yet been settled. When we look at the highest Call OI strike, it indicates a level where a large number of Call options have been sold (written). These Call writers generally expect the underlying asset's price not to rise above this strike, effectively acting as a potential resistance or "ceiling." Conversely, the highest Put OI strike signifies a level where many Put options have been written. Put writers typically anticipate the price not to fall below this strike, thus forming a potential support or "floor."

For the NIFTY 50, the heaviest Call OI is observed at 24,000, which is just 0.1% above the spot price, suggesting a strong immediate ceiling. The heaviest Put OI is at 23,950, also very close, acting as a firm floor. Today's fresh Call writing was most concentrated near 24,000, reinforcing the expectation among sellers that this level might cap upward movement. Simultaneously, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 23,950, indicating conviction that this level will hold as support.

On the SENSEX, while specific Call and Put wall proximities weren't highlighted, fresh Call writing was significant near 77,500, suggesting option sellers are building resistance there. Fresh Put writing was concentrated near 75,500, indicating a potential base.

For BANK NIFTY, the heaviest Call OI sits at 56,800, a mere 0.1% above spot, acting as a very tight ceiling. The heaviest Put OI is at 56,700, also just 0.1% below spot, forming an immediate floor. Today's fresh Call writing was most active near 56,800, signaling that option sellers anticipate this level to be a cap. Fresh Put writing was heaviest near 56,700, reflecting expectations of support at that level.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator calculated by dividing the total Open Interest of Put options by the total Open Interest of Call options. A PCR(OI) value above 1 generally suggests that more Put options have been written than Call options, which can be interpreted as a bullish leaning among option writers who expect prices to rise or remain stable. Conversely, a PCR(OI) below 1 might indicate a bearish sentiment, with more Call options written, suggesting expectations of prices falling or staying capped.

For the NIFTY 50, the PCR(OI) stands at 0.93, which suggests a relatively balanced book with a slight lean towards Call writing. Today's flow, represented by ΔPCR at -2.49, indicates that fresh writing activity was also quite balanced, showing no strong directional bias from new positions.

The SENSEX recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.01, indicating a balanced book with slightly more Put writing than Call writing overall. The ΔPCR for today's flow was 0.82, suggesting that fresh writing activity was also relatively balanced, leaning neither strongly bullish nor bearish.

BANK NIFTY has a PCR(OI) of 0.7, which points to a heavier concentration of Call writing compared to Put writing, suggesting a cautious or slightly bearish undertone from option writers. Today's flow, with a ΔPCR of 0.57, confirms that fresh writing activity was indeed Call-heavy, further reinforcing the expectation of a potential cap on upward movement.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is a concept in options theory that refers to the strike price at which the maximum number of options (both Calls and Puts) would expire worthless, causing the greatest financial loss to option buyers and, conversely, the maximum profit to option sellers (writers). It's often seen as a gravitational pull, suggesting that the underlying index tends to gravitate towards this level as expiry approaches, as option writers try to ensure their positions expire profitably.

For the NIFTY 50, the Max Pain strike is at 24,000, which is currently at the spot price. This suggests a strong magnetic pull towards this level by expiry.

The SENSEX also has its Max Pain strike at 76,800, very close to its current spot price. This indicates a similar gravitational tendency for the index to hover around this level.

For BANK NIFTY, the Max Pain strike is at 57,000, which is approximately 0.4% above its current spot price. This suggests a slight upward pull or a tendency for the index to move higher towards this level as the expiry date nears.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 8.8%. This means that only 8.8% of their total Index futures positions were on the long side, with the vast majority (91.2%) being short. Historically, such a low long ratio in Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from FIIs on the broader market direction.

In contrast, their Stock futures long ratio stood at 54.4%. This indicates a more balanced to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks, with a slightly higher proportion of long positions compared to short positions in stock futures.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When multiple option data points, such as concentrated Open Interest walls, Max Pain at or near spot, and balanced PCRs, align to suggest a narrow range, the market has historically tended to consolidate or trade sideways in the subsequent session. For NIFTY 50 and SENSEX, the strong Call and Put writing near current levels, coupled with Max Pain at spot, suggests that option writers are actively trying to keep these indices within a tight band. BANK NIFTY, while also showing tight immediate walls, has a Max Pain slightly above spot, which historically has tended to coincide with a mild upward bias towards that Max Pain level, even within an overall range-bound outlook. The FIIs' notably low long ratio in index futures historically tends to coincide with a cautious sentiment from institutional players on the broader market, which could contribute to range-bound or subdued movements.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals daily can empower you to interpret market movements more effectively. You can access options data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" for NIFTY, BANK NIFTY, and FINNIFTY. Many stockbrokers also provide this data within their trading platforms in an easily digestible format.

To combine these insights, first, identify the highest Call OI and Put OI strikes for the upcoming expiry; these act as your immediate resistance and support levels. Next, check the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) for overall sentiment – a PCR above 1 suggests bullishness from writers, below 1 suggests caution. Then, locate the "Max Pain" strike; this is often a strong magnet for the index. Finally, look at the FII derivative positioning, usually available on the NSE website under "Participant-wise Open Interest," to gauge institutional sentiment. By observing how these data points shift daily, you can build a clearer picture of where option writers and large participants are positioning themselves, helping you understand the market's underlying structure.
