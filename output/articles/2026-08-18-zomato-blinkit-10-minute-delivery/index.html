Why Deepinder Goyal Bet Zomato on 10-Minute Grocery Delivery When Everyone Said It Was Impossible

On June 24, 2022, Deepinder Goyal, the co-founder and CEO of Zomato, announced a decision that sent shockwaves through the Indian startup ecosystem and saw Zomato's stock tumble. His company, a giant in food delivery, was acquiring Blinkit (formerly Grofers), a quick commerce startup, for ₹4,447 crore (approximately $568 million) in an all-stock deal. The market reacted with skepticism, with Zomato's shares falling by over 6.6% on the day of the announcement, and over 20% in the following six sessions. Analysts questioned the wisdom of a loss-making company acquiring another cash-strapped business, especially one operating in the notoriously challenging quick commerce space. Many believed Goyal was making a foolish, perhaps even suicidal, bet. They were wrong.

The World Before: A Cash-Guzzling Conundrum

The quick commerce market in India, around 2022, was a battlefield of burning cash and uncertain futures. The promise of delivering groceries and essentials in 10-30 minutes was alluring to urban consumers, particularly millennials and Gen Z who craved instant gratification. However, the operational reality was brutal. Companies like Blinkit, Swiggy Instamart, and Zepto were locked in an intense race for market share, which necessitated heavy investments in "dark stores" – small warehouses strategically located close to residential areas – and a vast, efficient delivery fleet.

Grofers, the precursor to Blinkit, had initially operated on a scheduled delivery model, but pivoted to 10-minute delivery and rebranded as Blinkit in 2021 to align with the quick commerce trend. Despite its technological prowess and a dense network of dark stores, Blinkit was struggling financially, bleeding cash and seeking urgent funding to survive. Zomato had already invested $100 million in Grofers in August 2021, acquiring a 9.3% stake, which had briefly pushed Grofers into unicorn status. Yet, the quick commerce model, with its high operational costs and intense competition, remained largely unproven in terms of profitability. Analysts from Credit Suisse and JM Financial predicted that the acquisition would likely extend Zomato's path to profitability by at least a year. Even as late as May 2022, Blinkit was losing approximately ₹84 per order, with an average order value of ₹509. The market saw a cash-guzzling entity being absorbed by another company already under pressure to show profitable growth.

The Crack: A Strategic Gambit and Operational Discipline

Deepinder Goyal, however, saw something different. He believed quick commerce was a natural extension of Zomato's food delivery business and a critical move to capture a larger share of the customer's wallet. He also recognized that an entrenched quick commerce player could eventually pose a threat to Zomato's core food delivery business. The acquisition, finalized by August 2022, was not merely about acquiring a failing business; it was a strategic pivot.

Zomato's strategy for Blinkit hinged on several key decisions:

  1. Leveraging Existing Logistics and Technology: Zomato already possessed a robust last-mile delivery network and technological expertise from its food delivery operations. This allowed them to integrate Blinkit's dark store network and delivery fleet, improving efficiency and reducing delivery costs during non-peak hours.
  2. Density, Not Speed at All Costs: Goyal consistently emphasized that Blinkit's 10-minute delivery promise was enabled by the density of dark stores around customer homes, not by pressuring delivery partners to drive fast. Delivery partners do not even have a timer on their app indicating the promised delivery time. Orders are picked and packed within 2.5 minutes, with riders covering an average of under 2 km in about 8 minutes. This focus on a well-designed system, rather than reckless speed, allowed for sustainable quick deliveries.
  3. Operational Efficiency over Deep Discounts: Unlike many quick commerce players who relied heavily on subsidies and promotions, Blinkit, under Zomato, focused on streamlining its supply chain and delivery networks. This approach helped improve delivery times and order fulfillment rates while maintaining a sustainable cost structure, driving long-term growth without unsustainable cash burn.
  4. Expanding Assortment and Penetration: Instead of spreading thin across numerous smaller towns, Blinkit concentrated on deepening its engagement in existing markets, particularly top metros. In the National Capital Region (NCR), for instance, Blinkit expanded its product assortment from 35,000-40,000 items to around 80,000 within a year. This wider selection helped strengthen customer loyalty and increased the average order value (AOV).
  5. A "Dissatisfied Culture": Deepinder Goyal attributed Blinkit's quiet ascent to a culture of "dissatisfaction and continuous improvement." This mindset pushed the team to constantly seek improvements, rather than resting on past successes.

What Happened Next: A Remarkable Turnaround

The gamble paid off spectacularly. By 2024, Blinkit had not only outgrown Swiggy Instamart in market share but had also become a significant driver of Zomato's overall profitability.

  • Revenue Growth: Blinkit's revenue from operations nearly tripled in FY24, rising from ₹806 crore in FY23 to ₹2,301 crore. In the March quarter of FY24 alone, it reported ₹769 crore in revenue. The quick commerce segment's Gross Order Value (GOV) almost doubled, reaching ₹12,469 crore in FY24, with an average delivery time of just 12.5 minutes. By Q4 FY26, Blinkit's quick-commerce Net Order Value (NOV) reached ₹14,386 crore, exceeding Zomato's food delivery NOV for the first time.
  • Profitability: The quick commerce unit turned adjusted EBITDA positive in March 2024, a significant milestone. Zomato itself achieved its first profitable year in FY24, reporting a net profit of ₹351 crore, a stark contrast to the ₹971 crore loss in FY23. This profitability was largely driven by the growth in both its food business and quick commerce, with Blinkit's adjusted EBITDA losses decreasing.
  • Market Share: As of mid-2025, Blinkit leads the Indian quick commerce market with an estimated 44-46% market share, followed by Zepto at 29-30% and Swiggy Instamart at 23-25%. Some estimates even placed Blinkit's share at 45-50% in early 2026.
  • Valuation: Goldman Sachs analysts, who were initially skeptical, upgraded Blinkit's implied valuation to $13 billion by April 2024, up from $2 billion in March 2023 – a six-fold increase year-on-year. This made Blinkit more valuable than Zomato's core food delivery business, aligning with Deepinder Goyal's earlier forecast that Blinkit would become larger than its parent company.
  • Expansion: Blinkit rapidly expanded its dark store network, opening 75 new stores in Q4 FY24, bringing the total to 526. The company aims to reach the 1,000-store mark by the end of FY25. By Q4 FY26, the total number of dark stores reached 2,243.

Lessons to Take Away

  1. Strategic Vision Over Short-Term Noise: Deepinder Goyal's decision to acquire Blinkit was met with significant market skepticism and a drop in Zomato's share price. Yet, his long-term strategic vision, seeing quick commerce as a complementary rather than competing business, proved prescient. Sometimes, a leader must bet against the prevailing sentiment if they truly believe in the underlying opportunity and its strategic fit.
  2. Operational Excellence as a Differentiator: In a cash-intensive and competitive market like quick commerce, Blinkit's success wasn't built on unsustainable discounts but on rigorous operational efficiency. Focusing on optimizing dark store density, quick packing, and efficient delivery routes, rather than merely pushing riders to be faster, allowed for sustainable growth and a path to profitability. This highlights that "quick" doesn't have to mean "reckless."
  3. Leveraging Synergies for Growth: The acquisition of Blinkit allowed Zomato to unlock significant synergies, combining their existing logistics and technological infrastructure. This reduced redundancy, improved unit economics, and enabled faster scaling, demonstrating the power of strategic acquisitions when capabilities align.
  4. Adaptability and Evolution are Key: Grofers' pivot to Blinkit, and Zomato's subsequent acquisition, underscore the importance of businesses adapting to evolving consumer demands. The initial scheduled delivery model wasn't working; the shift to quick commerce tapped into a burgeoning desire for instant gratification, proving that flexibility in business models can be crucial for survival and growth.
  5. Focus on Core Strengths and Density: Instead of a scattered approach, Blinkit's strategy of deepening penetration and expanding assortment in key urban areas proved more effective than broad, unfocused expansion. This "density-first" approach for a hyperlocal service optimized operational costs and built stronger customer loyalty.
