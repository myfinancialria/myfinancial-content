India F&O Pulse — 25 Sep 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Mixed Signals Emerge as Nifty and Bank Nifty Show Mildly Bullish Leanings, Sensex Cautious

TL;DR

  • Nifty 50: The options data for Nifty 50 suggests a mildly bullish sentiment for the upcoming session, with significant Put writing providing a floor.
  • Sensex: Sensex options data indicates a mildly bearish leaning, with Call writers actively capping upside movement.
  • Bank Nifty: Bank Nifty's positioning points towards a mildly bullish outlook, supported by a higher 'max pain' level.

What the indices did today

The Nifty 50 closed the day at 23,140.50, marking a gain of 0.34%. It settled comfortably within its implied option-chain range of 23,000 to 24,000, suggesting market participants are broadly expecting the index to remain within these boundaries.

The Sensex advanced by 0.43% to finish at 73,895.74. The index closed very close to its upper option-chain wall of 74,000, indicating that this level might act as a significant resistance point going forward.

Meanwhile, the Bank Nifty also saw a positive close, up 0.26% at 55,580.40. The index's implied range, surprisingly narrow at 57,500 – 57,500, suggests a strong concentration of open interest at this specific strike, which is quite a distance from the current spot.

Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options contracts provides a crucial glimpse into where market participants, particularly option sellers or "writers," are positioning themselves. The strike price with the highest Call OI typically acts as a strong resistance, indicating a level above which Call writers believe the underlying index is unlikely to move. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI often serves as a robust support, suggesting a floor below which Put writers expect the index to hold.

For the Nifty 50, the heaviest Call OI is observed around the 24,000 strike, while the strongest Put OI base sits at 23,000. Today's fresh writing activity saw significant new Call writing near 23,383, suggesting that Call sellers anticipate the index might find a cap around this level. On the Put side, heavy fresh writing was seen near 23,050, indicating that Put sellers expect this to act as a floor.

In the Sensex, the most substantial Call OI wall is at 74,000, which is very close to the day's closing spot. The heaviest Put OI is concentrated around 72,000. Today, fresh Call writing was most prominent near 74,433, implying that Call writers are positioning for a potential ceiling above the current levels. New Put writing was significant near 73,766, suggesting Put sellers are comfortable defending this level as a support.

For Bank Nifty, the option chain shows the highest Call OI at 57,500 and the strongest Put OI also at 57,500, which is an interesting convergence and quite far from the current spot. Today's fresh Call writing was most active near 55,766, indicating that Call sellers see this as a potential resistance. On the Put side, fresh writing was heaviest near 55,366, suggesting Put writers are establishing a floor around this point.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that helps gauge market sentiment. It is calculated by dividing the total Open Interest of Put options by the total Open Interest of Call options. A PCR value above 1 generally suggests a bullish bias, as it implies more Put writing (expecting prices to rise or stay above a certain level) than Call writing. Conversely, a PCR below 1 often indicates a bearish bias, with more Call writing (expecting prices to fall or stay below a certain level).

For the Nifty 50, the PCR based on Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.92, which suggests a largely balanced book with a slight lean towards Call writing. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) was -1.49, indicating a mixed to balanced flow of new positions being added.

The Sensex recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.06, pointing to a relatively balanced options book. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) was 1.25, suggesting a mixed to balanced flow of fresh positions.

The Bank Nifty saw its PCR(OI) at 0.83, indicating a slight tilt towards Call writing. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) was -1.66, which again suggests a mixed to balanced flow of new options being written.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that specific price. This strike often acts as a magnetic pull, especially as the expiry date approaches, as option writers tend to adjust their positions to minimise their potential losses.

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain strike is at 23,300. This is approximately 0.7% above the current spot price, suggesting a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry nears.

The Sensex has its Max Pain strike at 74,000, which is very close to the day's closing spot. This indicates that option writers are currently positioned for the index to potentially expire around this level.

For the Bank Nifty, the Max Pain strike is significantly higher at 56,700, which is about 2.0% above its current spot price. This suggests a notable upward bias from the perspective of option writers for Bank Nifty's expiry.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant players in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment. Today's data shows that FIIs maintained a low long ratio in Index futures, standing at just 11.4%. This means that out of their total Index futures positions, a much larger proportion are short positions (358,306 contracts) compared to long positions (46,305 contracts). Historically, such a low long ratio in FII Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from these institutional participants on the broader market indices.

In contrast, FIIs held a slightly net long position in Stock futures, with a long ratio of 53.8% (3,498,393 long contracts versus 3,003,382 short contracts). This suggests a relatively balanced to mildly positive sentiment from FIIs regarding individual stocks in the F&O segment.

Stocks where money moved today

In the F&O segment, several stocks saw notable price and volume action today:

Among the top gainers, AXISBANK led the pack with a 3.03% increase, closing at ₹1222.40. BAJFINANCE also saw a respectable gain of 1.52%, ending at ₹996.90. TITAN climbed 1.07% to ₹4884.00, while HDFCBANK rose 0.92% to ₹735.60, witnessing significant volume of over 1.98 crore shares. MARUTI also moved up by 0.63% to ₹12065.00.

On the losing side, INFY declined by 1.41% to ₹1000.20, with high trading volume exceeding 1 crore shares. ICICIBANK fell 0.58% to ₹1326.80, also seeing substantial volume of over 1 crore shares. BHARTIARTL was down 0.58% at ₹1785.40. KOTAKBANK slipped 0.25% to ₹404.00, with over 1.8 crore shares traded, and TCS ended 0.24% lower at ₹2082.00.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we synthesise the various signals from today's F&O data, a nuanced picture emerges for the upcoming session. The Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty both show a mildly bullish leaning, primarily driven by their respective 'max pain' levels being above current spot prices and fresh Put writing activity providing immediate support. This suggests that option writers are, on balance, positioned for these indices to either hold steady or see some upward movement.

However, the Sensex presents a mildly bearish tilt, with its Call wall and Max Pain very close to the current spot, coupled with fresh Call writing activity suggesting a cap on upside. Adding to this caution is the significantly low FII Index futures long ratio, which historically has tended to coincide with institutional players adopting a more defensive or bearish stance on the broader market.

Therefore, when these signals align in this manner — a mixed sentiment across indices with institutional players showing caution in index futures — the next session has historically tended to exhibit some volatility, with potential for both upward and downward movements. The market may attempt to gravitate towards the 'max pain' levels for Nifty and Bank Nifty, while the Sensex might encounter resistance at its current levels.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to form your own informed perspective. You can access the underlying data points daily from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website or through your broker's platform.

To start, navigate to the F&O section on the NSE India website and look for the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, or Sensex. Here, you can observe the Open Interest (OI) for various Call and Put strikes. Identify the strikes with the highest Call OI and highest Put OI – these represent potential resistance and support levels, respectively. Also, pay attention to the "Change in OI" column, which tells you where fresh positions (new writing) were added today. For FII positioning, you can download the "FII Derivatives Data" CSV file from the NSE archives, usually found under the "Participant-wise Open Interest" section. By tracking these data points and observing how they change day-to-day, you can begin to discern the collective sentiment of market participants and build a more comprehensive view of potential market movements.
