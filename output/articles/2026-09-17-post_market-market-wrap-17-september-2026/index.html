Market Wrap — 17 September 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian equities witnessed a mixed session, with the Nifty 50 closing marginally higher while Bank Nifty dipped.
  • Global cues, particularly the US Federal Reserve's hawkish stance on interest rates and easing crude oil prices, were key drivers.
  • Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100 outperformed the benchmarks, indicating broader market participation.
  • Tega Industries Ltd. was a standout gainer, while Aditya Infotech Ltd. (CPPLUS) led the losers among Nifty 500 stocks.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net buyers.
  • F&O build-up data is unavailable for today's session and will be populated from the next wrap.

How the market behaved today

The Indian market experienced a mixed trading day, with the benchmark Nifty 50 managing a marginal gain while the Bank Nifty ended in the red. The Nifty 50 closed at 23270.60, up by 0.23%, after trading within a range of 23193.65 to 23363.55. In contrast, the Bank Nifty concluded the session at 56055.75, down 0.42%, having seen a high of 56570.45 and a low of 56038.50. Midcap and Smallcap indices showed stronger performance, reflecting a broader positive sentiment in the market.

What moved the market — the factors

Today's market movements were primarily influenced by a blend of global cues and domestic flows. The US Federal Reserve's recent interest rate hike, the first since 2023, signaled a hawkish turn in its inflation management strategy, initially creating some investor edginess globally. However, this was somewhat offset by easing crude oil prices, which provided relief to Indian markets, particularly for government bonds. US stocks, in fact, rose as oil prices eased pressure after the Fed rate hike.

Domestically, the market saw continued selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs), who were net sellers of ₹2,033 crore. This outflow was absorbed by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), who injected ₹3,908 crore, providing crucial support and preventing a sharper decline in the benchmark indices. Sector-specific action also played a role, with Pharma, Realty, and Media sectors witnessing healthy gains, while the Banking sector, including PSU Banks, faced selling pressure, contributing to Bank Nifty's decline. The news of Puravankara Ltd. eyeing ₹2,600 crore revenue from redevelopment projects likely boosted sentiment in the Realty sector.

Sectors — winners and losers

The day saw a clear divergence in sectoral performance, with defensive and growth-oriented sectors leading the charge, while rate-sensitive sectors like banking faced headwinds.

  • REALTY: +1.45% - Boosted by positive news flow, including redevelopment projects by major players like Puravankara.
  • MEDIA: +1.19% - Showed strength, indicating renewed investor interest in the segment.

Top 3 loser sectors:

  • BANK: -0.42% - Faced selling pressure, contributing to the Bank Nifty's decline, possibly due to global interest rate hike concerns.
  • PSU BANK: -0.16% - Also witnessed a dip, aligning with the broader weakness in the banking space.
  • FMCG: +0.03% - While technically a marginal gain, it was the weakest performing sector among the listed ones, indicating relative underperformance.

News that moved stocks

  • Fed raises interest rates, signals hawkish turn: The US Federal Reserve increased interest rates for the first time since 2023, adopting a hawkish stance to manage inflation. This move initially made investors edgy but US stocks later rose as oil prices eased.
  • NSE enters India’s mega-IPO club: The National Stock Exchange's (NSE) ₹22,561.57 crore IPO became India’s second-largest, attracting 189 anchor investors and raising ₹6,746.18 crore. This news generated significant market interest and positive sentiment around the exchange. Mauritius-based Soach Global is set to make nearly 25 times returns from its decade-old NSE investment.
  • Bank of England holds rates, sounds inflation alarm: The Bank of England maintained interest rates at 3.75 percent but expressed concerns about escalating inflation risks, potentially exceeding four percent. This highlights persistent global inflation worries.
  • Softer oil prices buoy Indian bonds: Indian government bonds recovered today as falling oil prices alleviated concerns about a potential rate hike from the Reserve Bank of India, indicating a positive impact on the bond market.
  • Puravankara Ltd. eyes ₹2,600 cr revenue from Mumbai redevelopment: Realty firm Puravankara Ltd. announced plans to redevelop three housing societies in Mumbai, with an expected revenue of ₹2,600 crore. This news likely contributed to the positive performance of the Realty sector today.
  • Sebi chief cautions on F&O trading losses: Sebi Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey reiterated that while regulatory measures have reduced aggregate F&O losses, individual traders continue to face losses, urging investors to assess if F&O trading works for them.

Market breadth

The market breadth was strongly positive today, with 363 advances against 135 declines out of a total of 498 traded stocks. This indicates that the positive sentiment was fairly broad-based across the market, despite the mixed performance of the benchmark indices.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree today, offloading Indian equities worth a net ₹-2,033 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, provided strong counter-support, injecting a net ₹3,908 crore into the market.

Conversely, DIIs have consistently been net buyers, absorbing the FII selling pressure with inflows between ₹1,026 crore and ₹3,908 crore over the past five sessions. This sustained DII buying has been crucial in cushioning the market from deeper corrections amidst FII outflows.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

The primary event influencing global and Indian markets today was the US Federal Reserve's decision to raise interest rates for the first time since 2023, signaling a more hawkish stance on inflation. This move initially created some apprehension, but its impact on US stocks was mitigated by easing oil prices, leading to a rise in Wall Street indices. In India, the softer crude oil prices were a significant positive, helping Indian government bonds recover from initial setbacks and easing concerns about potential domestic rate hikes by the RBI. The banking sector, however, still felt some pressure, likely due to the broader global tightening sentiment. On the domestic front, the news surrounding the NSE's mega IPO generated positive buzz, while specific company news like Puravankara's redevelopment plans provided a boost to the Realty sector. Overall, the session was driven by a careful balance between global monetary policy tightening and the relief provided by falling commodity prices, coupled with strong domestic institutional buying.

Setup for tomorrow

Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will continue to be important. US markets closed higher today, with the Dow Jones, S&P 500, and Nasdaq Composite all posting gains as oil prices eased. This positive sentiment from Wall Street could provide a favorable opening for Asian markets and, consequently, for India. Crude oil prices, with Brent at $103.61 and WTI at $100.84, showing a decline, will be closely watched as they directly impact India's import bill and inflation outlook. Investors will also keep an eye on the USD/INR movement and the US 10-year yield. Domestically, the performance of the banking sector and the continued FII/DII flow dynamics will be key. Sectors like Pharma and Realty, which showed strength today, might continue to be in focus.
