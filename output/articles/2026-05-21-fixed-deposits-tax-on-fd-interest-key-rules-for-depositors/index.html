Fixed Deposits: Tax on FD Interest & Key Rules for Depositors

TL;DR

  • Interest earned on Fixed Deposits (FDs) is fully taxable as 'Income from Other Sources' and added to your total income.
  • This interest is taxed according to your applicable income tax slab rate, potentially pushing you into a higher bracket.
  • Banks deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) if your FD interest exceeds ₹40,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens) in a financial year.
  • You can submit Form 15G (for non-senior citizens) or Form 15H (for senior citizens) to avoid TDS if your total income is below the taxable limit.
  • Certain FDs, known as 'Tax-Saving FDs,' offer deductions under Section 80C for the principal invested, but their interest remains taxable.

What this means in plain terms

When you invest in a Fixed Deposit, the money you earn as interest is considered income by the tax authorities. Unlike some other investments where returns might be tax-free or taxed at a special rate, FD interest is simply added to all your other earnings for the year. This total income then determines which income tax slab you fall into, and your FD interest is taxed at that slab rate.

This means that if you are in the highest tax bracket, your FD interest will also be taxed at that highest rate. Banks are also required to deduct a part of this interest as Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) if it crosses a certain limit in a financial year. While this might seem straightforward, understanding these rules is crucial for managing your overall tax liability and ensuring you don't pay more tax than necessary.

Understanding FD Interest Taxation

Interest earned on a Fixed Deposit is classified under the head 'Income from Other Sources' as per the Income Tax Act, 1961. This income is not treated separately but is clubbed with your salary, business income, or any other income you may have. The total gross income then determines your applicable income tax slab rate. For instance, if your total taxable income, including FD interest, falls into the 30% slab, then your FD interest will also be taxed at 30%. This is a crucial point, as many depositors assume FD interest has a separate, lower tax rate.

The taxation applies whether the interest is paid out periodically (non-cumulative FD) or reinvested and paid at maturity (cumulative FD). For cumulative FDs, the interest is taxable on an accrual basis each financial year, even if you don't physically receive it until maturity. This means you need to account for the accrued interest in your tax returns annually.

Decoding TDS on FD Interest

Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is a mechanism where the bank deducts a portion of your FD interest income before paying it to you and remits it to the government. This is not an additional tax but an advance payment of your tax liability. The current TDS thresholds are:

  • ₹40,000 for individuals below 60 years of age.
  • ₹50,000 for senior citizens (60 years and above).

If your annual FD interest from a single bank exceeds these limits, the bank will deduct TDS at a rate of 10% if you have provided your PAN. If you have not provided your PAN, the TDS rate jumps to 20%. It's critical to ensure your PAN is linked to all your FD accounts to avoid this higher deduction.

Even if TDS is deducted, it doesn't necessarily mean your final tax liability is settled. If your actual tax slab is higher than 10%, you will need to pay the remaining tax when filing your Income Tax Return. Conversely, if your total income is below the taxable limit and TDS has been deducted, you can claim a refund of the excess TDS paid by filing your ITR. You can track all TDS deductions made against your PAN in Form 26AS, accessible on the Income Tax India portal (https://incometax.gov.in).

Avoiding TDS with Form 15G/15H

If your total income for the financial year is below the basic exemption limit (e.g., ₹2.5 lakhs for individuals below 60, ₹3 lakhs for senior citizens), you can submit Form 15G (for non-senior citizens) or Form 15H (for senior citizens) to your bank. By submitting these forms, you declare that your income is below the taxable threshold and that no tax is payable by you. This instructs the bank not to deduct TDS on your FD interest. These forms must be submitted at the beginning of each financial year to be effective.

Tax-Saving FDs under Section 80C

While most FDs don't offer tax benefits on the principal, there's a special category known as 'Tax-Saving Fixed Deposits.' These FDs come with a mandatory lock-in period of 5 years. The principal amount invested in these FDs, up to a maximum of ₹1.5 lakhs in a financial year, is eligible for deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961. This deduction can significantly reduce your taxable income.

However, it's important to note that only the principal invested in a tax-saving FD qualifies for the 80C deduction. The interest earned on these FDs is still fully taxable as 'Income from Other Sources' and is added to your total income, just like any other FD interest. The TDS rules and slab-rate taxation apply equally to the interest from tax-saving FDs. These FDs typically offer cumulative or non-cumulative interest payout options, and the interest rates are generally comparable to regular FDs of similar tenure.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 45, ₹20L CTC, Mumbai. She has an annual taxable income of ₹16 lakhs after all deductions (excluding FD interest). She decides to invest in a new FD of ₹5 lakhs at 7% interest for 3 years.

Scenario:

  • FD Amount: ₹5,00,000
  • Interest Rate: 7% per annum
  • Annual Interest Income: ₹5,00,000 * 7% = ₹35,000

Tax Implications:

  1. TDS Calculation: Priya's annual FD interest is ₹35,000. Since this is below the ₹40,000 TDS threshold for non-senior citizens, the bank will not deduct any TDS.
  2. Total Taxable Income: Priya's initial taxable income is ₹16,00,000. Her FD interest of ₹35,000 will be added to this.

    3. * New Total Taxable Income = ₹16,00,000 + ₹35,000 = ₹16,35,000

  3. Income Tax Slab: Assuming Priya opts for the old tax regime, her income falls into the 30% tax slab (for income above ₹10 lakhs).
  4. Tax on FD Interest: The entire ₹35,000 FD interest will be taxed at 30%.

    5. Tax on FD Interest = ₹35,000 30% = ₹10,500

  5. Cess: An additional 4% Health and Education Cess will apply to this tax.

    6. Cess = ₹10,500 4% = ₹420 * Total Tax on FD Interest = ₹10,500 + ₹420 = ₹10,920

Even though no TDS was deducted, Priya still needs to declare this ₹35,000 income in her Income Tax Return and pay the additional tax of ₹10,920. If her FD interest from this or other FDs had crossed ₹40,000, the bank would have deducted 10% TDS, and she would have had to pay the remaining 20% (plus cess) at the time of filing her return.

What to do this week

  1. Check your Form 26AS: Log in to the Income Tax India portal (https://incometax.gov.in) and check your Form 26AS. Verify if all TDS deductions on your FD interest are accurately reflected.
  2. Identify FD Interest Income: Compile a list of all your FDs across different banks and calculate the estimated annual interest income from each. This will help you anticipate your total FD interest for the financial year.
  3. Assess TDS Applicability: If your total FD interest from any single bank is likely to exceed ₹40,000 (or ₹50,000 for senior citizens), consider if you need to submit Form 15G/15H to avoid TDS, provided your total income is below the taxable limit.
  4. Provide PAN to all banks: Ensure your PAN is updated with all banks where you hold FDs to avoid higher TDS deductions (20%) on your interest income.
  5. Review existing FDs: If you have multiple FDs, especially smaller ones, consider staggering their maturity dates or splitting large FDs across different banks or family members (e.g., spouse, minor child) to potentially keep individual interest incomes below the TDS threshold.

FAQ

Is FD interest fully taxable?

Yes, interest earned on Fixed Deposits is fully taxable. It is added to your total annual income and taxed according to your applicable income tax slab rate.

What is the TDS threshold for FD interest?

For individuals below 60 years, the TDS threshold is ₹40,000 annually per bank. For senior citizens (60 years and above), this limit is ₹50,000 annually per bank.

Can I avoid TDS on FD interest?

Yes, if your total income for the financial year is below the basic exemption limit, you can submit Form 15G (for non-senior citizens) or Form 15H (for senior citizens) to your bank to avoid TDS deduction.

Is interest from tax-saving FDs also taxable?

Yes, while the principal amount invested in a 5-year tax-saving FD qualifies for deduction under Section 80C, the interest earned on it is fully taxable as per your income tax slab.

How is interest from cumulative FDs taxed?

For cumulative FDs, the interest is taxable on an accrual basis each financial year, even if you receive the entire amount only at maturity. You must declare the accrued interest annually in your Income Tax Return.

What happens if I don't provide PAN to the bank for my FD?

If you do not provide your PAN to the bank, TDS on your FD interest will be deducted at a higher rate of 20%, instead of the standard 10%, once the interest crosses the threshold.
