Pre-Market Brief — 08 October 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets closed significantly lower overnight, with Asian peers also trading in the red this morning, setting a cautious tone.
  • GIFT Nifty is signalling a notable gap-down open for the Indian benchmark indices, down over 1%.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their aggressive selling spree, offloading ₹6,121 crore yesterday, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided crucial support.
  • Nifty 50 is just 100 points away from its 52-week low, facing pressure from persistent FII selling, rising crude oil prices, and RBI's policy tightening.
  • Key Nifty support to watch today is around the 22,000-22,070 zone, where significant Put Open Interest and technical support levels converge.
  • The day's main risks include surging Brent crude prices (+4%), continued FII outflows, and overall weak global sentiment.

Global markets closed on a negative note yesterday, with major US indices registering declines. The selling pressure extended to Asian markets this morning, with Japan's Nikkei and Hong Kong's Hang Seng falling sharply. This broad-based global weakness is likely to weigh heavily on Indian market sentiment at the open.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee depreciated against the US Dollar, with USD/INR rising by 0.43% to 96.79. The Dollar Index also saw a slight uptick. Crude oil prices surged significantly, with Brent Crude jumping 4.00% to $104.21 and WTI Crude rising 3.98% to $91.79. Gold saw a modest gain, while Silver declined. The US 10-year Treasury yield edged higher to 5.28%.

Interpretation: A rising USD/INR typically provides a tailwind for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters, as their dollar-denominated revenues translate into more rupees. However, it also increases the cost for importers and could fuel inflationary pressures. The sharp increase in crude oil prices is a significant negative for India, a major oil importer, potentially impacting sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paints, and aviation due to higher input costs. Rising US 10-year yields can make emerging markets like India less attractive to foreign investors, posing a risk to FII flows.

GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling

The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 22289.00, down 1.19%.

Interpretation: This substantial decline in GIFT Nifty indicates a significant gap-down opening for the Nifty 50 on the NSE today, aligning with the broader negative global sentiment.

FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers in the Indian equity market yesterday, offloading shares worth ₹6,121 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, continued to provide strong support, buying equities worth ₹4,597 crore. This trend of FII selling being largely absorbed by DII buying has been consistent over the last five trading sessions.

Corporate actions today

  • BHAGYANGR (Demerger): Today is the ex-date for the demerger. Shareholders will receive shares in the new entity, and the share price of BHAGYANGR will adjust downwards to reflect the value of the demerged business.
  • SHARDUL (Buy Back): Today is the ex-date for the share buyback. This means that to be eligible to participate in the buyback offer, investors must have purchased shares by yesterday. The buyback typically aims to reduce the number of outstanding shares, which can boost earnings per share.
  • BUILDPRO (Face Value Split (Sub-Division) - From Rs 10/- Per Share To Rs 2/- Per Share): Today is the ex-date for the stock split. For every one share held with a face value of ₹10, shareholders will now hold five shares, each with a face value of ₹2. While the number of shares increases, the total value of the investment remains the same, and the share price will adjust downwards proportionally.

    • The Reserve Bank of India has increased its key policy rate by 25 basis points to 5.50%, signaling a cautious monetary tightening approach in response to global economic factors. Why it matters: The RBI's rate hike implies higher borrowing costs for companies and individuals, which can impact economic growth and corporate profitability. It also influences bond yields and overall liquidity in the financial system, making debt instruments more attractive relative to equities for some investors.

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  • Global Crude Oil Prices: The significant jump in Brent Crude prices is a key event to watch today. Historically, rising crude prices are a negative for Indian markets due to import dependency, often leading to concerns about inflation, current account deficit, and pressure on sectors like OMCs, aviation, and paints.
  • FII/DII Investment Flows: The daily FII and DII flow data will be crucial. Continued heavy FII selling can exert sustained downward pressure, while strong DII buying can provide a floor. Markets typically react with volatility and follow the net flow direction.
  • Nifty's Proximity to 52-Week Low: The Nifty 50 being just 100 points away from its 52-week low is a significant psychological and technical level. A breach could trigger panic selling, while a bounce from this zone could signal a temporary relief rally. Markets often show heightened volatility around such critical levels.
  • Global Market Sentiment: The performance of US and Asian markets throughout the day will continue to influence Indian indices. Any further deterioration in global sentiment could exacerbate domestic selling.
  • Impact of RBI Policy Tightening: While the rate hike has occurred, its full impact on credit growth, corporate earnings, and inflation will continue to be digested by the market over the coming days and weeks. Central bank policy actions typically lead to increased volatility as markets adjust expectations.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Nifty's 22,000-22,070 Support Zone: Monitor if the Nifty 50 holds above its S1 technical level of 22069.75 and the highest Put OI strike of 22000. A break below this could signal further downside.
  2. GIFT Nifty's Implied Gap-Down: Observe how the market reacts to the expected gap-down open indicated by GIFT Nifty. Initial volatility is likely.
  3. Crude Oil Price Movement: Keep an eye on Brent crude prices. Any further significant rise could intensify inflationary concerns and pressure specific sectors.
  4. FII vs. DII Flows: The balance between FII selling and DII buying will be critical. Sustained DII support is essential to prevent a steeper decline.
  5. Performance of Global Peers: Track how other Asian and European markets trade through the day, as their performance will influence sentiment.
  6. Sectoral Impact of News: Watch for specific stock reactions to news, such as Paytm's continued performance or any ripple effects in the fintech space.

Bottom line

Indian markets are poised for a challenging open, heavily influenced by negative global cues, a significant surge in crude oil prices, and persistent FII selling. The Nifty 50 is precariously close to its 52-week low, making the 22,000-22,070 zone a critical support level to watch. While DIIs have provided a cushion, the overall sentiment remains cautious, and volatility is expected.
