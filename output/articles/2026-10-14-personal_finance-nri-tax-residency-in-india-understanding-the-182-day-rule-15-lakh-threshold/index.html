NRI Tax Residency in India: Understanding the 182-Day Rule, ₹15 Lakh Threshold

TL;DR

  • Indian tax residency for individuals is primarily determined by physical stay in India during a financial year.
  • The standard Non-Resident Indian (NRI) status requires staying in India for less than 182 days in a financial year.
  • For Indian citizens or Persons of Indian Origin (PIOs) with Indian income exceeding ₹15 lakh, a shorter 120-day stay rule can trigger tax residency.
  • A "deemed residency" rule applies to Indian citizens with Indian income over ₹15 lakh who are not liable to tax in any other country.
  • Even if deemed resident, such individuals are typically classified as Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR), limiting Indian tax to Indian-sourced income.

What this means in plain terms

If you're an Indian citizen or a Person of Indian Origin (PIO) living abroad, your tax status in India isn't just about your passport or where you primarily reside. The Indian Income Tax Act, 1961, has specific rules, primarily based on how many days you spend in India during a financial year (April 1 to March 31), and in some cases, the amount of income you earn in India.

Understanding these rules is crucial because your tax residency status determines what income is taxable in India. A Non-Resident Indian (NRI) is generally taxed only on income earned or received in India, while a Resident Indian is taxed on their worldwide income. Recent amendments, especially from the Finance Act, 2020, have tightened these rules, particularly for high-income individuals and those residing in countries with no or low income tax.

Determining Your Tax Residency: The Day Count Matters

The Income Tax Act, 1961, lays down the conditions to determine an individual's residential status for tax purposes. This status is assessed for each financial year.

The 182-Day Rule: The Primary Test

The most common and fundamental test for NRI status is the 182-day rule. An individual is considered a Resident in India if they stay in India for 182 days or more during a financial year. Conversely, if your total physical presence in India is 181 days or less in a financial year, you generally qualify as a Non-Resident Indian (NRI). This rule applies to all individuals.

Special Provisions: The 120-Day Rule for High-Income NRIs

The Finance Act, 2020, introduced a significant change for Indian citizens or Persons of Indian Origin (PIOs) visiting India. If your total income from Indian sources (excluding foreign income) exceeds ₹15 lakh during the financial year, the 182-day threshold is relaxed. In such cases, you will be considered a Resident if you meet either of these conditions:

  1. You are in India for 182 days or more during the financial year.
  2. You are in India for 120 days or more during the financial year and have been in India for 365 days or more in the four financial years immediately preceding the current one.

This means that for high-income NRIs, the "safe limit" for staying in India without becoming a resident can drop from 182 days to 119 days (i.e., less than 120 days) if their Indian-sourced income is over ₹15 lakh and they meet the 365-day condition over the preceding four years.

The ₹15 Lakh Threshold and Deemed Residency

The ₹15 lakh income threshold plays a critical role in two key aspects of NRI tax residency:

Triggering the 120-Day Rule

As mentioned, if an Indian citizen or PIO has Indian-sourced income (excluding foreign income) exceeding ₹15 lakh, the 120-day rule can apply, potentially making them a tax resident even with a shorter stay. Income from foreign sources specifically excludes income that accrues or arises outside India, except for income derived from a business controlled in or a profession set up in India.

The "Deemed Residency" Provision (Section 6(1A))

Another crucial amendment by the Finance Act, 2020, introduced the concept of "deemed residency" under Section 6(1A) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. This rule targets Indian citizens who, due to their domicile or residence, are not liable to tax in any other country or territory. An Indian citizen will be deemed a Resident in India if:

  1. Their total income, other than income from foreign sources, exceeds ₹15 lakh during the financial year.
  2. They are not liable to tax in any other country or territory by reason of their domicile, residence, or any other similar criteria.

Crucially, this deemed residency rule applies irrespective of the number of days the individual spends in India. It primarily impacts Indian citizens residing in zero or low-tax jurisdictions (e.g., some Gulf countries) who have significant Indian-sourced income.

Resident But Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR) Status

If an individual becomes a resident under the 120-day rule or the deemed residency rule, they are often categorised as a "Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident" (RNOR). This status provides a significant advantage: an RNOR is generally taxed in India only on their Indian-sourced income and income derived from a business controlled in or a profession set up in India. Their foreign income, such as salary from an overseas job or rent from foreign property, remains exempt from Indian tax.

ITR Exemption Conditions for NRIs

While NRIs are generally required to file an Income Tax Return (ITR) if their taxable income in India exceeds the basic exemption limit (currently ₹2.50 lakh), there are specific conditions under which an NRI might be exempt from filing.

Exemption for Specific Investment Income

Section 115G of the Income Tax Act, 1961, provides an exemption from filing an ITR for NRIs if their total income in India consists only of:

  • Investment income (e.g., interest, dividends)
  • Long-term capital gains from specified assets (e.g., shares, mutual funds, property)
  • Or both.

This exemption is conditional on the tax due on such income having been deducted at source (TDS). If all tax liabilities on these specific income types are covered by TDS, and the NRI has no other taxable income in India, they might not need to file an ITR.

Exempt Income for NRIs

Certain incomes are specifically exempt from tax for NRIs, and therefore do not contribute to their taxable income or filing requirement:

  • Interest on NRE and FCNR Accounts: Interest earned on Non-Resident External (NRE) accounts and Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR) accounts is fully exempt from tax for NRIs. This exemption is crucial for Gulf-based NRIs.
  • Income from foreign sources: As an NRI or RNOR, income earned or received outside India is generally not taxable in India.

However, interest on NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) accounts is taxable in India.

A real example

Priya, 42, ₹60L CTC (Dubai), Bengaluru

Priya is an Indian citizen working in Dubai with an annual CTC of ₹60 lakh (converted from AED). She also owns a flat in Bengaluru which she rents out, generating an annual income of ₹6 lakh. In the financial year 2025-26, she visited India for a total of 140 days. In the preceding four financial years (2021-22 to 2024-25), her total stay in India was 400 days.

Let's determine Priya's tax residency status for FY 2025-26:

  1. Indian-sourced income: Priya's rental income from Bengaluru is ₹6 lakh. Her Dubai salary is foreign income.

    2. Since her Indian-sourced income (₹6 lakh) is less* than ₹15 lakh, the special 120-day rule for high-income NRIs does not apply to her.

  1. Applying the standard residency tests:

    2. * 182-day rule: Priya stayed in India for 140 days. This is less than 182 days. * 60-day rule (with relaxation for Indian citizens visiting India): For Indian citizens visiting India, the 60-day threshold is extended to 182 days. Since Priya is an Indian citizen visiting India, and her Indian income is below ₹15 lakh, the 182-day rule is the applicable threshold for her. Her stay of 140 days is less than 182 days.

  1. Deemed Residency (Section 6(1A)): Priya is an Indian citizen with Indian income of ₹6 lakh (less than ₹15 lakh). Even though she might not be liable to tax in Dubai on her global income (as Dubai has no personal income tax), the ₹15 lakh Indian income threshold for deemed residency is not met.

Conclusion: Based on the above, Priya's stay of 140 days in India, coupled with her Indian income being below ₹15 lakh, means she does not meet any of the conditions to be considered a Resident. Therefore, for FY 2025-26, Priya will be classified as a Non-Resident Indian (NRI).

Tax implication: As an NRI, Priya will only be taxed on her Indian-sourced income, which is the ₹6 lakh rental income. Her ₹60 lakh Dubai salary will not be taxable in India.

What to do this week

  1. Track Your Days: Maintain a meticulous record of your entry and exit dates to and from India. This is the most fundamental step for determining your tax residency.
  2. Estimate Indian Income: Calculate your expected Indian-sourced income for the current financial year. This includes rental income, interest from NRO accounts, capital gains from Indian assets, etc.
  3. Review Previous Years' Stay: If your Indian income is approaching or exceeds ₹15 lakh, check your cumulative stay in India over the past four financial years. This is crucial for the 120-day rule.
  4. Understand Deemed Residency Risk: If you are an Indian citizen residing in a country with no or low income tax and your Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh, understand the implications of the deemed residency rule.
  5. Consult a Professional: If your situation is complex, or you are close to the day-count or income thresholds, consult a Certified Financial Planner or a tax advisor specialising in NRI taxation.

FAQ

What is the 182-day rule for NRI tax residency?

The 182-day rule is the primary test. An individual is considered a tax resident in India if they stay in India for 182 days or more during a financial year (April 1 to March 31). If they stay for less than 182 days, they are generally classified as a Non-Resident Indian (NRI).

How does the ₹15 lakh income threshold affect NRI residency?

For Indian citizens or Persons of Indian Origin (PIOs) visiting India, if their Indian-sourced income (excluding foreign income) exceeds ₹15 lakh in a financial year, the standard 182-day rule can be replaced by a 120-day rule. They become resident if they stay 120 days or more in the current year AND 365 days or more in the preceding four years.

What is "deemed residency" for NRIs?

Deemed residency applies to an Indian citizen whose Indian-sourced income (excluding foreign income) exceeds ₹15 lakh and who is not liable to tax in any other country due to domicile, residence, or similar criteria. Such an individual is deemed a resident of India, regardless of their physical stay.

Is foreign income taxable for a "deemed resident"?

No. An individual who is a "deemed resident" is automatically classified as a Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR). As an RNOR, only their Indian-sourced income and income from a business controlled in or profession set up in India are taxable in India; their other foreign income is exempt.

Are NRIs exempt from filing Income Tax Returns (ITR) in India?

An NRI may be exempt from filing an ITR if their total income in India consists only of investment income or long-term capital gains, or both, and the tax due on such income has been fully deducted at source (TDS).

Is interest from NRE/FCNR accounts taxable in India for NRIs?

No, interest earned on Non-Resident External (NRE) accounts and Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR) accounts is fully exempt from income tax for NRIs.
