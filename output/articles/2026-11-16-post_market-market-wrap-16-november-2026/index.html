Market Wrap — 16 November 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed higher, while broader markets saw profit booking.
  • Strong FII buying was a key driver for the main indices, despite mixed global cues.
  • Belrise Industries Ltd. emerged as the top gainer in the Nifty 500 pack.
  • Kaynes Technology India Ltd. was the biggest loser among Nifty 500 stocks.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net buyers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers.
  • F&O build-up data for today is unavailable and will populate from the next session.

How the market behaved today

Indian equities witnessed a mixed trading session today, with frontline indices Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing in positive territory, while the broader market indices, Nifty Midcap 100 and Nifty Smallcap 100, experienced declines. The Nifty 50 closed at 25291.10, up by 0.58%, after trading in a range between 25172.10 and 25337.60. The Bank Nifty also showed strength, ending at 61575.90, gaining 0.89%, with its day's high at 61712.80 and low at 61213.20. The day saw a relatively positive start for the benchmarks, which largely maintained their gains through the session, though mid and small caps faced selling pressure.

What moved the market — the factors

Today's market action was primarily driven by a combination of sustained Foreign Institutional Investor (FII) buying and a cautious sentiment in the broader market, influenced by global cues. The consistent inflow of FII funds, amounting to ₹5,737 crore today, provided strong support to the benchmark Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, pushing them higher. This FII liquidity seems to be concentrated in large-cap stocks, explaining the divergence from the mid and small-cap indices, which saw declines of 0.51% and 1.10% respectively.

Globally, there were concerns emanating from the US markets, particularly a selloff in AI firms and news regarding Nvidia's leadership change, which led to a slide in the Nasdaq. This broader tech sector weakness in the US might have contributed to the cautious mood in Indian mid and small-cap segments, as investors possibly booked profits in riskier assets. Additionally, Barclays lowering its 2027 year-end target for the S&P 500 index to 8,700 also reflected a more conservative global outlook.

Domestically, sector rotation was evident. Defensive sectors like FMCG (+1.87%) and banking stocks (Bank Nifty +0.89%, PSU Bank +0.50%) performed well, indicating a flight to quality amidst global uncertainties. Conversely, sectors like Pharma (-1.53%), Metal (-1.50%), and Realty (-1.34%) faced selling pressure, suggesting profit booking after recent rallies or a shift away from cyclical sectors.

Sectors — winners and losers

Top 3 gainer sectors:

  • FMCG: +1.87% - This defensive sector saw strong buying interest, possibly as investors sought stability amidst broader market volatility and global uncertainties.
  • BANK: +0.89% - Banking stocks, particularly those in the Bank Nifty, benefited from FII inflows and relatively stable domestic economic outlook.
  • PSU BANK: +0.50% - Public Sector Banks also contributed to the banking sector's gains, indicating broad-based strength in financial services.

Top 3 loser sectors:

  • PHARMA: -1.53% - The pharmaceutical sector witnessed significant selling pressure, potentially due to profit booking or a shift in investor preference.
  • METAL: -1.50% - Metal stocks declined, possibly reacting to global commodity price movements or concerns about industrial demand.
  • REALTY: -1.34% - The realty sector faced headwinds, indicating a cautious approach from investors towards interest-rate sensitive segments.

Market breadth

The market breadth for the day was negative, with 176 advances and 319 declines out of a total of 495 stocks traded. This indicates that while benchmark indices gained, the broader market saw more stocks falling than rising, reflecting underlying caution and profit booking in mid and small-cap segments.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their buying spree today, injecting a net of ₹5,737 crore into the Indian equity market. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers, offloading equities worth ₹2,254 crore.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's session was primarily driven by broad market flows, particularly the strong buying interest from Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) in large-cap stocks. While there wasn't a single defining domestic event, global cues, especially the weakness in US tech stocks following news around Nvidia's leadership and broader AI sector concerns, contributed to the cautious sentiment in the broader Indian market. This led to a divergence where Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty gained, supported by FII inflows, while mid and small-cap indices faced selling pressure. Sector-specific movements also indicated a rotation towards defensive sectors like FMCG and banking.

Setup for tomorrow

Looking ahead, global cues will continue to play a significant role. The weakness in US markets, particularly the tech sector, and the downward revision of the S&P 500 target by Barclays, suggest a cautious global sentiment. Crude oil prices also saw a significant decline today, with Brent Crude down 3.59% and WTI Crude down 3.41%, which could be a positive for India as an oil importer, potentially easing inflationary pressures. The Dollar Index saw a slight uptick, while the USD/INR closed marginally lower. Investors will be watching for any further developments from the US markets overnight and commodity price movements. Key levels for Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty will be important to monitor, alongside the performance of sectors sensitive to global sentiment and commodity prices.
