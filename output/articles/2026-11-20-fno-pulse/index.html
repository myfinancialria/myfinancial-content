Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options contracts provides a window into the collective positioning of market participants, especially option writers (sellers). The strike prices with the highest Call Open Interest are often seen as significant resistance levels, as many writers believe the index will not rise above these points. Conversely, the strike prices with the highest Put Open Interest are typically viewed as strong support levels, where writers expect the index to hold firm. These act as "walls" that define the market's expected trading range.

For the NIFTY 50, the heaviest Call OI is observed at 26,000, acting as a potential ceiling just 0.8% above today's spot. On the Put side, the 24,500 strike holds the most OI, indicating a strong foundational support much further below. Today's fresh Call writing was concentrated near 26,200, suggesting that new sellers anticipate the index will struggle to move past these elevated levels. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 25,616, implying that some participants expect this level to act as a floor.

The SENSEX saw its thickest Call OI at 84,500, a mere 0.7% above its closing price, suggesting immediate resistance. The strongest Put OI wall stands at 81,500. Today, fresh Call writing was most prominent around 84,666, reinforcing the idea of a cap on upside. New Put writing was significant near 82,333, indicating that some market participants are comfortable selling puts at these levels, expecting them to hold.

For BANK NIFTY, the 65,000 strike has accumulated the highest Call OI, forming a close resistance just 0.6% from spot. The 63,000 strike holds the most Put OI, providing a solid base. Today's heaviest fresh Call writing was seen near 65,833, aligning with the view that the index may find it challenging to sustain moves much higher. Conversely, significant fresh Put writing occurred near 63,833, suggesting confidence in these levels as support. Notably, the aggregate flow for Bank Nifty showed a net build of 5.9 million Put OI contracts versus 2.3 million Call OI contracts today, indicating a stronger conviction from Put writers.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that reflects the overall sentiment of option writers. It's calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest (PCR(OI)), or by dividing the fresh Put writing by fresh Call writing for the day (PCR(today flow ΔOI)). A PCR above 1 generally suggests that more puts are being written than calls, often interpreted as a bullish or supportive sentiment from option writers. A PCR below 1 indicates more calls are being written, which can suggest a bearish or resistive sentiment.

The NIFTY 50 ended with a PCR(OI) of 1.38, which is above 1, indicating that overall Put Open Interest significantly dominates Call Open Interest. This suggests that option writers have built substantial support for the index. However, today's flow ΔPCR was -162.71 (a negative value indicates a shift towards more Call writing relative to Put writing today), suggesting a mixed or balanced flow for the day, despite the overall put-heavy book.

For the SENSEX, the PCR(OI) stands at 1.14, also above 1, pointing to a balanced to slightly supportive overall options book. Today's flow ΔPCR was 1.54, which is strong, indicating that fresh Put writing significantly outweighed fresh Call writing today. This suggests that new positions are leaning towards expecting support.

The BANK NIFTY recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.97, which is just below 1, implying a relatively balanced options book in terms of overall open interest. However, its today's flow ΔPCR was a robust 2.56, indicating that today's fresh writing was heavily skewed towards puts. This suggests that despite the balanced overall book, new money entering the market today was more inclined to sell puts, anticipating support.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is a concept in options trading that refers to the strike price at which option sellers (writers) would collectively incur the minimum financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that specific level. As expiry approaches, there's often an observed "magnet effect" where the index tends to gravitate towards this Max Pain strike. This is because option writers, who are typically large institutional players, have a vested interest in the index expiring at a level that minimizes their collective losses.

For the NIFTY 50, the Max Pain strike is currently at 25,350, which is approximately 1.7% below its spot closing price. This indicates a potential downward pull from the Max Pain level.

The SENSEX has its Max Pain strike at 83,100, about 1.0% below its current spot. This also suggests a downward bias from the Max Pain perspective.

The BANK NIFTY shows a Max Pain strike of 63,900, which is around 1.1% below its spot price. This implies a similar downward magnetic pull for the banking index as well.

It's important to remember that the influence of Max Pain becomes more pronounced as the weekly or monthly expiry date draws closer.

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into institutional sentiment.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 34.1%, with 75,864 long contracts against 146,318 short contracts. A long ratio below 50% indicates that FIIs are net short in index futures. Historically, a net short position from FIIs in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or slightly bearish outlook from these large institutional players on the broader market.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of 57.9%, with 3,673,303 long contracts versus 2,671,683 short contracts. This indicates a net long position in individual stock futures, suggesting a more positive or selective stance on specific stocks.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market dynamics can empower you to interpret daily F&O data independently. You can access Open Interest data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, or through your broker's trading platform.

To start, look for the "Option Chain" for NIFTY, SENSEX, or BANK NIFTY. Identify the strike prices with the highest Call Open Interest (on the right side of the chain) and the highest Put Open Interest (on the left side). These are your potential resistance and support "walls." Next, observe the "Change in Open Interest" column for both Calls and Puts. Significant increases in Call OI at strikes above the current spot suggest fresh resistance, while increases in Put OI at strikes below spot indicate fresh support. You can also calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI to gauge overall sentiment. By tracking these key data points daily, you can begin to form your own informed perspective on market positioning.
