Hindalco Q4 Results: Profit Dips 31.6% to ₹2,597 Cr; ₹5 Dividend

TL;DR

  • Hindalco Industries reported a consolidated net profit of ₹2,597 crore for the fourth quarter of FY24.
  • This represents a 31.6% year-on-year decline from the ₹3,787 crore reported in Q4 FY23.
  • Consolidated revenue saw a marginal increase of 0.6% to ₹78,133 crore compared to ₹77,559 crore in the same period last year.
  • The profit dip was primarily attributed to higher interest expenses, increased depreciation, and a one-time charge related to a fire incident at its US subsidiary, Novelis.
  • The company's board has recommended a final dividend of ₹5 per equity share for the financial year 2023-24.

What this means in plain terms

When a large company like Hindalco, a major player in the metals industry, announces its quarterly results, it offers a snapshot of its financial health and operational efficiency. For the quarter ending March 31, 2024 (Q4 FY24), Hindalco reported a consolidated net profit of ₹2,597 crore. While this is a significant sum, it marks a substantial drop of 31.6% compared to the ₹3,787 crore profit it made in the same quarter last year.

This decline in profit, despite a slight increase in overall revenue to ₹78,133 crore, indicates that the company faced higher costs or one-time expenses that ate into its earnings. Specifically, the company cited increased interest payments on its borrowings, higher depreciation on its assets, and a particular expense related to a fire at its US-based subsidiary, Novelis. For investors and market watchers, these results highlight the challenges Hindalco faced in managing its expenses and the impact of unforeseen events on its bottom line, even as its core business continued to generate revenue.

Decoding the Headline Numbers

Hindalco's Q4 FY24 results present a mixed picture, with a notable decline in consolidated net profit contrasting with a slight uptick in revenue. Understanding these figures requires looking beyond the headline and examining the underlying components.

Consolidated Performance Overview

For the fourth quarter of the financial year 2023-24, Hindalco reported a consolidated net profit of ₹2,597 crore. This figure is crucial as "consolidated" means it includes the performance of all its subsidiaries, most notably the US-based aluminium rolling and recycling giant, Novelis. When compared to the ₹3,787 crore net profit recorded in Q4 FY23, this represents a significant year-on-year decline of 31.6%.

On the revenue front, the company posted a consolidated revenue of ₹78,133 crore for Q4 FY24, which is a marginal increase of 0.6% from ₹77,559 crore in the corresponding quarter of the previous fiscal year. This indicates that while the company's sales volume or pricing might have held steady or improved slightly, the costs associated with generating that revenue, or other financial expenses, escalated considerably. The divergence between revenue growth and profit decline is a key area of focus for analysts, pointing towards margin pressures or exceptional items impacting profitability.

Standalone vs. Novelis Contribution

To get a clearer picture, it's essential to differentiate between Hindalco's standalone performance (primarily its Indian operations) and the contribution from Novelis. Hindalco's standalone net profit for Q4 FY24 stood at ₹1,364 crore, showing a modest increase of 1.6% year-on-year. Standalone revenue also saw a marginal rise of 1.9% to ₹20,581 crore. This suggests that the Indian operations maintained a relatively stable, albeit slow, growth trajectory.

In contrast, Novelis, Hindalco's global rolled products leader, reported a net income of $124 million (approximately ₹1,035 crore at an exchange rate of ₹83.5/$) for Q4 FY24, an increase of 2% year-on-year. Its adjusted EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortisation) was $486 million, up 11% year-on-year. While Novelis's operational performance, as indicated by EBITDA, showed strength, the consolidated profit decline implies that the higher interest expenses, depreciation, and the specific one-time charge related to the fire incident at Novelis significantly impacted the overall group's net profit. This highlights the substantial influence of Novelis on Hindalco's consolidated financial results.

Factors Impacting Profitability

The primary drivers behind Hindalco's consolidated net profit decline are multifaceted, stemming from both operational challenges and financial overheads.

The Novelis Effect

A significant contributor to the dip in consolidated net profit was a one-time charge incurred due to a fire incident at one of Novelis's facilities in the United States. Such incidents, while often covered by insurance, can lead to immediate expenses for damage assessment, repair, business interruption, and other related costs that are typically booked in the quarter they occur. Even if insurance eventually covers a portion, the initial accounting impact can be substantial, directly reducing net profit. This highlights the vulnerability of large-scale industrial operations to unforeseen events and their immediate financial consequences.

Rising Costs and Financial Overheads

Beyond the Novelis incident, Hindalco also reported higher interest expenses and increased depreciation. Higher interest expenses suggest that the company either took on more debt, or the cost of its existing floating-rate debt increased due to rising interest rates in the global economy. This is a common challenge for capital-intensive industries that rely on significant borrowings for expansion and operations. Every rupee spent on interest directly reduces the profit available to shareholders.

Similarly, higher depreciation indicates that the company might have invested in new assets or that older assets are being depreciated at a faster rate. While depreciation is a non-cash expense, it reduces taxable income and, consequently, reported net profit. In essence, while the company's core operations might be performing, the financial architecture and external events have created headwinds, eroding the bottom line.

Dividend and Shareholder Returns

Despite the consolidated profit decline, Hindalco's board has recommended a final dividend of ₹5 per equity share for FY24. This decision reflects the company's commitment to shareholder returns, even in a challenging quarter.

Sustaining Shareholder Payouts

A dividend declaration signifies that the company believes it has sufficient distributable profits and a healthy cash flow to reward its shareholders. For investors, dividends represent a direct return on their investment, providing regular income. The ₹5 per share dividend, while lower than some previous years, still demonstrates the company's financial discipline and its intent to share its success, or at least maintain a consistent payout, with its investors. This can be particularly appealing to long-term investors seeking stable income streams from their equity holdings. The payment of this dividend is subject to approval by shareholders at the upcoming Annual General Meeting (AGM).

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 42, a software engineer with ₹35L CTC, based in Hyderabad, who holds 500 shares of Hindalco Industries in her investment portfolio. She bought these shares over time for long-term growth and dividend income.

When Hindalco announced a final dividend of ₹5 per equity share for FY24, Priya would calculate her potential dividend income as follows:

Number of shares held: 500 Dividend per share: ₹5

Total Dividend Income = Number of shares × Dividend per share Total Dividend Income = 500 shares × ₹5/share = ₹2,500

Now, let's consider the taxation aspect. In India, dividends received from domestic companies are taxable in the hands of the shareholder at their applicable income tax slab rates. This system came into effect from April 1, 2020, replacing the Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) which was paid by the company.

Priya falls into the 30% income tax bracket. Her tax liability on this dividend income would be:

Tax on Dividend = Total Dividend Income × Income Tax Slab Rate Tax on Dividend = ₹2,500 × 30% = ₹750

So, out of the ₹2,500 dividend, Priya would effectively receive ₹1,750 after paying tax. This example illustrates how a dividend declaration directly impacts an investor's cash flow and how understanding the current taxation rules is crucial for calculating the actual post-tax return.

FAQ

What is consolidated net profit?

Consolidated net profit represents the total profit of a parent company and all its subsidiaries, treated as a single economic entity. It provides a comprehensive view of the group's overall profitability.

How does a fire incident affect company financials?

A fire incident can lead to immediate financial impacts such as property damage, loss of inventory, business interruption costs, and potential legal liabilities. These are often recorded as one-time charges, reducing the net profit for that period, even if insurance claims are pending.

What does a dividend declaration mean for shareholders?

A dividend declaration means the company's board has approved distributing a portion of its profits to shareholders. For shareholders, it translates to a direct cash payment per share held, providing a return on their investment.

Why is standalone performance different from consolidated?

Standalone performance refers only to the parent company's individual operations, typically its domestic business. Consolidated performance includes the results of all its subsidiaries globally. Differences arise because subsidiaries may have varying operational results, expenses, or accounting treatments.

What is EBITDA?

EBITDA stands for Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortisation. It's a measure of a company's operating performance, often used to compare profitability between companies by removing the effects of financing and accounting decisions.
