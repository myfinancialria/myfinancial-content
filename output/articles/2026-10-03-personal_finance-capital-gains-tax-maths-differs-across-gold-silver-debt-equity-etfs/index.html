Capital Gains Tax Maths Differs Across Gold, Silver, Debt, & Equity ETFs

TL;DR

  • The capital gains tax on ETFs varies significantly based on the underlying asset class and holding period.
  • Equity ETFs enjoy preferential tax treatment for long-term gains, with a ₹1 lakh exemption per financial year.
  • Gold and Silver ETFs, along with Debt ETFs, are now taxed differently for investments made on or after April 1, 2023.
  • Gains from Gold, Silver, and Debt ETFs (for new investments) are now treated as short-term capital gains and taxed at your income tax slab rate.
  • Understanding these differences is crucial for effective tax planning and maximising your investment returns.

What this means in plain terms

When you invest in Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs) and sell them for a profit, that profit is considered a 'capital gain'. The Indian tax laws don't treat all capital gains equally. The amount of tax you pay depends on what type of asset the ETF invests in – whether it's stocks (equity), gold, silver, or bonds (debt) – and for how long you held that ETF before selling.

For instance, if you sell an equity ETF after holding it for over a year, you might pay less tax than if you sold a gold ETF after the same period. This difference in tax calculation, known as 'capital gains tax maths', can significantly impact your net returns. It's important for investors to be aware of these distinctions to make informed decisions and avoid unexpected tax liabilities.

Equity ETFs: The Benefit of Long-Term Holding

Equity ETFs primarily invest in stocks listed on the Indian stock exchanges. Their tax treatment is generally considered favourable for long-term investors.

Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) on Equity ETFs

If you sell your equity ETF units within 12 months of purchase, any profit you make is classified as Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG). This STCG is taxed at a flat rate of 15%, plus applicable surcharge and cess. This rate applies irrespective of your income tax slab.

Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) on Equity ETFs

For equity ETFs held for more than 12 months, the gains are categorised as Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG). Under Section 112A of the Income Tax Act, LTCG from the sale of equity-oriented mutual funds (including equity ETFs) is exempt up to ₹1 lakh in a financial year. Any LTCG exceeding this ₹1 lakh threshold is taxed at a rate of 10%, without the benefit of indexation. Again, applicable surcharge and cess would apply.

Gold & Silver ETFs: The New Tax Landscape

Gold and Silver ETFs are generally structured as non-equity oriented mutual funds. They invest in physical gold or silver or instruments representing their value. Their tax treatment has undergone a significant change for investments made on or after April 1, 2023.

Tax Treatment for Investments made ON or AFTER April 1, 2023

For any investments made in Gold ETFs or Silver ETFs on or after April 1, 2023, the capital gains are now treated as short-term capital gains (STCG) irrespective of the holding period. This means that any profit you make from selling these ETFs will be added to your total income and taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate. The benefit of indexation for long-term holdings has been removed for these categories.

Tax Treatment for Investments made BEFORE April 1, 2023

If you invested in Gold or Silver ETFs before April 1, 2023, the old rules still apply to those specific units.

  • Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG): If held for less than 36 months, gains are added to your income and taxed at your slab rate.
  • Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG): If held for more than 36 months, gains were taxed at 20% after applying the benefit of indexation. Indexation adjusts the purchase price for inflation, thereby reducing the taxable gain. This benefit is no longer available for new investments.

Debt ETFs: Impact of the Finance Act 2023

Debt ETFs primarily invest in fixed-income securities like government bonds, corporate bonds, and other money market instruments. Similar to Gold and Silver ETFs, the tax treatment for Debt ETFs has also been significantly revised by the Finance Act 2023.

Tax Treatment for Investments made ON or AFTER April 1, 2023

For investments made in Debt ETFs on or after April 1, 2023, all capital gains, regardless of the holding period, are now treated as short-term capital gains (STCG). These gains will be added to your total income and taxed at your individual income tax slab rate. The earlier benefit of long-term capital gains with indexation, which applied for holdings over 36 months, has been completely removed for these new investments.

Tax Treatment for Investments made BEFORE April 1, 2023

For Debt ETF units purchased before April 1, 2023, the previous tax rules continue to apply to those specific units.

  • Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG): If held for less than 36 months, gains are added to your income and taxed at your slab rate.
  • Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG): If held for more than 36 months, gains were taxed at 20% after applying the benefit of indexation. This indexation benefit allowed investors to adjust their purchase cost for inflation, thereby reducing the taxable profit.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 35, earning ₹20 lakh per year, based in Mumbai, who falls into the 30% income tax slab (excluding cess and surcharge for simplicity in calculation). She made the following investments and sales:

  1. Equity ETF: Invested ₹2 lakh in an Equity ETF on January 15, 2024, and sold it on January 15, 2025, for ₹2.4 lakh.

    2. * Holding Period: Exactly 12 months. * Gain: ₹40,000. Tax Treatment: This is STCG as the holding period is exactly 12 months (not more than* 12 months). Tax Calculation: ₹40,000 15% = ₹6,000.

  1. Gold ETF (New Regime): Invested ₹1 lakh in a Gold ETF on May 1, 2023, and sold it on May 1, 2025, for ₹1.2 lakh.

    2. * Holding Period: 24 months (2 years). * Gain: ₹20,000. * Tax Treatment: Since the investment was made after April 1, 2023, irrespective of holding period, it's treated as STCG. Tax Calculation: ₹20,000 added to her income. Taxed at her slab rate (30%). So, ₹20,000 30% = ₹6,000.

  1. Debt ETF (Old Regime): Invested ₹1.5 lakh in a Debt ETF on June 1, 2022, and sold it on June 1, 2026, for ₹2 lakh.

    2. * Holding Period: 48 months (4 years). * Gain: ₹50,000. * Tax Treatment: Investment made before April 1, 2023, and held for over 36 months, so LTCG with indexation applies. * Assume Cost Inflation Index (CII) for purchase year (FY 2022-23) is 331 and for sale year (FY 2026-27) is 380 (illustrative). Indexed Cost: ₹1,50,000 (380 / 331) = ₹1,72,205 (approx). * LTCG: ₹2,00,000 - ₹1,72,205 = ₹27,795. Tax Calculation: ₹27,795 20% = ₹5,559.

This example clearly shows how the tax maths differs significantly across various ETF categories and investment timelines.

FAQ

What is the difference between an ETF and a mutual fund for tax purposes?

For tax purposes, ETFs are generally treated similarly to mutual funds, with distinctions primarily based on the underlying asset class (equity, debt, gold, etc.) rather than the fund structure itself.

Do I get indexation benefit for Gold ETFs?

For Gold ETFs purchased on or after April 1, 2023, the indexation benefit for long-term capital gains has been removed. All gains are now taxed at your income slab rate. For units purchased before this date, indexation benefit for LTCG (held over 36 months) still applies.

Is LTCG on equity ETFs completely tax-free?

No, LTCG on equity ETFs is exempt up to ₹1 lakh in a financial year. Gains exceeding this limit are taxed at 10% (plus surcharge and cess), without indexation benefit.

How does the new tax regime affect my existing Debt ETF investments?

The new tax regime (where gains are taxed at slab rates) only applies to Debt ETF units purchased on or after April 1, 2023. Your existing investments made before this date will continue to be governed by the old rules, including indexation benefit for LTCG.

What is the holding period for LTCG in equity ETFs?

For equity ETFs, the holding period to qualify for Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) is more than 12 months.

Are there any specific exemptions for NRI investors on ETF capital gains?

Taxation for NRIs on capital gains from ETFs generally follows the same rules as resident Indians, but Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAA) with their country of residence might offer some relief. It's advisable for NRIs to consult a tax expert.
