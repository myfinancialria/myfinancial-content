Your Digital Life Just Got a Boost: Understanding India's Push for Digital Public Infrastructure

TL;DR

  • India is investing more in Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI), which are shared digital systems.
  • DPI includes familiar tools like Aadhaar for identity and UPI for payments, making digital services easier.
  • The government recently launched a fund to help other countries build their own DPI, showcasing India's success.
  • This push aims to expand digital access to more areas like health, education, and business for everyone.
  • The goal is to strengthen India's digital economy and make public and private services more efficient and inclusive.

What's this about?

Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) refers to foundational digital systems and platforms that allow many different services to be built and offered to people. Think of them as the basic digital "roads" and "utilities" that governments and private companies can use to deliver services efficiently. In India, these include systems like Aadhaar for digital identity, the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) for instant payments, and DigiLocker for secure document storage. These are designed to be open, interoperable (meaning they can work together), and accessible to a large number of people.

Why it's in the news right now

Just recently, on September 19, 2026, the Indian Prime Minister announced a $25 million (around ₹200 crore) Social Impact Fund at the Virtual G20 Summit. This fund is specifically for helping other developing nations, often called "Global South" countries, to build and expand their own Digital Public Infrastructure. This move highlights India's leadership in DPI and its commitment to sharing its successful models, like Aadhaar and UPI, with the world. It also reflects the ongoing focus on expanding and evolving India's own DPI, with discussions around moving towards "AI public infrastructure" in the coming years.

How it actually works

  1. Foundational Identity: At its core, DPI often starts with a digital identity system. In India, this is Aadhaar, which provides a unique 12-digit number to residents after biometric verification (like fingerprints and iris scans). This allows for secure and instant digital authentication.
  2. Seamless Payments: Built on top of digital identity are payment systems. UPI is a prime example, allowing instant, bank-to-bank money transfers using just a mobile phone number or QR code. It makes transactions fast, secure, and low-cost.
  3. Secure Document Exchange: Systems like DigiLocker enable citizens to securely store and share their official documents and certificates digitally. This reduces the need for physical paperwork and makes verification easier.
  4. Open Networks for Services: Beyond identity and payments, DPI is expanding to create open networks for various services. For instance, the Open Network for Digital Commerce (ONDC) aims to democratise e-commerce by allowing buyers and sellers to connect across different platforms. Similarly, the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) is building a digital health infrastructure.
  5. Interoperability: A key feature of DPI is that these different systems are designed to talk to each other. This means, for example, your Aadhaar can be used to open a bank account, which then allows you to use UPI. This interconnectedness makes services more efficient.
  6. Government's Role: The government plays a crucial role in building and maintaining these open, non-proprietary digital platforms. This ensures they are accessible to everyone and foster innovation by both public and private sectors.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine your city or village. It has basic physical infrastructure like roads, electricity lines, and water pipes. These are not owned by one private company; they are shared resources that everyone uses to travel, get power, and access water.

Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) is exactly like this, but for the digital world. Aadhaar is like your unique house number, making it easy to identify you. UPI is like a fast, free road for money, letting it travel instantly between different banks. DigiLocker is like a secure digital cupboard for all your important documents. The government is building more of these digital "roads" and "utilities" for health, education, and business, so everyone can connect and access services easily.

Why it matters to you

  1. Easier Access to Services: You can access government schemes, banking, healthcare, and other services much faster and often without needing to visit physical offices or fill out endless forms. For example, opening a bank account or getting a subsidy can become simpler.
  2. Reduced Costs: Digital transactions are generally cheaper than cash or traditional methods. This can save you money on fees and also reduce costs for businesses, potentially leading to more affordable goods and services.
  3. More Choices and Competition: Platforms like ONDC can connect you to a wider range of sellers and service providers, potentially giving you more options and better prices for products and services.
  4. Financial Inclusion: DPI helps bring more people into the formal economy. Even those in remote areas or without traditional bank accounts can access digital banking and payment services, helping them save, borrow, and transact more easily.
  5. New Opportunities: The growth of India's digital economy, driven by DPI, can create new jobs in technology, service delivery, and related sectors. Small businesses can also reach more customers through digital platforms.

Common confusions cleared up

  • Myth: Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) is just another name for UPI.

    • * Fact: UPI is a very successful part of India's DPI, but DPI is a much broader concept. It includes digital identity (Aadhaar), document storage (DigiLocker), data sharing frameworks (Account Aggregator), and open networks for e-commerce (ONDC), health (ABDM), and education (DIKSHA).

  • Myth: DPI means the government will have control over all my personal data.

    • * Fact: India's DPI is designed with principles of consent-based data sharing and privacy by design. For instance, the Account Aggregator framework allows you to securely share your financial data with your explicit consent, giving you control over who sees your information.

  • Myth: Only people who are comfortable with technology will benefit from DPI.

    • * Fact: While digital literacy helps, DPI systems are designed to be user-friendly and accessible. The goal is to make digital services so simple that even those with limited tech experience can use them, fostering greater inclusion across all sections of society.

FAQ

What is the main goal of DPI?

The main goal of DPI is to create a robust, open, and interoperable digital foundation that enables equitable access to public and private services at a societal scale, driving inclusion, efficiency, and innovation.

How is DPI funded?

DPI is typically funded by governments, often through budgetary allocations and strategic investments. For example, the Indian Prime Minister recently launched a $25 million Social Impact Fund to support DPI in other countries.

Is my data safe on DPI platforms?

DPI systems are built with security and privacy in mind, often incorporating consent-based data sharing mechanisms and adherence to legal frameworks like the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act.

What are some examples of DPI beyond UPI?

Beyond UPI, key examples of DPI in India include Aadhaar for digital identity, DigiLocker for digital documents, the Account Aggregator framework for consent-based data sharing, and ONDC for open digital commerce.

How does DPI help small businesses?

DPI helps small businesses by providing them with digital tools for payments (UPI), easier access to government procurement (GeM), and wider market reach through open networks like ONDC, reducing costs and increasing opportunities.
