Nifty 100 Streak Ends: Your SIP Strategy for 2026 Volatility

TL;DR

  • The Nifty 100's decade-long winning streak may conclude in 2026, signalling potential market volatility ahead.
  • Instead of panicking, investors should view market corrections as opportunities to accumulate more units at lower prices through SIPs.
  • Continuing your Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs) allows you to benefit from rupee cost averaging, which smooths out returns over time.
  • Reviewing your financial goals, risk tolerance, and asset allocation is crucial to ensure your investment strategy remains aligned.
  • Strategic adjustments like increasing SIPs during dips or rebalancing your portfolio can be considered based on your personal financial situation.

What this means in plain terms

The Nifty 100 index, which represents the 100 largest and most liquid companies listed on the National Stock Exchange (NSE), has enjoyed a remarkable run over the past ten years, consistently delivering positive returns. However, market cycles are a natural phenomenon, and a period of sustained growth is often followed by a correction or consolidation phase. Financial analysts are now suggesting that this winning streak could potentially end in 2026, implying that the market might not offer the same high returns, or could even see a temporary decline.

For you, as an investor, this doesn't necessarily spell doom. Instead, it's a signal to be prepared and understand how market dynamics influence your investments, particularly your mutual fund SIPs. Volatility means prices can fluctuate more significantly, and while this might seem unsettling, it also presents unique opportunities for long-term wealth creation. The key is to avoid impulsive decisions and instead adopt a well-thought-out strategy that aligns with your financial objectives.

Understanding Market Cycles and Volatility

Equity markets inherently operate in cycles, characterised by periods of expansion (bull markets) and contraction (bear markets), interspersed with corrections and consolidations. A "winning streak" like the Nifty 100 has experienced signifies a prolonged bull run, where investor sentiment is largely positive, and asset prices tend to rise consistently. However, no market can go up indefinitely. Factors such as economic slowdowns, changes in interest rates, geopolitical events, or even overvaluation can trigger a correction.

A market correction is typically defined as a decline of 10% or more from a recent peak, while a bear market implies a fall of 20% or more. The anticipation of the Nifty 100's streak ending in 2026 suggests that we might be heading into a period where returns could be subdued or even negative for a while. This volatility, though often perceived negatively, is a normal part of investing. For long-term investors, these periods can be beneficial as they allow for accumulation of assets at lower prices, which can yield substantial returns when the market eventually recovers. Understanding this cyclical nature helps in maintaining a calm perspective and making rational investment decisions rather than reacting emotionally to short-term fluctuations.

The Enduring Power of SIPs: Rupee Cost Averaging

Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs) are a disciplined approach to investing in mutual funds, where a fixed amount is invested at regular intervals, regardless of market conditions. This strategy is particularly powerful during volatile or declining markets due to a concept called rupee cost averaging. When the market is high, your fixed SIP amount buys fewer units of the mutual fund. Conversely, when the market falls, the same fixed amount buys more units.

Over time, this averaging effect helps reduce your average cost per unit, leading to potentially better returns when the market eventually recovers. If the Nifty 100's streak ends and the market enters a corrective phase in 2026, continuing your SIPs means you will be acquiring more units at lower prices. This accumulation of units at reduced costs can significantly boost your overall portfolio value when the market eventually rebounds. Stopping SIPs during a downturn, on the other hand, means you miss out on this crucial opportunity to buy low and average down your costs, potentially hindering your long-term wealth creation. The Association of Mutual Funds in India (AMFI) consistently advocates for the benefits of SIPs for long-term wealth creation.

Reviewing Your Goals and Portfolio Allocation

A potential shift in market dynamics in 2026 makes it an opportune time to revisit your financial goals and current portfolio allocation. Your investment strategy should always be aligned with your specific objectives, whether it's saving for a child's education, a down payment for a house, or retirement. Each goal has a different time horizon and risk tolerance. For instance, a goal five years away might warrant a different asset allocation than a goal twenty years away.

Assessing Risk Tolerance

Consider if your current risk tolerance matches your portfolio's equity exposure. If you are nearing a critical financial goal (e.g., retirement in 2-3 years), you might consider gradually de-risking your portfolio by shifting some equity exposure to less volatile assets like debt funds. However, if your goals are still far away, continuing with your equity-heavy SIPs might still be the optimal strategy.

Rebalancing Your Portfolio

Rebalancing involves adjusting your portfolio back to your target asset allocation. For example, if your target is 60% equity and 40% debt, and a strong bull run has pushed your equity allocation to 75%, you might consider selling some equity and investing in debt to restore the 60:40 ratio. Conversely, if a market correction brings your equity allocation down, you might consider increasing your equity exposure. This disciplined approach helps manage risk and ensures your portfolio remains aligned with your long-term strategy. SEBI regulations guide mutual fund operations and portfolio management, ensuring transparency and investor protection.

Considering Strategic Adjustments to Your SIPs

While continuing SIPs is generally advisable, there are situations where strategic adjustments might make sense, provided they are based on careful analysis and not panic.

Increasing Your SIP Contributions

If your financial situation allows, increasing your SIP amount during a market downturn can significantly accelerate your wealth creation. Since you're buying more units at lower prices, a higher SIP during such periods can amplify the benefits of rupee cost averaging. This is often referred to as "SIP top-up" or "step-up SIP," which many mutual funds offer.

Pausing or Stopping SIPs (with caution)

Pausing a SIP should be a last resort, typically only considered if there's a significant, unforeseen change in your personal financial situation, such as job loss or a medical emergency, that severely impacts your ability to meet current expenses. Stopping SIPs purely due to market volatility is generally counterproductive for long-term investors, as it means missing out on potential recovery. If you must pause, ensure it's a temporary measure and resume as soon as your finances stabilise.

Shifting Funds (only if necessary)

If your risk profile has fundamentally changed, or if your existing funds are consistently underperforming their benchmarks and peers over extended periods (3-5 years), then considering a shift to better-performing funds or a different asset class might be warranted. However, this should always be done after thorough research and ideally, consultation with a financial advisor, rather than reacting to short-term market noise.

A real example

Let's consider Rahul, 38, ₹28L CTC, Bengaluru. Rahul has been investing ₹20,000 per month via SIPs in a Nifty 100 index fund for the past five years. His primary goal is retirement in 22 years.

Assume as of December 2025, his SIPs have accumulated 1,500 units at an average NAV of ₹200, making his total investment value ₹3,00,000 (1,500 units * ₹200).

Now, let's say the Nifty 100's streak ends in 2026, and the market sees a correction. The NAV of his fund drops by 20% to ₹160.

Scenario 1: Rahul stops his SIPs. He stops investing at ₹20,000 per month. His existing investment of ₹3,00,000 would now be worth ₹2,40,000 (1,500 units * ₹160). He misses the opportunity to buy at lower prices.

Scenario 2: Rahul continues his SIPs. He continues his ₹20,000 SIP for the next 12 months (2026).

  • In January 2026, with NAV at ₹160, his ₹20,000 buys 125 units (₹20,000 / ₹160).
  • Let's assume the NAV stays around ₹160-₹170 for the next year. Over 12 months, he invests an additional ₹2,40,000 (₹20,000 * 12).
  • If the average NAV during this period is ₹165, he would accumulate approximately 1,454 units (₹2,40,000 / ₹165).
  • His total units would now be 1,500 (initial) + 1,454 (new) = 2,954 units.
  • His total investment would be ₹3,00,000 (initial) + ₹2,40,000 (new) = ₹5,40,000.
  • His average cost per unit would be ₹5,40,000 / 2,954 units = ₹182.73.

Now, imagine the market recovers in 2027, and the NAV climbs back to ₹220.

  • If Rahul had stopped SIPs (Scenario 1), his 1,500 units would be worth ₹3,30,000 (1,500 units * ₹220).
  • If Rahul continued SIPs (Scenario 2), his 2,954 units would be worth ₹6,49,880 (2,954 units * ₹220).

Despite the initial market dip, continuing his SIPs allowed Rahul to accumulate significantly more units at a lower average cost, leading to a much larger portfolio value upon market recovery. This clearly illustrates the power of rupee cost averaging for long-term goals.

What to do this week

  1. Review your financial goals and timelines: List out all your major financial goals and their respective target dates. This will help you determine if your current investment strategy is still appropriate.
  2. Check your asset allocation: Look at your mutual fund statements and assess your current split between equity, debt, and other asset classes. Compare this to your ideal asset allocation based on your risk tolerance and goals.
  3. Analyse your emergency fund: Ensure you have a robust emergency fund, typically covering 6-12 months of essential expenses, in a liquid savings account or short-term fixed deposits. This buffer prevents you from needing to redeem investments during market downturns.
  4. Consider a SIP top-up: If your income has increased, evaluate if you can comfortably increase your monthly SIP contributions. Even a small increase can make a significant difference over the long term, especially if markets are volatile.
  5. Educate yourself: Spend 30 minutes reading about market cycles and the benefits of long-term investing. Understanding these concepts helps build conviction and reduces the likelihood of making emotional decisions during market corrections.

FAQ

What is the Nifty 100 index?

The Nifty 100 index represents the top 100 companies by market capitalisation listed on the National Stock Exchange (NSE). It is a broad market index reflecting the performance of large and liquid Indian companies.

Should I stop my SIPs if the market falls?

Generally, no. Stopping SIPs during a market fall means you miss the opportunity to buy more units at lower prices, which is crucial for rupee cost averaging and long-term wealth creation.

When does it make sense to increase my SIP amount?

It makes sense to increase your SIP amount if your income has risen and you have a stable financial situation. Increasing SIPs during market corrections can be particularly beneficial as it allows you to accumulate more units when prices are low.

Is rebalancing my portfolio necessary during volatility?

Yes, rebalancing is important to maintain your desired asset allocation. Market volatility can cause your portfolio's equity or debt component to drift significantly from your target, and rebalancing helps bring it back in line with your risk profile.

How does rupee cost averaging benefit my investments?

Rupee cost averaging works by ensuring you buy fewer units when prices are high and more units when prices are low, over time. This strategy helps reduce your average purchase cost per unit, potentially leading to better returns when the market recovers.

Should I switch all my equity funds to debt funds if the market becomes volatile?

Making drastic shifts like moving all equity to debt funds based on short-term market predictions is generally not advisable for long-term investors. Such moves can lead to missing out on market recoveries. Any changes should align with your long-term goals and risk profile.

What role do my financial goals play in my SIP strategy?

Your financial goals dictate your investment horizon and risk tolerance. For long-term goals, continuing equity SIPs through volatility is often appropriate. For short-term goals, you might consider de-risking your portfolio as you approach the goal.
