How the market behaved today

Indian equities witnessed a day of divergence, with the benchmark Nifty 50 closing lower, while the Bank Nifty showed resilience to end in positive territory. The Nifty 50 opened with a slight gap down and remained under pressure for most of the session, making a low of 23231.40 before recovering some ground to close at 23398.10, down 0.34%. In contrast, the Bank Nifty traded with strength, hitting a high of 56645.85 and closing at 56606.55, up 0.24%. The broader market, however, reflected weakness, with both the Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100 indices declining.

What moved the market — the factors

The market's performance today was a blend of domestic policy action and evolving global cues. A key domestic driver was the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) announcement to drain ₹1 lakh crore from the banking system through bond sales, aimed at absorbing excess liquidity. This move, following the failure of Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions to fully absorb liquidity, likely contributed to the cautious sentiment in the broader market.

Globally, initial concerns over higher-than-expected US consumer inflation in August, which rose 3.4% year-on-year primarily due to increasing gasoline prices, sparked fears of a potential interest rate hike by the US Federal Reserve. However, US stocks later rebounded as oil prices eased, helping to stabilise market sentiment towards the end of India's trading session.

FIIs continued their selling streak in the Indian cash market, while DIIs provided crucial buying support, preventing a sharper decline. Sector-specific movements also played a role, with the Realty, Metal, and Auto sectors experiencing significant declines, while Banking, Media, and IT sectors showed some strength. The resilience of the Bank Nifty, despite the RBI's liquidity tightening measures, suggests underlying strength in the banking sector or specific heavyweights.

News that moved stocks

  • RBI's Liquidity Drain Announcement: The Reserve Bank of India announced plans to drain ₹1 lakh crore from the banking system through bond sales this month. This move follows the failure of Variable Rate Reverse Repo (VRRR) auctions to absorb excess liquidity and is aimed at managing surplus liquidity. This news has broad implications for market liquidity and interest rates, potentially impacting all sectors.
  • US Inflation and Global Market Reaction: US consumer prices accelerated in August, rising 3.4% year-on-year, primarily driven by increasing gasoline prices. This data initially fuelled concerns about potential interest rate hikes by the Federal Reserve. However, US stocks later rebounded as oil prices eased, helping to stabilise global market sentiment.
  • Real Estate Sector Developments: Smartworld Developers successfully raised ₹800 crore from Kotak Realty Fund for two housing projects in Gurugram. Separately, SEBI imposed a ₹2 lakh penalty on Emami Realty for regulatory lapses involving a related-party loan and investment classification. These developments, alongside the broader market sentiment, likely contributed to the Realty sector being the top loser today.
  • Oracle's AI-fueled Growth: Oracle shares rose in early US trading after a significant increase in its revenue backlog, driven by AI, eased concerns over its debt-funded AI spending. This positive news from a global tech giant could provide some tailwind for the IT sector.

Market breadth

The market breadth was overwhelmingly negative today, with declines significantly outnumbering advances. Out of 495 stocks traded, 347 declined, while only 148 advanced, indicating broad-based selling pressure across the market.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling trend in the Indian equity market today, recording a net outflow of ₹438 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net buyers, infusing ₹1,026 crore into the market, providing crucial support amidst FII selling.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

The most significant event today was the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) announcement of an Open Market Operation (OMO) sale of ₹1 lakh crore in three tranches to manage surplus liquidity in the banking system. This move, aimed at draining excess funds, signals the RBI's proactive stance on liquidity management and inflation control. While such measures can sometimes lead to tighter credit conditions and impact market sentiment, the immediate reaction saw the Nifty 50 decline, while the Bank Nifty managed to hold its ground, possibly due to specific stock movements or the market having already factored in some liquidity tightening.

Globally, the release of higher-than-expected US consumer inflation data for August initially caused jitters, raising fears of a potential US Fed rate hike. However, a subsequent rebound in US stocks as oil prices eased helped to temper these concerns, preventing a sharper decline in Indian markets. The Realty sector, in particular, reacted negatively, becoming the top losing sector, likely influenced by the broader cautious sentiment and specific news regarding penalties and funding.

Setup for tomorrow

Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will continue to play a significant role. US markets closed higher overnight, with Brent crude oil prices falling by 2.38% to $105.07 and WTI crude down 2.80% to $99.61. This easing of oil prices, coupled with a slight dip in the US 10-year yield to 4.94%, could provide some positive momentum. However, the RBI's announced OMO sales will keep liquidity conditions in focus. Investors will be watching for follow-through in the Realty and Metal sectors, while the resilience of Banking and IT might continue. Key levels for Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty will be crucial to observe for directional cues.
