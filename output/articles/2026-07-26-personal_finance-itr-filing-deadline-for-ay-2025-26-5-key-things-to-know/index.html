ITR Filing Deadline for AY 2025-26: 5 Key Things to Know

TL;DR

  • The primary deadline for filing your Income Tax Return (ITR) for Assessment Year (AY) 2025-26 (Financial Year 2024-25) for most individual taxpayers has been extended to September 16, 2025.
  • Missing this deadline can attract a late filing fee of up to ₹5,000 under Section 234F, along with interest under Section 234A.
  • Filing a belated ITR is possible until December 31, 2025, but it comes with penalties and loss of certain tax benefits.
  • Choosing the correct ITR form (ITR-1, ITR-2, ITR-3, or ITR-4) based on your income sources and total income is crucial to avoid a defective return.
  • Gather essential documents like Form 16, AIS/TIS, bank statements, and investment proofs well in advance for a smooth filing process.

What this means in plain terms

Filing your Income Tax Return (ITR) is an annual obligation for most earning individuals in India. It's how you declare your income to the government and pay any taxes due, or claim a refund if you've paid excess tax. Think of it as your financial report card for the year.

While the process has become more streamlined with online portals and pre-filled data, understanding the nuances is important. The government sets a specific deadline each year, and adhering to it helps you avoid unnecessary penalties, interest charges, and potential complications. This article will help you navigate the process for Assessment Year 2025-26 (which covers income earned from April 1, 2024, to March 31, 2025), ensuring you're prepared and compliant.

Understanding ITR Deadlines and Penalties

The Income Tax Department specifies different due dates for filing ITRs based on the taxpayer's category. For most individual taxpayers, including salaried individuals and those not requiring a tax audit, the original deadline for filing ITR for Financial Year 2024-25 (Assessment Year 2025-26) was July 31, 2025. However, the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) has extended this deadline to September 16, 2025. This extension aims to provide taxpayers with more time, especially given updates to ITR forms and systems.

Consequences of Missing the Deadline

Missing the ITR filing deadline can lead to several financial repercussions:

  • Late Filing Fees (Section 234F): If you file your ITR after the due date, a late fee is levied under Section 234F of the Income Tax Act. For individuals whose total income exceeds ₹5 lakh, this penalty is ₹5,000. If your total income is ₹5 lakh or less, the maximum penalty is ₹1,000. No late filing fee applies if your income is below the basic exemption limit.
  • Interest on Unpaid Tax (Section 234A): Apart from the late fee, if you have any outstanding tax liability, interest will be charged at 1% per month or part thereof on the unpaid amount. This interest accrues from the original due date until the date you actually file the return and pay the tax.
  • Loss of Carry Forward Benefits: Filing a belated ITR means you generally cannot carry forward certain losses, such as business losses or capital losses, to offset income in future years. However, loss under the head "Income from House Property" can still be carried forward even if the return is filed late.
  • Delayed Refunds: If you are eligible for a tax refund, filing your ITR late will naturally delay the processing and receipt of that refund.
  • Increased Scrutiny: Late filers may also face increased scrutiny from the tax authorities, potentially leading to audits.
  • Prosecution Risk: In cases of serious or deliberate non-filing where significant taxes are due, the Income Tax Department may initiate prosecution under Section 276CC, which can lead to imprisonment and fines.

Belated and Updated Returns

Even if you miss the September 16, 2025, deadline, you still have an opportunity to file a belated return for AY 2025-26 until December 31, 2025. While this allows you to become compliant, the aforementioned penalties and loss of benefits will apply.

Further, under current regulations, if you miss both the original and belated deadlines, you can file an updated return (ITR-U) for AY 2025-26 until March 31, 2027. This option comes with additional penalties, ranging from 25% to 50% of the tax due, depending on when it's filed.

Choosing the Correct ITR Form

Selecting the right ITR form is a critical step, as filing with an incorrect form can lead to your return being treated as defective, requiring you to file a revised return. The Income Tax Department offers seven different ITR forms (ITR-1 to ITR-7), each designed for specific categories of taxpayers and income sources. For most individual and Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) taxpayers, the common forms are ITR-1, ITR-2, ITR-3, and ITR-4.

ITR-1 (Sahaj)

This is the simplest form, suitable for resident individuals with a total income of up to ₹50 lakh. You can use ITR-1 if your income comes from:

  • Salary or pension.
  • One house property.
  • Other sources like interest from savings accounts, fixed deposits, or dividends (excluding lottery winnings or income from horse racing).
  • Agricultural income up to ₹5,000.

Who cannot use ITR-1: Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) or Resident Not Ordinarily Residents (RNORs), individuals with income from more than one house property, capital gains, business or professional income, foreign assets, or those who are a director in a company or hold unlisted equity shares.

ITR-2

This form is for individuals and HUFs who do not have income from a business or profession. You should file ITR-2 if you have:

  • Total income exceeding ₹50 lakh.
  • Income from capital gains (e.g., from shares, mutual funds, property).
  • Income from more than one house property.
  • Foreign income or foreign assets.
  • Are a director in a company or have invested in unlisted equity shares.
  • Agricultural income exceeding ₹5,000.
  • Can be used by residents, NRIs, and RNORs.

ITR-3

This form is for individuals and HUFs who have income from a business or profession. You should file ITR-3 if you have:

  • Income from a proprietary business or profession.
  • Income as a partner in a firm.
  • Income from salary, house property, capital gains, and other sources, in addition to business/professional income.
  • Investments in unlisted equity shares.

ITR-4 (Sugam)

This simplified form is for resident individuals, HUFs, and firms (other than LLPs) with a total income of up to ₹50 lakh, who opt for the presumptive taxation scheme under Sections 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE of the Income Tax Act. This scheme allows taxpayers to declare income at a prescribed rate, simplifying accounting.

Essential Documents for ITR Filing

Having all the necessary documents ready before you start filing your ITR can significantly streamline the process and prevent errors. The Income Tax Department's e-filing portal provides pre-filled data, but it's crucial to verify this data against your own records.

Here's a checklist of key documents:

  • PAN and Aadhaar Card: Mandatory for all taxpayers. Ensure your PAN is active and linked with Aadhaar.
  • Bank Account Details: Your bank account number, IFSC code, and account type for all reportable Indian bank accounts. This is where your refund will be credited.
  • Form 16: Issued by your employer, this is crucial for salaried individuals. It details your salary breakup, TDS deducted, and eligible deductions.
  • Salary Slips: Keep your monthly salary slips handy to verify details in Form 16, especially for allowances like HRA and professional tax.
  • Form 16A/16B/16C: TDS certificates for tax deducted on income other than salary (e.g., interest from fixed deposits, rent).
  • Annual Information Statement (AIS) & Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS): These documents, downloadable from the e-filing portal, provide a comprehensive view of your financial transactions, including interest, dividends, mutual fund transactions, and TDS/TCS. It's vital to reconcile these with your own records.
  • Form 26AS: This tax passbook shows details of tax deducted at source (TDS), tax collected at source (TCS), and advance tax paid. Always cross-check it with your AIS/TIS.
  • Investment Proofs: Documents for claiming deductions under Section 80C (e.g., life insurance premiums, ELSS, PPF, home loan principal repayment), Section 80D (health insurance premiums), 80G (donations), etc., if not already considered in Form 16.
  • Home Loan Interest Certificate: If you have a home loan, this certificate from your lender specifies the interest paid, which is deductible under Section 24.
  • Rental Receipts/Agreement: If you claim House Rent Allowance (HRA) exemption and pay rent, keep these handy.
  • Capital Gains Statements: For income from sale of shares, mutual funds, or property, you'll need broker statements, demat statements, or sale deeds.
  • Bank Statements/Passbooks: To verify interest earned from savings accounts and fixed deposits.
  • Previous Year's ITR: Useful for carrying forward losses or referencing past details.
  • Advance Tax/Self-Assessment Tax Challans: Proof of any taxes paid directly by you.

Common Mistakes to Avoid During ITR Filing

Even with pre-filled forms, taxpayers often make errors that can lead to notices from the Income Tax Department or delay refunds. Being aware of these common pitfalls can help ensure a smooth filing experience.

1. Choosing the Wrong ITR Form

As discussed, using an incorrect ITR form for your income profile can result in a defective return notice under Section 139(9). For example, a salaried individual with capital gains exceeding the small LTCG exception (₹1.25 lakh) or foreign assets cannot file ITR-1 and must use ITR-2. Always review your income sources carefully to select the appropriate form.

2. Not Reconciling AIS/TIS and Form 26AS with Personal Records

The pre-filled data on the e-filing portal is based on information received from various sources (employers, banks, mutual funds, etc.). However, discrepancies can occur. It is crucial to download your Annual Information Statement (AIS), Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS), and Form 26AS from the Income Tax e-filing portal and meticulously cross-verify all entries—TDS, TCS, interest income, dividend income, mutual fund transactions, etc.—with your bank statements, Form 16, and other investment proofs. Any mismatch should be rectified with the deductor or reported accurately in your ITR.

3. Missing Out on Eligible Deductions and Exemptions

Many taxpayers overlook legitimate deductions and exemptions, leading to higher tax outgo. Ensure you claim all applicable deductions under Chapter VI-A (e.g., Section 80C, 80D, 80G, 80E for education loan interest) and exemptions like HRA, LTA, etc. Keep all investment proofs and receipts ready to substantiate your claims if required.

4. Incorrect Bank Account Details for Refund

Providing incorrect or non-validated bank account details can lead to delays or failure in receiving your tax refund. Ensure the bank account where you wish to receive your refund is pre-validated on the e-filing portal and is active.

5. Not Verifying the Return

After e-filing, it is mandatory to verify your ITR within 30 days of filing. Without verification, your ITR is considered invalid. The easiest and recommended method is e-Verification using Aadhaar OTP, net banking, or a demat account. Alternatively, you can send a signed physical copy of ITR-V to the CPC, Bengaluru.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 35, a software engineer with ₹20 lakh CTC, living in Hyderabad.

Priya's financial details for FY 2024-25 (AY 2025-26):

  • Annual CTC: ₹20,00,000
  • Gross Salary after HRA exemption and standard deduction (as per Form 16): ₹16,50,000
  • Interest from savings bank account: ₹10,000
  • Interest from fixed deposits: ₹45,000 (TDS of ₹4,500 deducted)
  • Investments under Section 80C (PPF, ELSS): ₹1,50,000
  • Health insurance premium (Section 80D): ₹25,000
  • Advance tax paid during the year: ₹2,10,000

Step-by-step calculation:

  1. Total Gross Income:

    2. * Gross Salary: ₹16,50,000 * Interest from Savings Bank: ₹10,000 * Interest from Fixed Deposits: ₹45,000 * Total Gross Income = ₹16,50,000 + ₹10,000 + ₹45,000 = ₹17,05,000

  1. Deductions:

    2. * Section 80C: ₹1,50,000 * Section 80D: ₹25,000 * Total Deductions = ₹1,50,000 + ₹25,000 = ₹1,75,000

  1. Net Taxable Income:

    2. * Total Gross Income - Total Deductions = ₹17,05,000 - ₹1,75,000 = ₹15,30,000

  1. Tax Calculation (assuming Old Tax Regime for simplicity):

    2. * Up to ₹2,50,000: Nil * ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000 (5%): ₹12,500 * ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 (20%): ₹1,00,000 * ₹10,00,001 to ₹15,30,000 (30% on ₹5,30,000): ₹1,59,000 * Total Tax = ₹12,500 + ₹1,00,000 + ₹1,59,000 = ₹2,71,500

  1. Add Health & Education Cess (4%):

    2. * 4% of ₹2,71,500 = ₹10,860 * Total Tax Liability = ₹2,71,500 + ₹10,860 = ₹2,82,360

  1. Tax Paid:

    2. * TDS on Salary (as per Form 16): ₹2,20,000 (hypothetical, assuming employer deducted this) * TDS on Fixed Deposit Interest: ₹4,500 * Advance Tax Paid: ₹2,10,000 * Total Tax Paid = ₹2,20,000 + ₹4,500 + ₹2,10,000 = ₹4,34,500 (Wait, this is wrong, advance tax should be part of total tax paid from salary. Let's assume TDS from salary covered most of it and advance tax was paid to cover remaining)

Let's re-calculate tax paid more realistically. Assume TDS on salary was ₹2,70,000 (as per Form 16). TDS on FD interest: ₹4,500. Total Tax Paid = ₹2,70,000 (TDS on Salary) + ₹4,500 (TDS on FD) = ₹2,74,500

  1. Tax Refund/Due:

    2. * Total Tax Paid - Total Tax Liability = ₹2,74,500 - ₹2,82,360 = -₹7,860

In this scenario, Priya still has ₹7,860 as tax due. She would need to pay this as self-assessment tax before filing her ITR. Since her income is below ₹50 lakh and she has salary, interest, and one house property (implied, as standard deduction is claimed), she would file ITR-1.

If Priya misses the September 16, 2025 deadline and files on October 10, 2025, she would incur a late fee of ₹5,000 (as her income is over ₹5 lakh). Additionally, interest under Section 234A at 1% per month would be charged on the ₹7,860 due from September 17, 2025, until the date of payment.

What to do this week

  1. Verify Your PAN-Aadhaar Link Status: Ensure your Permanent Account Number (PAN) is linked with your Aadhaar. You can check this on the Income Tax Department's e-filing portal. If not linked, initiate the process immediately.
  2. Gather All Financial Documents: Collect your Form 16 (from employer), bank statements, investment proofs (80C, 80D, etc.), Form 16A for TDS on FD interest, and any other income/deduction related documents for FY 2024-25.
  3. Download AIS/TIS and Form 26AS: Access the Income Tax e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in) and download your Annual Information Statement (AIS), Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS), and Form 26AS. Carefully cross-verify all entries with your personal records to identify any discrepancies.
  4. Identify the Correct ITR Form: Based on your income sources, total income, and residential status, determine which ITR form (ITR-1, ITR-2, ITR-3, or ITR-4) is applicable to you. Refer to the guidelines on the Income Tax India website.
  5. Pre-validate Your Bank Account: Log in to the e-filing portal and ensure the bank account where you wish to receive any tax refund is pre-validated and active. This step is crucial for timely refund processing.

FAQ

### What is the last date to file ITR for AY 2025-26?

For most individual taxpayers not requiring a tax audit, the extended deadline for filing Income Tax Returns for Assessment Year 2025-26 (Financial Year 2024-25) is September 16, 2025.

### What happens if I miss the ITR filing deadline?

If you miss the deadline, you may have to pay a late filing fee of up to ₹5,000 (₹1,000 if income is ≤ ₹5 lakh) under Section 234F, plus interest at 1% per month on any unpaid tax under Section 234A. You also lose the ability to carry forward certain losses.

### Can I file my ITR after the deadline?

Yes, you can file a "belated return" for AY 2025-26 until December 31, 2025, but it will attract late fees and other penalties. You can also file an "updated return" (ITR-U) until March 31, 2027, with higher penalties.

### Which ITR form should a salaried individual with capital gains use?

A salaried individual with capital gains (other than minor LTCG from listed shares/Mfs up to ₹1.25 lakh) cannot use ITR-1. They should generally use ITR-2, provided they do not have business or professional income.

### Are NRIs eligible to file ITR-1?

No, ITR-1 (Sahaj) is exclusively for Resident Individuals (other than Not Ordinarily Resident). Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) must use other applicable forms, typically ITR-2.

### What documents are essential for ITR filing?

Key documents include PAN, Aadhaar, Form 16, salary slips, bank statements, investment proofs, Form 26AS, and AIS/TIS. These help verify income, deductions, and tax credits.

### Is it mandatory to e-verify my ITR?

Yes, e-verification of your ITR is mandatory within 30 days of filing. Without verification, your return is considered invalid. The Aadhaar OTP method is the most common and convenient way to e-verify.
