Direct vs. Regular Mutual Funds: SEBI's New Default Plan Rule

TL;DR

  • Regular mutual fund plans typically include a commission for distributors, leading to higher expense ratios.
  • Direct mutual fund plans do not involve distributor commissions, resulting in lower expense ratios and potentially higher returns.
  • SEBI has mandated that online execution-only platforms must offer direct plans as the default option for mutual fund transactions.
  • This new rule aims to empower investors by promoting direct plans, which can significantly boost long-term wealth accumulation.
  • Investors retain the choice to opt for a regular plan if they require advisory services from a distributor.

What this means in plain terms

When you invest in a mutual fund, you generally have two choices: a 'regular plan' or a 'direct plan'. The fundamental difference lies in how much you pay for the fund's management and operations. A regular plan includes a commission that is paid to the financial advisor or distributor who helped you invest. This commission is embedded within the fund's expense ratio, making it slightly higher.

On the other hand, a direct plan means you are investing directly with the Asset Management Company (AMC) without any intermediary. Since there's no distributor involved, there's no commission to pay, which translates to a lower expense ratio for the direct plan. Recently, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) introduced a significant rule change: online execution-only platforms must now offer direct plans as the default option. This means when you go to invest through such a platform, the system will automatically show you the direct plan first, encouraging you to choose the option with lower costs.

Understanding Direct vs. Regular Plans

The distinction between direct and regular mutual fund plans is crucial for every investor, especially given its long-term impact on your wealth. The core difference lies in the expense ratio, which is the annual fee charged by the mutual fund to cover its operating and management costs.

The Expense Ratio Advantage

In a regular plan, a portion of the expense ratio is paid out as commission to the distributor or agent who facilitated your investment. This commission acts as their remuneration for advising you, processing your paperwork, and providing ongoing service. Consequently, the expense ratio for a regular plan is always higher than that of an equivalent direct plan. For instance, if a regular plan has an expense ratio of 1.5% per annum, its direct counterpart might have an expense ratio of 0.75% to 1% per annum. While this difference might seem small, perhaps 0.5% to 0.75% annually, its compounding effect over decades can be substantial.

The Role of Distributors

Regular plans are typically chosen by investors who prefer to receive guidance and support from a financial advisor or distributor. These advisors can help with fund selection, portfolio allocation, rebalancing, and paperwork. For many, especially those new to investing or who prefer hands-on assistance, the value provided by a distributor justifies the higher expense ratio. However, it's important for investors to be aware of this cost and evaluate if the advisory services received are commensurate with the additional expense.

Direct Access, Lower Costs

Direct plans are designed for investors who are comfortable doing their own research, making their own investment decisions, and managing their own portfolios. These plans can be purchased directly from the Asset Management Companies (AMCs) or through online platforms that offer execution-only services. The primary benefit is the absence of distributor commissions, leading to a lower expense ratio and, consequently, higher returns over the long term. This cost saving directly translates into more units purchased for the same investment amount, which then compounds over time.

SEBI's Mandate for Default Direct Plans

In a significant move to enhance investor protection and promote transparency, SEBI has mandated that all online execution-only platforms for mutual funds must offer direct plans as the default option for transactions. This directive aims to nudge investors towards the lower-cost option unless they specifically choose otherwise.

The Rationale Behind the Rule

SEBI's intention behind this rule is multifold. Firstly, it seeks to address the information asymmetry where many investors might not have been fully aware of the existence or benefits of direct plans. By making direct plans the default, SEBI is ensuring that investors are presented with the most cost-efficient option upfront. Secondly, it empowers investors by giving them a clear choice and encouraging them to take a more active role in their investment decisions. This move aligns with SEBI's broader objective of fostering a more investor-friendly environment in the Indian securities market.

Impact on Online Platforms

For online execution-only platforms, this means their user interface and workflow must be designed to display the direct plan as the primary option when an investor initiates a mutual fund purchase, switch, or systematic investment plan (SIP). While investors will still have the option to select a regular plan, they will need to actively choose it, indicating a conscious decision to opt for distributor-assisted services. This change primarily affects platforms that do not offer advisory services themselves but merely facilitate transactions. Platforms that provide investment advice will likely have different compliance requirements, ensuring that the advice is suitable for the investor, regardless of the plan type.

What it Means for Investors

For you, as an investor, this rule simplifies the process of choosing a direct plan. If you are comfortable with self-directed investing, the default option will now automatically lead you to the plan with a lower expense ratio. This can result in significant savings over your investment horizon, leading to greater wealth accumulation. If you prefer the guidance of a financial advisor, you can still opt for a regular plan, but you will do so with a clearer understanding of the cost implications. This encourages investors to consciously decide whether the value of advisory services outweighs the additional expense ratio.

Long-Term Impact on Your Wealth

The seemingly small difference in expense ratios between direct and regular plans can have a profound impact on your investment returns over the long term, thanks to the power of compounding.

The Compounding Advantage

Let's consider an example. An investor putting ₹10,000 every month into a mutual fund for 25 years. If the fund generates an average annual return of 12%, a direct plan with a 0.5% lower expense ratio could result in a significantly larger corpus. That 0.5% difference, compounded over decades, means more money remains invested and grows for you, rather than being paid out as commissions. This difference can easily run into several lakhs or even crores of rupees over a long investment horizon.

Enhanced Transparency and Investor Awareness

SEBI's new rule is a step towards greater transparency in mutual fund investing. By making direct plans the default, it forces investors to acknowledge the cost difference and make an informed choice. This increased awareness is crucial for building a financially literate investor base. Investors are now more likely to question the value proposition of regular plans versus the cost savings of direct plans, leading to more informed decision-making.

Shifting Landscape for Advisors

While the rule promotes direct plans, it doesn't eliminate the need for financial advisors. Instead, it encourages a shift towards a fee-based advisory model where advisors charge a direct fee for their services, rather than relying solely on commissions embedded in regular plans. This can lead to a more transparent relationship between advisors and clients, where the value of advice is explicitly paid for and understood. Investors seeking comprehensive financial planning or personalised portfolio management may still benefit immensely from a qualified Certified Financial Planner (CFP) or Registered Investment Advisor (RIA), regardless of whether they choose direct or regular plans for their execution.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 32, earning ₹22L CTC, residing in Pune. Priya plans to invest ₹15,000 per month in an equity mutual fund for the next 25 years.

Scenario 1: Regular Plan

  • Monthly Investment: ₹15,000
  • Expected Annualised Return (before expenses): 12%
  • Expense Ratio (Regular Plan): 1.5% per annum
  • Net Annualised Return for Investor: 12% - 1.5% = 10.5%

Using a SIP calculator for 25 years (300 months) with a 10.5% annualised return: Future Value ≈ ₹2.31 crores

Scenario 2: Direct Plan

  • Monthly Investment: ₹15,000
  • Expected Annualised Return (before expenses): 12%
  • Expense Ratio (Direct Plan): 0.75% per annum
  • Net Annualised Return for Investor: 12% - 0.75% = 11.25%

Using a SIP calculator for 25 years (300 months) with an 11.25% annualised return: Future Value ≈ ₹2.75 crores

Difference in Corpus: ₹2.75 crores (Direct Plan) - ₹2.31 crores (Regular Plan) = ₹44 lakhs

In this example, by choosing a direct plan over a regular plan, Priya could accumulate an additional ₹44 lakhs over 25 years, purely due to the difference in expense ratio. This demonstrates the significant long-term impact of seemingly small annual cost savings.

What to do this week

  1. Review your existing mutual fund investments: Check your mutual fund statements or portfolio tracker to identify if your current holdings are in direct or regular plans. Look for "Direct Plan" or "Regular Plan" in the fund name.
  2. Compare expense ratios: For any regular plans you hold, search for the equivalent direct plan on the AMC's website or AMFI India's website (https://amfiindia.com) and compare their expense ratios. Note down the difference.
  3. Evaluate advisory needs: Honestly assess if you require ongoing, personalised financial advice for your mutual fund investments. If you are comfortable with self-research and managing your portfolio, a direct plan might be more suitable.
  4. Consider switching (if applicable): If you find you are in a regular plan and wish to switch to a direct plan of the same fund, understand the process. This usually involves redeeming from the regular plan and reinvesting in the direct plan, which might have tax implications (short-term/long-term capital gains) and exit loads. Consult your financial advisor or AMC for specific steps.
  5. Utilise online platforms for new investments: When making new mutual fund investments through execution-only platforms, ensure you are selecting the direct plan, which will now be the default option.

FAQ

What is the primary difference between direct and regular mutual funds?

The main difference lies in the expense ratio. Regular plans include a commission for distributors, leading to a higher expense ratio, while direct plans have no distributor commission, resulting in a lower expense ratio and potentially higher returns.

Why did SEBI introduce the new default plan rule?

SEBI introduced this rule to promote transparency, empower investors by making them aware of lower-cost options, and encourage self-directed investing by making direct plans the default choice on online execution-only platforms.

Will I still be able to invest in regular plans?

Yes, you will still have the option to invest in regular plans. The new rule only mandates that direct plans be offered as the default option on execution-only platforms, but you can actively choose a regular plan if you prefer distributor services.

How does a lower expense ratio benefit me?

A lower expense ratio means a smaller portion of your investment is deducted annually for fund management. This leads to more units being allocated to you, which then compounds over time, potentially resulting in a significantly larger corpus in the long run.

Can I switch my existing regular plan investments to direct plans?

Yes, you can switch your existing regular plan investments to direct plans of the same fund. However, this process typically involves redeeming from the regular plan and reinvesting in the direct plan, which may trigger capital gains taxes and exit loads, depending on your holding period.

Where can I find the expense ratio for a mutual fund?

You can find the expense ratio for any mutual fund scheme on the respective Asset Management Company's (AMC) website, on the Association of Mutual Funds in India (AMFI) website (https://amfiindia.com), or in the scheme's offer document.
