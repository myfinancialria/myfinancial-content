Pre-Market Brief — 23 September 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight, with Asian indices showing divergence, while US market data was unavailable.
  • GIFT Nifty is signalling a positive open for Indian equities, pointing to a potential gap-up.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong buying support.
  • Crude oil prices saw a significant dip, offering a potential tailwind for India's import bill and related sectors.
  • Nifty's key pivot level to watch is 23422.25, with resistance around 23494.95 and support at 23377.6.
  • Persistent global inflation concerns and hawkish central bank commentary remain the primary risk factors for market sentiment.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Global markets traded with a mixed sentiment overnight. While US market data was unavailable, European markets saw the FTSE 100 close marginally lower. In Asia this morning, the Nikkei 225 posted a healthy gain, but the Hang Seng and Shanghai indices declined, partly due to tempered expectations around a US-China summit. This divergence suggests a cautious but not overtly negative global backdrop.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) saw a marginal appreciation against the US Dollar, closing at ₹95.69. The Dollar Index, which measures the greenback against a basket of major currencies, strengthened slightly to 100.78. A significant development was the sharp decline in crude oil prices, with Brent Crude falling over 4% to $95.21 and WTI Crude dropping over 5% to $89.67. Gold prices eased slightly to $4358.70, while Silver saw a minor uptick. The US 10-year Treasury Yield softened to 4.96%, indicating some easing in global bond market pressure.

Interpretation: The significant drop in crude oil prices is a major positive for India, which is a net oil importer. This could ease inflationary pressures, reduce the current account deficit, and benefit sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paints, and aviation. A stronger Dollar Index could put some pressure on the Rupee, but the USD/INR's slight appreciation yesterday suggests resilience. A softening US 10Y yield is generally positive for emerging markets like India, potentially reducing the risk of FII outflows.

GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling

The GIFT Nifty is trading at 23463.00, up by +0.58%.

Interpretation: This indicates a positive bias for the Indian benchmark Nifty 50, suggesting a potential gap-up open for the market today.

FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape

Yesterday, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the Indian cash equity segment, offloading shares worth ₹3,810 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, continued to provide strong support, buying equities worth ₹4,120 crore. This trend of FII selling being absorbed by DII buying has been consistent over the last five sessions.

However, the strong and consistent buying by DIIs has been crucial in providing a floor to the market, indicating robust domestic liquidity and confidence. This dynamic suggests that domestic flows are currently the primary support for the market.

Global Market: Singapore core inflation hits nearly two-year high in August Singapore's core inflation rose to 2.2% in August, exceeding expectations and the previous month's rate, with headline inflation also climbing to 2.3% year-on-year. Why it matters: Rising inflation in a key Asian economy like Singapore could reinforce global central banks' hawkish stance and fuel concerns about persistent inflation, potentially impacting global liquidity and FII flows into emerging markets, including India.

US Market: Fed’s Collins backs rate hike, flags persistent inflation risks Boston Fed President Susan Collins supported the recent Fed rate hike, citing ongoing inflation risks and suggesting a more restrictive policy might be needed to control inflation. Why it matters: This hawkish commentary from a US Federal Reserve official reinforces expectations of higher-for-longer interest rates in the US. This can make US assets more attractive, potentially leading to continued FII outflows from Indian markets and increasing the cost of global capital.

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  • Global Inflation Data/Central Bank Commentary: The news about Singapore's inflation and Fed President Collins' hawkish remarks highlight ongoing global inflation concerns. Markets typically react with caution to signs of persistent inflation, as it often leads to tighter monetary policies and higher interest rates, which can dampen economic growth and corporate earnings.
  • FII/DII Flow Trends: The continued FII selling and DII buying trend will be closely watched. Sustained DII support can cushion market falls, but prolonged FII outflows can limit upside potential.
  • Crude Oil Price Movement: The sharp drop in crude oil prices is a significant positive. Markets generally react favorably to lower crude prices, as it improves India's macroeconomic outlook and benefits specific sectors. Any reversal in this trend could quickly turn sentiment cautious.

What to watch in today's session

  1. The opening bias indicated by GIFT Nifty and how Indian indices react to the mixed global cues.
  2. Nifty's ability to hold above its CPR pivot of 23422.25 and whether it can challenge R1 at 23494.95.
  3. The continued trend of FII and DII flows, and whether domestic buying can sustain market support.
  4. Movement in crude oil prices, as any reversal from yesterday's sharp fall could impact sentiment.
  5. Performance of sectors sensitive to crude oil prices (OMCs, aviation, paints) and those benefiting from index inclusion (Vedanta Power).
  6. Any further commentary on global inflation or central bank policy that could influence investor risk appetite.

Bottom line

The Indian market is poised for a potentially positive open, as indicated by GIFT Nifty, largely supported by the significant dip in crude oil prices. However, the persistent FII selling and global inflation concerns, coupled with hawkish central bank rhetoric, suggest underlying caution. The Nifty's ability to sustain above the 23400-23420 zone will be crucial in determining the day's direction, with domestic institutional flows likely to play a key role in providing support.
