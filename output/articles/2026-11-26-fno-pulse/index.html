India F&O Pulse — 26 Nov 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Nifty shows mild bearish signals, while Bank Nifty leans bullish amidst mixed FII index futures positioning.

TL;DR

  • NIFTY 50: The day's data suggests a mildly bearish outlook for tomorrow's session, with resistance expected near current levels.
  • SENSEX: Signals point towards a range-bound or mixed session ahead, with both upside and downside limited by option writers.
  • BANK NIFTY: The positioning indicates a bullish bias, with potential for upward movement.

What the indices did today

The NIFTY 50 closed at 25,884.50 today, marking a modest gain of 0.25%. The index ended very close to its heaviest Call Open Interest (OI) wall at 26,000, suggesting that option writers are expecting this level to act as a significant ceiling for the immediate future.

The SENSEX advanced by 0.46% to close at 84,328.59. Interestingly, the Sensex found itself positioned right between its heaviest Call OI wall at 84,000 and its heaviest Put OI wall also at 84,000, indicating a potential struggle for direction as writers are positioned for a tight range.

Meanwhile, the BANK NIFTY saw a slight dip of 0.26%, closing at 63,535.30. Despite the small decline, the index closed well below its heaviest Put OI wall at 65,000, which could imply that option writers are comfortable with the index finding support at lower levels and potentially moving higher towards that base. The India VIX, a measure of market volatility expectations, also cooled down by 4.70% to 16.84, suggesting a reduction in anticipated market swings.

Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options refers to the total number of outstanding contracts that have not yet been settled. For Calls, the highest Call OI strike typically represents a significant resistance level, as many option writers (sellers) believe the price will not go above this point. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike often acts as a strong support level, where option writers expect the price to hold. These levels are often referred to as "walls" because they can be difficult for the underlying index to breach.

Today, for the NIFTY 50, the heaviest Call OI was observed at the 26,000 strike, which is quite close to the spot price. This indicates that many call writers are confident the Nifty will not comfortably cross 26,000 soon. Fresh Call writing, meaning new selling of Call options, was notably heavy near the 26,166 level, reinforcing the expectation of a cap on upside movement.

On the SENSEX, the heaviest Call OI was at 84,000, while the heaviest Put OI was also at 84,000. This unique situation suggests a strong battleground around this level, with writers on both sides expecting the index to hover around it. Fresh Call writing was concentrated near 84,933, indicating potential resistance there, while fresh Put writing was seen near 83,333, suggesting a floor.

For the BANK NIFTY, the heaviest Put OI was at 65,000, which is a fair distance above the current spot. This could imply that put writers are comfortable with the index potentially moving higher towards this level. Fresh Put writing was particularly strong near 63,666, suggesting that put sellers are actively building support at levels just above the current spot.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR(OI) value below 1.0 typically suggests that more Call options are open than Put options, which can be interpreted as a mildly bearish sentiment from option writers. A value above 1.0, conversely, indicates more Put options are open, potentially signaling a bullish bias.

For the NIFTY 50, the PCR(OI) stood at 0.9, indicating a slightly higher concentration of Call options. However, today's flow, represented by the change in Open Interest (ΔOI), showed a PCR of 1.62. This suggests that fresh writing today was significantly put-heavy, meaning more new Put options were sold than Call options, which is often seen as a bullish signal for the day's fresh activity.

The SENSEX recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.92, also leaning slightly towards more Call options. Its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was 1.13, suggesting a relatively balanced flow of fresh writing today, with a slight tilt towards put selling.

The BANK NIFTY had a PCR(OI) of 0.88, indicating a higher volume of Call options outstanding. However, its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was 1.39, showing a strong preference for fresh Put writing today, which typically points towards a bullish sentiment for the short term.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers, particularly those who have sold options, would experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that level. It's often considered a "magnet" because the underlying index tends to gravitate towards this strike as expiry approaches.

For the NIFTY 50, the Max Pain point was at 25,900, which is very close to its spot price of 25,884.50. This suggests that option writers are positioned for the Nifty to expire around current levels.

The SENSEX also had its Max Pain at 84,300, nearly identical to its closing spot of 84,328.59. This indicates a similar expectation of expiry around the current market price.

Interestingly, for the BANK NIFTY, the Max Pain was at 64,100, which is about 0.9% above its current spot of 63,535.30. When Max Pain is notably above the spot price, it can suggest that option writers are expecting the index to move upwards towards that level as expiry draws nearer.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 23.0% (37,660 long contracts versus 126,129 short contracts). A low FII Index futures long ratio, such as today's 23.0%, historically tends to suggest a cautious or bearish stance from these large institutional participants on the broader market indices. In contrast, their Stock futures long ratio was 57.4% (3,424,190 long contracts versus 2,540,254 short contracts), indicating a relatively more bullish stance on individual stocks compared to the indices.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When Nifty's Call walls are close to the spot price and fresh Call writing is prominent at higher strikes, alongside a relatively low FII index futures long ratio, the market has historically tended to see some resistance at higher levels in the subsequent session. The Nifty's Max Pain being at spot further supports an expectation of consolidation.

For Sensex, the balanced PCR(OI) and Max Pain at spot, coupled with both Call and Put walls at the same level, historically suggests that the index might remain range-bound, with strong support and resistance levels limiting significant directional moves.

Conversely, Bank Nifty's bullish signals, including Max Pain significantly above spot and strong fresh Put writing, when combined with a relatively high Put OI base, have historically tended to coincide with an upward bias for the index. The overall picture suggests a market that might see divergent performance across its major indices.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these signals can empower you to interpret market movements more effectively. You can access options Open Interest data, including PCR and Max Pain calculations, directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex. Many brokerage platforms also provide these analytics in a user-friendly format.

To combine these insights, first, look at the overall Open Interest walls to identify key support and resistance levels. Then, observe the "fresh writing" (change in OI) to gauge where new positions are being taken, which often indicates the immediate expectations of option writers. Compare the PCR(OI) and PCR(today's flow) to understand both the standing sentiment and the day's fresh activity. Finally, check the Max Pain point relative to the spot price; as expiry nears, its "magnet effect" can become more pronounced. By consistently tracking these data points, you can develop a more informed perspective on potential market direction.
