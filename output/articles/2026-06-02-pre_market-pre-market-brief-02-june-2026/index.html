Pre-Market Brief — 02 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets presented a mixed picture, with US indices closing higher, while European and Japanese markets saw declines.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their significant selling streak, pulling out ₹3,912 crore from Indian equities yesterday.
  • Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong counter-support, injecting ₹5,109 crore into the market.
  • The Indian Rupee depreciated against the US Dollar, while gold and silver prices surged, indicating safe-haven demand.
  • Alphabet announced a massive $80 billion fundraise for AI expansion, while NHPC shares tumbled following a government OFS.

Where global markets closed

Global equity markets closed with a mixed performance yesterday. US indices showed positive momentum, with the Nasdaq leading gains, driven by technology stocks. In contrast, European markets, including the FTSE 100, ended in negative territory. Asian markets were also mixed, with Hong Kong's Hang Seng index registering a strong rally, while Japan's Nikkei 225 saw a slight decline. The overall global tone appears cautious, with investors weighing economic data and geopolitical developments.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee depreciated against the US Dollar, with USD/INR closing at 95.20, up 0.21%. A weaker Rupee can make imports more expensive and potentially impact the profitability of companies with significant import bills. The Dollar Index, measuring the greenback against a basket of major currencies, saw a slight dip of 0.13% to 99.07.

In the commodities market, crude oil prices softened, with Brent Crude down 0.65% to $94.36 and WTI Crude falling 0.73% to $91.49. Lower crude prices are generally beneficial for India, a major oil importer, as they help ease inflationary pressures and reduce the current account deficit.

Gold and silver witnessed significant gains, with gold rising 2.01% to $4565.20 and silver surging 2.72% to $77.04. This rise in precious metals often signals increased safe-haven demand amidst global uncertainties or inflation concerns.

The US 10-Year Treasury Yield rose by 0.49% to 4.47%. An increase in US bond yields can sometimes make emerging markets less attractive to foreign investors, potentially impacting FII flows into India.

Companies reporting results today

LIBAS: The company is scheduled to report its financial results today, along with discussing fund-raising and other business matters. Investors will be keen to watch for any updates on its performance and future growth plans.

Corporate actions today

  • SETCO: The stock will trade ex-dividend for an Interim Dividend of ₹13 per share. Shareholders on record will be eligible for this dividend.
  • NXT-INFRA: This unit will trade ex-distribution for a payout of ₹4.3475 per unit, comprising ₹2.8887 per unit as interest and ₹1.4588 per unit as a return of capital.
  • NDRINVIT: The unit goes ex-distribution for ₹1.92 per unit, consisting of ₹0.949 per unit as interest and ₹0.971 per unit as a return of capital.
  • OSEINTRUST: This unit will trade ex-distribution for ₹3.9155 per unit, which includes ₹1.2084 per unit as interest, ₹0.9735 per unit as dividend, and ₹1.7336 per unit as a return of capital.

Recent FII/DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree, offloading Indian equities worth ₹3,912 crore yesterday. This marks a consistent trend of outflows over the past five sessions, with FIIs having pulled out a significant amount, erasing a decade's worth of inflows. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, provided strong support, pumping ₹5,109 crore into the market, cushioning the impact of FII selling.

| Investor Type | Net Cash Position (₹ crore) | | :------------ | :-------------------------- | | FII | -3,912 | | DII | +5,109 |

Over the last five trading sessions, FIIs have been net sellers in four out of five sessions, while DIIs have consistently been net buyers, indicating robust domestic participation.

Overnight news that matters

The $80 billion AI gamble: Decoding Alphabet's mega fundraise Alphabet plans a historic $80 billion equity raise, partly backed by Berkshire Hathaway, to expand its AI infrastructure, data centers, and cloud capabilities. This news highlights the intensifying global race in AI investment and could influence sentiment towards technology and AI-related stocks globally, including those in India with AI exposure or partnerships.

Relentless Selloff: FIIs pull out 10 years' worth of India equity inflows Foreign investors' cumulative net equity investments in India have fallen to their lowest level since 2016 due to sustained selling. This significant outflow underscores a weakening appeal for India's stock market among foreign investors and could continue to exert pressure on benchmark indices.

Nomura names GE Vernova, CG Power, 4 others among top plays on India’s decadal data centre opportunity Nomura identifies India's data centre industry as entering a rapid growth phase, driven by digitalisation, cloud adoption, and AI demand, highlighting strong opportunities for industrial equipment and power solution providers. This report could bring specific focus and investor interest to companies involved in the data centre ecosystem in India.

NHPC shares tumble 5% as govt’s OFS worth up to Rs 4,300 crore opens at 8% discount NHPC shares saw a significant drop after the government launched an offer for sale (OFS) to divest up to a 6% stake in the public sector company at an 8% discount, aiming to raise up to ₹4,300 crore. This development will directly impact NHPC's stock performance and could influence sentiment towards other public sector undertakings (PSUs) undergoing disinvestment.

IT rally rages on! Infosys, TCS, other stocks jump up to 4% despite overall market weakness Indian IT stocks, including Infosys and TCS, surged despite broader market weakness, extending a three-day rally. This resilience in the IT sector, following an earlier sell-off, suggests a potential rotation of funds or renewed confidence in the sector's outlook, possibly driven by global tech trends like AI.

What to watch in today's session

  1. FII Selling Trend: The persistent selling by Foreign Institutional Investors remains a key concern. Investors should monitor whether this trend continues and how Domestic Institutional Investors respond.
  2. NHPC OFS Impact: The government's offer for sale in NHPC will be closely watched for subscription levels and its sustained impact on the stock price and the broader PSU segment.
  3. IT Sector Momentum: Following yesterday's rally, the performance of major IT stocks like Infosys and TCS will be crucial to gauge if the sector's positive momentum can be sustained.
  4. LIBAS Results: The financial results and any announcements regarding fund-raising from LIBAS today could lead to stock-specific movements.
  5. Global Cues and Commodities: Movements in the US 10-year yield, crude oil prices, and the Dollar Index will continue to influence market sentiment and currency movements.

Bottom line

The Indian market is likely to open with mixed cues today, influenced by a combination of positive signals from US markets and strong gains in some Asian indices, tempered by continued FII outflows and a depreciating Rupee. While domestic institutional buying has provided a significant cushion, the sustained foreign selling remains a point of caution. Investors should remain vigilant, focusing on sector-specific developments and global economic indicators.
