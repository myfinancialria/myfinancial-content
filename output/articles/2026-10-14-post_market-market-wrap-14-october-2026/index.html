Market Wrap — 14 October 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed lower for the day.
  • Global cues, particularly hotter-than-expected US inflation data, fueled bets of further rate hikes and weighed on sentiment.
  • Transformers And Rectifiers (India) Ltd. (TARIL) was the standout gainer, surging nearly 15%.
  • LIC Housing Finance Ltd. (LICHSGFIN) emerged as the top loser, shedding over 5%.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support.
  • F&O positioning data is unavailable for today's wrap and will be included from the next session.

How the market behaved today

Indian equities witnessed a subdued session, with benchmark indices closing in the red. The Nifty 50 opened flat and largely trended downwards, closing at 22622.70, a decrease of 0.44%. The Bank Nifty also followed suit, ending at 55316.00, down 0.30%. Broader markets, represented by the Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100, also saw minor declines.

What moved the market — the factors

The primary driver for today's market weakness stemmed from negative global cues, particularly from the US. Hotter-than-expected US Consumer Price Index (CPI) data for September, which showed a surprising rise of 0.6% month-over-month and 3.7% year-over-year, significantly boosted expectations for further interest rate hikes by the US Federal Reserve. This led to a jump in US Treasury yields and a sell-off in US equities, with Wall Street's major indices opening lower.

The sentiment was further dampened by Bank of England Chief Economist Huw Pill questioning market bets on four rate hikes over the next year, assessing the inflationary fallout of the Iran war. This global hawkish stance on interest rates translated into sustained selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs), who were net sellers of ₹1,305 crore today. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided some counterbalance, infusing ₹2,439 crore into the market.

Sectorally, strength was observed in Energy, FMCG, and IT, which helped cushion the overall market fall. However, the Financial Services sector, particularly some NBFCs and banks, saw significant selling pressure, contributing to the broader market's decline.

The market breadth was slightly negative today, with 239 advances against 255 declines out of a total of 494 stocks traded, indicating a marginal tilt towards selling pressure.

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree, registering a net outflow of ₹1,305 crore in the cash segment today. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net buyers, providing support with an inflow of ₹2,439 crore.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's session was primarily driven by global macroeconomic developments. The hotter-than-expected US Consumer Price Index (CPI) data released yesterday significantly impacted market sentiment. This inflation report fueled expectations of further interest rate hikes by the US Federal Reserve, leading to a surge in US Treasury yields and a sell-off in US equities.

The ripple effect was seen in the Indian market through sustained selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs), who have been net sellers for the fifth consecutive session. While Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided crucial support, the overall negative global sentiment, particularly concerns over rising interest rates, weighed on the Nifty and Bank Nifty, leading to their decline. The Financial Services sector, in particular, saw some of the biggest losers, reflecting sensitivity to interest rate outlooks.

Separately, the announcement of the Jio Platforms IPO price band and dates generated significant news flow, but its direct impact on today's broader market movement was limited, though it will be a key event to watch in the coming days. The approval for Fairfax's stake acquisition in IDBI Bank was a stock-specific positive event, leading to a sharp rise in IDBI Bank shares.

Setup for tomorrow

Global cues will continue to be crucial for tomorrow's session. The US markets' reaction to the hot CPI data and rising bond yields overnight will set the tone. Investors will be closely watching for any further commentary from central banks regarding interest rate trajectories. On the domestic front, the upcoming Jio Platforms IPO, opening on October 21, will likely dominate headlines and could influence sentiment around the telecom and digital sectors. Key levels to watch for Nifty 50 would be today's low of 22558.95 as immediate support and 22757.95 as resistance.
