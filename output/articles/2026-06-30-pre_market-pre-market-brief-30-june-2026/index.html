Pre-Market Brief — 30 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets presented a mixed picture, with US indices closing significantly higher, while Asian and European markets showed varied performance.
  • The Indian Rupee depreciated against the US Dollar, and the Dollar Index strengthened, indicating broader dollar demand.
  • Crude oil prices were mixed, with Brent seeing a marginal gain and WTI declining, while gold and silver registered gains.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers yesterday, though Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided robust buying support.
  • Several prominent companies, including Bajaj Holdings, Bajaj Finance, and Bajaj Finserv, have ex-dividend dates today.

Where global markets closed

Global equity markets closed with a mixed sentiment on Monday. US indices saw strong gains, led by technology stocks, reflecting renewed investor confidence. However, European markets ended slightly lower, and Asian markets were largely mixed, with the Nikkei 225 posting gains while the Hang Seng declined. This overall tone suggests a cautious optimism, with specific regional factors influencing market direction.

Currency, commodities and yields

The USD/INR pair closed at ₹94.60, marking a 0.25% increase, indicating a depreciation of the Indian Rupee against the US Dollar. This movement, coupled with a 0.21% rise in the Dollar Index to 101.32, suggests a strengthening of the greenback globally, which could put some pressure on import costs for India.

In the commodities market, Brent Crude saw a marginal uptick of 0.05% to $73.19 per barrel, while WTI Crude declined by 1.17% to $69.92. Mixed crude prices may offer some relief on the import bill front, but sustained higher Brent prices remain a concern for India, a major oil importer. Gold prices rose by 0.20% to $4030.20, and Silver saw a significant gain of 2.14% to $59.42, reflecting a potential safe-haven demand or inflationary concerns.

The US 10-Year Yield decreased by 0.46% to 4.37%. A falling US yield typically suggests an easing of interest rate hike expectations or a flight to safety, which can sometimes make emerging markets, including India, more attractive to foreign investors, though the strengthening dollar might offset some of this appeal.

Recent FII/DII flows

Yesterday, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the Indian equity market, offloading shares worth ₹1,350 crore. However, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong counter-support, with net purchases amounting to ₹2,801 crore.

Overnight news that matters

RBI's FCNR move as a tailwind for banks The Reserve Bank of India's latest foreign currency deposit scheme is being viewed by analysts as a potential positive for banking stocks. After being heavily sold by foreign investors in May, this move could lower funding costs for banks, providing a fresh tailwind for the sector. This development could lead to renewed interest and improved sentiment for Indian banking stocks.

KEC International shares climb 7% on ₹1,754 crore order win KEC International, an infrastructure EPC firm, announced fresh orders worth ₹1,754 crore across its Transmission & Distribution and Cables & Conductors businesses. This significant order win is a positive development for the company and could see its shares react positively today, reflecting improved revenue visibility and order book strength.

Nifty IT hits fresh 52-week low as Infosys, TCS, Wipro & others tumble up to 3% The Nifty IT index experienced a significant decline, hitting a fresh 52-week low, with major players like Infosys, TCS, and Wipro seeing drops of up to 3%. This downturn is attributed to persistent inflation concerns and uncertainty surrounding the US Federal Reserve's interest rate trajectory, which impacts the outlook for the export-oriented IT sector. This trend suggests continued headwinds for Indian IT companies.

PFC-REC merger: Which stock should you buy before the mega demerger creates ₹11 lakh cr power financing giant The boards of Power Finance Corporation (PFC) and REC have approved a merger, which is set to create India's largest power financing institution with a massive ₹11 lakh crore loan book. This strategic move aims to capitalize on the growing opportunities in India's power sector, potentially creating a dominant entity. This development is significant for both companies and the broader power financing sector.

Delayed monsoon may weigh on auto demand, but long-term outlook stays strong: Aditya Shah A delayed monsoon poses a temporary challenge to rural auto demand, particularly for tractors and two-wheelers. While experts believe the sector's long-term outlook remains robust, the near-term impact on sales in rural areas could be a concern. Investors in auto and auto ancillary stocks should monitor monsoon progress closely.

What to watch in today's session

  1. IT Sector Performance: Given the Nifty IT index hitting a 52-week low and ongoing global concerns, monitor how IT stocks perform today. Any further weakness could drag the broader market.
  2. Banking Stocks: Watch for the reaction of banking stocks following the RBI's FCNR move, which analysts view as a potential tailwind. This could provide support to the financial sector.
  3. Corporate Action Impact: Observe the opening price movements of companies like Bajaj Holdings, Bajaj Finance, and Welspun Corp, which are trading ex-dividend today, as well as PATINTLOG which is ex-buyback.
  4. Auto Sector Trends: Keep an eye on auto stocks, particularly those with significant rural exposure, as concerns over a delayed monsoon could impact demand in the near term.
  5. FII/DII Activity: Continue to monitor the FII and DII flow data. Sustained DII buying support will be crucial in offsetting any potential FII outflows.

Bottom line

The Indian market is likely to open with mixed cues, influenced by strong gains in US markets but tempered by varied global performance and continued FII selling pressure. While the banking sector may find some tailwinds from the RBI's recent move, the IT sector faces headwinds, and concerns over monsoon progress could impact auto demand. Investors should remain cautious and focus on sector-specific developments and domestic institutional support.
