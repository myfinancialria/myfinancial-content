Market Wrap — 13 July 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, staged a remarkable recovery from early losses to close marginally higher.
  • Strong inflows from Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), coupled with a robust performance from the IT sector, were the primary drivers behind the market's rebound.
  • Newgen Software Technologies Ltd. emerged as the top gainer in the Nifty 500, surging over 14%.
  • Jammu & Kashmir Bank Ltd. was the biggest laggard among Nifty 500 constituents, declining over 5%.
  • Both FIIs and DIIs were net buyers in the cash segment today, injecting significant capital into the market.
  • F&O build-up data is unavailable for today's session, so no clear positioning signal can be derived.

How the market behaved today

Indian equity benchmarks displayed resilience today, recovering sharply from nearly 1% intraday losses to close marginally higher. The Nifty 50 ended the session at 24211.00, posting a modest gain of +0.02%. Similarly, the Bank Nifty closed at 58131.45, up +0.15%. Both indices witnessed a volatile day, bouncing back from their lows to finish in positive territory. The broader market also mirrored this trend, with Midcap and Smallcap indices registering slight gains.

The India VIX, a key measure of market volatility, saw a notable increase of +8.41%, closing at 13.28. This rise in the volatility index suggests that market participants anticipate higher price fluctuations in the near term, despite the indices closing in the green.

What moved the market — the factors

The Indian market's ability to recover from a weak opening was primarily driven by a combination of strong domestic buying interest and a significant rally in the Information Technology (IT) sector. Despite escalating global tensions between the US and Iran, which rattled sentiment across international markets and led to a decline in US stock futures, Indian equities found support.

A major factor contributing to the rebound was the robust performance of IT stocks. The sector surged over 3.5%, propelled by positive earnings news from heavyweights like HCL Technologies, which reported a 20% year-on-year profit growth and declared an interim dividend of ₹12 per share. This positive sentiment spilled over to other IT companies, leading to broad-based gains in the sector.

Furthermore, substantial inflows from both Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided a crucial cushion. FIIs injected ₹2,604 crore, while DIIs added ₹2,020 crore into the cash market today, demonstrating strong conviction in Indian equities. This consistent buying helped absorb the initial selling pressure and facilitated the market's recovery.

Global cues, however, remained a concern. The US-Iran escalation led to a surge in crude oil prices, with Brent Crude rising over 3.6%. This rekindled inflation concerns and put pressure on the Indian Rupee, which fell to its lowest level in over a month. The rising India VIX also indicated heightened investor anxiety regarding these geopolitical developments. Despite these headwinds, the strong performance of the IT sector and sustained institutional buying proved sufficient to push the indices back into positive territory.

News that moved stocks

HCL Technologies Reports Strong Q1 Results and Declares Dividend IT services major HCL Technologies announced a 20% year-on-year (YoY) increase in its consolidated net profit to ₹4,624 crore for the first quarter of FY27. The company also declared an interim dividend of ₹12 per share, marking its 94th consecutive quarter of payouts. This positive earnings report and dividend declaration significantly boosted sentiment for HCL Tech and the broader IT sector, which was the top-performing sector today.

US-Iran Escalation Fuels Oil Price Surge and Global Tensions Geopolitical tensions escalated between the US and Iran, leading to a decline in US stock futures and a significant surge in crude oil prices. Iran's announcement of a closure of the Strait of Hormuz pushed Brent Crude up by over 3.6%. This development rekindled inflation concerns in Eurozone bond markets and caused the Indian Rupee to fall to its lowest level in over a month, signaling potential headwinds for the Indian economy.

SBI Funds Management Plans Mega IPO to Revive Primary Market SBI Funds Management is looking to launch a substantial ₹9,813 crore IPO, with CEO Debasish Mishra expressing confidence that attractive pricing, strong governance, and industry growth will revive investor confidence and boost India's primary market. This news could potentially bring fresh liquidity and investor interest into the broader market.

SEBI Tightens Ethics Rules for Current and Former Employees India's market regulator, SEBI, has introduced stricter ethics rules, including a two-year cooling-off period for past officials and broader investment restrictions that now include family members of employees. This regulatory move aims to enhance transparency and integrity within the financial markets.

JSW Infrastructure Among Stocks Hitting 52-Week Highs Despite a generally subdued market, seven BSE 200 stocks, including JSW Infrastructure, Paytm, Nykaa, and Oracle Financial Services, hit fresh 52-week highs. This indicates strong investor confidence and sustained buying interest in these specific companies, reflecting their robust performance and growth prospects.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's session was significantly influenced by two primary factors: escalating geopolitical tensions and strong corporate earnings. The major event that cast a shadow over global markets was the heightened US-Iran conflict, which led to Iran's threat to close the Strait of Hormuz. This immediately sent crude oil prices soaring, with Brent Crude jumping over 3.6%, and triggered concerns about global inflation and economic stability. The Indian Rupee reacted negatively, weakening against the US Dollar.

However, the Indian market demonstrated remarkable resilience, primarily due to strong Q1 results from key companies, particularly in the IT sector. HCL Technologies' announcement of a 20% profit rise and a ₹12 interim dividend provided a significant boost to IT stocks. This positive news, combined with robust buying from both FIIs and DIIs, helped the Nifty and Bank Nifty recover all their morning losses and close in positive territory. While global headwinds from rising oil prices and geopolitical uncertainty remain, the domestic market found strong support from corporate performance and institutional flows.

Setup for tomorrow

As we head into tomorrow's session, global cues will remain critical. US stock futures are already showing declines due to the US-Iran escalation, which could impact Asian markets overnight. The continued surge in Brent Crude oil prices, now near $78.75, will be a key factor to watch, as sustained high oil prices could further pressure the Rupee and fuel inflation concerns. Investors will also monitor the Dollar Index and US 10-year Treasury yields for signs of global risk sentiment.

Domestically, the strength of the IT sector, following today's positive momentum, will be in focus. The Nifty 50's ability to hold above the 24,000 level, which acted as a strong technical support today, will be crucial. Any further news on the geopolitical front or corporate earnings could dictate market direction.
