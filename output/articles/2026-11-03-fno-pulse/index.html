Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. In options, the strike price with the highest Call OI is often seen as a potential resistance level, as many Call option sellers (who profit if the price stays below the strike) are positioned there. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI is often viewed as a potential support level, as Put option sellers (who profit if the price stays above the strike) have concentrated their positions there. These are often referred to as "OI walls."

For the NIFTY 50, the heaviest Call OI wall is observed at 24,000, which is approximately 0.8% above today's closing spot. This suggests that option writers believe the index might struggle to move significantly beyond this level in the near term. On the Put side, fresh writing was concentrated near 23,600, indicating that Put sellers are expecting this level to act as a floor.

The SENSEX saw notable fresh Put writing near 75,833. This positioning by Put sellers suggests an expectation of support around this level, implying they do not anticipate the index falling below it significantly.

In BANK NIFTY, the heaviest Put OI is currently at 58,000, which is a considerable distance below the current spot. However, today's fresh Put writing was concentrated closer to 56,000, indicating that option sellers are actively building positions expecting the index to find support around this level.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR(OI) value above 1 typically suggests more Put writing than Call writing, which can be interpreted as a bullish sentiment (Put writers expect prices to rise or stay stable). A value below 1, conversely, suggests more Call writing, which can be seen as bearish (Call writers expect prices to fall or stay stable). PCR(today's flow ΔOI) looks at only the change in Open Interest for the day, giving insight into fresh positioning.

For NIFTY 50, the PCR(OI) stands at 0.74, suggesting a slightly higher concentration of Call Open Interest overall, indicating a balanced to mildly cautious stance from option writers. Today's flow ΔPCR, however, was 1.1, indicating that fresh Put writing was slightly more prominent than fresh Call writing during the session, suggesting a balanced fresh flow.

SENSEX recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.83, also indicating a relatively balanced book with a slight lean towards Call Open Interest. Its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was 1.16, implying that fresh Put writing outweighed fresh Call writing today, suggesting a marginally positive sentiment from new option positions.

The BANK NIFTY showed a PCR(OI) of 0.84, similar to the other indices in terms of overall balance. However, its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was significantly high at 11.75. This exceptionally high value signals that today's fresh option writing was overwhelmingly dominated by Put options, a strong indication that option sellers are positioning for the index to hold or move higher.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It's often considered a "magnet" for the underlying index as expiry approaches, as option writers may try to influence the price towards this point to maximise their profits or minimise losses.

For NIFTY 50, the Max Pain level is at 23,800, which is very close to its current spot price of 23,810.85. This suggests that option writers are currently positioned for the index to expire around its current levels.

SENSEX has its Max Pain at 76,700, which is slightly below its spot of 76,917.85. This indicates a potential pull towards this level as expiry nears.

The BANK NIFTY's Max Pain is at 56,000, which is approximately 0.8% above its current spot of 55,540.05. This positioning of Max Pain above the current price suggests that option writers are collectively positioned for a slight upward movement or consolidation at higher levels by expiry.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 12.3%, meaning that only 12.3% of their total Index futures positions were long, while the vast majority were short. Historically, such a low long ratio in Index futures tends to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from FIIs on the broader market. In Stock futures, their long ratio was 58.4%, indicating a more balanced to mildly bullish stance on individual stocks.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these F&O data points can provide valuable context for market movements. You can access much of this information directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website. Look for the "Derivatives" section, where you'll find "Option Chain" data for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and other indices. This table displays the Open Interest for various Call and Put strikes, allowing you to identify the "walls" (highest OI strikes) and observe where fresh writing (change in OI) is occurring.

Additionally, your brokerage platform might offer tools to visualise this data more easily, including PCR and Max Pain calculations. By regularly checking the Option Chain, the daily change in Open Interest, and the FII participant-wise Open Interest data (also available on the NSE website under "Participant-wise Open Interest"), you can begin to identify patterns and understand how large participants are positioning themselves. Remember, these are tools for analysis, not guarantees of future price movements. The goal is to understand the market's underlying structure and participant positioning, not to predict exact prices.
