Market Wrap — 03 June 2026

TL;DR

  • The Nifty 50 closed marginally lower, while the Bank Nifty showed resilience with gains.
  • The IT sector witnessed a significant sell-off, emerging as the top loser of the day.
  • Banking and Pharma sectors were among the top performers, offsetting some of the broader market weakness.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong buying support.
  • Market breadth remained negative, indicating more declining stocks than advancing ones.

Indian equities experienced a volatile session today, with the Nifty 50 closing slightly in the red at 23405.60, down 0.33%. In contrast, the Bank Nifty demonstrated strength, ending the day with a gain of 0.88% at 54185.95.

Market breadth

The market breadth was negative today, with 206 advances against 293 declines out of a total of 499 stocks. This indicates that a larger number of stocks lost value compared to those that gained, suggesting a cautious sentiment across the broader market despite some indices recovering from their lows.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued to be net sellers in the Indian equity markets, offloading shares worth ₹8,363 crore today. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, provided strong counter-support, injecting ₹9,589 crore into the market.

| Investor Type | Net Cash Position (₹ crore) | |---|---| | FII | -8,363 | | DII | 9,589 |

Looking at the 5-day history, FIIs have consistently been net sellers, with today marking the largest outflow in the past five trading sessions. Conversely, DIIs have been consistent net buyers, absorbing the selling pressure from foreign investors.

What moved markets today

IT Sector Plunge: The Information Technology sector witnessed a sharp correction, with major IT services companies like TCS, Persistent Systems, LTM, Coforge, and Tech Mahindra all experiencing significant declines. The Nifty IT index was the biggest sectoral loser, dropping by 5.57%. This broad-based selling in IT stocks weighed heavily on the overall market sentiment.

Government Policy Hopes Fuel Recovery: Indian stock markets staged a strong comeback in the afternoon session, recovering significantly from their day's lows. This sharp rebound was reportedly driven by media reports indicating that the government plans to cut taxes and remove ownership caps in certain sectors. This news provided a much-needed boost, helping the Sensex recover 850 points from its low and the Nifty close above 23,400.

Rupee Weakens Amid Global Tensions and US Duties: The Indian Rupee depreciated by 31 paise to close at 95.67 against the US dollar. This weakening was influenced by proposed US additional duties on Indian imports due to alleged labor violations, strong dollar demand, and rising crude oil prices.

Rising Crude Oil Prices and Middle East Tensions: Global crude oil prices saw an uptick, with Brent Crude closing at $97.67 (+1.74%) and WTI Crude at $95.79 (+2.17%). This rise was attributed to renewed Middle East tensions following reports of Iranian missile activity. Higher crude prices typically pose an inflation risk for India, which is a major oil importer.

SEBI Action Against Rajesh Exports: Market regulator SEBI took action against Rajesh Exports and its promoter Rajesh Mehta, barring them from trading after alleging large-scale financial misrepresentation and possible revenue inflation of 97-99%. This regulatory action highlights ongoing efforts to ensure market integrity.

Setup for tomorrow

Tomorrow's session will likely see investors reacting to the overnight global cues and the continued FII selling trend. The strong recovery seen in the latter half of today's session, driven by hopes of government policy changes, could provide some positive momentum. However, the significant correction in the IT sector and rising crude oil prices will remain key factors to watch.

Globally, US stocks edged lower as investors consolidated after a string of records, with fresh Middle East tensions and rising crude oil prices pressuring sentiment. Asian markets will also be watched for their opening cues. The rupee's depreciation and the ongoing FII outflows will be crucial for market direction.
