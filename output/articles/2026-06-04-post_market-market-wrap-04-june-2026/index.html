Market Wrap — 04 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed marginally higher after a volatile session.
  • Broader markets, represented by Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100, outperformed the frontline indices.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, offloading ₹5,617 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support with net buying of ₹5,741 crore.
  • Media and Energy sectors were among the top gainers, while Metal and IT sectors faced selling pressure.
  • Global cues were mixed, with US markets reacting negatively to a Broadcom revenue miss, while the Indian Rupee depreciated ahead of the RBI's monetary policy review.

How the market closed

Indian equity benchmarks witnessed a day of cautious trading, with the Nifty 50 managing to close marginally higher at 23416.55, up by 0.05%. The Bank Nifty also ended in positive territory, gaining 0.22% to settle at 54307.85. Broader markets, however, showed stronger momentum, with both the Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100 indices posting more significant gains.

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, eased by 2.40% to 15.89, indicating a slight reduction in market apprehension.

Market breadth

The market breadth leaned positive today, with 266 advances against 234 declines out of the Nifty 500 stocks. This indicates that while the benchmark indices saw modest gains, a slightly larger number of stocks participated in the upward movement, suggesting a relatively healthy underlying sentiment in the broader market.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree, recording a net outflow of ₹5,617 crore today. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, provided crucial support to the market, with a net inflow of ₹5,741 crore, largely offsetting the FII selling pressure.

| Investor Type | Net Position (₹ crore) | |---|---| | FII | -5,617 | | DII | 5,741 |

Over the past five trading sessions, FIIs have been consistent net sellers, with cumulative outflows totaling ₹44,041 crore. In contrast, DIIs have consistently been net buyers, injecting a cumulative ₹40,924 crore into the market over the same period, providing a strong counter-balance to foreign selling.

What moved markets today

1. US Tech Sell-off on Broadcom's Revenue Miss The US stock markets, particularly the Nasdaq and S&P 500, opened lower today after Broadcom reported a revenue miss, which subsequently pressured chip stocks. This global tech sector weakness likely contributed to the cautious sentiment in India's IT sector. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq dropped at the open, as Broadcom's revenue miss pressured chip stocks, while equity investors took a breather after a strong rally to record highs.

2. Hopes for Tax Cuts on Indian Bonds Indian bond markets found some support amidst hopes that the government is considering scrapping the 12.5% capital gains tax on overseas investors and the 20% withholding tax on interest earned by foreign investors. Citi's Aditya Bagree suggests that the removal of withholding tax could unlock $25 billion in bond inflows. This potential policy shift could significantly boost foreign investment in Indian bonds.

3. RBI Monetary Policy Review Underway The Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) began its three-day meeting on June 3, with the decision set to be unveiled tomorrow, June 5. Ahead of this crucial announcement, the Indian Rupee declined for a second day, closing at 95.78 against the USD, as traders anticipate the central bank's stance on interest rates and measures to support the currency. The outcome of this review will be a key determinant for market direction tomorrow.

Setup for tomorrow

Tomorrow's market direction will largely hinge on the outcome of the Reserve Bank of India's monetary policy review, scheduled for 10 AM IST. Investors will be keenly watching for any cues on interest rates and the central bank's outlook on inflation and growth. Domestically, the continued FII selling trend versus DII buying will also be a critical factor.

Globally, US stock futures are pointing lower amid the tech sell-off triggered by Broadcom's disappointing outlook, which could weigh on Indian IT stocks. Asian markets will likely react to these overnight developments. Key data releases overnight and any further developments in global commodity prices, especially crude, will also be on the radar.
