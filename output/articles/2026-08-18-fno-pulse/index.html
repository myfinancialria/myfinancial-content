Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. In the options market, the strike price with the highest Call OI is often seen as a significant resistance level, as many writers (sellers) do not expect the price to rise above it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI is typically viewed as a strong support level, as Put writers do not anticipate the price falling below it.

For the NIFTY 50, the heaviest Call OI is observed at the 24200 strike, while the heaviest Put OI stands at 24150. Today, fresh Call writing was most prominent near 24200, indicating that Call sellers anticipate the upside to be capped around this level. Simultaneously, fresh Put writing was concentrated near 24100, suggesting Put sellers are comfortable establishing a floor at this strike.

In the SENSEX, the 78000 strike holds the heaviest Call OI, acting as a substantial ceiling. Today's heaviest fresh Call writing was seen around 77633, reinforcing the expectation among Call sellers that the index may struggle to advance beyond these levels. Fresh Put writing near 75900 suggests a base is being built around this area.

For BANK NIFTY, the heaviest Put OI is located at 58000. This concentration of Put writing suggests that option sellers believe the 58000 level could provide robust support for the index.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value below 1.0 generally suggests that Call Open Interest is greater than Put Open Interest, which can be interpreted as a bearish signal, indicating that more participants are betting on a fall or capping of the upside. Conversely, a PCR above 1.0 often suggests a bullish bias.

For the NIFTY 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.64. This value, being well below 1.0, indicates that Call options dominate, suggesting that upside movement might be capped. Today's flow, as measured by the change in PCR (ΔPCR), was -0.04, indicating a relatively mixed or balanced flow of fresh Call and Put writing during the session.

The SENSEX recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.68, also signalling a dominance of Call options and potential resistance. The ΔPCR for today's flow was 0.34, which suggests that fresh writing activity during the day was notably Call-heavy, further reinforcing a bearish sentiment from new positions.

BANK NIFTY has a PCR(OI) of 0.69, similar to the other indices, implying that Call positions are more prominent. However, its ΔPCR for today's flow was -0.22, indicating a more balanced fresh writing activity, despite the overall OI leaning bearish.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying index were to settle at that specific price on expiry. It is often considered a "magnet" for the index, especially as the weekly or monthly expiry date approaches, as market participants tend to gravitate towards this level.

For the NIFTY 50, the Max Pain level is identified at 24300, which is approximately 0.6% above its current spot price. This suggests a potential upward pull towards this strike as expiry draws closer.

The SENSEX has its Max Pain point at 78000, standing about 1.0% above the current spot. This indicates a significant potential upward attraction towards this level.

BANK NIFTY's Max Pain is at 58000, approximately 1.3% above its spot price. This also suggests a notable upward pull for the index towards this strike.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) play a significant role in the Indian markets, and their derivative positioning offers insights into their collective sentiment.

Today, the FII Index futures long ratio stands at 10.4%. This means that out of their total index futures positions, only 10.4% are long, while the vast majority (89.6%) are short. A long ratio this low, with 25,502 long contracts versus 219,660 short contracts, indicates a substantial net short position in index futures by FIIs. Historically, such a pronounced net short stance from institutional players has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from these significant market participants.

In contrast, FIIs hold a more balanced to slightly bullish view in stock futures, with a long ratio of 54.7% (3,554,988 long contracts against 2,949,625 short contracts). This suggests that while they are cautious on the broader index, they maintain a more constructive view on individual stocks.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to form your own informed perspective. To read this data yourself daily, you would typically look for several key data points. These include the spot price of the index, the Open Interest (OI) at various Call and Put strike prices, the Put-Call Ratio (PCR), and the Max Pain level.

You can find the option chain data, including OI, on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the 'Derivatives' section. Many brokerage platforms also provide this information in an easily digestible format. To calculate PCR, you'd sum up the total Put OI and total Call OI for the entire option chain and divide the former by the latter. Identifying Max Pain usually involves looking for the strike where the combined OI of Calls and Puts is lowest, or using readily available calculators online. By observing where the heaviest Call and Put OI "walls" are, where fresh writing is occurring, and how the PCR and FII positioning are trending, you can begin to build a comprehensive picture of market participants' expectations for the upcoming sessions.
