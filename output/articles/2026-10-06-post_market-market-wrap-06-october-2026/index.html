What moved the market — the factors

The Indian market's rebound today was primarily driven by a confluence of positive global cues and sustained domestic buying interest. US stocks, particularly the S&P 500 and Nasdaq, hit all-time highs, buoyed by falling Treasury yields and easing oil prices, which fueled an AI rally. This positive sentiment spilled over into Indian equities. Falling crude prices also provided a tailwind, easing concerns about inflation and current account deficit.

Domestically, strong Q2 business updates from key companies like Honasa Consumer and Titan provided stock-specific momentum. While Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) stepped in with robust buying, effectively cushioning the market and driving it higher.

Sector-wise, Pharma, FMCG, and Energy led the gains, indicating a broad-based recovery in defensive and consumption-oriented sectors. Conversely, IT and Realty sectors witnessed some profit-booking, acting as minor drags on the overall market. The significant drop in India VIX also suggested increasing investor confidence and reduced uncertainty.

F&O build-up — where traders are positioning

OI build-up needs the previous session's OI — it will populate from the next run.

Interpretation: Long build-up, where both price and Open Interest (OI) increase, typically signals fresh buying with conviction, suggesting that traders expect the upward trend to continue. Short build-up, characterized by a price drop and an increase in OI, indicates fresh selling pressure, implying a bearish outlook. Short covering, where the price rises while OI decreases, suggests that traders who had previously sold are now buying back to close their positions, leading to a bounce. This doesn't always reflect genuine underlying strength. Lastly, long unwinding, where both price and OI decrease, indicates that traders who had bought earlier are now selling their positions, often due to profit-taking or a weakening outlook.

Today's session was primarily driven by broad market flows and positive global cues rather than a single defining domestic event. The rebound in Indian equities was significantly influenced by the strong performance of US markets, where the S&P 500 and Nasdaq hit record highs, fueled by easing Treasury yields and an ongoing AI rally. Additionally, falling crude prices provided a positive sentiment boost for the Indian economy.

Domestically, a series of strong Q2 business updates from companies like Honasa Consumer and Titan provided stock-specific triggers, contributing to the overall positive momentum. While there was no major policy announcement or economic data release today, the consistent buying by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) helped absorb the continuous selling pressure from Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs), ensuring the market maintained its upward trajectory.
