Pre-Market Brief — 12 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets ended significantly higher, with US indices leading the rally, setting a positive tone.
  • Crude oil prices declined, while gold and silver saw substantial gains overnight.
  • FIIs continued their selling streak, offloading nearly ₹2,000 crore, while DIIs provided strong support with over ₹4,200 crore in net buying.
  • A slew of corporate actions, including dividends from major companies like Adani Enterprises, Tata Steel, and ACC, along with a bonus issue from CUB, are scheduled today.
  • The banking sector showed strength yesterday, with Nifty Bank surging, potentially influenced by global geopolitical developments.

Where global markets closed

Global equity markets closed on a strong positive note yesterday, reflecting broad-based optimism. US indices led the charge, with the Nasdaq surging over 2.5% and the Dow Jones and S&P 500 also posting significant gains. Asian and European markets followed suit, indicating a generally buoyant sentiment across major economies.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, with USD/INR closing at 95.36, down 0.29%. This indicates a slight appreciation for the Rupee, which can be a positive for import costs and inflation. The Dollar Index also saw a minor dip of 0.08% to 99.78, suggesting a broader weakening of the greenback.

In commodities, crude oil prices experienced a notable decline. Brent Crude fell 2.00% to $88.57, and WTI Crude was down 1.85% at $86.09. Lower crude oil prices are generally beneficial for India, as it is a major oil importer, potentially easing inflationary pressures and improving trade balance.

Gold and Silver, however, witnessed significant rallies. Gold surged 2.74% to $4202.50, and Silver jumped 4.44% to $66.72. This sharp rise in precious metals could signal renewed safe-haven demand or inflationary concerns despite the dip in crude.

The US 10-Year Yield decreased by 1.74% to 4.46%. A falling US yield can make emerging markets like India more attractive to foreign investors, as it narrows the interest rate differential.

Recent FII/DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling trend yesterday, offloading equities worth ₹1,987 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, provided strong counter-support, injecting ₹4,225 crore into the market.

Over the last five trading sessions, FIIs have consistently been net sellers, with cumulative outflows totaling ₹22,910 crore. Conversely, DIIs have been consistent net buyers, providing robust support to the Indian markets with cumulative inflows of ₹27,707 crore over the same period. This trend highlights the continued domestic resilience against foreign outflows.

Overnight news that matters

Elon Musk's net worth crosses $980 billion as blockbuster SpaceX IPO price set at $135 SpaceX's IPO has been priced at $135 per share, valuing the company at around $1.8 trillion and significantly boosting Elon Musk's wealth. This news, while specific to a US company, signifies strong investor appetite for high-growth tech companies globally and could positively influence sentiment towards Indian tech and innovation-driven stocks.

Nifty Bank surges 700 points to one-month high; HDFC Bank, Yes Bank, PNB, other stocks rise 2%. What lies ahead? The Nifty Bank index saw a substantial jump, reaching a one-month peak, reportedly driven by news of a potential peace deal between the US and Iran, which also led to a decline in oil prices. This development is crucial for Indian markets as a strong banking sector often underpins overall market health, and lower oil prices are a direct positive for India's economy.

Gold on course to log worst yearly fall since 2013 if West Asia troubles continue. Time to sell? Despite ongoing geopolitical tensions, gold has fallen over 25% from its peak, diverging from its traditional safe-haven role, partly due to profit booking and expectations of higher interest rates. This is relevant for Indian investors given the cultural significance and investment in gold; the article suggests a re-evaluation of gold's role in portfolios amidst changing market dynamics.

Vedanta demerger: Which demerged stock should you buy after their market debut on June 15? Vedanta's demerger will see four new companies listing on June 15, with analysts suggesting Vedanta Aluminium Metal as a strong buy. This news is highly significant for investors holding Vedanta shares, as it impacts their portfolio structure and offers new investment opportunities within the group.

What to watch in today's session

  1. FII/DII Activity: The persistent FII selling trend versus DII buying will be a key determinant of market direction. Any moderation in FII outflows or continued strong DII support will be closely watched.
  2. Crude Oil Prices: Following the overnight dip, further movements in Brent and WTI crude will be crucial. Sustained lower oil prices could provide tailwinds for Indian equities and help manage inflation.
  3. Banking Sector Momentum: After yesterday's surge, observe if the Nifty Bank index can sustain its upward momentum, especially given the potential geopolitical developments impacting oil prices.
  4. Stocks with Corporate Actions: Companies like Adani Enterprises, Adani Ports, Tata Steel, ACC, PNB, and Canara Bank going ex-dividend, and City Union Bank going ex-bonus, will see price adjustments and potentially increased trading volumes.
  5. Nifty Technical Levels: As per expert views, Nifty needs to close above 23,400 to trigger short covering. Traders will be monitoring these critical resistance levels.

Bottom line

The Indian market is likely to open with a positive bias, taking cues from strong global market performance and a decline in crude oil prices. However, the continued selling by FIIs remains a point of caution, with DIIs providing crucial domestic support. Investors should carefully monitor the banking sector's performance and the impact of numerous corporate actions scheduled for today.
