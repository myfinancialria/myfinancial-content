Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been closed out or expired. It essentially shows the total number of positions currently held by market participants. When we look at the option chain, the strike prices with the highest Call OI and highest Put OI are often referred to as "walls" because they indicate levels where a large number of option sellers (writers) have taken positions, expecting the index to stay below the Call wall and above the Put wall.

For the NIFTY 50, the heaviest fresh Put writing today was observed near the 22,700 strike. This suggests that Put option sellers believe 22,700 could act as a floor or support level, indicating a bullish expectation around that price point. Conversely, significant Call OI walls would typically suggest resistance.

On the SENSEX, the heaviest fresh Put writing was seen near 73,500. This implies that Put writers are positioning for 73,500 to potentially serve as a strong support level, anticipating the index to remain above it.

For the BANK NIFTY, while the overall picture is mixed, the heaviest Call Open Interest at 56,000, which is just 0.3% above the current spot price, suggests that this level could act as a strong resistance or ceiling in the immediate term. Call writers at 56,000 are betting the index will not rise significantly above this point.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator calculated by dividing the total Open Interest (OI) of Put options by the total Open Interest of Call options. It helps gauge overall market sentiment. A PCR above 1 generally suggests a more bullish sentiment, as more Put options are being written (sellers expect prices to rise). A PCR below 1 often indicates a bearish sentiment, as more Call options are being written (sellers expect prices to fall). We also look at the PCR based on today's change in Open Interest (ΔOI) to understand fresh activity.

For the NIFTY 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 1.14. This value, being above 1, suggests a slightly balanced to bullish bias from the overall options book. However, today's flow, represented by PCR(today flow ΔOI) at 0.88, indicates that fresh writing activity was more inclined towards Call options, suggesting a somewhat mixed or cautious sentiment for the day.

The SENSEX recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.96 and a PCR(today flow ΔOI) of 0.96. Both values being close to 1 suggest a relatively balanced options book and balanced fresh writing activity for the day, indicating neither a strong bullish nor bearish conviction from option writers.

For the BANK NIFTY, the PCR(OI) is 0.97, suggesting a near-balanced overall options book. Interestingly, today's flow, with a PCR(today flow ΔOI) of 1.45, shows a significant inclination towards fresh Put writing. This indicates that a notable portion of today's new options positions were Put options being sold, which is typically seen as a bullish signal for the immediate term.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying asset expires at that level. It's often considered a magnetic point because, as expiry approaches, the index tends to gravitate towards this strike, as it's the point of least pain for the majority of option sellers.

For the NIFTY 50, the Max Pain strike is at 22,950. With the spot closing at 23,146.15, this means Max Pain is approximately 0.8% below the current spot price. This suggests a potential downward pull towards 22,950 as the expiry date draws closer.

The SENSEX has its Max Pain at 74,500. Given the spot price of 74,729.27, Max Pain is about 0.3% below the current level. This also indicates a slight inclination for the index to potentially drift lower towards this strike by expiry.

The BANK NIFTY's Max Pain is at 55,700, which is very close to its closing spot price of 55,844.00. Being almost at the spot level, this suggests that option writers are currently positioned for the Bank Nifty to expire around its current levels, implying a neutral or range-bound outlook.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held a long ratio of 11.3% in Index futures. This means that out of their total open positions in Index futures, only 11.3% were long contracts, while the vast majority (88.7%) were short. Historically, a significantly low FII long ratio in index futures, indicating a predominantly net short position, has often coincided with periods of caution or consolidation in the broader market.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of 56.0%. This indicates a more balanced to slightly bullish stance by FIIs in individual stock futures compared to their cautious positioning in index futures.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these F&O data points daily can empower you to form your own informed perspective on market sentiment. You can start by looking at the Open Interest data for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty options chains, which is freely available on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the 'Derivatives' section. Look for the 'Option Chain' for the current expiry.

Once there, identify the strike prices with the highest Call OI and Put OI; these are your immediate resistance and support levels. Calculate the PCR by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI. Also, track the change in OI (ΔOI) to see where fresh positions are being built, and calculate a PCR based on ΔOI for today's flow. For Max Pain, some financial portals or your broker's platform might provide this calculation, or you can calculate it manually by finding the strike where total Call and Put premiums are minimised for option writers. Finally, keep an eye on the FII derivative statistics, also available on the NSE archives, to understand how institutional money is positioned. Combining these data points can help you build a comprehensive daily view of the market's underlying structure.
