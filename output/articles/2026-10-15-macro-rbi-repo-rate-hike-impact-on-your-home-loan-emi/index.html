RBI Repo Rate Hike: Impact on Your Home Loan EMI

TL;DR

  • The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has increased the repo rate by 25 basis points (bps) to 5.50% on October 7, 2026.
  • This hike will directly affect your home loan interest rates, especially if your loan is linked to an external benchmark.
  • Borrowers may see an increase in their monthly Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI) or an extension of their loan repayment tenure.
  • Understanding your loan's benchmark and current interest rate is crucial to assess the immediate impact.
  • Proactive steps like partial prepayments or switching lenders can help mitigate the financial burden.

What this means in plain terms

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) recently announced an increase in its key lending rate, known as the repo rate, by 0.25 percentage points. This means that commercial banks now have to pay slightly more to borrow money from the RBI. This change, while seemingly small, has a direct ripple effect on the interest rates that banks offer to their customers, especially for home loans.

For you, as a home loan borrower, this typically translates into higher interest costs. Your bank will likely pass on this increased cost of funds by raising the interest rate on your existing home loan. Depending on your loan agreement, this could mean that your monthly EMI goes up, or your loan tenure gets extended, or sometimes even a combination of both, increasing your overall financial outflow.

Understanding the Repo Rate and RBI's Role

The repo rate is the interest rate at which commercial banks borrow money from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) against government securities. It's a crucial tool used by the RBI to control the money supply and manage inflation in the economy. When the RBI wants to curb inflation, it typically raises the repo rate, making borrowing more expensive for banks. Conversely, to stimulate economic growth, the RBI might lower the repo rate, encouraging banks to lend more cheaply.

On October 7, 2026, the RBI's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) decided to raise the repo rate by 25 basis points, moving it from 5.25% to 5.50%. This decision was primarily driven by the need to manage inflationary pressures within the Indian economy. By making money more expensive for banks, the RBI aims to reduce overall borrowing and spending, thereby cooling down demand and helping to stabilise prices. This move signals the RBI's commitment to maintaining price stability as a key objective of its monetary policy.

How Repo Rate Influences Your Home Loan Interest

The repo rate hike directly impacts your home loan interest rate, especially if your loan is linked to an external benchmark. Since October 2019, most new floating rate retail loans, including home loans, have been mandated to be linked to an external benchmark. The most common external benchmark is the RBI's repo rate itself.

External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR)

For home loans linked to the External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR), the change is almost immediate and direct. Banks typically add a spread (a fixed margin) to the repo rate to arrive at your final EBLR. So, if the repo rate increases by 25 bps, your EBLR-linked home loan interest rate will also increase by 25 bps. For example, if your loan was at Repo Rate + 2.50%, and the repo rate moves from 5.25% to 5.50%, your interest rate will jump from 7.75% to 8.00%. The reset frequency for EBLR-linked loans is usually at least once every three months, meaning the impact of the hike will be felt within this period.

Marginal Cost of Funds-based Lending Rate (MCLR)

While less common for newer loans, some older home loans might still be linked to the Marginal Cost of Funds-based Lending Rate (MCLR). MCLR is an internal benchmark set by individual banks based on their marginal cost of funds. While not directly linked to the repo rate, an increase in the repo rate eventually pushes up banks' cost of funds, leading them to revise their MCLR upwards. However, the transmission of a repo rate hike to MCLR-linked loans is typically slower and less direct compared to EBLR-linked loans. MCLR rates are usually reset after a period of 6 months or 1 year, depending on the loan agreement.

Navigating the Impact: EMI Increase or Tenure Extension?

When your home loan interest rate rises due to a repo rate hike, banks generally offer two primary ways to adjust your loan repayment: either by increasing your monthly EMI or by extending your loan repayment tenure. The choice often depends on the bank's policy and sometimes, your preference.

Increasing Your EMI

If your bank decides to increase your EMI, your monthly outgo will rise. This option ensures that your loan is repaid within the original tenure. For many borrowers, this is the preferred option if their financial situation allows, as it means they pay less overall interest in the long run. A higher EMI helps you stick to your original repayment schedule and become debt-free sooner. However, it requires a higher disposable income each month.

Extending Your Loan Tenure

Alternatively, your bank might extend your loan repayment tenure while keeping your EMI amount largely constant. This option can be appealing if you wish to maintain your current monthly budget. However, extending the tenure means you will be paying interest for a longer period, significantly increasing the total interest paid over the life of the loan. For example, a loan that was initially for 20 years might get extended to 22 or 23 years, adding substantial interest costs. Banks typically have a maximum age limit for loan repayment (e.g., 60 or 65 years), which might restrict how much tenure can be extended.

It is crucial to communicate with your bank to understand which option they will implement and if you have a choice. Many banks default to extending tenure to avoid surprising customers with higher EMIs, but you can often request an EMI increase if you prefer.

A real example

Let's consider Rahul, 38, earning ₹28L CTC, residing in Bengaluru. He took a home loan of ₹50 lakhs for 20 years (240 months) at an initial interest rate of 8.50% per annum, linked to the RBI repo rate.

Before the Repo Rate Hike:

  • Loan Amount (P): ₹50,00,000
  • Annual Interest Rate (R): 8.50%
  • Monthly Interest Rate (r): 8.50% / 12 / 100 = 0.007083
  • Loan Tenure (N): 240 months
  • Using the EMI formula: EMI = P r (1 + r)^N / ((1 + r)^N – 1)
  • Rahul's initial EMI was approximately ₹43,391.

After the 25 bps Repo Rate Hike: The RBI increased the repo rate by 25 bps (0.25%). Assuming his bank passes on the full hike, Rahul's new interest rate will be 8.50% + 0.25% = 8.75% per annum.

Now, let's see the impact under two scenarios:

Scenario 1: EMI increases, tenure remains same

  • New Annual Interest Rate (R): 8.75%
  • New Monthly Interest Rate (r): 8.75% / 12 / 100 = 0.007291
  • Loan Tenure (N): 240 months
  • Rahul's new EMI will be approximately ₹44,057.
  • Increase in EMI: ₹44,057 - ₹43,391 = ₹666 per month.

Scenario 2: Tenure extends, EMI remains (roughly) same If Rahul's bank keeps his EMI at ₹43,391 and increases the interest rate to 8.75%, his remaining loan tenure will extend. To calculate the exact extension, we would need to know the remaining principal amount and re-calculate the tenure. However, generally, a 25 bps hike on a ₹50 lakh loan with a remaining tenure of, say, 18 years, could extend the tenure by approximately 12-18 months, leading to significantly higher total interest paid over the life of the loan. For instance, if Rahul had 15 years (180 months) remaining on his loan, his new tenure might increase to around 195-198 months, adding over a year to his repayment period and increasing the total interest paid by several lakhs.

This example clearly shows that even a small hike of 25 bps can have a noticeable impact on your monthly budget or the overall cost of your home loan.

What to do this week

  1. Check your loan statement: Locate your latest home loan statement to identify your current interest rate, outstanding principal, and whether your loan is linked to EBLR or MCLR. This will help you understand how quickly the rate hike will impact you.
  2. Contact your bank: Call your bank's customer service or visit your branch to inquire about the specific impact of the repo rate hike on your home loan. Ask if your EMI will increase or if your tenure will be extended, and if you have a choice in the matter.
  3. Review your budget: Assess your current monthly budget to see if you can comfortably accommodate a higher EMI. If not, start exploring options like partial prepayments or considering a balance transfer.
  4. Explore prepayment options: If you have any surplus funds, even a small partial prepayment can help reduce your principal outstanding, thereby lessening the impact of the interest rate hike.
  5. Research balance transfer options: If the rate hike makes your current loan significantly more expensive, investigate if other banks are offering lower interest rates for a home loan balance transfer.

FAQ

What is the repo rate?

The repo rate is the interest rate at which commercial banks borrow money from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) by selling government securities with an agreement to repurchase them later. It's a key monetary policy tool.

How often does the RBI change the repo rate?

The RBI's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) meets at least six times a year, typically every two months, to review the economic situation and decide on policy rates, including the repo rate. Changes can also happen off-cycle if deemed necessary.

Does a repo rate hike affect fixed-rate home loans?

No, a repo rate hike does not directly affect fixed-rate home loans. The interest rate on fixed-rate loans remains constant throughout the loan tenure, irrespective of changes in the repo rate or other market benchmarks.

Can I switch my home loan to another bank after a repo rate hike?

Yes, you can consider a home loan balance transfer to another bank offering a lower interest rate, especially if your current bank's rates have become uncompetitive after the hike. This can help reduce your EMI or shorten your tenure.

How does this hike impact new home loan applicants?

New home loan applicants will face higher interest rates compared to before the hike. Banks will price their new loans based on the revised repo rate, leading to higher EMIs for the same loan amount and tenure.

Is there any tax benefit related to home loan interest?

Yes, under Section 24(b) of the Income Tax Act, you can claim a deduction of up to ₹2 lakhs on the interest paid on a home loan for a self-occupied property. For rented properties, the entire interest paid can be deducted.

What is a basis point (bps)?

A basis point is a common unit of measure in finance, equal to one-hundredth of a percentage point. So, 25 bps equals 0.25%.
