Market Wrap — 18 August 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Sensex, extended their losing streak for the sixth consecutive session today.
  • The primary drivers for the market's decline were persistent foreign institutional investor (FII) selling, rising global bond yields, elevated crude oil prices, and geopolitical concerns.
  • Jyoti CNC Automation Ltd. emerged as the standout gainer among Nifty 500 stocks, surging over 8%.
  • SBI Cards and Payment Services Ltd. was the biggest loser in the Nifty 500, shedding over 4%.
  • FIIs were net sellers of Indian equities, offloading ₹2,535 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong buying support with net inflows of ₹5,101 crore.
  • F&O positioning data is unavailable for today, as it requires the previous session's Open Interest figures.

How the market behaved today

Indian equity markets witnessed another day of decline, with the Nifty 50 extending its losses for the sixth consecutive session. The benchmark index closed at 24154.90, down 0.55%, after trading in a narrow range and hitting its day's low at the close. The Bank Nifty also ended in the red, settling at 57262.40 with a 0.41% cut. The broader market indices, Nifty Midcap 100 and Nifty Smallcap 100, also saw muted to negative performance, reflecting the cautious sentiment across the board.

What moved the market — the factors

The Indian market's downward trajectory today was primarily influenced by a confluence of global and domestic factors. A significant drag came from the global front, where bond yields continued their ascent to multi-year highs, fueled by intensifying inflation concerns and fiscal worries in major economies like the US, Japan, and Germany. This rise in global borrowing costs often makes equities less attractive. Adding to the pressure were elevated crude oil prices, which further stoked inflation fears and weighed on sentiment.

Domestically, persistent selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) for the day, amounting to ₹2,535 crore, contributed to the market's weakness. This outflow, coupled with hesitant market sentiment influenced by renewed Middle East tensions, kept investors on edge. The IT sector was a major laggard, declining by nearly 2%, suggesting concerns about global growth and potential impact on technology spending. The Realty sector also faced significant selling pressure. Conversely, the Defence sector witnessed a strong rally after the government announced its sixth Positive Indigenisation List, boosting domestic manufacturing prospects.

News that moved stocks

  • Defence stocks fire up! Paras Defence, GRSE, other stocks jump up to 10% as govt notifies 6th indigenisation list

    • Defence stocks surged significantly after the Ministry of Defence announced its sixth Positive Indigenisation List, which includes 405 items with a business potential of ₹3,070 crore, aiming to boost domestic manufacturing. This news directly led to a rally in defence-related stocks.

  • Global bond yields rise to multi-year highs as inflation and fiscal concerns intensify

    • Global bond yields, including those in the US, Japan, and Germany, hit multi-year or multi-decade highs today, driven by rising oil prices, inflation concerns, and tighter monetary policies. This development contributed to a cautious sentiment across the broader market, making equities less attractive.

  • Rupee ends marginally lower as central bank shields from oil, yield strain

    • The Indian rupee saw a minor depreciation against the dollar, with speculation suggesting intervention by the Reserve Bank of India to support its position amidst high oil prices and climbing global bond yields. A weaker rupee can impact import costs and inflation.

  • Jefferies raises Belrise Industries target price, sees 18% upside on strong demand, business expansion

    • Jefferies maintained its 'Buy' rating on Belrise Industries and increased its target price to ₹280, citing robust automotive orders, strong two-wheeler demand, and expansion into renewable energy and aerospace sectors. This positive analyst coverage could influence investor sentiment towards the auto and related sectors.

  • European shares slip as rising oil prices, bond yields weigh on sentiment

    • European markets also experienced a downturn, with shares slipping due to rising crude prices and Eurozone bond yields, which intensified inflation concerns. This global weakness contributed to the negative sentiment observed in Indian markets.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's market session was largely driven by a combination of broad global flows and specific domestic news. The most impactful event was the Defence Ministry's announcement of its sixth Positive Indigenisation List. This list, covering 405 items with a substantial business potential, aims to boost domestic manufacturing in the defence sector. This news provided a clear catalyst for defence stocks, which saw significant buying interest and surged up to 10%.

Beyond this, the session was predominantly influenced by global macroeconomic factors. Rising global bond yields, particularly in the US, which hit multi-year highs, coupled with elevated crude oil prices, fueled inflation concerns worldwide. These factors contributed to a risk-averse sentiment, leading to FII outflows and a general cautious approach from investors. The Indian rupee also saw a marginal decline, with the RBI reportedly intervening to stabilize it amidst these global pressures. The IT and Realty sectors were particularly sensitive to these broader economic concerns, experiencing notable declines.

Setup for tomorrow

As we look towards tomorrow's session, global cues will continue to play a crucial role. US 10-year yields remain elevated at 4.70%, and Brent crude is hovering around $90.74, suggesting that inflation and interest rate concerns are likely to persist. The USD/INR closed higher at 95.67, indicating continued pressure on the rupee. Investors will be watching for any further developments on global bond markets and crude oil prices. Domestically, the consistent DII buying offers a cushion, but sustained FII selling could keep the market under pressure. Key levels for Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty will be closely monitored, with the IT and Realty sectors needing a rebound to support broader market sentiment.
