Decoding today's open interest

When we look at the options data, two key levels often stand out: the highest Call Open Interest (OI) strike and the highest Put Open Interest (OI) strike. The highest Call Open Interest (OI) strike represents the price level where the maximum number of Call options have been sold and remain open. Call sellers, often referred to as "call writers," typically profit if the market stays below this level or declines. Consequently, this strike often acts as a significant resistance level, indicating where market participants expect upside movement to be capped.

Conversely, the highest Put Open Interest (OI) strike indicates the price level where the maximum number of Put options have been sold. Put sellers, or "put writers," generally profit if the market stays above this level or rises. This level typically serves as a strong support zone, suggesting where market participants anticipate the downside to be limited.

For Nifty 50, today's fresh Call writing was heaviest near the 24166 strike. This suggests that call sellers are actively positioning themselves, expecting the index to face strong resistance around this level. The aggregate flow also showed a significant build in Net Call OI (68.4 million contracts) compared to Net Put OI (22.6 million contracts), reinforcing the view that option writers are establishing caps on potential upside.

In Sensex, the heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near 77500, indicating that call sellers anticipate resistance around this mark. Simultaneously, there was notable fresh Put writing near 75500, suggesting that put sellers are attempting to establish a floor at this level. However, the overall aggregate flow for Sensex also saw a much larger build in Net Call OI (22.5 million contracts) compared to Net Put OI (4.4 million contracts), pointing towards a predominantly call-heavy sentiment.

For Bank Nifty, today's data showed heaviest fresh Call writing near 57500, while substantial fresh Put writing was seen near 56800. This indicates a battle between call and put writers around current levels. While call writers are trying to cap the upside at 57500, put writers are actively defending the 56800 level, attempting to create a floor for the index.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total open interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests that there is more Put writing than Call writing, which can be interpreted as a bullish signal by some, as put writers profit when prices rise. Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more Call writing, which is often seen as a bearish signal, as call writers profit when prices fall or remain range-bound. We also look at the PCR based on today's change in Open Interest (ΔOI) to understand fresh sentiment.

For Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.64, and the PCR based on today's flow (PCR(today flow ΔOI)) is 0.33. Both figures are well below 1, with the fresh flow PCR being particularly low. This combination suggests a strong bearish sentiment from option writers, with significant fresh call writing dominating today's activity, indicating expectations of limited upside for the index.

Sensex shows a similar trend, with its PCR(OI) at 0.62 and PCR(today flow ΔOI) at 0.19. Both ratios are significantly below 1, and today's flow indicates an extremely call-heavy bias. This points to a clear bearish sentiment from option writers, who appear to be anticipating strong resistance levels for the index.

Bank Nifty presents a slightly different picture. Its PCR(OI) is 0.94, which is close to 1, suggesting a relatively balanced book of open positions. The PCR based on today's flow (PCR(today flow ΔOI)) is -0.51. While a negative ΔPCR can be complex to interpret, in this context, coupled with the overall PCR(OI) being near neutral, it suggests a mixed to balanced flow for the day, where there might have been a combination of call writing and significant put writing or unwinding of calls, leading to a somewhat balanced undertone.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max pain" is a concept in options theory that refers to the strike price at which option buyers (those who bought calls and puts) would collectively experience the maximum financial loss at expiry. Conversely, option writers (those who sold calls and puts) would collectively experience the maximum profit at this strike. It is often considered a magnetic point that the underlying asset price tends to gravitate towards as expiry approaches. This pull typically strengthens significantly as the weekly or monthly expiry date draws closer.

For Nifty 50, the max pain level is currently at 24400, which is approximately +2.2% above today's spot close of 23,882.05. This suggests a potential upward pull towards the 24400 level as expiry nears.

Sensex has its max pain level at 77800, positioned about +1.7% above today's spot close of 76,503.60. This also indicates an upward magnetic pull for Sensex towards 77800.

Bank Nifty's max pain is at 58000, which is approximately +2.2% above today's spot close of 56,742.60. This suggests an upward bias towards the 58000 level.

While the max pain levels for all three indices suggest a potential upward pull, it is important to consider this alongside the strong call writing observed, which could act as significant resistance.

How to read this yourself daily

To start understanding daily F&O data and form your own perspective, begin by looking at the Open Interest (OI) data for Call and Put options. This information is readily available on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the derivatives section, or through your stockbroker's trading platform. Identify the strikes with the highest Call OI and highest Put OI; these often represent key resistance and support levels, respectively, as option writers are most active at these points.

Next, observe the change in Open Interest (ΔOI) for the day to see where fresh positions (new buying or selling) have been added. Calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) for both total OI and for today's ΔOI. A falling PCR, especially if it drops significantly below 1, often suggests increasing bearish sentiment from option writers, indicating expectations of limited upside. Conversely, a rising PCR, particularly above 1, might point to increasing bullish sentiment. Finally, keep an eye on the India VIX, which reflects market volatility expectations. Combining these data points can help you form a more informed perspective on market sentiment and potential price ranges for the upcoming session.
