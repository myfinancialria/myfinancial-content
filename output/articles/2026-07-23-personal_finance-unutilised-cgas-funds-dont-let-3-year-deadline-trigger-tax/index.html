Unutilised CGAS Funds: Don't Let 3-Year Deadline Trigger Tax

TL;DR

  • The Capital Gains Accounts Scheme (CGAS) allows you to defer capital gains tax by depositing sale proceeds.
  • A common misconception is that tax deferral lasts until funds are withdrawn from CGAS, but this is incorrect.
  • The exemption period for utilising CGAS funds is strictly three years from the date of the original asset transfer.
  • If funds remain unutilised after this three-year period, they become taxable as long-term capital gains in the year the period expires.
  • Proactive tracking and timely utilisation of CGAS funds are crucial to avoid unexpected tax liabilities.

What this means in plain terms

When you sell certain assets like property and make a profit (a capital gain), you are usually liable to pay capital gains tax. To encourage reinvestment, the Indian Income Tax Act offers exemptions if you reinvest these gains into specific new assets, such as another residential house or certain bonds, within a stipulated timeframe. However, sometimes it's not possible to reinvest immediately. This is where the Capital Gains Accounts Scheme (CGAS) comes in. You can deposit the sale proceeds, or the capital gains amount, into a CGAS account with a bank before the income tax filing deadline, thereby temporarily deferring the capital gains tax.

Many individuals mistakenly believe that as long as the money sits in the CGAS account, the tax liability remains deferred indefinitely, or at least until they decide to withdraw it. This is a critical misunderstanding. The tax exemption is not tied to the withdrawal date from the CGAS account, but rather to a strict three-year deadline for utilisation of the funds, calculated from the date you sold the original asset. If you don't use the money for the specified purpose within this three-year window, the unutilised amount becomes taxable in your hands.

Understanding the Capital Gains Accounts Scheme (CGAS)

The Capital Gains Accounts Scheme (CGAS) was introduced by the Indian government to provide flexibility to taxpayers who wish to claim exemptions under various sections of the Income Tax Act, such as Section 54, 54F, or 54EC, but are unable to reinvest the capital gains immediately. [cite: Income Tax India] For instance, if you sell a house and intend to buy another one to claim exemption under Section 54, but the new house purchase takes time, you can deposit the capital gains into a CGAS account before the due date for filing your income tax return for that financial year. This deposit allows you to claim the exemption for that year, preventing the capital gains from being taxed immediately.

There are primarily two types of CGAS accounts:

  • Account A (Savings Deposit Account): This functions much like a regular savings account, offering liquidity and a modest interest rate. Funds can be withdrawn as needed for the specified reinvestment purpose.
  • Account B (Term Deposit Account): This is similar to a fixed deposit, offering a higher interest rate but with funds locked in for a specified period. Withdrawals generally require prior intimation.

The funds deposited in a CGAS account must be used exclusively for the purpose for which the exemption was claimed. For example, if you claimed exemption under Section 54 for reinvesting in a residential house, the funds can only be used for purchasing or constructing that house.

The Crucial 3-Year Utilisation Deadline

The most significant aspect of CGAS, and often the most misunderstood, is the deadline for utilising the deposited funds. The exemption from capital gains tax is conditional upon the funds being used for the specified purpose within a certain period. For most capital gains exemptions (e.g., under Sections 54 and 54F), this period is three years from the date of transfer of the original asset (the date you sold the property, shares, etc., that generated the capital gain). [cite: Income Tax India]

It is vital to note that this three-year clock starts ticking from the moment you sell the asset, not from when you deposit the money into CGAS, nor from when you decide to withdraw it. This is a common pitfall. Many taxpayers assume that as long as the money is in the CGAS account, the tax liability is deferred indefinitely, or until they physically withdraw the funds. This misconception can lead to significant tax notices. If the capital gains deposited in CGAS are not utilised for the intended purpose within this three-year window, the unutilised amount is treated as taxable long-term capital gain in the previous year in which the three-year period expires.

For example, if you sold a property on 15th July 2023, the three-year utilisation period would end on 14th July 2026. If funds remain unutilised by this date, they would become taxable in the Financial Year 2026-27 (Assessment Year 2027-28).

Tax Implications of Unutilised CGAS Funds

If you fail to utilise the funds deposited in your CGAS account within the stipulated three-year period, the unutilised amount will be treated as income in your hands. Specifically, it will be taxed as long-term capital gains (LTCG) in the previous year in which the three-year period from the date of transfer of the original asset expires. This means you will have to pay tax on this amount, along with any applicable interest and penalties if not declared correctly.

The tax rate for long-term capital gains on property is generally 20% with indexation benefit, plus cess. [cite: Income Tax India] This can be a substantial amount, especially if the capital gain was large. Furthermore, the interest earned on the CGAS account itself is taxable as 'Income from Other Sources' in the year it is earned, irrespective of whether the principal is utilised or not.

It's crucial to understand that once the three-year period lapses, the tax liability crystallises. The bank holding your CGAS account is mandated to report unutilised balances to the income tax authorities. This makes it highly likely that the Income Tax Department will be aware of such unutilised funds, potentially leading to a tax notice if you haven't declared them. Therefore, regular monitoring of your CGAS account and the associated deadlines is paramount to avoid unexpected tax burdens and compliance issues.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 42, a software engineer with ₹35L CTC, based in Hyderabad.

Priya sold a plot of land on 10th August 2023 for ₹1.20 crore, which she had purchased for ₹40 lakhs in 2010. After indexation, her long-term capital gain came to ₹70 lakhs. She plans to buy a new residential apartment but hasn't finalised one yet. To defer the capital gains tax under Section 54F, she deposited the entire ₹70 lakhs into a CGAS Account A on 25th July 2024, before the tax filing deadline for FY 2023-24.

Scenario 1: Priya utilises the funds on time. Priya finds a suitable apartment and completes its purchase on 1st June 2026, utilising the full ₹70 lakhs from her CGAS account. Since she utilised the funds within the three-year period (10th August 2023 to 9th August 2026), her capital gains of ₹70 lakhs remain exempt from tax.

Scenario 2: Priya fails to utilise the funds on time. Due to unforeseen delays, Priya is unable to finalise her apartment purchase by 9th August 2026. The full ₹70 lakhs remains unutilised in her CGAS account.

In this scenario, on 10th August 2026, the unutilised ₹70 lakhs becomes taxable as long-term capital gains in the Financial Year 2026-27 (Assessment Year 2027-28). Priya would be liable to pay tax on ₹70 lakhs. Assuming a 20% LTCG tax rate (plus 4% cess): Tax = 20% of ₹70,00,000 = ₹14,00,000 Cess = 4% of ₹14,00,000 = ₹56,000 Total tax liability = ₹14,56,000.

This significant tax amount, along with potential interest and penalties for non-declaration, would be an unexpected burden for Priya, all because she missed the crucial 3-year deadline.

What to do this week

  1. Check your CGAS account statements: If you have ever deposited funds into a CGAS account, locate your latest statement to confirm the deposit date and the original asset transfer date.
  2. Mark your calendar for the 3-year deadline: Calculate the exact date three years from when you sold the original asset (not when you deposited into CGAS). Set reminders well in advance.
  3. Review your reinvestment plan: Assess if your original plan for utilising the CGAS funds is still viable. If not, start exploring alternative eligible investments or assets immediately.
  4. Consult a tax advisor: If you are nearing your deadline and haven't utilised the funds, or are unsure about the rules, speak to a Certified Financial Planner or tax consultant. They can guide you on compliance and potential actions.
  5. Understand tax implications: Familiarise yourself with the tax consequences if you fail to utilise the funds, so you are prepared for any potential liability.

FAQ

What is the Capital Gains Accounts Scheme (CGAS)?

The CGAS allows taxpayers to deposit capital gains from asset sales into a special bank account to defer tax, provided they intend to reinvest these gains into specific new assets within a set timeframe. [cite: Income Tax India]

When does the 3-year period for utilising CGAS funds begin?

The three-year period begins from the date of transfer of the original asset (e.g., the date you sold your property or shares), not from the date you deposit funds into the CGAS account. [cite: Income Tax India]

What happens if I don't utilise the CGAS funds within three years?

If the funds are not utilised for the specified purpose within three years from the date of original asset transfer, the unutilised amount becomes taxable as long-term capital gains in the financial year in which the three-year period expires.

Can I withdraw money from a CGAS account for any purpose?

No, funds from a CGAS account can only be withdrawn for the specific purpose for which the capital gains exemption was claimed (e.g., buying a new residential house, purchasing specified bonds).

What are the types of CGAS accounts available?

There are two main types: Account A, which is like a savings account for liquidity, and Account B, which is a term deposit account for higher interest, similar to a fixed deposit.

Is the interest earned on a CGAS account taxable?

Yes, any interest earned on funds held in a CGAS account is taxable as 'Income from Other Sources' in the financial year it is accrued, irrespective of whether the principal amount is utilised.

Can the 3-year deadline for utilisation be extended?

Generally, no. The three-year deadline is a statutory provision under the Income Tax Act and cannot be extended. Missing this deadline triggers tax liability.
