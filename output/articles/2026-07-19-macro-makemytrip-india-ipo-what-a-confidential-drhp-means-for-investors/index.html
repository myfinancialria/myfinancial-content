MakeMyTrip India IPO: What a Confidential DRHP Means for Investors

TL;DR

  • MakeMyTrip has confidentially filed a Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) with SEBI for an India IPO.
  • This filing indicates the online travel giant's intent to list on Indian stock exchanges, 15 years after its Nasdaq debut.
  • The IPO will involve an equity share sale by MakeMyTrip Limited and its subsidiary, ibibo Group.
  • Key details like the issue size, price band, and exact timeline are currently undisclosed due to the confidential nature of the filing.
  • This move could offer Indian investors a direct opportunity to participate in a leading player in the country's growing online travel sector.

What this means in plain terms

MakeMyTrip, a well-known name in online travel, has taken the first formal step towards listing its shares on Indian stock exchanges. This means they've submitted a preliminary document, called a Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP), to the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI). What's interesting is that this filing is "confidential." This allows the company to engage with SEBI and potential investors privately, refining their offering details before making them public.

For you, as a potential investor, this signals an upcoming opportunity to buy shares in a prominent company within the travel industry. MakeMyTrip has been listed on the Nasdaq in the US for many years, but an India IPO makes it directly accessible to domestic investors. While the exact details like how many shares will be sold or at what price are not out yet, this move reflects the company's strategy to tap into the Indian capital markets and potentially increase its local investor base.

Understanding the Confidential DRHP Process

When a company decides to go public in India, it must file a Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) with SEBI. This document provides comprehensive information about the company's business, financials, promoters, risks, and the details of the proposed public offer. Traditionally, this DRHP is made public immediately upon filing. However, SEBI introduced the option for "confidential filing" for certain companies, primarily those that are already listed on a foreign exchange or meet specific criteria.

The confidential DRHP process allows companies to interact with SEBI regarding their IPO application without immediately disclosing sensitive financial and business information to the public. This can be particularly beneficial for companies operating in competitive sectors or those that wish to avoid premature market speculation. Once SEBI provides its observations and the company is ready to proceed, a revised, non-confidential DRHP is filed and made public, containing all the necessary details for investors to make informed decisions. This approach offers flexibility and strategic advantages to companies like MakeMyTrip, which is already a publicly traded entity in the US.

Why Companies Choose Confidential Filings

Companies opt for confidential filings for several strategic reasons. Firstly, it provides a window to engage with SEBI on the DRHP without public scrutiny, allowing for corrections and refinements away from the media spotlight. Secondly, it can help prevent competitors from gaining early access to detailed business strategies and financial performance, which would otherwise be immediately public with a traditional DRHP. Thirdly, for companies already listed on foreign exchanges, it allows them to test the waters in the Indian market and gauge investor interest before committing fully to a public disclosure, potentially streamlining the overall IPO process.

MakeMyTrip's Journey: From Nasdaq to India

MakeMyTrip's journey began in 2000, and it quickly grew to become a dominant player in India's nascent online travel sector. A significant milestone in its corporate history was its initial public offering (IPO) on the Nasdaq stock exchange in the United States in 2010. This made it one of the first Indian internet companies to list on a major international exchange, providing it with access to global capital markets and enhancing its international visibility.

Over the years, MakeMyTrip expanded its offerings and market reach, notably through strategic acquisitions. A key acquisition was that of the ibibo Group in 2016. The ibibo Group, backed by Naspers and Tencent, brought with it popular brands like Goibibo and RedBus, significantly consolidating MakeMyTrip's position in the Indian online travel agency (OTA) market, encompassing flights, hotels, buses, and holiday packages. This acquisition cemented MakeMyTrip's leadership in the Indian market, creating a formidable force in the sector. The decision to pursue an India IPO now, 15 years after its Nasdaq listing, suggests a strategic pivot to deepen its roots in the Indian capital market and allow domestic investors to directly participate in its growth story, aligning with the increasing maturity and liquidity of India's stock exchanges.

Market Leadership and Competitive Landscape

MakeMyTrip, through its various brands including MakeMyTrip, Goibibo, and RedBus, holds a substantial market share in India's online travel segment. It offers a comprehensive suite of services, from flight and hotel bookings to bus and train tickets, and holiday packages. The Indian online travel market is dynamic and competitive, with players like EaseMyTrip, Cleartrip (now part of Flipkart), and various smaller regional players vying for market share. MakeMyTrip's long-standing presence, brand recognition, and extensive network have allowed it to maintain a leading position. The potential IPO in India could further strengthen its local brand equity and provide capital for continued expansion and technological innovation in this rapidly evolving sector.

What an India IPO Means for the Travel Sector and Investors

An India IPO by MakeMyTrip is a significant development for both the domestic travel sector and retail investors. For the travel industry, it signals renewed confidence in the growth potential of online travel in India. The capital raised through the IPO could be deployed by MakeMyTrip for further expansion, technological upgrades, marketing, or even strategic acquisitions, thereby intensifying competition and innovation within the sector. It could also encourage other major unlisted travel tech players to consider similar moves, further deepening the public market's exposure to this segment.

For retail investors, this IPO offers a direct avenue to invest in a market leader within the Indian online travel space. While MakeMyTrip shares were previously accessible via Nasdaq, an India listing simplifies the investment process for domestic investors, eliminating the need for international brokerage accounts and currency conversion complexities. It allows them to participate in the growth of a company directly benefiting from India's rising disposable incomes, increasing internet penetration, and a growing propensity for travel among its vast population. Investors will need to carefully evaluate the company's financials, future growth prospects, and the IPO valuation once the public DRHP is available.

How Retail Investors Can Prepare for an IPO

Participating in an IPO requires a few fundamental steps. Firstly, ensure you have a valid Demat account and a trading account with a SEBI-registered broker. Secondly, you will need to apply through the ASBA (Application Supported by Blocked Amount) facility, which is mandatory for all IPO applications. This facility ensures that your application money is blocked in your bank account but only debited if shares are allotted to you. Thirdly, stay updated on the IPO details such as the issue opening and closing dates, price band, and lot size, which will be announced once the public DRHP is filed. Finally, conduct thorough due diligence by reading the Red Herring Prospectus (RHP) to understand the company's business, risks, and financial health before making an investment decision.

Rahul first checks his investment portfolio, which currently has a good mix of large-cap equities and a few mutual funds. He earmarks ₹75,000 from his liquid funds for potential IPO applications. Since the issue size and price band are not yet public, he estimates a hypothetical scenario. Let's assume MakeMyTrip's IPO price band is announced later at ₹1,200 - ₹1,250 per share, with a minimum lot size of 12 shares.

If Rahul decides to apply at the upper end of the price band (₹1,250 per share), one lot would cost him ₹1,250 12 = ₹15,000. He considers applying for 5 lots, which would amount to ₹15,000 5 = ₹75,000. He ensures this amount is available in his bank account linked to his ASBA facility.

Once the public DRHP is released and the subscription dates are announced, Rahul will download and carefully read the Red Herring Prospectus to understand MakeMyTrip's financials, its competitive landscape, and the risks involved. He will specifically look at the company's profitability, revenue growth, and valuation multiples compared to its peers. If he is satisfied, he will then submit his application through his bank's net banking portal using the ASBA facility, indicating his bid for 5 lots at the cut-off price. If allotted, the shares will reflect in his Demat account, and the blocked amount will be debited. If not allotted, the blocked amount will be released.

FAQ

What is a DRHP?

A Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) is a preliminary document filed by a company with SEBI when it intends to launch an Initial Public Offering (IPO). It contains detailed information about the company, its business, financials, risks, and the proposed offer.

What does "confidential DRHP" mean?

A confidential DRHP allows a company to file its IPO documents with SEBI without immediately making them public. This provides a window for SEBI to review and provide observations privately, before the company files a public DRHP with all details.

Why is MakeMyTrip listing in India now, after being on Nasdaq?

Listing in India allows MakeMyTrip to tap into the domestic capital market, broaden its investor base in its primary operating region, and potentially enhance its brand equity within India. It also simplifies investment for Indian retail investors.

How can I apply for MakeMyTrip's IPO?

Once the IPO dates are announced, you can apply through the ASBA (Application Supported by Blocked Amount) facility via your bank's net banking portal or through your stockbroker. You will need an active Demat and trading account.

What are the risks associated with IPO investments?

IPO investments carry risks, including market volatility, potential overvaluation of the company, and business-specific risks detailed in the Red Herring Prospectus. There is no guarantee of listing gains or sustained price appreciation.

Will NRIs be able to invest in this IPO?

Yes, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are generally eligible to invest in Indian IPOs, subject to certain regulations. They typically need an NRE or NRO bank account and a Demat account linked to it.
