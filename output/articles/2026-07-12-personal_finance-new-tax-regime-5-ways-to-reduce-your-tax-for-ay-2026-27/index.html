New Tax Regime: 5 Ways to Reduce Your Tax for AY 2026-27

TL;DR

  • The new tax regime offers lower tax rates but limits traditional deductions, leading to a common misconception that no tax saving is possible.
  • Salaried individuals can still claim a Standard Deduction of ₹50,000 under the new regime from AY 2024-25 onwards.
  • Employer contributions to your National Pension System (NPS) account are deductible under Section 80CCD(2).
  • Donations to specified funds and institutions under Section 80G can provide tax relief, subject to certain limits.
  • Interest paid on a housing loan for a let-out property remains deductible, though rules differ from self-occupied homes.

What this means in plain terms

The Indian income tax system offers two main ways to calculate your tax liability: the old tax regime and the new tax regime. The new tax regime, introduced to simplify tax filing, comes with lower tax rates across various income slabs. However, it significantly reduces the number of exemptions and deductions that taxpayers could claim under the old regime, such as Section 80C investments, House Rent Allowance (HRA), and most home loan interest deductions.

This often leads many salaried and self-employed individuals to believe that if they opt for the new regime, they have no avenues left to reduce their taxable income. This isn't entirely true. While the list of available tax-saving options is shorter, there are still specific deductions and exemptions that can be utilised effectively to lower your tax outgo, even when you choose the new tax regime for Assessment Year (AY) 2026-27. Understanding these provisions is key to optimising your tax planning.

Standard Deduction and Employer NPS Contributions

For salaried individuals, a significant relief under the new tax regime from Assessment Year 2024-25 (which applies to Financial Year 2023-24 and subsequent years, including FY 2025-26 for AY 2026-27) is the reintroduction of the Standard Deduction. This means salaried employees can claim a flat deduction of ₹50,000 from their gross salary income, irrespective of their actual expenses. This is a straightforward reduction in taxable income that was previously only available under the old regime.

Another powerful tax-saving tool, often overlooked, is the employer's contribution to your National Pension System (NPS) account. Under Section 80CCD(2) of the Income Tax Act, the employer's contribution to an employee's NPS account is deductible from the employee's taxable salary. For salaried employees, this deduction is capped at 10% of their basic salary plus Dearness Allowance (DA). For Central Government employees, this limit is 14%. This deduction is available under both the old and new tax regimes, making it a valuable benefit for those whose employers offer NPS contributions as part of their CTC (Cost to Company).

Understanding Standard Deduction for Salaried

The Standard Deduction simplifies tax calculations by providing a fixed deduction without requiring proof of expenses. For a salaried individual earning ₹15 lakhs or ₹50 lakhs, the deduction remains ₹50,000, directly reducing their taxable income.

Maximising Employer NPS Contributions

If your employer offers an NPS contribution, consider discussing increasing this component of your CTC, provided it aligns with your financial goals. This not only builds your retirement corpus but also reduces your current tax liability under the new regime.

Strategic Philanthropy: Section 80G Donations

While most investment-linked deductions like Section 80C are unavailable under the new tax regime, donations to certain funds and charitable institutions continue to offer tax benefits under Section 80G. This allows taxpayers to claim a deduction for contributions made to specified entities.

The deduction amount varies depending on the recipient organisation. Some donations qualify for a 100% deduction without any qualifying limit, such as contributions to the Prime Minister's National Relief Fund or the National Defence Fund. Other donations may qualify for a 50% or 100% deduction, subject to a qualifying limit of 10% of your Adjusted Gross Total Income. It's crucial to ensure that the institution is registered under Section 80G and you obtain a valid receipt with their registration number. This provision allows you to contribute to causes you believe in while simultaneously reducing your tax burden.

Types of Donations Eligible

Donations can be made to government-established funds, approved charitable institutions, or even certain political parties (under Section 80GGC, though this is separate from 80G). Always verify the eligibility of the recipient organisation.

Documentation is Key

To claim an 80G deduction, you must have a proper receipt from the donee institution, containing their name, address, PAN, and the amount donated. For cash donations exceeding ₹2,000, no deduction is allowed.

Home Loan Interest for Let-Out Property

Under the new tax regime, the deduction for interest paid on a home loan for a self-occupied property is generally not allowed. However, if you have a home loan for a property that is let out (rented out), you can still claim a deduction for the interest paid on that loan under Section 24(b) of the Income Tax Act.

This deduction is permitted against the rental income generated from the property. While the loss from house property (after deducting interest and other expenses) can be set off against other income up to ₹2 lakhs in a financial year, the full interest amount can be deducted against the actual rental income. Any unabsorbed loss can be carried forward for up to eight assessment years. This provision is particularly beneficial for individuals who have invested in multiple properties, one of which is rented out.

Distinguishing Self-Occupied vs. Let-Out

It's vital to understand the distinction. For a self-occupied property, you cannot claim interest deduction under the new regime. For a let-out property, the interest paid on the loan is fully deductible against the rental income, and any resulting loss (up to ₹2 lakhs) can be set off against other income.

Other Specific Exemptions and Deductions

Beyond the more common avenues, there are several specific exemptions and deductions that remain available under the new tax regime, which can be particularly relevant for certain individuals or circumstances.

Gratuity and Leave Encashment Exemptions

Certain forms of income, such as gratuity received at the time of retirement or leave encashment, are eligible for exemption from tax, subject to specified limits and conditions. For example, the maximum amount of gratuity exempt from tax for non-government employees is ₹20 lakhs (for those covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972) or ₹20 lakhs (for those not covered), subject to specific calculations. Similarly, leave encashment on retirement can be exempt up to ₹25 lakhs for non-government employees, subject to certain conditions. These exemptions are available under both tax regimes.

Voluntary Retirement Scheme (VRS) Exemption

Compensation received under a Voluntary Retirement Scheme (VRS) can also be exempt from tax, up to a limit of ₹5 lakhs, provided certain conditions laid out in Section 10(10C) of the Income Tax Act are met. This exemption applies to employees of public sector companies, other companies, and local authorities.

Agniveer Corpus Fund Contributions

For individuals who are part of the Agnipath scheme, contributions made to the Agniveer Corpus Fund are eligible for deduction under Section 80CCH. Both the individual's contribution and the matching contribution by the Central Government to the Agniveer Corpus Fund are deductible from taxable income. This is a specific provision designed for the Agniveer scheme participants.

A real example

Meet Priya, 42, earning ₹35 lakhs CTC, based in Gurugram. She has opted for the new tax regime for AY 2026-27 (FY 2025-26). Her salary structure includes a basic salary of ₹18 lakhs and her employer contributes 10% of her basic salary to her NPS account. She also owns a second property in Chennai, which is let out for ₹30,000 per month. The interest paid on the home loan for this let-out property is ₹2.5 lakhs annually. Priya also made a donation of ₹25,000 to the Prime Minister's National Relief Fund during the year.

Let's calculate her taxable income under the new regime:

  1. Gross Salary: ₹35,00,000
  2. Less: Standard Deduction: ₹50,000

    3. Income after Standard Deduction:* ₹34,50,000

  3. Less: Employer's contribution to NPS (Section 80CCD(2)):

    4. * 10% of Basic Salary (₹18,00,000) = ₹1,80,000 Income after NPS deduction:* ₹34,50,000 - ₹1,80,000 = ₹32,70,000

  4. Income from House Property (Let-out):

    5. Gross Annual Value (rent received): ₹30,000 12 = ₹3,60,000 Less: Standard Deduction (30% of GAV): ₹3,60,000 30% = ₹1,08,000 * Less: Interest on Home Loan (Section 24(b)): ₹2,50,000 Net Income from House Property:* ₹3,60,000 - ₹1,08,000 - ₹2,50,000 = ₹2,000 (a small profit in this case)

  5. Less: Donation to PM National Relief Fund (Section 80G): ₹25,000 (100% deduction without limit)

    6. Taxable Income:* ₹32,70,000 (from salary) + ₹2,000 (from house property) - ₹25,000 (donation) = ₹32,47,000

Without these deductions, her taxable income would have been ₹35,00,000. By utilising the available provisions, Priya successfully reduced her taxable income to ₹32,47,000, leading to a significant tax saving.

What to do this week

  1. Review your salary slip: Check if your employer contributes to your NPS account. If not, consider discussing with your HR department if it's an option to restructure your CTC to include an employer NPS contribution.
  2. Gather donation receipts: If you have made any donations to eligible charitable institutions or government funds during the financial year, ensure you have proper receipts with the organisation's PAN and 80G registration number.
  3. Identify let-out properties: If you own a property that is rented out, collect all documents related to its home loan, especially interest certificates, to claim the deduction under Section 24(b).
  4. Confirm Standard Deduction: For salaried individuals, simply remember that ₹50,000 will be automatically deducted from your gross salary for tax calculation under the new regime.
  5. Consult a professional: If your financial situation is complex, or you are unsure about specific exemptions, schedule a brief consultation with a Certified Financial Planner or a tax advisor.

FAQ

Can I claim Section 80C deductions under the new tax regime?

No, most common deductions under Section 80C, such as investments in EPF, PPF, ELSS, life insurance premiums, and home loan principal repayment, are not available under the new tax regime.

Is House Rent Allowance (HRA) exempt under the new tax regime?

No, the exemption for House Rent Allowance (HRA) is not available under the new tax regime.

Can I switch between the old and new tax regimes every year?

Yes, salaried individuals can choose to switch between the old and new tax regimes each financial year. However, self-employed individuals or those with business income generally have a one-time option to switch out of the new regime, with specific conditions for re-opting.

Is the Standard Deduction for salaried employees new for the new tax regime?

Yes, for the new tax regime, the Standard Deduction of ₹50,000 for salaried individuals and pensioners was reintroduced from AY 2024-25 (FY 2023-24) onwards. It was not available in the initial version of the new regime.

What if I have a home loan for a self-occupied property? Can I claim interest deduction?

No, under the new tax regime, interest paid on a home loan for a self-occupied property is not deductible. This deduction is only available for interest paid on a home loan for a let-out (rented out) property.

What is the maximum deduction allowed for employer's NPS contribution under Section 80CCD(2)?

The maximum deduction allowed for an employer's contribution to NPS under Section 80CCD(2) is 10% of your basic salary plus Dearness Allowance (DA). For Central Government employees, this limit is 14%.
