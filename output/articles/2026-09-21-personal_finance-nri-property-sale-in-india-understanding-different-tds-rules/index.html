NRI Property Sale in India: Understanding Different TDS Rules

TL;DR

  • When a Non-Resident Indian (NRI) sells property in India, the buyer must deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) at specific rates.
  • These TDS rates for NRIs are significantly different and often higher than the 1% applicable to resident Indian sellers.
  • The TDS for NRI sellers is directly linked to the capital gains tax rates, whether short-term or long-term.
  • NRIs can apply for a lower TDS certificate from the Income Tax Department to prevent over-deduction if their actual tax liability is less.
  • Proper documentation, including Form 15CA and 15CB, is essential for both TDS compliance and repatriation of sale proceeds.

What this means in plain terms

Selling a property in India involves a crucial step called Tax Deducted at Source, or TDS. This is essentially an advance tax that the buyer deducts from the payment to the seller and deposits with the government. While this mechanism applies to both resident and Non-Resident Indian (NRI) sellers, the rules are quite different for each.

For resident Indian sellers, the buyer typically deducts a flat 1% TDS if the property value exceeds ₹50 lakhs. However, for NRIs, the situation is more complex. The TDS rate isn't a fixed percentage but depends on whether the capital gains from the sale are considered short-term or long-term, and the tax slab applicable to these gains. This means NRI sellers often face a much higher TDS deduction, sometimes up to 20% or 30%, which can significantly impact the net amount they receive at the time of sale.

TDS on Property Sale for Resident Indians

For a quick comparison, let's briefly look at the TDS rules for resident Indian sellers. When a resident Indian sells an immovable property (excluding agricultural land) valued at ₹50 lakhs or more, the buyer is mandated to deduct TDS at a rate of 1% of the sale consideration. This is governed by Section 194-IA of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The buyer then deposits this deducted amount with the government using Form 26QB. The seller receives credit for this TDS when filing their income tax return.

Decoding TDS for Non-Resident Indian (NRI) Sellers

The rules for NRI sellers are governed by Section 195 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, and are distinctly different. The buyer is required to deduct TDS on the entire sale consideration, not just the capital gains, at rates applicable to the capital gains that arise from the sale. This is a crucial distinction from resident sellers.

Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG)

If an NRI sells a property held for 24 months or less, the gains are classified as Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG). These gains are added to the NRI's total income in India and taxed at the applicable slab rates. The TDS rate for STCG on property sale for an NRI can be as high as 30% (plus surcharge and cess).

Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG)

If an NRI sells a property held for more than 24 months, the gains are classified as Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG). For properties transferred on or after July 23, 2024, LTCG on immovable property for NRIs is taxed at a flat rate of 12.5% (plus surcharge and cess) without the benefit of indexation. For transfers before this date, the tax rate was 20% with indexation. The buyer must deduct TDS on the sale consideration at the applicable rate.

It's important to note that the buyer must deduct TDS at these rates on the entire sale consideration, not just the capital gains. This often leads to a higher deduction than the actual tax liability, necessitating an application for a lower TDS certificate.

Navigating the TDS Deduction Process

The responsibility for deducting and depositing TDS lies squarely with the buyer. This process involves specific forms and, in many cases, an application by the NRI seller to reduce the TDS burden.

Buyer's Responsibilities

The buyer must obtain a Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number (TAN) if they don't already have one, as it's mandatory for deducting TDS under Section 195. After deducting TDS, the buyer must deposit it with the government and issue Form 16A (TDS Certificate) to the NRI seller.

Applying for a Lower TDS Certificate (Section 197)

Since TDS for NRIs is deducted on the gross sale consideration at capital gains rates, it often results in an over-deduction of tax, especially if the actual capital gains are much lower due to indexation or available exemptions (e.g., reinvestment in another property under Section 54, 54EC, or 54F). To avoid this, an NRI seller can apply to the Assessing Officer for a certificate under Section 197 of the Income Tax Act for deduction of tax at a lower rate or even nil rate. This application needs to be made in Form 13, supported by relevant documents like property details, sale agreement, cost of acquisition, and details of any reinvestment plans. Once approved, the buyer will deduct TDS at the rate specified in the certificate.

Form 15CA and 15CB

For any remittance made to an NRI, including property sale proceeds net of TDS, specific documentation is required for compliance with foreign exchange regulations. This involves Form 15CA and Form 15CB. Form 15CB is a certificate issued by a Chartered Accountant, certifying the nature of remittance, TDS applicability, and compliance with DTAA (Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement) provisions. Based on this certificate, the remitter (buyer or bank facilitating repatriation) then furnishes Form 15CA online to the Income Tax Department. These forms are crucial for the bank to process the outward remittance of funds.

Repatriation of Sale Proceeds

NRIs are generally permitted to repatriate the net sale proceeds of immovable property held in India, subject to certain conditions and limits. The funds must be credited to an NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) account first. From the NRO account, up to USD 1 million per financial year can be repatriated overseas, subject to furnishing Form 15CA and 15CB, and an undertaking that all taxes have been paid or provided for. For properties acquired out of NRE/FCNR(B) funds, the full amount of sale proceeds can be repatriated without the USD 1 million limit, provided the original investment was made through these accounts. It is essential to maintain meticulous records of the original investment and all tax deductions.

A real example

Let's consider Aisha, 48, an NRI residing in Dubai, who bought a flat in Mumbai in January 2018 for ₹80 lakhs. She is now selling it in September 2026 for ₹1.5 crores.

  1. Cost of Acquisition: ₹80,00,000 (January 2018)
  2. Sale Consideration: ₹1,50,00,000 (September 2026)
  3. Holding Period: More than 24 months, so it's Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG).
  4. Cost Inflation Index (CII):

    5. * CII for FY 2017-18 (purchase year) = 272 * CII for FY 2026-27 (sale year) = 384 (Notified by CBDT on July 15, 2026)

  5. Indexed Cost of Acquisition:

    6. (Cost of Acquisition / CII of purchase year) * CII of sale year = (₹80,00,000 / 272) * 369 = ₹1,08,52,941 (approx)

  6. Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG):

    7. Sale Consideration - Indexed Cost of Acquisition = ₹1,50,00,000 - ₹1,08,52,941 = ₹41,47,059

  7. TDS Rate for LTCG: 20% (plus surcharge and cess, if applicable. For simplicity, we'll use 20% here).
  8. TDS Deducted by Buyer (on gross sale consideration):

    9. 20% of ₹1,50,00,000 = ₹30,00,000

  9. Actual Tax Liability on LTCG:

    10. 20% of ₹41,47,059 = ₹8,29,412

In this scenario, the buyer is mandated to deduct ₹18,75,000 as TDS. However, Aisha's actual tax liability on the capital gain is only ₹5,18,382. To avoid this significant over-deduction, Aisha should apply for a lower TDS certificate under Section 197, requesting the buyer to deduct only ₹5,18,382 (or slightly more to cover cess/surcharge). Without the certificate, she would receive ₹1,31,25,000 (₹1.5 crores - ₹18,75,000) and would have to claim a refund of ₹13,56,618 (₹18,75,000 - ₹5,18,382) when filing her income tax return, which can take time.

What to do this week

  1. Understand Your Capital Gains: Determine if your property sale will result in Short-Term or Long-Term Capital Gains based on the holding period.
  2. Calculate Estimated Tax Liability: Work with a tax consultant to estimate your actual capital gains and the tax payable after considering indexation and potential exemptions.
  3. Apply for Lower TDS Certificate: If your estimated tax liability is significantly lower than the statutory TDS rate on the gross sale value, immediately apply for a Section 197 certificate with the Income Tax Department.
  4. Gather Documentation: Ensure you have all necessary property documents, purchase deeds, sale agreements, and bank statements ready for the sale and subsequent repatriation process.
  5. Consult a Professional: Engage a qualified Chartered Accountant or financial advisor experienced in NRI taxation to guide you through the TDS, tax filing, and repatriation procedures.

FAQ

What is the primary difference in TDS for resident vs. NRI sellers?

For resident sellers, TDS is typically 1% on property value above ₹50 lakhs. For NRI sellers, TDS is linked to capital gains tax rates (e.g., 20% for LTCG, 30% for STCG) and is deducted on the entire sale consideration.

Can an NRI seller avoid high TDS deduction?

Yes, an NRI seller can apply for a Lower TDS Certificate under Section 197 from the Income Tax Department. This allows the buyer to deduct TDS at a rate closer to the NRI's actual tax liability.

What are Forms 15CA and 15CB used for?

Form 15CB is a certificate from a Chartered Accountant verifying the tax compliance of a remittance to an NRI. Form 15CA is a declaration filed online with the Income Tax Department by the remitter, based on Form 15CB, before transferring funds out of India.

Is there a limit on how much an NRI can repatriate from property sale proceeds?

Generally, NRIs can repatriate up to USD 1 million per financial year from NRO account funds, subject to tax compliance. However, if the property was originally purchased with NRE/FCNR(B) funds, the entire sale proceeds can be repatriated without this limit.

What if the NRI seller has no capital gains?

Even if there are no capital gains or a capital loss, the buyer is still obligated to deduct TDS on the gross sale consideration at the prescribed rates. The NRI seller would then need to claim a refund by filing their income tax return, or apply for a nil TDS certificate under Section 197.

Does Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) apply to property sales?

Yes, DTAA provisions between India and the NRI's country of residence can be considered. A CA certifying Form 15CB will take DTAA into account, potentially offering tax relief, though the TDS will still be deducted in India as per Indian tax laws.
