Why CRED Spent ₹400 Cr on 'Useless' Celebrity Ads — and Cracked Premium Distribution

In April 2021, a collective gasp, followed by a burst of laughter, rippled across India's internet. The usually calm, composed, and utterly unflappable Rahul Dravid, cricket's revered 'The Wall', was caught in a Bengaluru traffic jam, screaming "Indiranagar ka gunda hoon main!". He was smashing rearview mirrors, throwing coffee, and generally embodying an unhinged rage nobody had ever imagined from him. The ad was absurd, hilarious, and completely unexpected. It quickly became the most viral ad of IPL 2021, sparking millions of views, memes, and conversations across social media.

This wasn't an isolated incident. Before Dravid's explosive debut, CRED had already baffled audiences with a series of equally peculiar commercials featuring Bollywood veterans like Anil Kapoor, Madhuri Dixit, Govinda, and Bappi Lahiri. These ads, launched in 2020, showed the celebrities comically auditioning for CRED campaigns, often getting rejected or performing bizarre acts. Many viewers scratched their heads, wondering if the fintech startup had lost its mind, especially given the reported ₹400 crore spent on these campaigns during the IPL season. To many, it seemed like a colossal waste of money on "useless" ads. But behind the apparent madness was a calculated strategy by founder Kunal Shah to build one of India's most exclusive and aspirational brands.

The World Before

Before CRED's unconventional entry in 2018, India's credit card landscape was largely functional, dominated by traditional banks like HDFC, SBI, ICICI, and Axis Bank. The market was growing, with over 10.5 crore cards in circulation by 2023, primarily used by salaried professionals in metropolitan areas. While banks offered various cashback and reward programs, the experience of managing multiple credit cards was often fragmented and lacked a cohesive, premium feel. Financial advertising typically adhered to a serious, trust-focused, or conventionally aspirational tone, emphasizing security, returns, or lifestyle benefits through predictable imagery.

In this environment, a new fintech app that simply helped users pay credit card bills would struggle to stand out. Furthermore, a company that chose to spend hundreds of crores on seemingly illogical celebrity endorsements, especially when targeting a niche audience, seemed financially reckless. Critics questioned the relevance of these ads, arguing that they turned a financial product into a joke and that the company should focus on its core utility rather than chasing virality. The prevailing wisdom was to cast a wide net, not to deliberately exclude. But Kunal Shah saw an opportunity in this very exclusion.

The Crack

CRED's strategy hinged on a powerful psychological principle: exclusivity, often referred to as the "velvet rope" strategy. From its inception, CRED wasn't for everyone. It built a strict entry barrier, requiring users to have a credit score of 750 or above. In India, where credit scores range from 300 to 900, a score above 750 places an individual in the top tier of financial trustworthiness. This immediately flipped the script: instead of CRED chasing users, users aspired to be accepted by CRED.

The absurd celebrity ads were a crucial amplification of this strategy. The 2020 campaign, featuring yesteryear stars like Anil Kapoor and Madhuri Dixit comically auditioning, was a self-referential poke at celebrity advertising itself. It conveyed a subtle message: CRED was so cool, so exclusive, that even these big names had to "audition" for it, and often failed. This created a buzz and primed the audience for something different.

Then came the "Indiranagar ka Gunda" ad in April 2021, strategically launched on the opening day of the IPL. Rahul Dravid's uncharacteristic outburst was a masterstroke in "surprise marketing". The ad, conceptualized by a team including comedian Tanmay Bhat and directed by Ayappa KM, played on Dravid's universally calm image, making his rage all the more shocking and memorable. It went viral instantly, generating over 3.4 million views on YouTube within days and trending higher than the IPL matches themselves. Influencers and even other brands like Zomato and Dunzo jumped on the bandwagon, creating memes and moment marketing campaigns, further amplifying its reach organically.

Kunal Shah understood that for a brand targeting India's "aspirational class" – the top 10% of spenders who value exclusivity and unique experiences – conventional ads wouldn't cut it. The goal wasn't just direct conversion but building enduring brand equity and trust. The ads weren't meant to explain product features; they were designed to create cultural moments, spark conversations, and reinforce CRED's premium, aspirational positioning. Shah himself articulated this, stating that CRED aimed to build a "trust brand" rather than a "product brand," comparing the approach to luxury brand building.

While the reported ₹400 crore spend on IPL ads in 2021 seemed exorbitant, the impact was tangible. Daily sign-ups reportedly increased 6-7 times immediately after the Dravid ad launch, and app downloads saw an 8x spike. The ads generated an estimated 10-15x earned media value compared to the paid media investment. This effectively reduced the customer acquisition cost (CAC) for high-value users, making the initial outlay a strategic investment in long-term brand building and premium distribution.

What Happened Next

CRED's audacious marketing, coupled with its exclusive model, propelled its rise. By April 2021, just days after the "Indiranagar ka Gunda" ad, CRED achieved unicorn status with a valuation of $2.2 billion. This soared to $6.4 billion by 2022. While its valuation saw a correction to around $3.5 billion by May 2025, investors continued to show faith, viewing it as a long-term play.

The platform saw significant user growth, with approximately 5.9 million users by 2021, processing about 20% of all credit card bill payments in India. By FY23, CRED boasted an impressive user base of around 12 million. This premium user base, characterized by high credit scores and responsible financial behavior, became a goldmine for monetization beyond just bill payments.

CRED diversified its revenue streams significantly. What started as a credit card payment app evolved into a comprehensive financial and lifestyle platform. Key revenue drivers now include CRED Cash (lending), CRED Pay (transaction fees from merchants), CRED Store (commissions from premium brand partnerships), and services like rent payments and insurance. In FY23, CRED's operating revenue reached an all-time high of ₹1,400.6 crore, a 255.9% year-on-year growth. By FY25, operating revenue further surged to ₹2,735 crore, with operating losses narrowing significantly by 51% to ₹298 crore. The average revenue per user (ARPU) reached approximately ₹2,000 in FY25, indicating strong monetization of its affluent user base.

Lessons to Take Away

  1. The Power of Contrarian Exclusivity: In a market often driven by mass-market acquisition, CRED deliberately chose to be exclusionary. By targeting only high credit score users (750+), it created an aspirational "club" that users actively sought to join. This "velvet rope" strategy made membership a status symbol, proving that for premium segments, scarcity can be more powerful than ubiquity.
  2. Attention-Grabbing Absurdity as Brand Building: CRED's intentionally bizarre and star-studded ad campaigns, despite their high cost and initial perception of being "useless," were a masterclass in brand recall and differentiation. They cut through the noise of conventional advertising, generated massive organic conversations, and embedded the brand deeply in public consciousness, demonstrating that sometimes, the most memorable marketing is the most unexpected.
  3. Brand as a Unique Brag-worthy Proposition (UBP): Kunal Shah's concept of a "Unique Brag-worthy Proposition" was perfectly executed. CRED transcended its utility as a bill payment app to become a lifestyle brand and a social signal. Using CRED implicitly communicated financial discipline and access to exclusive perks, making it something users were proud to be a part of and talk about.
  4. Strategic Investment in High-Value User Acquisition: While the upfront ad spend seemed astronomical, it was a long-term investment in acquiring a highly desirable, creditworthy user base. These users, with their strong financial behavior, offered multiple avenues for monetization (lending, premium e-commerce, financial services), justifying the initial high customer acquisition cost over their lifetime value.
