What moved the market — the factors

Today's market sentiment was largely influenced by a mix of global cues and strong domestic institutional flows. On the global front, fresh concerns over inflation in the US took centre stage. New data revealed that US consumer prices rose faster than anticipated in October, prompting traders to raise their odds of a 50 basis point rate hike by the US Federal Reserve. This led to a downturn in Wall Street, with the Dow Jones falling and tech stocks like Nvidia experiencing significant declines, raising worries about potential interest rate hikes. US Treasury yields also saw an uptick following remarks from Fed Governor Christopher Waller, who pressed the case for rate hikes.

Despite these global headwinds, the Indian market saw robust buying from Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs), who were net buyers of ₹7,212 crore today. This continued a trend of significant FII inflows into Indian equities in November, with a total of ₹53,739 crore poured in so far this month. This strong foreign buying has been a key factor in supporting the market, with the Nifty 50 extending its winning streak to seven consecutive sessions.

Domestically, the Nifty PSU Bank index rallied sharply, posting its biggest one-day gain in nearly two years. This surge was attributed to strong quarterly profits, improving asset quality, and renewed interest from foreign investors in state-owned bank stocks. Meanwhile, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers, offloading ₹7,173 crore from the market.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

The primary event driving global markets today was the release of US consumer price data for October, which showed a larger-than-expected increase. This immediately fueled speculation among traders about the Federal Reserve potentially implementing a more aggressive 50 basis point interest rate hike. The prospect of higher interest rates led to a sell-off in US stocks, particularly in the tech sector, with the Dow Jones falling and Nvidia shares sinking. US Treasury yields also rose in response to these inflation concerns and hawkish comments from Fed officials.

While global markets reacted negatively to these developments, the Indian market saw strong countervailing forces. Significant inflows from Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) helped to absorb selling pressure and maintain positive momentum. Domestically, the Nifty PSU Bank index witnessed a sharp rally, driven by positive fundamental news regarding strong quarterly profits and improving asset quality, indicating sector-specific strength. Thus, the session was characterized by global inflation fears creating headwinds, but strong FII buying and specific sector rallies providing support to the Indian indices.

Setup for tomorrow

Tomorrow's session will likely continue to be influenced by the fallout from the US inflation data and the ongoing speculation around the Federal Reserve's monetary policy. Global cues suggest a cautious start, with the Dollar Index strengthening (+0.66%) and the US 10-year Treasury yield rising (+3.00%) to 5.01, which typically indicates a risk-off sentiment for emerging markets. The USD/INR also closed slightly higher at 93.92. However, falling crude oil prices (Brent -0.76%, WTI -0.61%) could offer some relief for India, a major oil importer. Gold and Silver also saw declines. Investors will be closely watching how US markets react overnight to the inflation fears and any further comments from Fed officials. Domestically, the momentum in PSU banks could continue, and any spillover from the global tech sector's performance, particularly related to AI stocks, will be keenly observed.
