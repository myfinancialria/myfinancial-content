Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides insights into the total money flowing into the market.

When we look at the option chain, the strike price with the highest Call Open Interest often acts as a significant resistance level. This is because a large number of Call options have been sold (written) at this strike, and these writers would prefer the market to stay below this level to profit. Conversely, the strike price with the highest Put Open Interest typically indicates a strong support level. Here, many Put options have been sold, and these writers would benefit if the market remains above this level.

For Nifty 50, the implied range from maximum OI walls stands between 21,500 and 23,000. Today's fresh Put writing was heaviest near 22,400, suggesting option sellers anticipate this level to act as a floor. For Sensex, the range is 70,000 to 74,000. Fresh Call writing near 73,500 suggests a potential cap, while fresh Put writing near 71,500 indicates an expected floor. Bank Nifty's range is tighter, from 55,000 to 56,000. Heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near 55,400, potentially capping upside, while significant fresh Put writing around 55,266 suggests immediate support.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to the total Open Interest of Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests that more Put options are outstanding than Call options, which can be interpreted as a bullish signal by some, as Put writers often expect the market to rise or remain stable. Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more Call options, potentially hinting at a bearish sentiment.

For Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) is 1.19, indicating a relatively balanced book with a slight tilt towards Put writing. Today's flow, however, showed a ΔPCR of -1.13, suggesting a mixed or balanced fresh writing activity during the session.

Sensex recorded a robust PCR(OI) of 1.45, implying that Put options significantly dominate Call options, which could be seen as a strong indication of downside protection from option writers. The ΔPCR for today's flow was even higher at 2.91, signifying that today's fresh option writing was heavily skewed towards Puts, reinforcing a bullish signal from this metric.

Bank Nifty's PCR(OI) stands at 0.94, suggesting a relatively balanced options book, leaning slightly towards Call options. The ΔPCR for today's flow was -1.32, indicating a mixed fresh writing activity, similar to Nifty.

Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would experience the least aggregate financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that specific price. As expiry approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this Max Pain strike, as option writers, who are typically large institutional players, try to manage their positions to minimise their losses. This creates a "magnet effect."

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain point is at 22,400, which is about 0.5% below its spot close of 22,520.45. This suggests a potential downward pull towards this level as expiry nears.

Sensex's Max Pain is at 72,000, approximately 0.7% below its spot of 72,472.33. This also implies a potential magnetic pull downwards.

In contrast, Bank Nifty's Max Pain is positioned at 55,700, which is about 0.8% above its spot close of 55,256.65. This indicates a potential upward pull towards the Max Pain level for Bank Nifty.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer valuable insights. Their activity in index futures, in particular, is closely watched.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 9.6%. This means that out of their total index futures contracts, only 9.6% were long positions, while the remaining 90.4% were short. This is a notably low long ratio. Historically, such a low long ratio in FII index futures has often tended to coincide with periods where institutional players are exhibiting cautious or bearish sentiment towards the broader market. Their overall exposure in stock futures, however, shows a long ratio of 55.3%, indicating a more balanced to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

Synthesising today's data presents a nuanced picture. While Nifty 50's options data suggests a range-bound or mixed outlook, both Sensex and Bank Nifty showed mildly bullish inclinations from their options positioning, particularly driven by significant Put writing. The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, also declined by 5.89% to 14.38, which historically tends to coincide with a more stable or upward trending market environment.

However, the cautious stance of FIIs in index futures, reflected in their very low long ratio of 9.6%, introduces a counter-signal. Historically, such low FII index futures long ratios have tended to coincide with periods where institutional money managers are hedging their portfolios or anticipating potential downside. Therefore, when these signals align in this direction – a mix of mildly bullish options sentiment from domestic participants and cautious FII futures positioning – the next session has historically tended to see a market that might attempt to hold its ground or show limited upside, but with an underlying potential for volatility if FIIs continue to unwind long positions or add to shorts. The market may attempt to respect the immediate support levels indicated by Put Open Interest, but could face resistance at Call Open Interest walls.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals daily can empower you to form your own informed perspective. You can start by looking at the Option Chain data, which is readily available on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section. Here, you'll find the Open Interest for various Call and Put strikes. Identify the strikes with the highest Call OI and Put OI to gauge potential resistance and support levels.

Next, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI. You can also track the change in OI (ΔOI) throughout the day to see where fresh positions are being built. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in an easily digestible format. Finally, keep an eye on the India VIX for overall market volatility and the FII derivative statistics, also available on the NSE website, to understand how large institutional players are positioning themselves. Combining these data points consistently will help you develop a deeper understanding of the market's underlying structure and sentiment.
