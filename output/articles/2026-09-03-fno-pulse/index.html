India F&O Pulse — 03 Sep 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Nifty and Sensex options data point to a cautious undertone, while Bank Nifty shows signs of relative strength.

  • Sensex: Sensex also appears to have a mildly bearish bias, as significant fresh call writing emerged today, potentially capping upward moves.

Decoding today's open interest

"Open Interest" (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as Calls and Puts, that have not yet been settled or closed. It gives us a snapshot of how many contracts are currently "open" in the market. The strike price with the highest Call OI is often seen as a significant resistance level, where many traders expect the price not to go above. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI is typically considered a strong support level, where many expect the price not to fall below. These are areas where option writers (sellers) have taken positions, indicating their collective view on where the market might be capped or find a floor.

For Nifty 50, the heaviest Call OI is observed at the 24,000 strike, which is just about 0.5% above today's spot close. This suggests that option writers are comfortable selling calls at this level, anticipating that Nifty may struggle to move significantly higher. Today's fresh writing activity saw a net build of 55.5 million Call OI versus 24.9 million Put OI, indicating a stronger conviction among call sellers to cap upside moves.

In Sensex, the heaviest Call OI is positioned at 76,200, which is very close to the spot price (+0.1%). This indicates a strong immediate resistance level. Today's fresh call writing was particularly heavy near the 76,500 strike, suggesting that sellers expect the index to remain capped below this level in the near future.

Bank Nifty presents a slightly different picture, with its heaviest Call and Put OI both concentrated at the 57,500 strike. This implies a potential battleground around this level, where both upside and downside expectations are clashing. Today's fresh call writing was notable near 57,666, while fresh put writing was significant near 58,066. The higher strike for fresh put writing suggests some confidence among put sellers that the index might hold above certain levels.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The "Put-Call Ratio" (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total open interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests more put writing than call writing, which is often interpreted as a bullish sentiment from option writers who expect prices to rise or stay stable. Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more call writing, typically signalling a bearish sentiment from option writers who expect prices to fall or stay stable. We also look at the PCR of today's flow (ΔOI) to see where fresh money is being deployed, which can indicate immediate sentiment shifts.

For Nifty 50, the overall PCR(OI) stands at 0.7, indicating a relatively balanced options book with a slight bias towards more call open interest. However, today's fresh flow ΔPCR is notably lower at 0.45, suggesting that today's new option writing was significantly skewed towards calls, reinforcing a cautious or mildly bearish outlook for the immediate session.

Sensex's PCR(OI) is 0.72, also suggesting a slightly call-heavy overall open interest. The ΔPCR for today's flow is even lower at 0.31, highlighting a strong preference for fresh call writing, which typically signals an expectation of limited upside potential.

Bank Nifty's PCR(OI) is at 1.01, suggesting a relatively balanced overall options book, with put and call open interest nearly equal. Crucially, today's flow ΔPCR is a robust 2.48, indicating that fresh writing activity was heavily skewed towards puts. This strong put writing activity often points to option writers expecting the index to either hold its ground or move higher, suggesting a mildly bullish undertone for Bank Nifty.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is a specific strike price at which option writers (sellers) would theoretically experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset (like an index or stock) were to expire at that exact level. It's often viewed as a magnetic point that the index tends to gravitate towards as expiry approaches, as option writers try to maximise their profits by having the market expire at a level where their net payout is minimised.

For Nifty 50, the max pain level is identified at 24,150, which is approximately 1.2% above today's spot price. This suggests a potential upward pull towards this level, especially as we get closer to the weekly or monthly expiry.

Sensex's max pain is at 76,700, sitting about 0.7% above its spot close. This also indicates a potential upward bias from the max pain perspective, suggesting some room for movement towards this level.

For Bank Nifty, max pain is at 57,500, which is very close to its spot price. When max pain is near the current spot, it often suggests that the market might consolidate around these levels, with participants expecting the index to hover close to this strike by expiry.

It's important to remember that the "magnet effect" of max pain typically strengthens as the weekly or monthly expiry date draws nearer, as option writers' positions become more influential.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are major participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer valuable insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 11.1%. This means that out of their total index futures positions, only 11.1% were long contracts, while the vast majority (88.9%) were short. Historically, a very low FII index futures long ratio has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from this segment of participants, as they are net short on index futures.

In Stock futures, their long ratio stood at 54.8%. This indicates a more balanced, slightly long-biased position in individual stock derivatives, suggesting a more selective approach to specific companies.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we observe a combination of factors like Nifty and Sensex showing call-heavy fresh writing (indicated by a low ΔPCR) alongside a very low FII index futures long ratio, it historically tends to coincide with a cautious or range-bound outlook for the broader market in the immediate next session. While the max pain levels for Nifty and Sensex suggest a potential upward pull, the strong resistance from call walls and the dominance of fresh call writing indicate that any significant upward movement might face headwinds.

Bank Nifty, however, with its strong fresh put writing (high ΔPCR) and a relatively balanced overall PCR, historically tends to show relative strength or a mildly bullish bias compared to the broader indices in such scenarios. Overall, the data suggests that market participants are positioning for potential caps on upside moves for the Nifty and Sensex, with Bank Nifty potentially offering some support or even leading the market.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can empower you to form your own informed perspective on market sentiment. You can typically find the "Option Chain" data on the NSE website under the "Derivatives" section, updated throughout the day and at market close. Look for the "Open Interest" column for both Calls and Puts across various strike prices. The strike with the highest OI for calls and puts will give you an idea of the immediate resistance and support levels. You can also track the "Change in Open Interest" (ΔOI) to see where fresh positions are being added or closed, which is crucial for understanding today's dominant activity.

Additionally, many brokerage platforms now offer tools to visualise these option chain dynamics, including PCR and Max Pain calculations. For FII positioning, the NSE publishes a daily "FII Derivatives" CSV file that details their long and short positions in index and stock futures. By consistently reviewing these data points – Open Interest, PCR, Max Pain, and FII positioning – you can develop a more nuanced understanding of where market participants are collectively positioning themselves, helping you observe potential shifts in market sentiment.
