Pre-Market Brief — 29 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets closed mixed, with Western indices slightly down while Asian counterparts showed gains.
  • Crude oil prices are on the rise, while gold and silver saw minor declines.
  • FIIs were net buyers yesterday, though DIIs continue to be strong net purchasers.
  • Several companies are trading ex-dividend today, and one for a buyback.
  • Key news includes a major power sector merger, developments in the private banking space, and competition in quick commerce.

Where global markets closed

Global markets presented a mixed picture as they closed yesterday. Major US indices like the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all registered minor declines, indicating a cautious sentiment. European markets, represented by the FTSE 100, also closed in negative territory. In contrast, Asian markets showed strength, with Japan's Nikkei 225 posting a modest gain, and both Hong Kong's Hang Seng and China's Shanghai Composite closing significantly higher. The overall global tone appears to be one of mixed sentiment, with a slight cautious undertone in Western markets while Asian markets demonstrated resilience.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee showed a slight appreciation against the US Dollar, with USD/INR trading at ₹94.34, down 0.06%. This move aligns with a broader weakening of the US Dollar, as indicated by the Dollar Index which fell by 0.15% to 101.21. A stronger Rupee can be a positive for Indian importers as it makes imports cheaper, and can also signal confidence in the domestic economy.

In the commodities space, crude oil prices saw an uptick. Brent Crude rose by 1.57% to $73.12 per barrel, and WTI Crude was up 1.01% at $69.93 per barrel. Rising crude oil prices are generally a concern for India, a major oil importer, as they can lead to higher import bills and potentially fuel inflation. Meanwhile, precious metals experienced a slight dip, with Gold down 0.11% to $4074.10 and Silver down 0.48% to $58.94.

The US 10-Year Treasury Yield decreased by 0.46% to 4.37%. A falling US 10-year yield can sometimes make emerging markets, including India, more attractive for foreign investors seeking higher returns.

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned net buyers yesterday, infusing ₹384 crore into the Indian equity market. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their robust buying trend, with a net inflow of ₹5,748 crore.

Overnight news that matters

PFC-REC merger explained: Swap ratio, rationale, other key details as merger set to create Rs 11 lakh cr power financing giant The proposed merger of Power Finance Corporation (PFC) and REC is set to create India's largest power sector financing institution, boasting a combined loan book exceeding ₹11 lakh crore. This development is significant for the power sector, as the merged entity will have enhanced financial muscle to support large-scale infrastructure projects, potentially leading to greater efficiency and stability in power financing.

HDFC Bank overhang eases, Kotak CEO exit creates fresh uncertainty: What Pranav Gundlapalle is watching now India's private banking sector is at a crucial juncture. HDFC Bank is awaiting the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) approval for its CEO, a step that could boost governance confidence and potentially its stock performance. Conversely, Kotak Mahindra Bank faces new uncertainty as its CEO, Ashok Vaswani, has opted out of a second term, leading to some profit booking in its shares. These developments will be closely watched by investors for their implications on the leadership and stability of two of India's largest private sector banks.

Amazon’s rapid delivery push triggers $15 billion rout for Eternal, Swiggy Amazon's aggressive expansion into rapid delivery services in India is intensifying competition in the 10-minute delivery space. This has triggered concerns for existing players like Eternal and Swiggy, whose shares have reportedly fallen sharply. This news highlights the increasing competitive pressure in the quick commerce segment and could impact the valuations and future prospects of companies operating in this space.

India bonds edge up, supported by steady oil prices, halt to US-Iran strikes Indian government bonds saw an early rise, supported by strong foreign investment and stable oil prices. A diplomatic solution to recent US-Iran strikes also helped ease geopolitical tensions. This positive sentiment in the bond market suggests improved investor confidence and could lead to lower borrowing costs for the government.

Avoid expensive themes, focus on valuations and stock picking: Samit Vartak Despite a significant rebound in Indian equities, investor sentiment remains cautious due to ongoing geopolitical uncertainties. An expert suggests focusing on valuations and careful stock picking rather than chasing expensive themes. This advice underscores the importance of a disciplined investment approach in the current market environment.

What to watch in today's session

  1. FII and DII Flows: The continuation of DIIs' strong buying trend and any sustained positive momentum from FIIs will be crucial for market direction.
  2. Crude Oil Prices: With Brent and WTI crude on the rise, monitoring their trajectory will be important due to India's import dependence and potential inflationary impact.
  3. Banking Sector Developments: The news surrounding HDFC Bank's CEO approval and the leadership transition at Kotak Mahindra Bank will keep the private banking sector in focus.
  4. Power Sector Stocks: Companies like PFC and REC will be in the spotlight following the announcement of their proposed merger and its potential to create a power financing giant.
  5. Quick Commerce Competition: The intensifying competition from Amazon in the rapid delivery space could impact stocks of existing players like Eternal and Swiggy.

Bottom line

The Indian market is likely to open with a mixed to cautious bias, influenced by varied global cues and the absence of a clear signal from GIFT Nifty. While domestic institutional buying remains strong, the mixed FII activity and rising crude oil prices warrant careful observation. Investors should remain vigilant regarding sector-specific news, particularly in banking and power, and global developments throughout the day.
