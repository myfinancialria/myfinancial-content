Intel (INTC) Stock Q2 2026 Earnings Preview: What to Expect

TL;DR

  • Intel is set to announce its Q2 2026 earnings after market close on July 23, 2026, with analysts expecting modest profitability and revenue growth.
  • Consensus estimates project revenue around $14.42 billion and non-GAAP earnings per share (EPS) of $0.21-$0.22.
  • Management had guided for Q2 revenue between $13.8 billion and $14.8 billion, and non-GAAP EPS of $0.20.
  • Investors will closely watch the performance of the Data Center and AI (DCAI) segment and Intel Foundry, crucial for its turnaround.
  • Gross margins are expected to be a key focus, with management guiding for approximately 39% for Q2, a slight dip from Q1.

What this means in plain terms

When a large company like Intel announces its quarterly earnings, it's a significant event for investors. An "earnings preview" means we're looking at what financial experts (analysts) and the company itself (management guidance) expect the results to be, before the actual numbers are released. Think of it like a cricket match preview – you know the teams' past performance and current form, and based on that, you predict how they might perform in the upcoming game.

For Intel, this Q2 2026 report is particularly important because the company is undergoing a major transformation, focusing on regaining its leadership in semiconductor manufacturing and capitalising on the booming Artificial Intelligence (AI) market. Investors will be scrutinising these numbers to see if Intel is on track with its turnaround strategy, especially after its stock has seen a significant rally this year. The figures like revenue (total sales) and EPS (profit per share) give a snapshot of the company's financial health and operational efficiency over the past three months.

Expected Revenue and Profitability

For the second quarter of 2026, analysts are largely aligned on their expectations for Intel. The consensus estimate for revenue stands at approximately $14.42 billion. This falls comfortably within Intel's own guidance, which projected Q2 revenue to be in the range of $13.8 billion to $14.8 billion. This anticipated revenue growth would imply an increase of roughly 11-12% year-over-year.

On the profitability front, the Street's consensus for non-GAAP earnings per share (EPS) is around $0.21 to $0.22. This represents a significant turnaround from a year ago, when Intel reported a non-GAAP net loss of $0.10 per share in Q2 2025. Intel's management had guided for a non-GAAP EPS of $0.20 for Q2 2026. The company has a recent history of exceeding its own guidance, having delivered six consecutive quarters of revenue above expectations. In Q1 2026, Intel reported a robust non-GAAP EPS of $0.29 on revenue of $13.6 billion, both well ahead of analyst expectations.

Segment Performance and Margins in Focus

Investors will be keenly watching the performance of Intel's key business segments. The Data Center and AI (DCAI) group is particularly important, as it was Intel's strongest-performing segment in Q1 2026, with revenue reaching $5.1 billion, up 22% year-over-year. The continued momentum in Xeon 6 adoption and early contributions from partnerships (e.g., Google Cloud, Nvidia) will be critical for DCAI's growth.

The Intel Foundry business, which aims to manufacture chips for external customers, is another area of intense focus. In Q1 2026, Intel Foundry revenue grew 16% year-over-year to $5.4 billion. However, it remains unprofitable, and investors will look for updates on its path to profitability, new customer wins (like the reported manufacturing agreement with Tesla), and progress on advanced manufacturing nodes like Intel 18A.

Client Computing Group (CCG) revenue was $7.7 billion in Q1 2026, up 1% year-over-year, driven by demand for client CPUs and the growing adoption of AI PCs. Management had previously indicated prudent planning for a potential weakening in PC demand in the second half of 2026, while expecting sustained server CPU growth.

Gross margins will also be a critical metric. Management guided for Q2 non-GAAP gross margin to be approximately 39%, a modest decline from 41% reported in Q1 2026. This expected dip is attributed to a larger contribution from the Intel 18A node early in its ramp-up and certain inventory benefits in Q1 that are not expected to recur. Any deviation from this guidance could significantly impact investor sentiment, as it reflects progress on yield improvements and product mix across Intel's manufacturing processes.

Management Commentary and Industry Context

Beyond the headline numbers, the earnings call will provide crucial insights into management's outlook. CEO Lip-Bu Tan's commentary on the "sixth consecutive quarter of revenue above expectations" and the ongoing turnaround strategy will be closely scrutinised. Recent layoffs within the Data Center and AI Group, despite its strong performance, were explained as a "necessary discipline" for a durable recovery amidst competitive pressures from AMD's EPYC processors and ARM architecture. This highlights Intel's aggressive restructuring efforts to enhance efficiency.

The broader semiconductor industry context is also vital. The AI-driven demand for chips continues to be a major tailwind. Intel has positioned its CPUs as indispensable for AI inference and agentic workloads. Updates on the Panther Lake chip ramp and server CPU market share recovery will indicate Intel's ability to compete effectively in this high-growth area. The stock's impressive rally, up over 160% year-to-date in 2026, reflects significant investor optimism about its future, but also sets a high bar for the Q2 results.

FAQ

What is non-GAAP EPS and why is it important?

Non-GAAP EPS (Earnings Per Share) is a measure of a company's profitability that excludes certain non-recurring or non-cash expenses, such as stock-based compensation, restructuring charges, or acquisition-related costs. It is important because it often provides a clearer picture of a company's core operational performance, as it strips out items that may distort the underlying business trend.

How does Intel Foundry contribute to Intel's strategy?

Intel Foundry is a crucial part of Intel's IDM 2.0 strategy, aiming to become a major provider of chip manufacturing services for external customers, similar to TSMC. This diversification is intended to leverage Intel's manufacturing capabilities, enhance its technological leadership, and create a new revenue stream.

What are "AI PCs" and why are they relevant to Intel?

AI PCs are personal computers equipped with specialised hardware, like Neural Processing Units (NPUs), designed to efficiently handle AI workloads directly on the device, rather than relying solely on cloud-based AI. Intel is pushing for the adoption of AI PCs as it drives demand for its client CPUs, integrating AI capabilities into its Core Series processors.

What is the significance of gross margin in Intel's earnings?

Gross margin (revenue minus cost of goods sold, divided by revenue) indicates how efficiently a company produces its goods. For Intel, it's a key indicator of manufacturing efficiency, pricing power, and the cost of ramping up new, advanced process nodes. A higher gross margin generally implies better profitability from core operations.

How do analyst estimates influence stock performance?

Analyst estimates set the "bar" for a company's performance. If a company reports results that "beat" these estimates, its stock often rises, as it signals stronger-than-expected business health. Conversely, a "miss" can lead to a stock decline. The magnitude of the beat or miss, along with management's future guidance, heavily influences market reaction.
