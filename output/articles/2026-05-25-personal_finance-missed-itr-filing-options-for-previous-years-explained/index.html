Missed ITR Filing? Options for Previous Years Explained

TL;DR

  • If you missed filing your Income Tax Return (ITR) for previous years, you might still have options to rectify the situation.
  • The primary avenue for most taxpayers is filing an "Updated Return" (ITR-U) within two years from the end of the relevant assessment year.
  • Filing an ITR-U requires paying an additional tax of 25% or 50% on the tax and interest due, depending on when it's filed.
  • In specific cases of genuine hardship, you can apply for "Condonation of Delay" to the CBDT, especially for refund claims or foreign tax credit.
  • Failing to file ITR on time or not rectifying it can lead to penalties, interest, and loss of various tax benefits.

What this means in plain terms

Life happens, and sometimes, despite best intentions, we miss important deadlines – including filing our Income Tax Returns. For salaried individuals, self-employed professionals, or even Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) with income taxable in India, not filing an ITR can lead to complications. The good news is that the Indian tax system provides mechanisms to correct these omissions, though often with an additional cost.

Essentially, there are two main pathways if you've missed filing your ITR for a past year: filing an "Updated Return" or, in specific circumstances, seeking "Condonation of Delay." The Updated Return is a more streamlined process introduced to encourage voluntary compliance, allowing you to declare previously undisclosed income. Condonation of Delay, on the other hand, is a special request to the tax authorities to excuse your delay due to genuine reasons, typically for specific types of claims like refunds.

Understanding the Updated Return (ITR-U)

The concept of an "Updated Return" (ITR-U) was introduced in the Union Budget 2022, under Section 139(8A) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. This provision allows taxpayers to update their ITRs within a specified period, even if the original deadline for filing a belated or revised return has passed. It's a significant opportunity for those who missed reporting some income, made errors, or simply forgot to file their return.

An ITR-U can be filed within two years from the end of the relevant assessment year. For instance, if you missed filing for the financial year (FY) 2021-22 (Assessment Year (AY) 2022-23), you could file an updated return until March 31, 2025. The primary condition for filing an ITR-U is that it must result in an increase in tax liability or a reduction in a refund already claimed. You cannot file an ITR-U to claim a refund, increase a refund, or reduce your tax liability. This ensures it's used for voluntary compliance and disclosure, not for reducing tax burdens after the original deadlines.

When filing an ITR-U, you are required to pay additional tax on the income being declared. If the updated return is filed within 12 months from the end of the relevant assessment year, an additional tax of 25% of the aggregate tax and interest due is payable. If it's filed after 12 months but within 24 months, this additional tax increases to 50%. This additional tax is over and above any regular tax and interest (under Sections 234A, 234B, 234C) that would have been payable. This provision, as detailed on the Income Tax India portal (https://incometax.gov.in), aims to provide a final opportunity for taxpayers to come clean without facing severe penalties, while still ensuring the government collects its due revenue.

Condonation of Delay: When and How?

While the Updated Return is a general provision for rectifying omissions, "Condonation of Delay" is a specific power granted to the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) under Section 119(2)(b) of the Income Tax Act. This provision allows the CBDT to authorise income tax authorities to admit an application or claim for any exemption, deduction, refund, or any other relief under the Act, even if the application is made after the expiry of the period specified under the Act.

This option is generally available in cases of genuine hardship and is not a routine pathway for simply missing a deadline. The CBDT has issued various circulars and notifications over the years outlining the circumstances under which such applications may be considered. Common scenarios include:

  • Refund Claims: Where a taxpayer missed the deadline to claim a legitimate refund (e.g., TDS refund, excess advance tax paid) due to reasons beyond their control.
  • Foreign Tax Credit (FTC): If a taxpayer missed the deadline to claim FTC for taxes paid in a foreign country, leading to double taxation.
  • Other Specific Reliefs: Any other claim for deduction or exemption where the delay was due to genuine hardship.

The application for condonation of delay must be made to the Principal Commissioner of Income Tax or Commissioner of Income Tax, explaining the reasons for the delay in detail, along with supporting evidence. The CBDT, through its circulars (e.g., Circular No. 9/2015, dated 09.06.2015, accessible via incometaxindia.gov.in), has delegated powers to various income tax authorities to deal with such applications, subject to monetary limits and specific conditions. It's crucial to demonstrate that the delay was not due to negligence or lack of diligence but rather due to circumstances genuinely beyond the taxpayer's control. This is a discretionary power, and approval is not guaranteed.

Consequences of Not Filing ITR

Ignoring the ITR filing deadline can lead to several adverse consequences, ranging from monetary penalties to loss of crucial financial benefits. Understanding these implications underscores the importance of timely compliance or using the available rectification methods like the Updated Return.

Firstly, late filing of an ITR attracts a late filing fee under Section 234F of the Income Tax Act. If your total income exceeds ₹5 lakhs, the fee is ₹5,000. If your total income is up to ₹5 lakhs, the fee is ₹1,000. This fee is applicable even if you have no tax liability.

Secondly, interest is levied on unpaid tax. Under Section 234A, if you file your return after the due date, you are liable to pay interest at 1% per month or part of a month on the unpaid tax amount from the due date until the date of filing. Additionally, interest under Section 234B and 234C may apply for default or deferment of advance tax payments.

Beyond monetary penalties, not filing ITR can result in the loss of certain benefits:

  • Carry forward of losses: You cannot carry forward capital losses (short-term or long-term), business losses (except unabsorbed depreciation), or losses from house property to future years to set them off against future income.
  • Claiming certain deductions/exemptions: While some deductions (like Section 80C, 80D) can still be claimed if you file a belated return, others might be restricted. For instance, if you don't file, you can't claim a refund of excess TDS deducted.
  • Difficulty in loan applications and visa processing: Banks and foreign embassies often require ITR copies as proof of income and financial stability. A missing ITR can hinder loan approvals for home, car, or personal loans, and even affect visa applications.
  • Prosecution: In severe cases of significant tax evasion, the Income Tax Department can initiate prosecution proceedings, which may lead to imprisonment.

These consequences highlight why it's always better to file your ITR, even if belatedly, or use the Updated Return option to correct past omissions, rather than leaving them unaddressed.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 42, a self-employed graphic designer in Mumbai, earning ₹22 lakhs/year. Priya diligently pays her advance tax. For FY 2021-22 (AY 2022-23), her total tax liability after all deductions and advance tax payments was ₹1,50,000, and she had already paid ₹1,40,000 as advance tax. She had a remaining tax payable of ₹10,000. Due to a personal emergency, she completely missed filing her ITR for AY 2022-23 by the deadline (31st July 2022 for individuals, or 31st December 2022 for belated return).

Now, in August 2024, she realises this omission. She wants to rectify it by filing an Updated Return (ITR-U) for AY 2022-23. The deadline for filing ITR-U for AY 2022-23 is March 31, 2025. Since she is filing after 12 months but within 24 months from the end of AY 2022-23 (i.e., after 31st March 2024 but before 31st March 2025), she will be liable for an additional tax of 50%.

Here's how her payment would be calculated:

  1. Original Tax Payable: ₹10,000
  2. Interest under Section 234A:

    3. * Due date for filing was 31st July 2022. She is filing in August 2024. * Period of delay: August 2022 to August 2024 = 25 months. * Interest = 1% per month on ₹10,000 = ₹100 per month. Total 234A interest = ₹100 25 months = ₹2,500

  3. Interest under Section 234B: (for default in advance tax)

    4. * Assuming she paid advance tax but not 90% of her total liability by 31st March 2022, or if the remaining ₹10,000 was due to underpayment. Let's assume this ₹10,000 was indeed an underpayment from the advance tax stages. * Interest = 1% per month from 1st April 2022 to August 2024 = 29 months. Total 234B interest = ₹100 29 months = ₹2,900

  4. Late Filing Fee under Section 234F:

    5. * Since she missed the belated return deadline (31st December 2022) and her income is above ₹5 lakhs, the fee is ₹5,000.

  5. Total Tax and Interest Due (before additional tax):

    6. * ₹10,000 (Tax) + ₹2,500 (234A) + ₹2,900 (234B) = ₹15,400

  6. Additional Tax for ITR-U (50%):

    7. * 50% of ₹15,400 = ₹7,700

Priya's total payment to file the Updated Return for AY 2022-23 would be: ₹15,400 (Tax + Interest) + ₹7,700 (Additional Tax) + ₹5,000 (234F Fee) = ₹28,100

This example illustrates the significant cost associated with delayed compliance, making timely filing the most economical option.

What to do this week

  1. Identify Missing ITRs: Check your income tax portal (incometax.gov.in) to see your filing status for previous assessment years. Make a list of any years where you missed filing or need to update.
  2. Gather Necessary Documents: For any identified missing years, start collecting all relevant financial documents like Form 16/16A, bank statements, investment proofs, and business ledgers.
  3. Consult a Professional: If your situation is complex, involves multiple years, or you're unsure about the calculations for an Updated Return or Condonation of Delay, reach out to a Chartered Accountant (CA) or tax advisor for guidance.
  4. Understand Deadlines: For each missing year, determine the exact deadline for filing an Updated Return (2 years from the end of the relevant assessment year). This will help you calculate the applicable additional tax percentage (25% or 50%).
  5. Calculate Potential Liability: Based on your documents, make a preliminary calculation of your tax liability, interest, late filing fees, and the additional tax for an Updated Return. This will give you an idea of the financial implications.

FAQ

What is an Updated Return (ITR-U)?

An Updated Return allows taxpayers to correct omissions or errors in their previously filed or unfiled ITRs. It enables declaration of undisclosed income within two years from the end of the relevant assessment year, by paying additional tax.

Can I file an Updated Return to claim a refund?

No, an Updated Return (ITR-U) cannot be filed if it results in a refund, an increase in a refund, or a decrease in your total tax liability. It is specifically for increasing tax liability or reducing a loss.

What is Condonation of Delay?

Condonation of Delay is a special provision under Section 119(2)(b) of the Income Tax Act, allowing the CBDT to accept applications for claims (like refunds or deductions) even after the prescribed deadline, typically in cases of genuine hardship.

What are the penalties for not filing ITR on time?

If your total income exceeds ₹5 lakhs, a late filing fee of ₹5,000 is levied under Section 234F. For income up to ₹5 lakhs, the fee is ₹1,000. Additionally, interest under Section 234A, 234B, and 234C may apply on unpaid tax.

When is the deadline for filing an Updated Return?

An Updated Return can be filed within two years from the end of the relevant assessment year. For example, for AY 2022-23 (FY 2021-22), the deadline for ITR-U is March 31, 2025.

Can I carry forward losses if I file an ITR-U?

If you missed filing your original return, you generally lose the ability to carry forward most types of losses (e.g., capital losses, business losses) to future years. Filing an ITR-U does not retroactively restore this benefit for a year where the original return was not filed on time.
