Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options contracts provides valuable insights into where market participants, particularly option writers (sellers), are positioning themselves. The strike price with the highest Call Open Interest typically acts as a ceiling or a resistance level. This is where a large number of call options have been sold, implying that call writers expect the underlying index to not rise above this level. Conversely, the strike price with the highest Put Open Interest usually indicates a floor or a support level, where put options have been heavily sold, suggesting put writers anticipate the index will not fall below this point.

For the NIFTY 50, the heaviest Call OI was observed at the 23,650 strike, which is very close to today's closing spot price. This suggests that option writers are positioning for the upside to be capped around this level. On the Put side, the heaviest Put OI was at 23,600, indicating a strong support base just below the current spot. Today's fresh writing activity further reinforced these levels, with the heaviest new Call writing near 23,650 and significant fresh Put writing around 23,450, signalling expectations of a cap and a floor, respectively.

In SENSEX, the options chain showed the heaviest Put OI around the 75,000 mark, suggesting a significant support base. While specific heaviest fresh Call writing wasn't highlighted in the data, the overall Call wall at 77,000 remains a key resistance. Today's heaviest fresh Put writing was noted around 74,166, with put sellers expecting this level to hold as a floor.

For BANK NIFTY, the 57,500 strike saw the heaviest Call OI, suggesting a strong resistance. Interestingly, the heaviest Put OI was also observed at 57,500, which is unusual and implies a strong battleground or a potential expiry point if the index were to converge there. Today's fresh Call writing was concentrated near 57,500, with call sellers expecting this level to act as a cap. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 56,000, indicating that put sellers anticipate this level to serve as a floor.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that compares the total open interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests more Put options are open, often interpreted as a bullish signal because put writers expect prices to rise. A PCR below 1 indicates more Call options are open, often seen as a bearish signal as call writers expect prices to fall or stay capped. We also look at the PCR based on today's fresh writing (ΔOI) to understand the immediate sentiment.

For NIFTY 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stood at 0.65. This figure is below 0.7, indicating a dominance of Call options in the open interest, which typically suggests that option writers see the upside as capped. However, the PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR) was -0.05, suggesting that fresh writing activity was relatively balanced between Calls and Puts, indicating a mixed sentiment for the day's fresh positions.

The SENSEX also had a PCR(OI) of 0.66, similarly below 0.7, implying a call-heavy overall book and potential resistance for upward movement. Today's flow ΔPCR was 0.58, which is also below 1 and indicates that fresh writing activity was predominantly in Calls, reinforcing a bearish signal from the day's new positions.

BANK NIFTY presented a more balanced picture with a PCR(OI) of 0.96, close to 1. This suggests a relatively balanced open interest between Calls and Puts. The ΔPCR for today's flow was -0.01, indicating that fresh writing activity was also largely balanced, aligning with a neutral to mildly bullish sentiment from the PCR perspective.

Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It's often viewed as a 'magnet' for the index as expiry approaches, as option writers, who hold significant market power, tend to manage their positions to push the expiry towards this point.

For NIFTY 50, the Max Pain level is at 23,850. This is approximately 0.9% above today's spot price, suggesting a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry draws closer.

The SENSEX Max Pain stands at 76,500, which is about 1.2% above its current spot. This indicates a notable upward pull, with option writers potentially aiming for expiry around this strike.

BANK NIFTY has its Max Pain at 57,500, approximately 1.3% above its spot price. This level also coincides with the heaviest Call OI, suggesting a strong magnetic effect towards this strike, potentially acting as both a resistance and an expiry target for option writers.

It is worth noting that the 'magnet effect' of Max Pain tends to strengthen as the weekly or monthly expiry date approaches, as option writers adjust their positions to minimise potential losses.

This significant net short position suggests a cautious or negative outlook on the broader market indices from FIIs. Historically, such a low FII index futures long ratio has tended to coincide with periods of market weakness or consolidation.

In contrast, FIIs held a net long position in Stock Futures, with a long ratio of 54.7%. This suggests a more positive view on specific stocks, even while maintaining a bearish outlook on the overall indices.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding options and futures data can empower you to form your own perspective on market positioning. To read this yourself daily, you can primarily refer to the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website or your broker's trading platform.

On the NSE website, look for the "Option Chain" under the "Derivatives" section. This table provides real-time Open Interest data for various strike prices for Calls and Puts. Here, you can identify the strike prices with the highest Call OI and Put OI, which represent potential resistance and support levels, respectively. You can also calculate the total Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI. For FII and DII participant-wise open interest data, look for the "Participant-wise Open Interest" file under "Daily Reports" in the "Derivatives" section. This CSV file will give you the breakdown of long and short positions for various participant types. Combining these data points and observing their changes day-over-day can help you gauge shifts in market sentiment and positioning.
