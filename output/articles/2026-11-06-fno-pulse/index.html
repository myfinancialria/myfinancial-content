India F&O Pulse — 06 Nov 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, like Calls and Puts, that have not yet been settled. It provides insight into the total money committed to a position and where participants are building their bets.

When we talk about the highest Call OI strike, it indicates the price level where the maximum number of Call options are currently open. Call options give the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy an underlying asset at a specified price. For Call option writers (sellers), this strike often acts as a potential resistance level – a price point they believe the underlying index may struggle to cross, as they stand to lose money if it does.

Conversely, the highest Put OI strike represents the price level with the maximum open Put options. Put options give the buyer the right to sell an underlying asset. For Put option writers, this strike often serves as a potential support level – a price point they expect the index to hold above, as they profit if the index stays above this level.

Today's fresh writing activity offers crucial clues about immediate expectations. For Nifty 50, the aggregate flow showed a significant build in Put Open Interest (55.4 million contracts) compared to Call Open Interest (1.1 million contracts). This suggests that many new Put options were sold today, particularly at levels that indicate sellers expect the index to remain strong or find support.

On the Sensex, while the overall PCR(OI) is balanced, today's flow showed fresh Put writing, especially near 79,833. This indicates that Put sellers are establishing positions, anticipating a floor around these levels. However, the heaviest Call OI at 81,000, precisely where the index closed, suggests a strong ceiling that writers believe the index might not easily breach.

For Bank Nifty, similar to Nifty, there was a substantial net build in Put OI (1.8 million contracts) versus Call OI (0.1 million contracts). The heaviest fresh Put writing was observed near 58,500, implying that option writers are confident that Bank Nifty will find support around this level and potentially move higher.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a sentiment indicator calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 generally suggests a bullish sentiment, as there are more outstanding Put options (often sold by those expecting prices to rise) than Call options. A value below 1 typically indicates a bearish sentiment, while a value around 1 suggests a balanced or neutral market.

For Nifty 50, the PCR(OI) stands at 1.04, indicating a largely balanced book of outstanding positions. However, the PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) is a very high 49.32. This exceptionally high figure means that today's fresh option writing was heavily skewed towards Puts, suggesting that a large number of Put options were sold. Put selling often reflects a belief that the market will not fall below those strike prices, or may even rise, hence contributing to a bullish undertone for the day's activity.

The Sensex shows a PCR(OI) of 1.05, also pointing to a balanced overall options book. Its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) is 1.7, which, while not as extreme as Nifty's, still indicates that more Put options were written today than Call options, suggesting a mild bullish bias from fresh positioning.

Bank Nifty has a PCR(OI) of 0.8, which might suggest a slightly bearish bias from the overall open interest. However, today's flow ΔPCR is a significant 16.39. This strong Put-heavy fresh writing activity suggests that despite the overall PCR(OI), new participants are leaning towards a bullish view for the immediate future, expecting support levels to hold.

Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the options were to expire at that price. It's often seen as a "magnet" because, as expiry approaches, the underlying index has historically tended to gravitate towards this level. Option writers, who make up a significant portion of the market, have a vested interest in the index closing near this point.

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain is at 24,700, which is -1.3% below the current spot price of 25,030.35. This suggests that option writers, on aggregate, would prefer the index to drift lower towards 24,700 by expiry.

The Sensex Max Pain is at 80,000, also -1.3% below its spot of 81,028.02. Similar to Nifty, this indicates a potential downward pull towards 80,000 from the perspective of option writers.

Bank Nifty has its Max Pain at 58,000, which is -1.8% below its spot of 59,086.00. This is the furthest below spot among the three indices, implying a stronger gravitational pull towards 58,000 from the perspective of option writers.

It's important to remember that while Max Pain can act as a magnet, especially closer to expiry, other factors like fresh buying/selling pressure and global cues can override this effect.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are often considered 'smart money' due to their substantial capital and research capabilities. Their positioning in derivative segments, particularly futures, is closely watched.

Today, FIIs held a 24.6% long ratio in Index futures, meaning that out of their total open index futures contracts, only about a quarter were long positions, with the majority being short. Historically, a lower FII Index futures long ratio has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from this segment of participants.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a 58.8% long ratio. This indicates a more balanced to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks compared to their index futures positioning. A higher long ratio in stock futures has historically tended to coincide with selective buying interest in specific companies.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we combine today's signals, we see a nuanced picture. The dominant signal from fresh options writing, particularly the very high ΔPCR for Nifty and Bank Nifty, suggests that option writers are positioning for continued strength or at least strong support at current levels. This has historically tended to coincide with a mildly bullish bias for the next session, as these writers stand to profit if the market stays above their sold Put strikes.

However, the Max Pain levels for all three indices are below their current spot prices, indicating a potential pull towards lower levels from the perspective of option writers as expiry approaches. For Sensex, the heavy Call OI at its closing spot price of 81,000 also suggests a strong ceiling that may cap upward movement.

The FIIs' cautious stance in index futures, with a low long ratio, historically tends to coincide with a market where big institutions are not aggressively betting on a sustained upward trend in the broader index, even if they are selectively bullish on individual stocks.

Therefore, historically, when these signals align – strong fresh Put writing indicating immediate support, but Max Pain suggesting a potential downward drift towards expiry, and FIIs remaining cautious in index futures – the market has tended to consolidate or show a mild upward bias in the immediate next session, but with potential for volatility or a retest of lower levels as expiry draws closer.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily F&O data. You can access options chain data from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, or through your broker's trading platform.

To read this yourself, first, check the spot price of the index. Then, look at the options chain for the nearest expiry. Identify the strikes with the highest Open Interest for Calls and Puts; these are your potential resistance and support levels. Next, observe the "Change in Open Interest" column to see where fresh positions were added or closed today. A significant increase in Put OI at lower strikes, or Call OI at higher strikes, can give clues about where option writers expect the market to move or consolidate. Finally, calculate the PCR by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI, and also look at the PCR for today's change in OI to gauge immediate sentiment. Combining these data points daily will help you form your own informed perspective on market positioning.
