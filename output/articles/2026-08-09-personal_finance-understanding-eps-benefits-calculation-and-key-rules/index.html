Understanding EPS: Benefits, Calculation, and Key Rules

TL;DR

  • The Employee Pension Scheme (EPS) is a social security scheme managed by EPFO, providing pension benefits to eligible employees.
  • To qualify for a pension, an employee must have completed at least 10 years of service under the Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) scheme.
  • A fixed portion (8.33%) of the employer's EPF contribution, capped at ₹1,250 per month, is diverted to the EPS.
  • Pension payouts are calculated based on the employee's pensionable salary and the total pensionable service period.
  • EPS offers various types of pensions, including superannuation, widow, and children's pensions, ensuring financial support.

What this means in plain terms

For many salaried individuals in India, the Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) is a familiar savings tool. What often goes unnoticed is its crucial companion: the Employee Pension Scheme (EPS), established in 1995. While EPF is a lump sum retirement corpus, EPS is designed to provide a regular monthly income after you retire, or to your family in case of your untimely demise. It acts as a safety net, ensuring a basic financial cushion during your non-working years.

Think of it this way: a small part of what your employer contributes to your EPF account isn't actually going into your EPF savings, but instead into a separate fund – the EPS. This fund then pools contributions from millions of employees to pay out pensions to those who have retired or to their beneficiaries. The idea is to provide a predictable, albeit modest, income stream, complementing your other retirement savings.

EPS Eligibility and Contributions

The Employee Pension Scheme (EPS) is an integral component of the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) framework, specifically designed to provide social security in the form of a pension. Understanding who is eligible and how contributions are made is the first step towards appreciating its benefits.

Who is eligible for EPS?

Any employee who is a member of the Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) scheme automatically becomes a member of the EPS. However, to be eligible for a monthly pension under EPS, certain conditions must be met:

  • EPF Membership: You must be an active member of the EPF scheme.
  • Service Period: You need to have completed at least 10 years of pensionable service. This service can be cumulative across different employers, provided your EPF account was transferred.
  • Age for Superannuation Pension: The standard age for drawing a superannuation pension is 58 years. You can opt for a reduced pension from age 50, provided you meet the 10-year service criterion.

How are EPS contributions made?

Unlike the EPF where both employee and employer contribute, the EPS contribution comes solely from the employer's share. Here’s how it works:

  • Employer's Contribution Split: Out of the employer's total 12% contribution to EPF, 8.33% is directed towards the EPS. The remaining 3.67% goes into the employee's EPF account.
  • Wage Ceiling: The contribution to EPS is calculated on a maximum wage ceiling of ₹15,000 per month. This means, irrespective of how high your actual salary is, the EPS contribution is capped.
  • Maximum Monthly Contribution: 8.33% of ₹15,000 calculates to ₹1,250 per month. This is the maximum amount that goes into your EPS account each month from your employer's side. If your basic salary is less than ₹15,000, 8.33% of your actual basic salary will be contributed to EPS.
  • No Employee Contribution: Employees do not directly contribute any portion of their salary to the EPS.

Understanding EPS Pension Payouts

The primary objective of EPS is to provide a regular income stream post-retirement. The amount you receive as pension is determined by a specific formula, taking into account your service period and pensionable salary. Furthermore, EPS offers various types of pensions to cover different life situations.

How is the pension calculated?

The monthly pension under EPS is calculated using a straightforward formula:

Monthly Pension = (Pensionable Salary × Pensionable Service) / 70

Let's break down the components:

  • Pensionable Salary: This is the average of your last 60 months' wages (basic salary + dearness allowance) immediately preceding the date of your exit from the EPS scheme. Crucially, this is capped at ₹15,000 per month, even if your actual salary was much higher.
  • Pensionable Service: This refers to the total number of years you have contributed to EPS. If your service period includes a fraction of a year (e.g., 19 years and 6 months or more), it's rounded up to the next full year for calculation purposes (e.g., 19 years and 7 months would be counted as 20 years). If you have completed 20 or more years of service, an additional benefit of 2 years is added to your pensionable service, making it (Actual Service + 2) years.

Different types of EPS pension

EPS provides a safety net not just for the retired employee but also for their family members in various circumstances:

  • Superannuation Pension: This is the most common type, payable to a member upon attaining 58 years of age, provided they have completed at least 10 years of pensionable service.
  • Early Pension: A member can opt for a reduced pension from age 50, provided they have completed 10 years of service. The pension amount is reduced by 4% for each year the pension is drawn before the age of 58.
  • Widow/Widower Pension: If an EPS member passes away while in service, or after retirement but before receiving pension for a certain period, their spouse is eligible for a monthly pension.
  • Children's Pension: If a deceased member leaves behind children, each child (up to two children) is eligible for a pension until they turn 25 years old. This is typically 25% of the widow/widower pension, subject to a minimum.
  • Orphan Pension: If the deceased member has no surviving spouse but has children, the children are eligible for an orphan pension.
  • Disabled Member Pension: If an EPS member becomes permanently and totally disabled while in service, they are eligible for a monthly pension, irrespective of the length of service.

Key Features and Withdrawal Rules

Beyond eligibility and payout calculations, it's important to understand the specific rules governing withdrawals, portability, and other features of the EPS scheme. This helps in strategic financial planning and ensures you can leverage the benefits effectively.

Can I withdraw EPS before retirement?

Unlike the EPF corpus, which allows for partial withdrawals for specific needs (like house purchase, education, or marriage), the EPS is primarily designed as a pension scheme and generally does not permit lump-sum withdrawals before retirement.

  • Service Less than 10 Years: If your total pensionable service is less than 10 years, you have two options:

    • * Withdrawal Benefit: You can withdraw a lump sum amount as per the "Table D" provided in the EPS Scheme, which is linked to your service period and pensionable salary. This effectively closes your EPS account. * Scheme Certificate: Alternatively, you can opt for a Scheme Certificate. This certificate records your pensionable service and allows you to carry forward your service period when you join a new employer. This is crucial for accumulating the minimum 10 years required for a monthly pension.

  • Service More than 10 Years: If you have completed 10 or more years of service, you are only eligible for a monthly pension upon attaining the age of 50 or 58, as discussed earlier. There is no provision for a lump sum withdrawal in this scenario.

What is a Scheme Certificate?

A Scheme Certificate is a document issued by the EPFO to members who have completed less than 10 years of service but wish to preserve their pensionable service. It acts as a record of your service period and is essential for continuity. When you change jobs, you can submit this certificate to your new employer, and your previous service will be added to your current service, helping you meet the 10-year criterion for a monthly pension. This ensures that your pensionable service is not lost when you switch employers.

Nomination for EPS benefits

It is critical for every EPS member to nominate beneficiaries. In the unfortunate event of the member's demise, the nominated individual(s) will be eligible to receive the pension benefits (such as widow/widower or children's pension). Updating your nomination details with the EPFO ensures that the benefits reach the intended recipients smoothly and without unnecessary delays. You can update your nomination online through the EPFO portal.

A real example

Let's consider Rahul, 38, earning ₹28L CTC, based in Bengaluru. Rahul started his first job at age 23 and has been consistently employed, always earning above the ₹15,000 wage ceiling for EPF/EPS contributions. He has completed 15 years of continuous service under the EPF scheme.

Current Situation:

  • Age: 38 years
  • Total Service: 15 years
  • Monthly Basic Salary: Always above ₹15,000

Calculating Rahul's Projected EPS Pension:

  1. Pensionable Salary: Since Rahul's basic salary has always been above ₹15,000, his pensionable salary for EPS calculation will be capped at ₹15,000 per month.
  2. Pensionable Service: Rahul has completed 15 years of service.
  3. Applying the Formula:

    4. Monthly Pension = (Pensionable Salary × Pensionable Service) / 70 Monthly Pension = (₹15,000 × 15) / 70 Monthly Pension = ₹2,25,000 / 70 Monthly Pension = ₹3,214.28

So, based on his 15 years of service and the current EPS rules, Rahul can expect a monthly pension of approximately ₹3,214 from the EPS scheme upon reaching 58 years of age.

Impact of Continued Service: If Rahul continues to work until age 58, accumulating 35 years of service (15 years already + 20 more years), his pensionable service would be considered as (35 + 2) = 37 years (due to the 2-year bonus for 20+ years of service). In that scenario: Monthly Pension = (₹15,000 × 37) / 70 Monthly Pension = ₹5,55,000 / 70 Monthly Pension = ₹7,928.57

This example highlights that while EPS provides a guaranteed pension, the ₹15,000 wage ceiling significantly limits the maximum payout, making it a basic social security benefit rather than a comprehensive retirement income solution for high-earning individuals.

What to do this week

  1. Check Your EPF Passbook: Log in to the EPFO member portal (unifiedportal-mem.epfindia.gov.in) and check your EPF passbook. Verify your service history and ensure your pensionable service years are correctly recorded.
  2. Update Nomination Details: Ensure your nomination for EPS benefits (Form 2) is up-to-date. This is crucial for your family to receive benefits smoothly in case of an unforeseen event. You can update this online through the EPFO portal.
  3. Understand Your Pensionable Service: Calculate your total pensionable service across all employers. If you have less than 10 years, consider obtaining a Scheme Certificate if you are changing jobs, to ensure your service is carried forward.
  4. Review Your Retirement Plan: While EPS provides a base pension, for those earning above ₹15,000, it will likely be a small fraction of your pre-retirement income. Use this understanding to assess if your other retirement savings (like NPS, mutual funds, or real estate) are sufficient to bridge the gap.

FAQ

Is EPS mandatory for all employees?

Yes, if you are an employee whose basic salary plus dearness allowance is up to ₹15,000 per month, and you are covered under the EPF Act, then membership to EPS is mandatory. For those earning above ₹15,000, EPF is mandatory, and consequently, EPS also applies, although the contribution is capped at the ₹15,000 wage ceiling.

Can I contribute more to EPS?

No, the contribution to EPS is fixed at 8.33% of the employer's share of EPF contribution, calculated on a maximum wage ceiling of ₹15,000 per month. There is no provision for an employee to voluntarily contribute more to EPS.

What happens if I change jobs?

If you change jobs, your pensionable service can be transferred from your old EPF account to your new one. If your service is less than 10 years, you should obtain a Scheme Certificate to carry forward your service period. If you have completed 10 or more years, your pensionable service is already recorded, and you will be eligible for a pension at retirement.

What is the minimum pension under EPS?

The Central Government has a provision for a minimum pension of ₹1,000 per month for EPS pensioners, subject to certain conditions and notifications. This ensures a basic income for even those with minimal contributions or service.

Can NRIs get EPS benefits?

Yes, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) who were EPF members while working in India and met the eligibility criteria (e.g., 10 years of service) can claim EPS benefits upon retirement, provided they follow the due process and submit the necessary documents to EPFO.

Is EPS pension taxable?

Yes, the monthly pension received under the Employee Pension Scheme (EPS) is considered taxable income under the head "Income from other sources" and is subject to income tax as per the applicable slab rates.

What is a Scheme Certificate?

A Scheme Certificate is a document issued by the EPFO to members who have completed less than 10 years of pensionable service. It records the member's service period and allows them to carry forward this service to a new employer, enabling them to accumulate the minimum 10 years required for a monthly pension.
