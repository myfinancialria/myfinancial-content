Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, like Call and Put options, that have not yet been settled. It provides a snapshot of the total money committed to the market.

For Call options, the highest Call OI strike typically represents a significant resistance level, as many sellers (writers) of these calls expect the price to stay below this level. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike often acts as a strong support level, as put writers anticipate the price remaining above this point.

Today's activity showed distinct patterns. For Nifty 50, the heaviest fresh Put writing was observed near the 22,533 strike. This means a substantial number of new Put option sellers entered the market at this level, expecting the Nifty to find a floor and not fall below 22,533. This fresh writing, significantly below the current spot price, signals confidence among writers that the market will hold these levels. The aggregate flow further reinforces this, with a net Put OI build of 59.4 million contracts, substantially outweighing the 11.2 million Call OI build.

In Bank Nifty, the heaviest fresh Put writing was seen near the 54,300 strike. Similar to Nifty, this indicates that Put option sellers are establishing positions at this level, anticipating that Bank Nifty will not drop below 54,300. This fresh writing, combined with a net Put OI build of 0.6 million contracts against a 0.1 million Call OI build, suggests a strong conviction among put writers for an upward or stable trajectory.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to that of Call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests a bullish sentiment, as it means there are more Put options outstanding (often implying more Put writing, which is bullish). A PCR below 1 typically indicates a bearish sentiment.

For Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 1.2, which is generally considered a balanced to mildly bullish book. However, the PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR) is significantly higher at 5.31. This high ΔPCR indicates that today's fresh option writing activity was heavily skewed towards Puts. This strong Put-heavy fresh writing suggests that option sellers are expecting the Nifty to either rise or find strong support, making today's flow distinctly bullish.

Bank Nifty presents a PCR(OI) of 0.89, which is slightly below 1, suggesting a relatively balanced book with a slight lean towards Call writing. However, similar to Nifty, its ΔPCR for today's flow is a robust 4.57. This substantial Put-heavy fresh writing in Bank Nifty signals a strong bullish conviction from option writers for the day, anticipating an upward movement or a firm floor for the index.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that particular price. It often acts as a kind of "magnet" for the index, especially as the expiry date approaches, as option writers tend to try and steer the market towards this point to maximise their profits or minimise losses.

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain level is currently at 22,700. This is approximately 1.0% below the current spot price of 22,936.75. This positioning suggests that, from the perspective of option writers, a slight downward pull towards 22,700 might be preferred for expiry, although today's fresh writing signals a strong floor above this.

Bank Nifty's Max Pain is at 55,300, which is about 0.7% above its current spot price of 54,909.65. This indicates that option writers would ideally prefer Bank Nifty to expire slightly higher than its current level, implying an upward pull. This aligns with the bullish sentiment observed in today's fresh Put writing.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant players in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into institutional sentiment. Today's data shows that FIIs have an Index futures long ratio of 11.5%. This means that out of their total open index futures positions, only 11.5% are long (bets on price increase), while the vast majority are short (bets on price decrease).

This low long ratio suggests a continued cautious or bearish stance from FIIs in index futures. Historically, a significantly lower long ratio from FIIs in index futures has often tended to coincide with periods of market consolidation or downward pressure, although this is just one data point among many and does not guarantee future market direction. Their stock futures long ratio, however, stands at a more balanced 57.4%, indicating a relatively neutral to slightly bullish stance in individual stocks.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we synthesise today's options data, several signals align. The Nifty 50's mildly bullish bias, driven by strong fresh Put writing and a high ΔPCR, suggests that option writers are positioning for support at lower levels and potentially an upward move. The Bank Nifty shows an even stronger bullish conviction from option writers, with substantial Put writing and its Max Pain level sitting above the current spot.

Historically, when there is a significant build-up of Put Open Interest and a high Put-Call Ratio based on daily flow, it has tended to coincide with market participants expecting the index to either find strong support or move higher. This is because Put option sellers profit when the underlying asset stays above their strike price. While FIIs maintain a cautious stance in index futures, the options market participants, particularly the Put writers, appear to be anticipating a positive or stable session.

Therefore, when these signals align in this direction, the next session has historically tended to see the indices either consolidate with a positive bias or attempt to move higher, as the market absorbs the implications of these options positioning.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements more effectively. You can access options data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" for Nifty and Bank Nifty. Many brokerage platforms also offer detailed option chain analysis tools.

To interpret the data, look for the highest Call OI and Put OI strikes to identify potential resistance and support levels. Observe the "Change in OI" column to see where fresh buying or selling is concentrated – a significant increase in Put OI at lower strikes suggests strong support, while a rise in Call OI at higher strikes indicates resistance. Calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI to gauge overall sentiment. For Max Pain, some platforms calculate this for you, or you can manually find the strike with the lowest total premium value if the index expires there. Combining these data points daily can provide a clearer picture of market participants' collective expectations.
