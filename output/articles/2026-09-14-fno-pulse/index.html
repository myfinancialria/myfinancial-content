Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options contracts provides a crucial glimpse into where market participants, particularly option writers (sellers), are positioning themselves. A high Call Open Interest (Call OI) at a particular strike typically acts as a resistance level, as Call writers anticipate the price will not rise above that point. Conversely, a high Put Open Interest (Put OI) indicates a support level, as Put writers expect the price to remain above that strike.

For the Nifty 50, the heaviest Put OI is currently observed at the 23,300 strike, suggesting a strong foundational support for the index. On the Call side, the 24,000 strike holds the highest Call OI, indicating a significant resistance zone. Today's fresh Call writing was notably concentrated near the 21,950 strike. While this strike is considerably below the current spot, it reflects the expectations of some Call sellers for a potential cap on prices at those lower levels, perhaps looking at a broader range or hedging strategies.

The Sensex sees its most substantial Put OI at 72,000, acting as a key support. The heaviest Call OI is at the 75,000 strike, which appears to be a formidable ceiling for the index. Fresh Call writing today was most prominent near the 71,900 strike, suggesting that some market participants are anticipating resistance around that level.

In the Bank Nifty, an interesting dynamic is observed with the heaviest Put OI and Call OI both at the 57,500 strike. This suggests that 57,500 could be a significant pivot point or a very tightly contested level for the index. Fresh Call writing today was concentrated around the 53,700 strike, indicating that some Call writers are positioning for potential resistance at lower levels.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that helps gauge the overall sentiment in the options market. It is calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 generally suggests that more Puts have been written than Calls, often interpreted as a bullish signal by option writers who expect the market to find support. Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more Call writing, which can be seen as a bearish signal, as Call writers anticipate resistance.

For the Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 1.12. This level is generally considered balanced to mildly bullish, suggesting that Put writers have a slight edge in their positioning. Today's flow, as indicated by the change in Open Interest (ΔPCR), was 0, implying a balanced influx of new Calls and Puts during the session.

The Sensex recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.19, also leaning towards a balanced to mildly bullish sentiment from the perspective of option writers. Similar to Nifty, its ΔPCR for today's flow was 0, indicating an even distribution of fresh Call and Put writing.

The Bank Nifty has a PCR(OI) of 0.94. This figure is slightly below 1, suggesting that there is a marginally higher concentration of Call Open Interest compared to Put Open Interest, which could be interpreted as a balanced to mildly bearish signal from option writers. Its ΔPCR was also 0, reflecting a neutral flow of new contracts today.

Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is a concept in options theory that refers to the strike price at which the maximum number of options contracts (both Calls and Puts) would expire worthless, causing the least amount of aggregate loss for option writers. As expiry approaches, the underlying asset's price often tends to gravitate towards this Max Pain strike, almost as if pulled by a "magnet."

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain strike is currently at 23,400, which is very close to its closing spot price of 23,398.10. This proximity suggests that the index might tend to consolidate around its current levels as expiry draws nearer.

The Sensex has its Max Pain strike at 74,500. This level is approximately 0.4% below its current spot price, indicating a potential downward pull or a tendency for the index to drift lower towards this point.

In the case of Bank Nifty, the Max Pain strike is at 57,500. This is about 1.6% above its current spot price. This positioning suggests a potential upward pull, implying that the index might tend to rise towards the 57,500 level as expiry approaches.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding the nuances of options data can significantly enhance your market perspective. To read this yourself daily, you can begin by visiting the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website and navigating to the "Option Chain" section for the respective index (Nifty, Sensex, Bank Nifty). Here, you can observe the total Open Interest for Calls and Puts across various strike prices. Identify the strikes with the highest Call OI and Put OI, as these often act as major resistance and support levels, respectively. Also, pay attention to the "Change in OI" column to see where fresh buying or selling (writing) activity is concentrated for the day.

Additionally, you can calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by summing up the total Put OI and dividing it by the total Call OI. Many broker platforms also provide this data readily. While "Max Pain" is a more complex calculation, some financial websites or advanced broker terminals offer this figure. By combining these data points – identifying key OI walls, understanding the PCR sentiment, and noting the Max Pain level – you can develop a more informed understanding of market participants' positioning and the potential gravitational pulls on the index for the upcoming expiry. Remember, this is about understanding the landscape, not predicting the future with certainty.
