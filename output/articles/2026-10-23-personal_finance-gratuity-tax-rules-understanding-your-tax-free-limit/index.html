Gratuity Tax Rules: Understanding Your Tax-Free Limit

TL;DR

  • Gratuity is a lump sum payment from an employer as a token of appreciation for long and dedicated service.
  • To be eligible, an employee typically needs to complete at least five years of continuous service with an organisation.
  • For most private sector employees, the maximum tax-exempt gratuity amount is ₹20 lakhs, as per government notification.
  • The actual tax-free portion depends on specific calculations based on service tenure and last drawn salary.
  • Gratuity received by Central, State, or Local Government employees is fully exempt from income tax.

What this means in plain terms

Gratuity is essentially a thank-you bonus from your employer for the years you've dedicated to their company. Think of it as a small reward for your loyalty and service, paid out when you leave the job, typically after retirement, resignation, or in certain other circumstances like death or disablement. While it's a welcome financial boost, it's important to understand that not all of this money might be entirely free from income tax.

The government has set specific rules to determine how much of your gratuity is tax-exempt and how much might be added to your taxable income. These rules vary significantly depending on whether you work for the government or a private company, and also on how long you've worked for your employer. Understanding these nuances is crucial for effective financial planning, especially when you anticipate receiving this significant payout, to avoid any unexpected tax liabilities.

Who is eligible for gratuity?

Basic Eligibility Criteria

To be eligible for gratuity, an employee typically needs to complete a minimum of five years of continuous service with the same employer. This fundamental rule is primarily governed by The Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, which aims to provide a social security net for employees. The Act defines "continuous service" quite broadly, encompassing not just active working days but also periods of absence due to sickness, accident, authorised leave, maternity leave, or temporary lay-offs. This ensures that genuine breaks in service do not unfairly disqualify an employee from receiving this benefit.

When is Gratuity Payable?

Gratuity becomes payable to an employee upon the termination of their employment due to various reasons, signifying the end of their service with an organisation. These include:

  • Superannuation: This refers to the employee's retirement upon reaching a specified age as per company policy or statutory requirements.
  • Resignation: An employee voluntarily leaving their job after fulfilling the minimum service period.
  • Death or Disablement: In these unfortunate circumstances, the five-year service condition is waived, and the gratuity becomes immediately payable to the employee (in case of disablement) or their nominee/legal heir (in case of death), irrespective of the length of service.
  • Voluntary Retirement Scheme (VRS): Employees opting for early retirement under a specific scheme offered by the employer.
  • Retrenchment: Termination of employment by the employer, typically due to economic reasons or restructuring.

Calculating Tax-Exempt Gratuity for Private Sector Employees

The income tax treatment of gratuity received by private sector employees is nuanced and depends significantly on whether the employer falls under the purview of The Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972. This distinction is crucial as it dictates the specific formula used to determine the tax-exempt portion.

For Employees Covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972

Employers with 10 or more employees on any day of the preceding 12 months are generally covered under this Act. For employees of such organisations, the least of the following three amounts is exempt from income tax under Section 10(10)(ii) of the Income Tax Act, 1961:

  1. Actual gratuity received: The total lump sum amount disbursed by the employer.
  2. ₹20,00,000 (Twenty Lakh Rupees): This is the maximum statutory limit for tax exemption, as notified by the Central Government. This limit was last revised in 2018.
  3. 15 days' salary for each completed year of service or part thereof exceeding six months: This is a calculated amount based on your tenure and last drawn remuneration. For this calculation, "salary" specifically includes your basic pay and dearness allowance (DA), and the last drawn salary immediately preceding the termination of service is considered. The number of working days in a month is typically taken as 26 for this specific calculation.

    4. Formula: (15/26) Last Drawn Monthly Salary (Basic + DA) * Completed Years of Service (rounded up if > 6 months) * For example, if an employee served for 10 years and 7 months, it would be counted as 11 completed years. If they served for 10 years and 4 months, it would be 10 completed years.

For Employees Not Covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972

Some private sector employers, particularly smaller establishments or those not meeting the criteria of the Act, may still pay gratuity as per their employment contracts or internal policies. For employees of such companies, the tax exemption rules are slightly different, as per Section 10(10)(iii) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The least of the following three amounts is exempt from tax:

  1. Actual gratuity received: The total amount paid out by the employer.
  2. ₹20,00,000 (Twenty Lakh Rupees): This remains the overall maximum statutory limit for tax exemption, consistent with those covered by the Act.
  3. Half month's average salary for each completed year of service: Here, "average salary" is calculated based on the average of the last 10 months' basic salary and dearness allowance immediately preceding the month in which the employment terminates. Unlike the previous category, only completed years of service are considered, without rounding up for a part of the year.

    4. Formula: (1/2) Average Monthly Salary (Basic + DA for last 10 months) * Completed Years of Service (no rounding for part year) * For example, if an employee served for 10 years and 7 months, it would be counted as 10 completed years.

Gratuity for Government Employees

For individuals employed by the Central Government, State Government, or any Local Authority, the rules for gratuity are considerably simpler and more favourable from a tax perspective. Gratuity received by such employees is fully exempt from income tax, regardless of the amount received. This means that the entire gratuity payout, whether received upon retirement, resignation, or in case of death or disablement, is not subject to any income tax deductions. This full exemption is a significant benefit for government sector employees, providing them with a substantial tax-free lump sum upon the cessation of their service.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 45, ₹30L CTC, Mumbai, who worked for a private company covered under The Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972. She resigned after 15 years and 8 months of service. Her last drawn basic salary was ₹1,20,000 per month, and her dearness allowance was ₹30,000 per month. Her employer paid her a gratuity of ₹10,00,000.

Step 1: Calculate completed years of service. Priya completed 15 years and 8 months of service. Since the part thereof exceeds six months, it's rounded up to 16 completed years for gratuity calculation under the Act.

Step 2: Calculate 15 days' salary for each completed year of service.

  • Last drawn monthly salary (Basic + DA) = ₹1,20,000 + ₹30,000 = ₹1,50,000
  • 15 days' salary = (₹1,50,000 / 26) * 15 = ₹86,538.46 (approximately)
  • Amount for 16 years = ₹86,538.46 * 16 = ₹13,84,615.36

Step 3: Determine the tax-exempt amount. The tax-exempt gratuity will be the least of the following three figures:

  1. Actual gratuity received: ₹10,00,000
  2. Maximum statutory limit: ₹20,00,000
  3. Calculated amount based on service: ₹13,84,615.36

The least of these three is ₹10,00,000.

Conclusion: Priya's entire gratuity of ₹10,00,000 is tax-exempt, and she will not have to pay any income tax on this amount.

Now, let's consider a scenario where Rohan, 50, ₹40L CTC, Delhi, worked for a private company not covered under The Payment of Gratuity Act. He retired after 22 years and 3 months of service. His average basic salary + DA for the last 10 months was ₹2,50,000 per month. He received a gratuity of ₹35,00,000.

Step 1: Calculate completed years of service. Rohan completed 22 years and 3 months. Since the part thereof is less than six months, it's considered 22 completed years for gratuity calculation when not covered by the Act.

Step 2: Calculate half month's average salary for each completed year of service.

  • Average monthly salary (last 10 months) = ₹2,50,000
  • Half month's salary = ₹2,50,000 / 2 = ₹1,25,000
  • Amount for 22 years = ₹1,25,000 * 22 = ₹27,50,000

Step 3: Determine the tax-exempt amount. The tax-exempt gratuity will be the least of the following three figures:

  1. Actual gratuity received: ₹35,00,000
  2. Maximum statutory limit: ₹20,00,000
  3. Calculated amount based on service: ₹27,50,000

The least of these three is ₹20,00,000.

Conclusion: Rohan's tax-exempt gratuity is ₹20,00,000. The remaining ₹15,00,000 (₹35,00,000 - ₹20,00,000) will be added to his taxable income for the financial year in which he receives it.

What to do this week

  1. Check your employment contract: Understand if your employer is covered under The Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, as this significantly impacts your eligibility and the method of calculation for tax exemption.
  2. Estimate your potential gratuity: If you're nearing retirement or considering a job change, use the relevant formula (based on whether your employer is covered by the Act) to get a rough idea of your expected gratuity amount.
  3. Keep accurate salary records: Ensure you have precise records of your basic salary and dearness allowance, especially for your last 10 months of service, as these figures are crucial for gratuity calculations.
  4. Consult your HR department: Clarify your company's specific gratuity policy and how it aligns with the statutory provisions, especially regarding the calculation methodology and payout procedures.
  5. Factor gratuity into financial planning: If you anticipate receiving a taxable portion of your gratuity, consider its impact on your overall income tax liability for the year you receive it, and plan accordingly.

FAQ

What is the primary purpose of gratuity?

Gratuity is a statutory benefit paid by an employer to an employee as a token of appreciation for long and dedicated service. It is a lump sum payment made upon termination of employment, providing a financial cushion.

Is gratuity always tax-free?

No, gratuity is not always fully tax-free. While gratuity for government employees is fully exempt, for private sector employees, there's a maximum tax-exempt limit of ₹20 lakhs, and the actual exempt amount is calculated based on specific rules.

Do I need to complete 5 years of service to receive gratuity?

Generally, yes, a minimum of five years of continuous service is required for gratuity eligibility under The Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972. However, this condition is waived in cases of death or disablement, where gratuity is payable irrespective of service length.

How does the ₹20 lakh limit apply to gratuity?

The ₹20 lakh limit is the maximum amount of gratuity that can be exempt from income tax for private sector employees, as notified by the Central Government. The actual exempt amount will be the least of the actual gratuity received, ₹20 lakh, or the amount calculated by specific formulas.

What happens if I receive gratuity from multiple employers?

If you receive gratuity from more than one employer, either in the same financial year or across different financial years, the aggregate of the tax-exempt gratuity from all employers cannot exceed the overall statutory limit of ₹20 lakhs.

Is gratuity considered part of my salary for tax purposes?

The taxable portion of your gratuity, if any, is considered "income from salaries" and is added to your total income for the financial year in which it is received. It is then taxed at your applicable income tax slab rates.

Can I claim any deductions on the taxable portion of gratuity?

No, there are no specific deductions available against the taxable portion of gratuity. It is simply added to your 'income from salaries' and taxed as per your income tax slab, without further exemptions or deductions.
