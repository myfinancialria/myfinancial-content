Pre-Market Brief — 29 July 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets show a mixed to cautious tone, with significant weakness in some Asian indices.
  • GIFT Nifty indicates a slightly negative open for the Indian market.
  • FIIs were net buyers yesterday, but DIIs continue to provide strong support, offsetting FII selling over the past week.
  • A sharp crash in South Korea's Kospi index, triggered by chipmaker earnings and broader AI bubble concerns, is a key global sentiment driver.
  • Nifty's key battleground appears to be around the 24200-24270 zone, with narrow CPR suggesting potential for trending moves.
  • Rising crude oil prices and global inflation/debt concerns remain the primary risks for today's session.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight and this morning in Asia. While US and UK market data is unavailable, Asian markets saw significant divergence. Japan's Nikkei 225 plunged nearly 6%, and South Korea's Kospi index crashed over 11% following chipmaker earnings, extending its monthly drop to 43% and raising concerns about the AI boom. In contrast, Hong Kong's Hang Seng posted a healthy gain, while China's Shanghai Composite closed lower.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, while crude oil prices saw a significant jump. Gold remained largely stable, and silver gained.

  • USD/INR: The Rupee appreciated by 0.10% to 95.68 against the US Dollar.

    • Interpretation: A stronger Rupee is generally positive for importers as it makes imports cheaper. It can also provide a slight tailwind for sectors like IT and pharmaceuticals if the appreciation is moderate, but a sharp appreciation can impact export competitiveness.

  • Dollar Index: The Dollar Index weakened by 0.23% to 101.28.

    • Interpretation: A weaker Dollar Index typically indicates a broader risk-on sentiment globally, which can be supportive for emerging market currencies and equities.

  • Brent Crude: Brent crude surged by 3.57% to $87.09 per barrel.

    • Interpretation: Rising crude oil prices are a significant concern for India, a major oil importer. This can lead to higher inflation, increased current account deficit, and put pressure on sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paints, and aviation.

  • WTI Crude: WTI crude also rose by 3.39% to $81.95 per barrel.

    • Interpretation: Similar to Brent, the increase in WTI crude signals inflationary pressures and potential headwinds for the Indian economy and specific sectors.

  • Gold: Gold prices saw a marginal dip of 0.19% to $4028.70.

    • Interpretation: A slight dip in gold suggests some risk appetite returning or profit booking, but its overall movement is relatively muted compared to other commodities.

  • Silver: Silver gained 1.14% to $57.95.

    • Interpretation: Silver's rise, often seen as an industrial metal, could indicate some optimism about industrial demand, but it can also track gold as a safe-haven asset.

  • US 10Y Yield: Data unavailable.

Option-chain signals (OI) — where support & resistance sit

Option chain data for the nearest expiry provides insights into where market participants expect support and resistance to hold.

NIFTY (PCR 0.93)

  • Highest CALL OI (resistance): 24200 (10,710,505 contracts), followed by 25000 and 24500.
  • Highest PUT OI (support): 24200 (14,332,825 contracts), followed by 24000 and 24100.

    • Interpretation: The 24200 strike has the highest Call OI and also the highest Put OI, making it a critical pivot point and a battleground for bulls and bears. High Call OI at 24200 suggests sellers believe Nifty might struggle to move significantly above this level, while high Put OI at 24200 indicates buyers are actively defending this level. A PCR of 0.93 leans neutral to slightly cautious.

BANK NIFTY (PCR 0.85)

  • Highest CALL OI (resistance): 58000 (1,826,760 contracts), followed by 57000 and 57500.
  • Highest PUT OI (support): 58000 (1,253,940 contracts), followed by 57000 and 56000.

    • Interpretation: For Bank Nifty, 58000 stands out as a strong resistance level due to the highest Call OI. The highest Put OI is also at 58000, but with fewer contracts than Call OI, suggesting stronger resistance than support at this level. A PCR of 0.85 indicates a cautious sentiment. The 57000 level also shows significant activity on both sides.

SENSEX (PCR 1.29)

  • Highest CALL OI (resistance): 78000 (2,589,160 contracts), followed by 77600 and 77500.

Overall levels to respect today: For Nifty, the 24200 level is a crucial pivot. Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

Today, the focus will largely be on global market sentiment and commodity price movements, as no major Indian economic data or central bank events are scheduled.

  • Global Market Warning Signals (Oil, AI, Debt Risks): The news highlights elevated oil prices, concerns about the AI boom, and rising debt risks globally.

    • Typical market reaction: Such warnings usually lead to increased market volatility, risk aversion, and potential outflows from emerging markets. Rising crude oil specifically can trigger inflation concerns and impact sectors like OMCs, aviation, and paints.

  • AI Boom Complicating Central Bank Policy (BIS): The Bank for International Settlements (BIS) noted that AI could complicate central banks' inflation assessments and monetary policy decisions.

    • Typical market reaction: Uncertainty regarding central bank policy, especially concerning inflation and interest rates, can lead to investor caution and sideways market movement until clarity emerges.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Nifty's response to 24200: This level is a crucial pivot based on both technicals (CPR) and option chain data (highest Call & Put OI). A sustained move above or below this could dictate the day's trend.
  2. Crude Oil Prices: The significant jump in Brent and WTI crude will be closely monitored. Any further escalation could weigh heavily on Indian markets and specific sectors.
  3. Global Market Sentiment: The performance of other Asian markets, especially the recovery or further decline in Kospi and Nikkei, will influence Indian market sentiment.
  4. FII/DII Flows: While FIIs turned positive yesterday, their sustained buying or a return to selling, alongside DIIs' continued support, will be key to market direction.
  5. Corporate Actions: Stocks going ex-dividend today, such as TTKPRESTIG, ROUTE, SIEMENS, and GODREJAGRO, might see price adjustments and increased trading activity.
  6. Sectoral Performance: Watch out for sectors like NBFCs (Tata Capital), FMCG (Varun Beverages, HUL), and Cement (Ambuja Cements) based on recent earnings and news flow.

Bottom line

The Indian market is likely to open with a cautious tone, indicated by the GIFT Nifty, influenced by mixed global cues and significant weakness in some Asian markets. The 24200 level for Nifty will be a critical point to watch, acting as a battleground for market participants. Investors should remain vigilant about rising crude oil prices and global inflation concerns, which pose key risks for the day.
