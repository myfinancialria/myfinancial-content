India F&O Pulse — 20 Oct 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Indian Indices Signal Range-Bound Movement Amidst Mixed Open Interest and Futures Data

TL;DR

  • NIFTY 50: Open interest and flow data suggest a range-bound to mixed outlook for tomorrow's session, with a potential upward pull towards the max pain level.
  • SENSEX: Similar to Nifty, Sensex is indicating a range-bound and mixed sentiment, with option writers positioning for a move towards the max pain point.
  • BANK NIFTY: The banking index also points to a range-bound and mixed trading day, with strong support and resistance levels visible from option activity.

What the indices did today

The NIFTY 50 closed the day at 22,888.95, marking a decline of 1.11%. The index ended the session well within its option-chain implied range of 22500 to 24000, suggesting market participants expect it to largely stay within these boundaries.

The SENSEX also experienced a downturn, settling at 73,852.90, down 1.17% for the day. Its closing price remained comfortably within the broader option-chain implied range of 72000 to 75000, indicating a similar expectation of contained movement.

The BANK NIFTY saw a comparatively smaller dip of 0.64%, closing at 55,488.10. This index finished the day nestled between its immediate option-chain walls of 55000 and 56000, highlighting a tight expected trading range.

Decoding today's open interest

Open interest (OI) in options provides valuable insights into where market participants, particularly option writers (sellers), are positioning themselves. The strike with the highest Call OI typically acts as a significant resistance level, indicating a ceiling where many believe the market will struggle to rise further. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI often serves as a strong support level, suggesting a floor below which the market is not expected to fall easily.

For the NIFTY 50, today's heaviest fresh Call option writing was observed near the 21450 strike. While this strike is significantly below the current spot price, the presence of fresh call sellers here suggests that some market participants are positioning for the index to not rise above this level, or even potentially decline towards it, implying a perceived 'cap'.

In the SENSEX, the heaviest fresh Call option writing was noted near the 71000 strike. Similar to Nifty, this strike is considerably below the current spot price, and its writing indicates that some call sellers anticipate the index may not ascend beyond this point, or could even move lower.

The BANK NIFTY showed the heaviest Call OI at the 56000 strike, which is just 0.9% above its spot price, suggesting a close ceiling for the index. On the Put side, the heaviest Put OI was found at 55000, approximately 0.9% below spot, indicating a robust floor. Additionally, fresh Call option writing was concentrated near the 52600 strike, again a level far below the current spot, with call sellers expecting a potential cap.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator that compares the number of put options traded to the number of call options traded. A PCR (OI) value below 1 generally suggests more call options are open than put options, which can be seen as a bearish leaning, while a value above 1 suggests more put options, often interpreted as bullish.

Today, the NIFTY 50 recorded a PCR (OI) of 0.85, indicating a relatively balanced book with slightly more open call positions. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) was 0, signalling a mixed or balanced flow of new positions being added during the session.

For the SENSEX, the PCR (OI) stood at 0.73, also pointing to a balanced book with a slight inclination towards open call positions. The ΔPCR for today's flow was 0, reflecting a neutral and mixed sentiment from new option activity.

The BANK NIFTY had a PCR (OI) of 0.89, suggesting a balanced open interest book. Its ΔPCR for today's flow was also 0, indicating an equilibrium in fresh put and call writing activity for the day.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset expires at that level. As expiry approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this "max pain" strike, acting like a magnet.

For the upcoming expiry, the NIFTY 50's max pain level is at 23100, which is approximately 0.9% above its current spot price. This suggests a potential upward pull for the index as expiry nears.

The SENSEX has its max pain positioned at 74600, about 1.0% above its spot price. This indicates that option writers are collectively positioned for the Sensex to potentially settle higher towards this strike.

The BANK NIFTY's max pain point is at 55800, which is around 0.6% above its current spot. This implies a relatively close magnetic pull towards a slightly higher level for the banking index.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) play a significant role in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer clues about broader market sentiment. It is important to note that today's FII data reflects yesterday's activity, as the NSE archive for today is not yet published.

As of yesterday, FIIs held a low Index futures long ratio of 10.4%, with 36,661 long contracts against 316,591 short contracts. Historically, such a low long ratio in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from these large participants. In contrast, their Stock futures long ratio was 55.7%, indicating a more balanced to slightly bullish stance in individual stock futures, with 3,613,429 long contracts versus 2,875,948 short contracts.

Stocks where money moved today

In the F&O watchlist, we observed some notable movements in individual stocks. Among the top gainers, SBIN advanced by 1.15% to ₹973.70, seeing significant volume of over 1.5 crore shares. LT also posted gains, rising 0.96% to ₹3661.90.

On the losing side, AXISBANK was a significant laggard, declining 4.19% to ₹1236.00 with a volume of over 1.2 crore shares. INFY shed 2.63% to ₹1130.70, while BAJFINANCE dropped 2.52% to ₹957.35. HDFCBANK closed lower by 2.31% at ₹712.00, registering a notably high volume of over 3.3 crore shares. HINDUNILVR also saw a decline of 2.14% to ₹1853.20.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When the open interest data across major indices like Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty consistently signals a range-bound or mixed outlook, as indicated by balanced PCRs and option walls, the market has historically tended to consolidate rather than exhibit strong directional moves. The presence of max pain levels slightly above current spot prices suggests a mild upward bias for expiry, but this pull is often counterbalanced by significant call writing activity, even at lower strikes, which implies a cautious sentiment from some option sellers. Coupled with FIIs maintaining a low long ratio in index futures, this combination of signals has historically tended to coincide with sessions characterised by volatility within a defined range, with participants closely watching key support and resistance levels.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding the daily pulse of the F&O market can be a powerful tool for any investor. To read this data yourself, you'll want to focus on a few key data points. First, examine the Open Interest (OI) chain for the current expiry. Look for the highest Call OI strikes, which typically act as resistance, and the highest Put OI strikes, which often provide support. Pay attention to where fresh writing (increase in OI) is occurring, as this shows where new positions are being taken.

Next, calculate or find the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) for both total OI and daily change in OI. A PCR near 1 suggests balance, while deviations can hint at bullish or bearish leanings. Also, identify the "max pain" strike; this is often a magnet for the index as expiry approaches. Finally, keep an eye on FII derivative positioning data, particularly their long-short ratios in index futures, as these large participants can influence market direction. You can find most of this data on the NSE India website under the derivatives section, or through your broker's platform. By combining these different perspectives, you can build a more holistic understanding of market sentiment and positioning, helping you interpret potential movements.
