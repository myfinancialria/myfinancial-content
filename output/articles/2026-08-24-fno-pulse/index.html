Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding options contracts that have not yet been closed or exercised. It provides a snapshot of the total market participation and conviction at various strike prices. When we talk about the "highest Call OI strike," it indicates the price level where the maximum number of Call options have been written (sold). These Call writers typically expect the underlying index to not rise above this level, effectively creating a potential resistance point or a "Call wall." Conversely, the "highest Put OI strike" represents the price where the most Put options have been written. Put writers generally anticipate the index will not fall below this level, thus forming a potential support base or a "Put wall."

For the Nifty 50, the heaviest Call Open Interest is positioned at 24,300, which is just 0.3% above the current spot price. This suggests that option sellers believe the upside might be capped around this level. On the Put side, the strongest base is at 24,000, approximately 0.9% below spot, indicating a potential floor. Today's fresh Call writing was most concentrated near 24,250, reinforcing the idea that sellers expect resistance close to the current levels. Simultaneously, fresh Put writing was significant near 24,100, suggesting some confidence in a floor slightly below spot.

The Sensex saw its heaviest Call OI at 77,500, a mere 0.2% above the spot price, acting as a clear ceiling for the immediate term. While the overall option-chain implied range is broad (75,500 – 77,500), the proximity of the Call wall is notable. Fresh Call writing today was most active around 77,400, indicating that new sellers are also positioning for a cap around these levels. Fresh Put writing, on the other hand, was observed around 76,433, suggesting a potential support level further down.

For Bank Nifty, the 58,000 strike holds the highest Call OI, approximately 0.8% above the spot, presenting a strong resistance zone. The heaviest Put OI is at 57,000, about 0.9% below spot, which could act as a crucial support. Today's trading session saw significant fresh Call writing near 57,633, suggesting that option sellers are expecting the index to struggle to move much higher. Fresh Put writing was concentrated around 56,800, indicating some belief in a base forming at lower levels. The aggregate flow for Bank Nifty showed a net Call Open Interest build of 4.5 million contracts, significantly outweighing the 0.6 million contracts of Put Open Interest build, pointing towards a predominantly bearish sentiment from option writers today.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total Open Interest of Put options to Call options. A PCR value below 1 generally suggests that more Call options have been written than Put options, often interpreted as a bearish sentiment among option writers, as they expect prices to either fall or remain range-bound with limited upside. Conversely, a PCR above 1 might indicate a bullish bias.

For the Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 0.66. This figure, being below 0.7, typically suggests that Call options dominate, implying that upside movements might be capped. The PCR based on today's fresh flow (ΔPCR) was -0.53, which indicates a mixed or balanced flow of new Call and Put writing during the day, not strongly favouring either side.

The Sensex recorded a PCR(OI) of 0.73, which is considered a more balanced book, not strongly leaning bullish or bearish on an aggregate basis. However, today's flow ΔPCR was 0.26, suggesting that fresh writing activity was relatively Call-heavy, indicating a cautious or mildly bearish sentiment for the day.

Bank Nifty's PCR(OI) is at 0.68, similar to Nifty, suggesting a dominance of Call options and a potential cap on upward movement. The ΔPCR for today's flow was 0.14, indicating that fresh writing was predominantly on the Call side, reinforcing a bearish undertone for the banking index.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is a concept in options theory that refers to the strike price at which the maximum number of options contracts (both Calls and Puts) would expire worthless, causing the least financial loss to option writers. As expiry approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this "Max Pain" strike, as option writers, who are typically large institutional players, might try to influence the price to expire at a level most favourable to their positions.

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain strike is at 24,400, which is approximately 0.7% above the current spot price of 24,219.05. This suggests that, from the perspective of option writers, the ideal expiry point for them is slightly higher than where the index currently stands.

The Sensex has its Max Pain strike at 77,800, about 0.6% above its spot price of 77,369.11. Similar to Nifty, this indicates a potential upward pull towards this level as expiry nears.

Bank Nifty's Max Pain is located at 58,000, which is roughly 0.8% above its current spot of 57,525.95. This also suggests an upward bias from the Max Pain perspective, implying that option writers might prefer the index to settle higher. It is important to remember that the magnetic pull of Max Pain typically strengthens as the weekly or monthly expiry date draws closer.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements more effectively. You can access the Open Interest data for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty options directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, specifically the "Option Chain" tab. Many brokerage platforms also provide this data in an easily digestible format.

When you look at the option chain, pay close attention to the strikes with the highest Open Interest on both the Call and Put sides – these are your potential "walls." Observe where fresh Open Interest (Change in OI) is building up for the day, especially near the current spot price. A significant build-up in Call OI suggests resistance, while a build-up in Put OI indicates support. Calculating the PCR (total Put OI / total Call OI) and its daily change can give you a broad sense of market sentiment. Combining these observations with the Max Pain level can offer a more comprehensive view of how option writers are positioning themselves for the upcoming expiry. Remember, these are tools for analysis, not guarantees of future price movements.
