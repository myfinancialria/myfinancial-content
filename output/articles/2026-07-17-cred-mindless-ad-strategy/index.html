Why CRED Spent ₹400 Cr on 'Useless' Celebrity Ads — and Cracked Premium Distribution

April 2021. The Indian Premier League (IPL) season was in full swing, and millions of eyes were glued to their screens. Suddenly, a familiar face appeared, but not in the way anyone expected. Rahul Dravid, "The Wall," India's famously calm and composed cricketer, was losing his cool. Seething in Bengaluru traffic, he yelled, "Indiranagar ka gunda hoon main!" ("I am the goon of Indiranagar!") smashing rearview mirrors with a cricket bat. The ad was absurd, hilarious, and instantly viral.

This wasn't just a quirky ad; it was a deliberate, expensive, and seemingly counter-intuitive marketing masterstroke by CRED, a fintech startup. While many scratched their heads wondering why a company would spend a fortune on such "useless" celebrity antics, CRED founder Kunal Shah knew exactly what he was doing. It was a calculated gamble, designed not for mass appeal, but for premium distribution, turning every user into a status signal.

The World Before

Before CRED burst onto the scene in 2018, paying credit card bills in India was a fragmented, unrewarding, and often clunky experience. Users typically had to navigate individual bank portals, which were rarely designed for a seamless user experience. There was no single, elegant platform that consolidated all credit card management, let alone rewarded users for responsible financial behavior.

The broader fintech landscape was largely focused on acquiring users en masse, often through aggressive discounts and cashback offers. Companies like Paytm and PhonePe chased volume, aiming to onboard as many users as possible across all segments of society. The credit card market itself, while growing, had a relatively low penetration in India (under 5% in 2020), with a significant portion of discretionary spending concentrated among a smaller, affluent segment. Many believed that to succeed in India, you had to cater to the "masses."

Kunal Shah, a serial entrepreneur who previously founded FreeCharge, held a different philosophy. He believed that the top 1-2% of Indian consumers, though a smaller group, accounted for 70-80% of discretionary spending. This segment, comprising urban professionals with high disposable incomes and strong credit scores (typically above 750), was underserved. They valued convenience, premium experiences, and exclusivity. Traditional marketing often overlooked this niche, or attempted to reach them with generic, uninspired campaigns.

The Crack

CRED's strategy was built on a core idea: build an exclusive club for creditworthy individuals and reward them for timely credit card payments. Only users with a high credit score could join, immediately creating a sense of exclusivity. This wasn't just about utility; it was about status. But how do you market exclusivity to a discerning audience that often tunes out traditional advertising?

Shah and his team decided to "change tracks" from conventional marketing. Their first major splash came during the IPL 2020, with a campaign titled "Not Everyone Gets It." These ads featured yesteryear Bollywood stars like Anil Kapoor, Madhuri Dixit, and Bappi Lahiri humorously "auditioning" for a CRED commercial and failing to make the cut, poking fun at celebrity endorsements themselves. The campaign was designed to be quirky, shareable, and to break through the clutter of typical IPL advertising. It worked, leading to a 700% increase in app downloads after its launch.

But it was the IPL 2021 campaign that truly solidified CRED's audacious approach. The "Indiranagar ka Gunda" ad featuring Rahul Dravid, along with other spots featuring Jackie Shroff and other sports personalities, became cultural phenomena. These ads were intentionally absurd, leveraging nostalgia by featuring iconic personalities from the 90s and 2000s, but casting them against their public personas. The humor relied on the audience's intimate knowledge of these celebrities, creating an inside joke that further reinforced the "not everyone gets it" mentality.

This wasn't cheap. CRED signed a three-year deal with IPL for ₹120 crore, making them an official league sponsor. In FY22 (the year of the Dravid campaign), CRED's marketing and promotion spend hit a staggering ₹976 crore. To put this in perspective, their revenue in FY22 was ₹394 crore, meaning they spent more than double their entire annual revenue on advertising. Many saw this as reckless spending, an unsustainable cash burn. However, CRED's founder Kunal Shah explained his philosophy: "Tech companies invest capital for several years in building large distribution and engagement before they monetize."

The strategy was not merely about impressions; it was about earned media value. The viral nature of the ads generated 10 to 15 times more free publicity (through social sharing, news coverage, and brand mentions) compared to the paid media investment. The Dravid ad alone garnered over 3.4 million views on YouTube within days and trended at #14 in India. This kind of virality built massive brand recall and positioned CRED as a cool, aspirational brand.

By focusing on a niche target audience (those with high credit scores) and creating content that resonated deeply with them, CRED turned a functional product into a status symbol. The ads weren't selling a product directly; they were selling a lifestyle, a sense of belonging to an exclusive club where financial responsibility was rewarded and celebrated. The "useless" ads were, in fact, highly effective filters, attracting precisely the premium users CRED wanted.

What Happened Next

The aggressive, unconventional marketing paid off in terms of user acquisition and valuation. In April 2021, shortly after the Rahul Dravid campaign, CRED's valuation soared to $2.2 billion, achieving unicorn status. By the end of 2021, it crossed $6.4 billion. In 2022, CRED's user base reached 11.2 million members, up from 7.5 million in FY21. By June 2026, the company reported 17 million monthly active members.

Revenue saw substantial growth too, from ₹88.6 crore in FY21 to ₹393.6 crore in FY22, and further to ₹1,400 crore in FY23. In FY25, CRED reported an operating revenue of ₹2,735 crore, a 16% year-on-year increase. The company expanded its offerings beyond credit card payments to include lending products (CRED Cash, CRED Mint), utility bill payments, and insurance through CRED Garage, diversifying its revenue streams.

However, the high marketing spend meant significant losses. In FY22, the year of the biggest ad campaigns, CRED reported a net loss of ₹1,280 crore, more than double the ₹524 crore loss from the previous year. While operating losses narrowed to ₹298 crore in FY25, total losses still stood at ₹1,457 crore.

Despite the losses, investors continued to bet on CRED's long-term vision of monetizing its high-value user base. The strategy of acquiring premium users, who are more likely to transact more and adopt multiple financial products, aims to boost the lifetime value of each customer. As of June 2026, CRED processed over 40% of all credit card bill payments in India.

Lessons to Take Away

  1. Exclusivity as a Differentiator: In a crowded market, catering to a niche premium audience can be more effective than a mass-market approach. CRED deliberately excluded those without high credit scores, turning membership into a status symbol and attracting a high-value user base.
  2. The Power of Absurdist, Shareable Content: Marketing doesn't always need to be direct or logical. CRED's intentionally "useless" ads were designed for virality, generating immense earned media and brand recall by playing on nostalgia and humor, creating an "inside joke" for its target audience.
  3. Long-Term Brand Building vs. Short-Term Profit: CRED's massive upfront investment in marketing, far exceeding its initial revenue, was a calculated move to build a strong brand and a loyal user base. This "acquire now, monetize later" strategy can lead to significant short-term losses but aims for substantial long-term value from a high-quality customer segment.
  4. Targeted Distribution through Cultural Moments: By associating with a major cultural event like the IPL and dropping ads at peak viewership, CRED ensured maximum visibility within its target demographic, amplifying the impact of its unconventional campaigns.
