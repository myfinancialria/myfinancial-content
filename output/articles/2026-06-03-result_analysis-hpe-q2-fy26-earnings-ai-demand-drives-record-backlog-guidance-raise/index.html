HPE Q2 FY26 Earnings: AI Demand Drives Record Backlog & Guidance Raise

TL;DR

  • Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) reported a strong Q2 FY26, with revenue up 40% year-on-year to $10.7 billion, significantly beating estimates.
  • Non-GAAP diluted EPS more than doubled to $0.79, comfortably surpassing analyst expectations of $0.53.
  • The company achieved a record-breaking backlog, driven by orders more than doubling year-on-year and outpacing revenue growth.
  • Networking revenue surged by 148.2%, partly due to the Juniper Networks acquisition, while Cloud & AI revenue grew 22.9%.
  • HPE dramatically raised its full-year FY26 guidance for revenue growth (29%-33%) and non-GAAP EPS ($3.35-$3.45), now exceeding its previous FY28 targets.

What this means in plain terms

When a company like Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) announces its quarterly results, investors and analysts closely examine the numbers to understand its financial health and future prospects. This quarter, HPE delivered what's called a "blowout" performance, meaning their actual results were far better than what the market expected. This is a big deal because it signals that the company is not just doing well, but potentially accelerating its growth.

The key takeaways are that HPE saw a massive increase in revenue and profits, primarily fuelled by strong demand for its networking solutions and, crucially, its AI-related infrastructure. The term "record backlog" indicates that they have a lot of confirmed orders for future delivery, suggesting continued strong revenue in the coming quarters. Furthermore, the company's decision to "raise guidance" means their own internal forecasts for the rest of the financial year are now much more optimistic, even surpassing goals they had set for two years down the line. This combination of current strong performance and a confident outlook is what caused the stock to surge.

Headline Numbers and Market Reaction

Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) delivered an exceptional second quarter for fiscal year 2026, significantly outperforming market expectations. The company reported total revenue of $10.7 billion, marking a substantial 40% increase year-over-year. This figure comfortably surpassed the Zacks Consensus Estimate of $9.77 billion.

On the profitability front, HPE's non-GAAP diluted earnings per share (EPS) came in at $0.79, more than doubling the $0.38 reported in the prior-year period and crushing the analyst consensus estimate of $0.53 per share. The non-GAAP gross margin expanded to 36.9%, an increase of 750 basis points year-over-year, while the non-GAAP operating margin improved to 13.3%, up 530 basis points from the year-ago quarter.

The market reacted very positively to these results, with HPE's stock surging by approximately 25-30% in early trading following the announcement. This strong reaction highlights the extent to which the results redefined market expectations for the company.

Segment Performance and AI-Driven Demand

HPE's strong Q2 performance was broad-based, with key segments showing robust growth, particularly driven by demand for AI infrastructure.

Networking Segment Leads Growth

The Networking segment was a standout performer, with revenue surging an impressive 148.2% year-over-year to $2.7 billion. While some of this growth is attributable to the acquisition of Juniper Networks, demand signals remained strong even after accounting for this. Within Networking, Campus & Branch revenue increased by 50.2%, Data Center Networking jumped 233.3%, and Security revenue rose 155.1%. Routing contributed $775 million, a significant leap from just $1 million in the prior-year period. The operating profit margin for Networking was 21.6%.

Cloud & AI Segment Fuels Future Growth

The Cloud & AI segment, which is larger by revenue and critical for HPE's long-term strategy, saw its revenue climb 22.9% year-over-year to $7.7 billion. Server revenue, a crucial component for AI inferencing and infrastructure, increased by 32.7% to $5.5 billion. HPE noted that traditional server orders also increased in triple digits year-on-year as customers modernise their compute infrastructure and invest in AI inferencing. Storage revenue grew 2.4% to $1.2 billion, and Financial Services revenue was up 5.6% to $0.9 billion. The operating profit margin for Cloud & AI was 12.4%, a substantial improvement from 6.6% in the prior-year period.

Record Backlog and Cash Flow

A significant highlight was the "record-breaking backlog," with orders more than doubling year-on-year and significantly outpacing revenue growth. This indicates strong future revenue visibility. HPE also generated $1.4 billion in cash from operating activities and $915 million in free cash flow, representing a significant increase of $1.8 billion from the prior-year period and a Q2 record. This robust cash generation is vital for an infrastructure company needing heavy spending for AI build-outs. Additionally, HPE completed the divestiture of its remaining stake in H3C Technologies, receiving approximately $1.357 billion in cash proceeds.

Management Commentary and Outlook

HPE's management expressed strong confidence in the company's trajectory, attributing the exceptional performance to strategic initiatives and robust demand across its business. CEO Antonio Neri highlighted that HPE delivered record-breaking revenue, higher-than-anticipated profitability, and increased free cash flow, reflecting strong execution and healthy demand. He specifically noted that customers are continuing to invest in modernising their infrastructure and scaling AI, underscoring the strength of HPE's combined networking portfolio.

Significantly Raised Guidance

Perhaps the most impactful aspect of the earnings report was the substantial upward revision of HPE's fiscal year 2026 guidance.

  • Revenue Growth: The full-year FY26 revenue growth outlook was raised to a range of 29% to 33%, a significant increase from the prior guidance of 17% to 22%.
  • Non-GAAP Diluted EPS: The non-GAAP diluted net EPS guidance for FY26 was increased to $3.35 to $3.45, up from the previous range of $2.30 to $2.50. This new range comfortably exceeds the consensus estimate of $2.43.
  • Free Cash Flow: HPE also raised its free cash flow expectation for FY26 to at least $3.5 billion, up from $2 billion.

Crucially, these updated FY26 outlook ranges for non-GAAP diluted net EPS and free cash flow are higher than what HPE had projected to achieve by fiscal year 2028. This means the company is reaching its long-term financial plan targets two years ahead of schedule.

Fiscal 2027 Outlook Framework

Looking further ahead, HPE also introduced its growth framework for fiscal year 2027, estimating:

  • Revenue growth in the range of 8% to 12%.
  • Non-GAAP diluted net EPS growth in the range of 12% to 16%.
  • Non-GAAP operating margin rate in the range of 12% to 16%.
  • Free cash flow of at least $4.5 billion.

Peer and Industry Context

HPE's strong results come amidst a broader industry trend of increasing demand for AI infrastructure and networking solutions. Competitors like Dell have also reported robust earnings, primarily driven by a surge in server revenues, indicating a strong underlying market for the kind of technology HPE provides. The significant growth in HPE's Networking segment, partly due to the Juniper Networks acquisition, positions it strongly in a rapidly evolving market. The overall shift towards modernising infrastructure and scaling AI capabilities across enterprises and cloud service providers is creating a tailwind for companies like HPE. This performance suggests that the investment in AI infrastructure is translating into tangible revenue and profit growth for hardware and solutions providers.

What to do this week

  1. Review your portfolio for global tech exposure: If you invest in US stocks, check if your portfolio has exposure to companies benefiting from the AI infrastructure build-out. HPE's results highlight the demand beyond just chipmakers.
  2. Understand the difference between GAAP and non-GAAP earnings: Many companies report both. GAAP (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles) is standard, while non-GAAP often excludes certain volatile or non-recurring items. Knowing the difference helps you understand the company's underlying operational performance.
  3. Look beyond just revenue growth: Pay attention to margin expansion and free cash flow generation. HPE's increase in operating profit margin and record free cash flow are as important as revenue growth, indicating healthy operations.
  4. Assess management guidance: A significant raise in full-year guidance, especially when it pulls forward long-term targets, is a strong indicator of management's confidence and operational momentum.
  5. Research the impact of acquisitions: HPE's Networking segment benefited from the Juniper acquisition. Understand how such strategic moves contribute to growth and synergies for companies you invest in.

FAQ

What caused HPE stock to surge after its Q2 FY26 earnings?

HPE's stock surged because the company reported significantly better-than-expected revenue and earnings, driven by strong demand for AI infrastructure and networking solutions. They also raised their full-year guidance substantially, indicating strong future prospects.

How much did HPE's revenue grow in Q2 FY26?

HPE reported total revenue of $10.7 billion for Q2 FY26, which is a 40% increase compared to the same period last year.

What was the key driver for HPE's strong performance?

The key drivers were robust demand across its portfolio, particularly in the Networking segment (up 148.2%) and the Cloud & AI segment (up 22.9%), with strong orders for servers related to AI inferencing.

Did HPE raise its guidance for the full fiscal year 2026?

Yes, HPE significantly raised its FY26 guidance. They now expect revenue growth of 29% to 33% and non-GAAP diluted EPS of $3.35 to $3.45, effectively pulling forward their FY28 targets to FY26.

What is a "record backlog" and why is it important?

A "record backlog" means the company has a historically high amount of confirmed orders for products or services that are yet to be delivered and recognised as revenue. It's important because it provides strong visibility into future revenue generation and indicates sustained demand.

How did HPE's cash flow perform this quarter?

HPE generated $1.4 billion in cash from operating activities and a record $915 million in free cash flow for Q2 FY26, a significant increase from the prior year.

What was the impact of the Juniper Networks acquisition?

The Juniper Networks acquisition contributed to the substantial 148.2% surge in HPE's Networking revenue, enhancing its capabilities and market share in this high-demand area.
