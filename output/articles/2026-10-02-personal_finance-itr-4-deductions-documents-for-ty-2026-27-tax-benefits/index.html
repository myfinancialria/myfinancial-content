ITR-4 Deductions: Documents for TY 2026-27 Tax Benefits

TL;DR

  • ITR-4 is filed by individuals, HUFs, and firms opting for the Presumptive Taxation Scheme under Sections 44AD, 44ADA, and 44AE.
  • This scheme simplifies tax filing by allowing you to declare income as a fixed percentage of your gross receipts or turnover.
  • Even while opting for presumptive taxation, you can still claim various deductions under Chapter VI-A (e.g., 80C, 80D) to reduce your taxable income.
  • Key documents required include PAN, Aadhaar, bank statements, proofs of investments, and details of gross receipts.
  • Proper record-keeping, even simplified, is crucial for verification and to ensure compliance with tax regulations.

What this means in plain terms

For many small businesses and professionals in India, filing income tax can seem complex. The Income Tax Department offers a simplified scheme called Presumptive Taxation, primarily through Sections 44AD, 44ADA, and 44AE of the Income Tax Act. If you opt for this scheme, you can file your taxes using ITR-4.

In essence, instead of maintaining detailed books of accounts and calculating your exact profit, you declare your income as a fixed percentage of your gross receipts or turnover. For example, a business might declare 6% or 8% of its turnover as profit, while certain professionals might declare 50% of their gross receipts as profit. This significantly reduces the compliance burden. What's important to understand is that even when you declare income this way, you are still eligible to claim various tax deductions and exemptions that can further lower your overall tax liability. This article will guide you through these deductions and the essential documents you'll need for Tax Year 2026-27 (Assessment Year 2027-28).

Understanding ITR-4 and Presumptive Taxation

ITR-4, also known as "Sugam," is a simplified Income Tax Return form designed for individuals, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), and firms (other than LLPs) who are residents and have opted for the Presumptive Taxation Scheme. This scheme is particularly beneficial for small taxpayers as it exempts them from maintaining detailed books of accounts and getting them audited.

The primary sections governing presumptive taxation are:

  • Section 44AD: Applicable to resident individuals, HUFs, and partnership firms (excluding LLPs) engaged in eligible businesses, with a total turnover or gross receipts not exceeding ₹3 crore in the previous year. Under this section, income is presumed to be 8% of the total turnover or gross receipts. However, if the receipts are received digitally (e.g., via bank transfer, UPI), the presumptive rate is reduced to 6% for that portion of the turnover.
  • Section 44ADA: Designed for resident individuals and partnership firms (excluding LLPs) engaged in specified professions, with gross receipts not exceeding ₹75 lakhs in the previous year. For these professionals, 50% of the total gross receipts are deemed as their net taxable income.
  • Section 44AE: Covers individuals, HUFs, and firms (other than LLPs) who own not more than 10 goods carriages at any time during the previous year and are engaged in the business of plying, hiring, or leasing such goods carriages. A fixed amount per vehicle per month is deemed as income under this section.

It's crucial to note that if your actual income is higher than the presumptive income, you can declare the higher income. However, if your actual income is lower than the presumptive income and you wish to declare the lower income, you will need to maintain proper books of accounts and get them audited, thereby making you ineligible for ITR-4.

Key Deductions and Exemptions Available for ITR-4 Filers

While the presumptive taxation scheme simplifies income calculation, it does not bar you from claiming legitimate deductions and exemptions under Chapter VI-A of the Income Tax Act. These deductions can significantly reduce your final taxable income.

Common Deductions under Chapter VI-A

  • Section 80C, 80CCC, 80CCD (1): This is one of the most popular sections, allowing a combined deduction of up to ₹1.5 lakhs. It covers investments like Public Provident Fund (PPF), Employees' Provident Fund (EPF), Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS), life insurance premiums, principal repayment of home loans, Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY), and tuition fees for children. Contributions to the National Pension System (NPS) Tier-I account also fall under 80CCD(1).
  • Section 80CCD (1B): An additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 for contributions to NPS Tier-I, over and above the ₹1.5 lakh limit of Section 80C.
  • Section 80CCD (2): Employer's contribution to NPS (up to 10% of salary, or 14% for central government employees) is deductible, without any monetary limit.
  • Section 80D: Deduction for health insurance premiums paid for yourself, your family (spouse and dependent children), and parents. The limits vary based on age (senior citizens get higher limits). Preventive health check-ups also qualify for a deduction within these limits.
  • Section 80DD: Deduction for maintenance, including medical treatment, of a dependent with a disability.
  • Section 80DDB: Deduction for medical treatment of specified diseases or ailments for yourself or a dependent.
  • Section 80E: Deduction for interest paid on education loans taken for higher education of self, spouse, or children. There is no upper limit on the amount of interest that can be deducted, but it is available for 8 consecutive assessment years or until the interest is fully repaid, whichever is earlier.
  • Section 80G: Deduction for donations made to certain approved charitable institutions and funds. The percentage of deduction (50% or 100%) and whether it's subject to a qualifying limit depends on the donee institution.
  • Section 80GG: Deduction for house rent paid by individuals who do not receive House Rent Allowance (HRA) from their employer. This is subject to certain conditions and limits.
  • Section 80TTA/80TTB: Section 80TTA allows a deduction of up to ₹10,000 on interest earned from savings bank accounts for individuals and HUFs. Section 80TTB, applicable to senior citizens, allows a deduction of up to ₹50,000 on interest income from savings accounts, fixed deposits, and recurring deposits.
  • Section 80U: Deduction for individuals suffering from a physical disability (including blindness) or mental retardation.

It's important to remember that while claiming these deductions, you must have the necessary proofs and documents ready for verification by the Income Tax Department if requested.

Essential Documents for ITR-4 Filing (TY 2026-27)

Even with the simplified presumptive taxation, certain documents are indispensable for accurately filing ITR-4 and claiming all eligible deductions. Keeping these organised will streamline your filing process.

Personal and Identity Documents

  • Permanent Account Number (PAN): Your unique 10-digit alphanumeric identifier is mandatory for all tax-related transactions.
  • Aadhaar Card: Linking your Aadhaar with PAN is mandatory for filing income tax returns.
  • Bank Account Details: Your bank account number, IFSC code, and account type for refund purposes. Ensure it's a pre-validated bank account on the income tax e-filing portal.

Income-Related Documents

  • Bank Statements/Passbook: For verifying gross receipts (turnover) from your business or profession. This is crucial to calculate your presumptive income under Section 44AD or 44ADA.
  • Digital Payment Records: If you receive payments digitally (e.g., UPI, NEFT, RTGS, debit card, credit card), keep records as these may qualify for a lower presumptive tax rate (6% under 44AD).
  • Form 26AS: This Annual Consolidated Tax Statement provides details of tax deducted at source (TDS), tax collected at source (TCS), and advance tax paid. It's vital to reconcile this with your own records.
  • AIS (Annual Information Statement) / TIS (Taxpayer Information Summary): These statements provide a comprehensive view of your financial transactions, including income from various sources, investments, and tax deducted. Always cross-check with your own records.

Investment and Deduction Proofs

  • Section 80C Investments:

    • * Life insurance premium receipts * PPF passbook/statements * ELSS investment proofs * Home loan principal repayment certificate * Tuition fee receipts * NPS contribution statements (for 80CCD(1))

  • Section 80D (Health Insurance): Premium payment receipts for health insurance policies.
  • Section 80CCD (1B) & (2) (NPS): NPS contribution statements, employer's contribution statement if applicable.
  • Section 80E (Education Loan): Interest payment certificate from the bank/financial institution.
  • Section 80G (Donations): Receipts from charitable institutions with their name, address, PAN, and donation amount, along with the 80G certificate.
  • Section 80TTA/80TTB (Interest Income): Bank statements showing interest credited from savings accounts and Fixed Deposits.

Other Relevant Documents

  • Advance Tax Challans: Proof of advance tax paid during the financial year.
  • Self-Assessment Tax Challans: If you paid any self-assessment tax.

Organising these documents beforehand will make the e-filing process smooth and help avoid last-minute hassles. The Income Tax Department portal (https://incometax.gov.in) is your primary resource for e-filing and accessing forms like 26AS and AIS.

Important Considerations for ITR-4 Filers

While ITR-4 simplifies tax compliance, there are several key points to remember to ensure you remain compliant and make the most of the scheme.

Maintaining Simplified Records

Even though detailed books of accounts are not mandatory for those opting for presumptive taxation, it is still advisable to maintain basic records. This includes:

  • Gross Receipts/Turnover Register: A simple record of all your sales, receipts, or professional income.
  • Bank Statements: As mentioned, these are crucial for verifying your turnover and for the 6% presumptive rate benefit.
  • Proof of Deductions: All investment proofs, insurance premium receipts, donation receipts, etc., should be meticulously kept.

    • These records serve as proof if your return is selected for scrutiny by the Income Tax Department.

Opting Out of Presumptive Taxation

If you opt for the presumptive taxation scheme in a financial year, and then opt out of it in any of the subsequent five financial years, you will not be eligible to opt for the presumptive taxation scheme for the next five financial years. In such a scenario, you would also be required to maintain books of accounts and get them audited as per Section 44AB. This rule, primarily under Section 44AD, is significant and requires careful planning.

Advance Tax Liability

Taxpayers opting for presumptive taxation under Section 44AD or 44ADA are required to pay their entire advance tax in a single instalment on or before 15th March of the financial year. However, if the tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 in a financial year, advance tax becomes applicable. Failing to pay advance tax or paying less than 90% of the total tax due can attract interest under Section 234B and 234C.

Declaring Higher Income

If your actual net profit is higher than the presumptive income calculated (e.g., 8% or 6% of turnover under 44AD, or 50% under 44ADA), you are always required to declare the higher actual income. The presumptive scheme sets a minimum, not a maximum.

Other Income Sources

ITR-4 also requires you to declare income from other sources like salary (if any), house property (one house property), capital gains (if any, though this is rare for ITR-4 filers and might necessitate ITR-3), and other sources (like interest income). Ensure all these are accurately reported.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 42, a freelance digital marketing consultant in Chennai, earning ₹70 lakhs in gross receipts annually.

Priya is a professional covered under Section 44ADA, as her gross receipts are below ₹75 lakhs. She opts for the presumptive taxation scheme.

  1. Gross Receipts: ₹70,00,000
  2. Presumptive Income (50% of gross receipts under Section 44ADA): ₹70,00,000 * 50% = ₹35,00,000

Now, let's look at her deductions for TY 2026-27:

  • Section 80C:

    • * PPF Contribution: ₹80,000 * ELSS Investment: ₹50,000 * Life Insurance Premium: ₹20,000 Total 80C:* ₹1,50,000 (Maxed out at ₹1.5 lakhs)

  • Section 80CCD(1B):

    • * NPS Tier-I Contribution: ₹50,000

  • Section 80D:

    • * Health Insurance for self & family (non-senior citizen): ₹25,000 * Health Insurance for parents (senior citizens): ₹35,000 Total 80D:* ₹60,000

  • Section 80TTA:

    • * Interest from Savings Bank Account: ₹12,000 (Deduction limited to ₹10,000)

Calculation of Total Deductions:

  • 80C: ₹1,50,000
  • 80CCD(1B): ₹50,000
  • 80D: ₹60,000
  • 80TTA: ₹10,000
  • Total Chapter VI-A Deductions: ₹1,50,000 + ₹50,000 + ₹60,000 + ₹10,000 = ₹2,70,000

Calculation of Taxable Income:

  • Presumptive Income: ₹35,00,000
  • Less: Total Chapter VI-A Deductions: ₹2,70,000
  • Net Taxable Income: ₹35,00,000 - ₹2,70,000 = ₹32,30,000

Priya's tax liability would then be calculated on ₹32,30,000, significantly lower than if she had not claimed her eligible deductions. She would also need to pay advance tax on this estimated income by March 15th of the financial year.

What to do this week

  1. Gather Income Proofs: Collect all bank statements for the financial year (April 2026 - March 2027) to accurately ascertain your gross receipts/turnover.
  2. Consolidate Investment Proofs: Compile all receipts and statements for investments made under Section 80C (PPF, ELSS, Life Insurance, etc.) and NPS contributions (80CCD).
  3. Organise Health Insurance Receipts: Locate premium payment receipts for all health insurance policies (for self, family, and parents) to claim deduction under Section 80D.
  4. Check Form 26AS and AIS: Log in to the Income Tax e-filing portal (https://incometax.gov.in) and review your Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement (AIS) for the relevant financial year to ensure all income and TDS details are correctly reflected.
  5. Identify Other Income: List any other income sources like interest from savings accounts (for 80TTA/80TTB), rental income from one house property, or any other income to be declared in ITR-4.

FAQ

Who is eligible to file ITR-4?

ITR-4 is for resident individuals, HUFs, and firms (excluding LLPs) whose total income includes business income under Section 44AD or 44AE, or professional income under Section 44ADA, and whose total income does not exceed ₹50 lakhs.

Can I claim expenses if I opt for presumptive taxation?

No, if you opt for presumptive taxation, you cannot claim any business expenses like rent, salaries, or depreciation. The presumptive income (e.g., 6%, 8%, or 50% of turnover/receipts) is considered your final profit after all expenses.

What if my actual income is lower than the presumptive income?

If your actual income is lower than the presumptive income and you wish to declare this lower income, you cannot file ITR-4. You would need to maintain proper books of accounts and get them audited under Section 44AB.

Is it mandatory to pay advance tax if I file ITR-4?

Yes, if your estimated tax liability for the year exceeds ₹10,000, you are required to pay advance tax. For ITR-4 filers under Sections 44AD/44ADA, the entire advance tax must be paid in a single instalment by 15th March of the financial year.

Can I claim deductions under Chapter VI-A (like 80C, 80D) even with presumptive income?

Yes, absolutely. Even after declaring income under the presumptive taxation scheme, you are fully eligible to claim deductions under Chapter VI-A of the Income Tax Act, such as those under Sections 80C, 80D, 80E, 80G, and 80TTA/80TTB, among others.

What is the turnover limit for ITR-4 under Section 44AD?

The turnover or gross receipts limit for businesses opting for presumptive taxation under Section 44AD is ₹3 crore for Tax Year 2026-27, provided certain conditions are met regarding cash receipts.

Do I need to maintain detailed books of accounts for ITR-4?

No, one of the main benefits of opting for presumptive taxation and filing ITR-4 is that you are exempt from maintaining detailed books of accounts under Section 44AA. However, it is prudent to keep basic records of your gross receipts and proofs for any deductions claimed.
