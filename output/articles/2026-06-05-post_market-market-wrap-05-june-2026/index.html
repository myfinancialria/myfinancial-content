Market Wrap — 05 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices saw a mixed close today, with Nifty 50 and Midcap 100 ending in the red, while Bank Nifty posted gains.
  • Media, Realty, and PSU Bank sectors emerged as top performers, driven by specific stock movements and positive sentiment.
  • Metal, IT, and Energy sectors were the biggest laggards, influenced by global cues and company-specific news.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong buying support.
  • The Indian Rupee witnessed its biggest daily gain in two months against the US Dollar, boosted by RBI's currency defence.

How the market closed

The Indian market experienced a day of mixed signals, with the Nifty 50 closing marginally lower, while the Bank Nifty managed to eke out gains. The broader market also saw some pressure, with both Midcap and Smallcap indices declining.

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, closed at 15.79, down 0.63%. This slight dip suggests a marginal easing of immediate market jitters.

Sectors — winners and losers

Top 3 Gainer Sectors:

  • MEDIA (+3.48%): The sector witnessed strong buying interest, largely propelled by individual stock performances like Zee Entertainment Enterprises Ltd..
  • REALTY (+0.56%): Real estate players expressed confidence in the sector, citing stable interest rates and steady demand following the RBI's pause on rate hikes.
  • PSU BANK (+0.48%): Public sector banks saw moderate gains today, contributing positively to the overall market sentiment.

Top 3 Loser Sectors:

  • METAL (-1.60%): The metal sector faced headwinds, with major players like Tata Steel seeing a dip after a fire incident at its UK plant and Hindustan Zinc experiencing significant declines.
  • IT (-0.99%): The Information Technology sector was under pressure, mirroring weakness in global tech stocks and a rotation out of chip stocks in international markets.
  • ENERGY (-0.25%): Despite some individual stocks in the power segment performing well, the broader energy sector closed in negative territory.

Market breadth

The market breadth was slightly negative today, with 241 advances against 259 declines out of 500 stocks. This indicates that while some pockets of the market saw strong buying, a slightly larger number of stocks ended the session in the red, suggesting a cautious sentiment across the broader market.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree, offloading Indian equities worth ₹4,447 crore today. Conversely, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained strong buyers, injecting ₹4,360 crore into the market, largely offsetting the FII outflows.

| Investor Type | Net Position (₹ crore) | | :------------ | :--------------------- | | FII | -4,447 | | DII | +4,360 |

Looking at the past five trading sessions, FIIs have been consistent net sellers, with cumulative outflows totaling ₹43,445 crore. During the same period, DIIs have provided robust support, with net inflows of ₹41,103 crore, indicating that domestic money is actively cushioning the impact of foreign selling.

What moved markets today

1. RBI's Stance on Rates and Currency Defence The Reserve Bank of India's policy decisions and statements were a key market mover. While the RBI kept the repo rate steady, significantly upgraded inflation forecasts signal a strong likelihood of rate hikes from August, according to some experts. However, other views suggest no immediate rate hike risks, calling the policy "on expected lines". The RBI also confirmed no plans to ease restrictions on banks' net open positions. Market Impact: The mixed signals on future rate hikes created some uncertainty, but the overall stability of the current rates was seen as a confidence booster for sectors like real estate.

2. Rupee's Strong Performance The Indian Rupee posted its biggest daily gain in two months, closing at 94.94 against the US Dollar. This significant appreciation was attributed to the RBI's efforts to defend the currency. Market Impact: A stronger rupee is generally positive for importers and helps in managing inflation, but it can impact export-oriented sectors.

3. Sector-Specific Developments

  • Real Estate Sector Confidence: Despite broader market fluctuations, the real estate sector saw gains, with industry stakeholders expressing optimism due to stable interest rates and steady demand.
  • Metal Sector Weakness: The metal sector faced pressure, partly due to a fire incident at Tata Steel's Port Talbot plant in the UK, which led to a nearly 3% fall in its shares. Hindustan Zinc also saw a significant decline.
  • Global Tech Sell-off Impacts IT: Indian IT stocks mirrored the weakness seen in global technology and semiconductor stocks, as US Nasdaq and S&P futures declined amidst a rotation out of chip stocks.

    • Market Impact: These sector-specific news items led to divergent performances across different segments of the market, highlighting the importance of bottom-up analysis.

Setup for tomorrow

Indian markets are likely to open with an eye on global cues, which appear mixed to slightly negative. US Nasdaq and S&P futures declined today as investors rotated out of chip stocks, suggesting a cautious sentiment in global tech. European shares also slipped amidst lingering Mideast tensions.

Overnight, the US 10-year yield saw a slight increase, while crude oil prices (Brent and WTI) dipped. Gold and silver also closed lower. The Dollar Index strengthened marginally, while the USD/INR pair saw the Rupee gain significantly. Investors will be watching for further developments on global geopolitical fronts and any fresh economic data releases from major economies. Sectors sensitive to global sentiment, such as IT and Metals, might remain in focus, along with any follow-through from today's strong moves in Media and Realty.
