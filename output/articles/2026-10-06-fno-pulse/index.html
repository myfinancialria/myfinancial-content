Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. When we look at the option chain, the strike price with the highest Call OI typically signifies a strong resistance level, as a large number of Call options have been sold at this strike. Option sellers (writers) at this level generally expect the underlying asset's price to stay below this point. Conversely, the strike price with the highest Put OI often indicates a significant support level, as many Put options have been sold here, with writers anticipating the price to remain above this level.

For the Nifty 50, today's heaviest fresh Call (CE) writing was observed near the 23100 strike. This indicates that Call sellers are actively building positions at this level, potentially expecting the index to find a cap around or below 23100 in the near term.

In the Sensex options, fresh Call writing was most prominent near 73866, suggesting that option sellers are expecting this level to act as a ceiling. However, there was also significant fresh Put writing near 72166, implying that Put sellers are establishing a floor around this level, expecting the index to hold above it. The aggregate flow for Sensex today showed a net build-up of 10.3 million Put Open Interest versus 5.1 million Call Open Interest, indicating a stronger conviction from Put writers.

For Bank Nifty, the 55200 strike saw the heaviest fresh Call writing, suggesting a potential resistance point. Simultaneously, the 55100 strike attracted the heaviest fresh Put writing, which is very close to the current spot price, indicating that Put sellers are actively defending this level as a potential floor.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator derived from open interest data. It's calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests a bullish sentiment, as it indicates more Put options have been written (sold) than Call options. Put writers profit if the market stays above their strike, implying they expect an upward or stable movement. Conversely, a PCR below 1 often points to a bearish sentiment, as it means more Call options have been written, with Call writers expecting the market to stay below their strike. We also look at PCR based on today's change in Open Interest (ΔOI) to gauge fresh sentiment.

For the Nifty 50, the overall PCR (OI) stands at 0.93, suggesting a relatively balanced book of Call and Put options. Today's flow, as indicated by the ΔPCR of 1.28, points to a mixed to slightly balanced flow of fresh writing, not showing a strong directional bias from new positions.

The Sensex has an overall PCR (OI) of 1.16, which leans slightly bullish, indicating more Put writing than Call writing in the total open interest. More notably, today's flow ΔPCR is a strong 2.0, signifying that fresh writing was heavily skewed towards Puts, which is a bullish signal from option writers who expect the market to hold or move higher.

Bank Nifty's PCR (OI) is 0.88, also suggesting a largely balanced options book. The ΔPCR for today's flow is -1.64, which indicates a mixed or balanced fresh writing activity, without a clear directional bias emerging from the day's new positions.

Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is a concept in options theory that refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would experience the minimum aggregate loss if the underlying asset expires at that price. It's often considered a magnetic point because, as expiry approaches, the underlying index tends to gravitate towards this strike, as option writers, who hold the majority of open interest, would benefit most from such a scenario. The closer the expiry, the stronger this "pin pull" effect tends to be.

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain strike is at 22750. This is very close to the current spot price, suggesting that option writers are broadly positioned for the index to expire around its current levels.

The Sensex Max Pain is at 72600, which is approximately 0.6% below its current spot price. This indicates a potential downward pull towards this level as expiry approaches, from the perspective of option writers.

Bank Nifty has its Max Pain strike at 56000, which is about 1.6% above its current spot price. This suggests a potential upward pull towards 56000, implying that option writers are positioned for the index to move higher by expiry.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into institutional sentiment.

Today, FIIs showed a very low Index futures long ratio of 8.5%. This means that out of their total index futures positions, only 8.5% were long contracts, while the vast majority were short. Historically, such a low long ratio has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish outlook from FIIs on the broader market indices. This is a notable signal indicating significant bearish positioning from these large participants in index futures.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of 54.9%. This indicates a relatively balanced to slightly bullish stance on individual stocks, suggesting that while they might be cautious on the overall index, they are selectively positive on specific equities.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals daily can empower you to interpret market sentiment. To do this yourself, you'll primarily need to access the Option Chain and Participant-wise Open Interest data, both readily available on the NSE India website under the 'Derivatives' section. Many brokerage platforms also provide these insights.

First, look at the Option Chain for Nifty, Sensex, and Bank Nifty. Identify the strike prices with the highest Call Open Interest (CE OI) and Put Open Interest (PE OI). These act as immediate resistance and support levels. Next, observe the change in Open Interest (ΔOI) for the day. Heavy fresh Call writing (increase in CE OI) at a particular strike suggests new resistance, while heavy fresh Put writing (increase in PE OI) suggests new support. Calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total PE OI by total CE OI for an overall sentiment gauge. Also, calculate the PCR based on today's ΔOI for fresh sentiment. Finally, locate the Max Pain strike, which is the point where option writers face minimum loss, often acting as a magnet as expiry nears. For institutional positioning, check the FII derivative data on the NSE site to see their long/short ratios in index and stock futures. By combining these data points, you can form a comprehensive view of market participants' positioning and the potential directional biases for the upcoming sessions.
