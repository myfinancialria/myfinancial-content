This covers the requested aspects and fits the 3-4 section constraint.# PRIM vs Mutual Funds vs PMS: Key Differences Explained

TL;DR

  • PRIM (Portfolio Managers' Route for Investing in Mutual Fund Units) is a new SEBI framework allowing PMS managers to build portfolios using mutual funds, ETFs, and SIFs.
  • Mutual Funds pool money from many investors into a diversified portfolio managed by an Asset Management Company (AMC), offering professional management at lower minimums.
  • Portfolio Management Services (PMS) offer highly customised portfolios of direct equities, debt, and other securities, tailored to individual investor goals and risk appetites.
  • PRIM requires a minimum investment of ₹25 lakhs, while conventional PMS has a minimum of ₹50 lakhs; mutual funds are accessible with much smaller amounts.
  • Costs and taxation structures vary significantly, with PRIM and PMS generally having higher fees and different tax implications compared to mutual funds.

What this means in plain terms

Investing in the Indian markets offers various avenues for professional management, each suited for different investor profiles and financial goals. Recently, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) introduced the Portfolio Managers' Route for Investing in Mutual Fund Units (PRIM) as part of an overhaul of its Portfolio Management Services (PMS) regulations, operating under the SEBI (Portfolio Managers) Regulations, 2026. This new framework creates another option for investors seeking professionally managed portfolios.

Essentially, PRIM is a hybrid offering. It sits between traditional mutual funds, which are pooled investment vehicles, and conventional PMS, which offers highly personalised direct equity or debt portfolios. PRIM allows a portfolio manager to construct a customised portfolio, not of individual stocks or bonds directly, but of direct plans of mutual funds, Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs), and Specialised Investment Funds (SIFs). This means investors get a layer of professional management over their mutual fund selections, offering a different blend of customisation, cost, and investment approach.

Portfolio Structure & Investment Approach

The fundamental difference between PRIM, mutual funds, and PMS lies in what they invest in and how those investments are managed.

Mutual Funds

Mutual funds operate on a pooled investment model. Investors buy units of a fund, and the Asset Management Company (AMC) manages a diversified portfolio of securities (stocks, bonds, gold, etc.) according to the fund's stated investment objective. The fund manager makes all decisions regarding the underlying securities within the scheme, aiming to achieve the fund's goals. The portfolio is standardised for all investors holding units in that particular scheme.

Portfolio Management Services (PMS)

Conventional PMS offers a highly personalised and often concentrated portfolio. Here, a dedicated portfolio manager directly manages a client's individual portfolio of securities, which can include direct equities, debt instruments, IPOs, primary market debt issuances, and even a wider range of overseas securities. The securities are held in the investor's own Demat account, providing direct ownership. PMS strategies can be highly flexible, allowing for significant cash calls or customisation based on an individual's specific tax situation or risk appetite.

Portfolio Managers' Route for Investing in Mutual Fund Units (PRIM)

PRIM introduces a new approach within the PMS framework. Instead of managing direct securities, a PRIM manager builds and manages a client's portfolio using direct plans of various mutual funds, ETFs, index funds, and SIFs. This means there are two layers of professional management: the PRIM manager selects, allocates, and rebalances across different fund schemes, while the underlying securities within each chosen fund are managed by their respective fund managers. This offers diversification through funds while providing a customised asset allocation strategy at the investor level.

Costs, Fees & Minimums

The financial commitment and ongoing charges vary significantly across these investment avenues.

Mutual Funds

Mutual funds are generally the most accessible in terms of minimum investment, often starting from as low as ₹100 via a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP). Their costs are primarily captured by the Total Expense Ratio (TER), which includes fund management fees, administrative costs, and other operational expenses. TERs for direct plans are typically lower than regular plans and generally range from 0.5% to 2.5% annually, depending on the fund category and asset size.

Portfolio Management Services (PMS)

Conventional PMS requires a substantially higher minimum investment, typically ₹50 lakhs. PMS fee structures are more complex and can include a fixed management fee (e.g., a percentage of Assets Under Management - AUM), a performance-linked fee (a percentage of returns above a hurdle rate), or a hybrid of both. These fees are generally higher than mutual fund TERs, often ranging from 1.5% to 2.5% of AUM annually, excluding performance fees. Operating expenses up to 0.5% per year may also be charged.

Portfolio Managers' Route for Investing in Mutual Fund Units (PRIM)

PRIM aims to make professionally managed fund portfolios more accessible with a lower minimum investment threshold of ₹25 lakhs, compared to the ₹50 lakhs for conventional PMS. The fixed management fee under PRIM is capped at a maximum of 1% of the client's AUM. Performance-based fees are also permitted. It's important to note that since PRIM invests in mutual funds, the investor will also bear the expense ratio of the underlying mutual fund schemes, creating a two-layered cost structure. SEBI has capped investments in schemes of affiliated AMCs at 25% under PRIM to prevent potential conflicts of interest.

Taxation Implications

The tax treatment for gains from these investment products also differs, which can impact net returns.

Mutual Funds

For mutual funds, capital gains tax is triggered only when an investor redeems their units. Any churn within the fund's portfolio by the fund manager does not create a direct tax liability for the unit holder. Equity-oriented mutual funds (investing >65% in Indian equities) are subject to Long Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax of 10% on gains exceeding ₹1 lakh in a financial year (if held for over 1 year) and Short Term Capital Gains (STCG) tax of 15% (if held for 1 year or less). Debt-oriented mutual funds are taxed as per the investor's income tax slab rate if held for up to 3 years (STCG), and at 20% with indexation benefit if held for more than 3 years (LTCG). [cite: Income Tax India]

Portfolio Management Services (PMS)

In PMS, investors directly own the underlying securities. Therefore, capital gains tax is incurred whenever the portfolio manager sells any security in the client's account, even if the client has not withdrawn funds. This means that portfolio churn by the manager can lead to frequent tax events. Dividends and interest income are also taxed at the investor's marginal income tax rate. Short-term capital gains on equities are taxed at 15%, while long-term capital gains on equities (over 1 year holding) exceeding ₹1 lakh are taxed at 10%. For debt instruments, STCG is taxed at the slab rate, and LTCG (over 3 years holding) is taxed at 20% with indexation. [cite: Income Tax India] This "tax inefficiency" due to churn can potentially reduce net returns compared to mutual funds, especially for high-frequency trading strategies.

Portfolio Managers' Route for Investing in Mutual Fund Units (PRIM)

Since PRIM falls under the PMS framework and the underlying assets are mutual fund units, the tax implications will generally follow the rules for mutual fund investments, but the "event" that triggers taxation is still the sale of the underlying mutual fund units by the PRIM manager. While the client owns the units, the manager makes the buy/sell decisions. This means that if the PRIM manager rebalances the portfolio by selling mutual fund units, capital gains could be triggered for the investor at that point, similar to direct mutual fund investments, but the decision is made by the manager. However, unlike conventional PMS, the internal churn within the underlying mutual funds does not trigger tax for the PRIM investor. The tax on the sale of mutual fund units within the PRIM portfolio would follow the equity/debt fund taxation rules as described above. [cite: Income Tax India]

Suitability & Flexibility

Choosing between these options depends heavily on an investor's financial capacity, risk tolerance, desire for customisation, and understanding of market nuances.

Mutual Funds

Mutual funds are suitable for a broad range of investors, from those just starting their investment journey with small amounts to experienced investors seeking diversified, professionally managed portfolios without the need for high customisation. They offer simplicity, high liquidity (for open-ended funds), and diversification across various asset classes and market caps. Investors have limited control over individual security selection but choose funds based on their specific objectives and risk profiles.

Portfolio Management Services (PMS)

PMS is designed for high-net-worth individuals (HNIs) with a substantial investable corpus (₹50 lakhs minimum) and a desire for highly customised, concentrated portfolios. It suits investors who seek a tailored investment strategy, direct ownership of securities, and potentially higher returns from active, often aggressive, stock-picking strategies. PMS offers greater transparency regarding individual holdings and transaction details compared to mutual funds. However, this customisation comes with higher costs and often higher risk due to concentration.

Portfolio Managers' Route for Investing in Mutual Fund Units (PRIM)

PRIM caters to investors who want professional management of their mutual fund portfolio but perhaps find the ₹50 lakh minimum for conventional PMS too high, or prefer diversification through funds rather than direct stock exposure. With a minimum of ₹25 lakhs, it broadens access to professionally managed portfolios for a wider affluent investor base. It's ideal for those who want expert guidance in fund selection, asset allocation, and rebalancing across various fund categories, without directly owning individual stocks. This route is particularly useful for investors who feel overwhelmed by the vast number of mutual fund schemes available and prefer an expert to navigate this for them, while still benefiting from the inherent diversification of mutual funds.

A real example

Priya, 42, ₹35L CTC, Mumbai

Priya has accumulated ₹30 lakhs and is looking to invest it for her long-term goals, including her child's education and retirement. She is a busy professional and prefers professional management but is also conscious of costs and tax efficiency. She has a moderate to high-risk appetite.

Let's compare her options:

  1. Investing in Mutual Funds directly:

    2. * Investment: Priya could invest her ₹30 lakhs across 5-6 diversified equity and hybrid mutual funds. Cost: Assuming an average TER of 1.5% for direct plans, her annual cost would be ₹30,00,000 \ 1.5% = ₹45,000. * Management: She would need to research and select the funds herself, monitor their performance, and rebalance her portfolio periodically. * Taxation: Capital gains would be triggered only when she redeems units. Internal churn by fund managers would not impact her tax liability.

  1. Investing via PRIM:

    2. * Investment: With ₹30 lakhs, Priya qualifies for PRIM (minimum ₹25 lakhs). A PRIM manager would build a portfolio of direct plans of mutual funds, ETFs, and SIFs for her. Cost: The PRIM management fee is capped at 1% of AUM. So, ₹30,00,000 \ 1% = ₹30,000. Additionally, she would bear the TER of the underlying mutual funds, say an average of 1.0% (since PRIM might choose lower-cost direct plans). This adds another ₹30,00,000 \* 1.0% = ₹30,000. Total annual cost: ₹30,000 + ₹30,000 = ₹60,000. * Management: The PRIM manager handles fund selection, allocation, and rebalancing, aligning it with Priya's risk profile and goals. This saves Priya time and leverages expert selection. * Taxation: Capital gains would be triggered when the PRIM manager sells underlying mutual fund units during rebalancing. This could lead to more frequent tax events compared to self-managing and holding funds for longer durations, but less frequent than a conventional PMS.

  1. Investing via conventional PMS:

    2. * Investment: Priya cannot invest her ₹30 lakhs in a conventional PMS as the minimum is ₹50 lakhs. If she had ₹50 lakhs, a PMS manager would build a direct equity/debt portfolio for her. Cost (hypothetical for ₹50L): A fixed fee of 2% of AUM would be ₹50,00,000 \ 2% = ₹1,00,000 annually, potentially with performance fees on top. * Management: Highly customised portfolio of direct securities managed by the PMS manager. * Taxation (hypothetical for ₹50L): Capital gains would be triggered on every sale of an underlying stock or bond by the PMS manager, potentially leading to higher tax outgo due to frequent churn.

In Priya's case, with ₹30 lakhs, PRIM offers a professionally managed solution that is more customised than direct mutual fund investing but more accessible and potentially less tax-intensive than a conventional PMS (if she had the higher corpus).

What to do this week

  1. Assess your investable corpus: Determine if your investable amount meets the minimums for PRIM (₹25 lakhs) or conventional PMS (₹50 lakhs). If not, mutual funds remain your primary option for professional management.
  2. Review your current investment strategy: Evaluate how you currently select and manage your mutual funds. Are you comfortable doing it yourself, or do you feel a need for more active, professional oversight on fund selection and asset allocation?
  3. Understand the fee structures: Research the Total Expense Ratios (TERs) of your existing mutual funds and compare them with the potential fixed and performance fees of PRIM or PMS providers. Factor in the two-layered fee structure for PRIM.
  4. Consider your tax efficiency needs: Reflect on your income tax bracket and how frequently you prefer to deal with capital gains tax events. If tax efficiency from infrequent churn is a high priority, mutual funds might be more appealing than conventional PMS.
  5. Speak to a Certified Financial Planner: Discuss your financial goals, risk appetite, and current portfolio with a professional to understand which of these options (or a combination) best aligns with your overall financial plan.

FAQ

What is PRIM and how is it different from PMS?

PRIM (Portfolio Managers' Route for Investing in Mutual Fund Units) is a new offering under the SEBI PMS framework. It differs from conventional PMS by investing solely in direct plans of mutual funds, ETFs, and SIFs, rather than direct stocks or bonds. PRIM has a lower minimum investment of ₹25 lakhs compared to ₹50 lakhs for conventional PMS.

What is the minimum investment for PRIM?

The minimum investment required for PRIM is ₹25 lakhs. This is half the minimum required for conventional Portfolio Management Services (PMS).

Are PRIM fees higher than mutual fund fees?

Yes, PRIM typically involves a two-layered fee structure. You pay a management fee to the PRIM manager (capped at 1% of AUM), plus the Total Expense Ratio (TER) of the underlying mutual funds, ETFs, or SIFs in which your portfolio is invested. Mutual funds only charge the TER.

How does taxation work for PRIM compared to mutual funds?

For PRIM, capital gains tax is triggered when the PRIM manager sells units of the underlying mutual funds in your portfolio during rebalancing. For direct mutual fund investments, tax is triggered when you redeem the units. The tax rates (STCG/LTCG) follow the rules of the underlying equity or debt funds. [cite: Income Tax India]

Can I invest in direct equities through PRIM?

No, PRIM is specifically designed for investing in a portfolio of mutual fund units, ETFs, and Specialised Investment Funds (SIFs). It does not allow for direct investment in individual stocks or bonds.

Who is PRIM best suited for?

PRIM is best suited for affluent investors with an investable corpus of ₹25 lakhs or more who seek professional management of their mutual fund portfolio, including expert fund selection, asset allocation, and rebalancing, but prefer diversification through funds rather than direct stock exposure.

What are the main benefits of PRIM?

The main benefits of PRIM include professional management of your mutual fund portfolio, customised asset allocation, periodic rebalancing by an expert, and a lower minimum investment threshold (₹25 lakhs) compared to conventional PMS, making it accessible to a wider affluent investor base.
