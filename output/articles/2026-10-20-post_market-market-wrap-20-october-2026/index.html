Market Wrap — 20 October 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, witnessed a decline today, closing over 1% and 0.6% lower respectively.
  • Weak global cues, particularly from US markets opening lower, and persistent selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) weighed on sentiment.
  • PSU Banks emerged as the standout sector, with Union Bank of India leading the Nifty 500 gainers.
  • Urban Company Ltd. was the top loser in the Nifty 500, dropping nearly 10%.
  • FIIs remained net sellers in the cash market, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support with net buying.
  • Futures & Options (F&O) build-up data is unavailable for today's session and will be populated from the next wrap.

How the market behaved today

Indian equities concluded the session in the red, with the Nifty 50 breaking below the 23,000 mark. The benchmark index opened lower and trended downwards for most of the day, registering a decline of 1.11%. The Bank Nifty also followed suit, closing 0.64% lower. Broader markets, represented by the Nifty Midcap 100 and Nifty Smallcap 100, also experienced significant cuts, falling over 1% each, indicating a broad-based selling pressure across segments.

What moved the market — the factors

The Indian market's decline today was primarily influenced by a combination of weak global cues and sustained selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs). US stocks opened lower, with AI-linked stocks declining and oil prices initially rising, which dampened investor sentiment globally. Although Wall Street later rebounded from its lows on upbeat earnings and falling oil prices, the initial weakness set a negative tone for Asian markets, including India.

FIIs continued their selling spree in the Indian cash market, offloading ₹1,149 crore today, extending a trend of net outflows over the past five sessions. This consistent foreign selling put pressure on the domestic indices. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided some counter-balance, pumping in ₹3,201 crore, but it wasn't enough to offset the broader market weakness.

Sectorally, the IT and FMCG sectors were among the biggest drags, contributing significantly to the Nifty's fall. The Realty sector also saw considerable selling. In contrast, PSU Banks showed remarkable resilience and strength, emerging as the sole gainer among major sectors, indicating some rotation of capital within the market. The overall market breadth was overwhelmingly negative, reflecting widespread selling pressure rather than a concentrated move in a few heavyweights.

News that moved stocks

  • US stocks open lower as AI stocks decline, oil prices rise; later rebound on upbeat earnings, falling oil prices: Wall Street initially saw a dip as weakness in AI-linked stocks and higher oil prices weighed on major indexes. However, later in the day, US stocks recovered from early losses, buoyed by strong corporate earnings and a decline in oil prices, which helped lift sentiment. This mixed global sentiment likely contributed to the cautious and volatile trading in the Indian market.
  • Oyo parent PRISM to cut IPO fresh issue nearly 50% to Rs 3,350 crore: PRISM, the parent company of Oyo, plans to significantly reduce its initial public offering (IPO) fresh issue size from ₹6,650 crore to ₹3,350 crore, citing robust cash flow and an enhanced balance sheet. This news highlights a shift in IPO strategy, potentially reflecting market conditions or internal financial strength, and could impact investor perception of upcoming large IPOs.
  • Jio IPO has lenders, brokerages pushing retail participation with loans, marketing reach out: Following strong interest from global institutions, Jio Platforms is now actively targeting retail investors for what could be India’s largest IPO, with non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) and brokerages offering loans and extensive marketing. This aggressive push for retail participation indicates high expectations for the Jio IPO and could draw significant liquidity from the market.
  • French bond yields near 24-year high as new protests loom: Government bond yields in France have surged to a 24-year high amidst ongoing protests and political uncertainty. This development in a major European economy signals heightened risk aversion in global debt markets, which can have ripple effects on international capital flows and investor confidence.
  • Emerging markets aren't catching a cold yet from Fed tightening: McGeever: Despite the Federal Reserve's tightening measures, emerging markets are showing surprising economic resilience, supported by stronger fiscal fundamentals and high bond yields. This perspective suggests that while global monetary policy remains a factor, some emerging economies, including potentially India, may be better positioned to withstand external pressures.

F&O build-up — where traders are positioning

OI build-up needs the previous session's OI — it will populate from the next run. Data for today's F&O build-up is unavailable.

Interpretation: Long build-up indicates fresh buying interest in a stock, often suggesting conviction among traders that the price will continue to rise. Short build-up signifies fresh selling pressure, with traders expecting the price to fall. Short covering occurs when traders who had previously sold a stock (shorted it) buy it back to close their positions, leading to a price rise that may not reflect genuine underlying strength. Long unwinding indicates that traders who held long positions are selling them off, often due to profit-taking or a weakening outlook for the stock.

Market breadth

The market breadth was overwhelmingly negative today, with 86 advances against a significant 407 declines out of a total of 493 traded stocks. This indicates that selling pressure was broad-based and affected a vast majority of stocks across the Indian market.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's session was largely driven by a confluence of global cues and FII selling rather than a single defining domestic event. The initial negative sentiment from US markets, where AI stocks declined and oil prices rose, set a cautious tone for Indian equities. This was compounded by persistent selling from FIIs, who have been net sellers for five consecutive days.

While there was no major Indian economic data or policy announcement today, news regarding the scaling back of Oyo parent PRISM's IPO and the aggressive retail push for the Jio IPO were significant in the primary market space. These developments, while not directly impacting the indices today, highlight ongoing activity and investor interest in the IPO segment. The resilience shown by PSU Banks, despite the overall market weakness, suggests a potential rotation of capital into value-oriented or domestically-focused sectors.

Setup for tomorrow

Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will continue to play a crucial role. US markets, after opening lower, rebounded from their lows on the back of upbeat corporate earnings and falling oil prices. This positive overnight sentiment from Wall Street, coupled with a decline in crude oil prices (Brent Crude down 1.58%, WTI Crude down 6.41%), could offer some relief to Indian markets. However, the consistent FII selling remains a concern. Investors will be watching for the final close of US markets, further movements in crude oil, and the Dollar Index (which closed lower today) for directional cues. Key levels to watch for Nifty 50 would be the 22,800 support and 23,000 resistance.
