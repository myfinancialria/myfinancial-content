Market Wrap — 22 July 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed lower for the day.
  • Rising crude oil prices globally and concerns over Middle Eastern supply disruptions weighed heavily on market sentiment.
  • Data Patterns (India) Ltd. was the standout gainer among Nifty 500 stocks, surging over 12%.
  • Bandhan Bank Ltd. was the biggest loser, plummeting nearly 17%.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers in the cash market, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers.
  • F&O positioning data is unavailable and will be included in the next wrap.

How the market behaved today

The Indian market witnessed a negative session today, with benchmark indices closing in the red. The Nifty 50 opened lower and trended downwards throughout the day, closing at 23996.25, a decline of 0.79%. Similarly, the Bank Nifty also saw significant selling pressure, ending at 57126.80, down 1.23%. Broader markets mirrored this weakness, with Midcap and Smallcap indices also registering losses.

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, rose by 5.48% to 13.29, indicating an increase in investor apprehension.

What moved the market — the factors

The primary driver behind today's market downturn was the sharp surge in global crude oil prices, which hit a six-week high with Brent crude racing past $95 a barrel. This was fueled by growing tensions between the U.S. and Iran, sparking fears of supply disruptions in key oil transit routes. The rising oil prices weighed heavily on Indian government bonds, pushing benchmark yields to their highest level since late June, and also caused the Indian rupee to hit a two-month low.

Despite Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) being net buyers in the cash market today, their buying was offset by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) turning net sellers. Sectoral weakness was pronounced in Media, Realty, and PSU Banks, which dragged the indices lower. Conversely, FMCG and Auto sectors showed some resilience. The increase in India VIX also reflected heightened uncertainty among market participants.

Today's session saw a mixed performance across sectors, with defensive pockets showing some strength while others faced significant selling pressure.

News that moved stocks

  • Oil rally weighs on Indian bonds, value buyers limit fall

    • Indian government bonds experienced a fourth consecutive session of decline as benchmark bond yields rose to their highest level since late June, intensified by rising oil prices and supply fears. This news impacted the broader bond market and contributed to the rupee's weakness.

  • Brent crude surges 4% to over $95 a barrel, nears 6-week high as conflict threatens key oil transit routes

    • Oil prices hit a six-week high on Wednesday, with Brent crude racing past ninety-five dollars a barrel due to growing tensions between the U.S. and Iran, sparking fears of supply disruptions. This global development was a significant factor in the Indian market's negative sentiment, affecting the rupee and bond yields.

  • Rupee languishes at two-month lows as supply worries raise oil prices

    • The Indian rupee hit a two-month low today amidst rising oil prices spurred by worries over Middle Eastern supply disruptions. This news directly impacted the Indian currency and contributed to broader market concerns.

  • IndusInd Bank Q1 Results: Profit soars 72% YoY to Rs 1,037 crore; NII flat

    • IndusInd Bank reported a significant 72% year-on-year jump in consolidated Q1 net profit to ₹1,037 crore, aided by lower provisions despite flat net interest income and weaker fee income. This earnings report was a key event for the banking sector.

  • HFCL Q1 results: Firm swings to black, net profit at Rs 246 crore, revenue soars 120% YoY

    • HFCL reported a sharp turnaround in Q1FY27, posting a net profit of ₹246 crore against a year-ago loss as revenue more than doubled, driven by strong export growth and an expanding AI infrastructure. This positive earnings news could influence sentiment around telecommunications and infrastructure stocks.

  • Bharat Coking Coal shares crash 8% after Coal India arm posts Rs 68 crore Q1 net loss

    • Bharat Coking Coal shares plunged over 8% after the company reported a net loss of ₹68 crore for the April-June quarter. This news directly impacted Bharat Coking Coal and could have a ripple effect on other PSU coal-related stocks.

  • Indo-MIM IPO opens tomorrow; GMP signals 37% listing premium. Check price band, dates and key details

    • Indo-MIM’s ₹3,811-crore IPO opens on July 23 with a strong grey market premium of about 37%, with the company planning to repay debt using fresh issue proceeds. This news provides an update on the primary market and could attract investor interest.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's market performance was largely dictated by a confluence of global and domestic factors. The most significant event was the sharp rise in Brent crude oil prices, surging over 4% to cross $95 a barrel, driven by escalating geopolitical tensions between the U.S. and Iran. This immediately translated into higher Indian bond yields and a weakening rupee, which hit a two-month low against the dollar. The financial services sector, particularly PSU Banks, felt the brunt of this pressure, with Bandhan Bank being a major loser.

On the earnings front, IndusInd Bank reported a robust 72% year-on-year jump in Q1 net profit, although its Net Interest Income remained flat. This news provided some support to the banking sector but was overshadowed by the broader negative sentiment. Conversely, Bharat Coking Coal's reported net loss led to an over 8% crash in its shares, impacting the broader energy/coal sector. The session was thus driven by a cautious sentiment stemming from global oil price concerns and mixed corporate earnings.

Setup for tomorrow

Looking ahead, global cues will continue to play a crucial role. The movement of crude oil prices and the geopolitical situation will remain key factors. Overnight, the USD/INR closed higher at 96.57, while Brent crude showed a significant decline of 6.70% to 84.91, and WTI crude rose by 2.59% to 87.11. The US 10Y Yield also saw a slight increase. Investors will be watching for any further developments on the global front. Domestically, the opening of the Indo-MIM IPO tomorrow will be in focus. Key levels for Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty will be crucial to watch for any potential reversals or continuation of the current trend.
