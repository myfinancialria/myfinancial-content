EPF Withdrawal Rules: Form 121 Replaces Forms 15G and 15H

TL;DR

  • From April 1, 2026, a new declaration, Form 121, will replace Forms 15G and 15H for reducing TDS on certain incomes.
  • This change impacts eligible taxpayers seeking relief from Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on specific types of income.
  • For EPF withdrawals, Form 121 will serve as the declaration for individuals whose total taxable income for the year is below the basic exemption limit.
  • Submitting Form 121 helps avoid TDS at the time of withdrawal, but it does not make a taxable EPF withdrawal tax-free.
  • Taxpayers must ensure they meet the eligibility criteria for Form 121 to prevent incorrect TDS deductions.

What this means in plain terms

The Indian tax system often requires Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on certain payments, including some types of income withdrawals. For years, individuals could submit Form 15G or Form 15H to declare that their total income for the financial year would fall below the basic exemption limit, thereby requesting the payer not to deduct TDS. This was particularly relevant for fixed deposit interest, rental income, and certain EPF withdrawals.

From April 1, 2026, the government is streamlining this process by introducing a single new form, Form 121, which will replace both Form 15G and Form 15H. This means if you are eligible and want to avoid TDS on specific incomes, including applicable EPF withdrawals, you will need to submit Form 121 instead of the previous forms. It's crucial to understand that this new form is a declaration to avoid TDS; it does not change the taxability of the income itself. If your EPF withdrawal is taxable, you will still need to include it in your income tax return and pay tax, even if no TDS was deducted.

Understanding TDS on EPF Withdrawals

The Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) is a popular retirement savings scheme for salaried individuals in India. While contributions to EPF are generally tax-exempt under Section 80C, and interest earned is also tax-free under certain conditions, withdrawals can sometimes attract tax.

TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) on EPF withdrawals primarily applies in two scenarios:

  1. Withdrawal before 5 years of continuous service: If an employee withdraws their EPF balance before completing five years of continuous service, the entire amount (employer's contribution, employee's contribution, and interest earned) becomes taxable. TDS is generally applicable if the withdrawal amount exceeds ₹50,000.
  2. Withdrawal after 5 years, but taxable amount: Even after 5 years, if the withdrawal includes contributions made after April 1, 2020, to which interest was paid on a balance exceeding ₹2.5 lakhs (or ₹5 lakhs for government employees with no employer contribution), that interest component can be taxable. However, TDS provisions typically focus on the early withdrawal scenario.

The current TDS rate for early EPF withdrawals is 10% if the Permanent Account Number (PAN) is provided. If PAN is not provided, the TDS rate can be significantly higher, often at the maximum marginal rate (currently 30% plus cess).

The Role of Forms 15G and 15H

Before the introduction of Form 121, Forms 15G and 15H served as crucial declarations for eligible individuals to avoid TDS on various incomes. These forms essentially allowed a taxpayer to declare to the payer (e.g., bank, EPFO) that their estimated total income for the financial year would be below the basic exemption limit, and therefore, no tax would be payable by them.

  • Form 15G: This form was used by resident individuals below 60 years of age and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs). To be eligible, the final tax liability for the financial year must be nil, and the total of specified incomes (like interest income, EPF withdrawals) should not exceed the basic exemption limit for that year.
  • Form 15H: This form was specifically for resident senior citizens (60 years and above). The primary condition for submitting Form 15H was that the final tax liability for the financial year should be nil. There was no restriction on the total amount of specified income, as long as the net tax liability was zero.

When submitted correctly and on time, these forms instructed the payer not to deduct TDS. However, the responsibility to correctly declare income and pay any applicable tax during the income tax filing process always remained with the taxpayer.

Introducing Form 121: The New Standard

From April 1, 2026, Form 121 will unify the functionality of both Form 15G and Form 15H. This new form is designed to simplify the process for taxpayers who wish to declare that no TDS should be deducted from certain incomes because their total taxable income for the financial year is below the threshold.

Key aspects of Form 121:

  • Purpose: To declare that the recipient's total income for the financial year is estimated to be below the basic exemption limit, and hence, no tax is payable. This allows the payer to not deduct TDS.
  • Eligibility: The form will likely consolidate the eligibility criteria previously applicable to Forms 15G and 15H, meaning both individuals below 60 and senior citizens may use it, provided they meet the specified income and tax liability conditions. Details regarding specific age criteria and income thresholds will be clarified in official notifications from the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT).
  • Validity: Similar to its predecessors, Form 121 is expected to be valid for one financial year. A new form would need to be submitted for each financial year if the conditions continue to be met.
  • Applicability to EPF: For qualifying EPF withdrawals where TDS would otherwise be applicable (e.g., early withdrawal before 5 years of service exceeding ₹50,000), an eligible taxpayer can submit Form 121 to the Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO) to prevent TDS deduction.

It is crucial to re-emphasise that submitting Form 121 is a declaration for TDS relief, not a declaration of tax exemption. If your EPF withdrawal is indeed taxable as per income tax laws, you are still liable to pay the tax when filing your Income Tax Return, even if no TDS was deducted at source. Misrepresenting facts in Form 121 can lead to penalties under the Income Tax Act, 1961.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 32, a marketing professional in Pune, earning ₹18L CTC. She worked at Company A for 3 years and 8 months, contributing to her EPF. Due to a career break for higher studies, she decided to withdraw her EPF balance of ₹4.5 lakhs. Since she has not completed 5 years of continuous service, her EPF withdrawal is taxable.

Priya's other income for the financial year (from part-time consulting) is estimated to be ₹1.5 lakhs. Her total estimated taxable income for the year, including the EPF withdrawal, would be ₹1.5 lakhs + ₹4.5 lakhs = ₹6 lakhs.

However, Priya knows that the basic exemption limit for individuals below 60 is ₹2.5 lakhs (for the financial year 2025-26, assuming no changes). She has deductions of ₹1.5 lakhs under Section 80C and other sections.

  • Scenario 1 (Today, pre-April 2026): Priya would submit Form 15G to the EPFO. If her total taxable income after all deductions is below the basic exemption limit and her tax liability is nil, the EPFO would not deduct TDS on her ₹4.5 lakh EPF withdrawal.
  • Scenario 2 (From April 1, 2026): Priya would submit Form 121 to the EPFO. She would declare that her estimated total income for the financial year, after considering all deductions, would result in a nil tax liability. For instance, if her only income was the EPF withdrawal of ₹4.5 lakhs and she had deductions of ₹2 lakhs, her net taxable income would be ₹2.5 lakhs, which is at the basic exemption limit. In this case, no tax would be payable, and Form 121 would be valid.

Important Note: In Priya's actual example, her total income before deductions is ₹6 lakhs. Even with ₹1.5 lakhs in deductions, her taxable income would be ₹4.5 lakhs (₹6 lakhs - ₹1.5 lakhs), which is above the basic exemption limit of ₹2.5 lakhs. Therefore, Priya would not be eligible to submit Form 15G (or Form 121 in the future) because her tax liability would not be nil. The EPFO would deduct TDS at 10% on her ₹4.5 lakh withdrawal, which is ₹45,000. She would then claim credit for this TDS when filing her income tax return. This example highlights that Form 121 (or 15G/15H) is only for those with nil tax liability.

What to do this week

  1. Understand Your EPF Taxability: Review your EPF contribution history. If you anticipate withdrawing your EPF balance, especially before completing five years of continuous service, assess if the withdrawal would be taxable. You can check your service history on the EPFO member portal.
  2. Stay Informed on Form 121 Details: Keep an eye on official notifications from the Income Tax Department (incometax.gov.in) and the CBDT (incometaxindia.gov.in) regarding the precise rules, eligibility criteria, and format for Form 121 as April 2026 approaches.
  3. Consult a Professional: If you are planning a significant EPF withdrawal in the coming years, especially around the 2026 timeframe, consider discussing your specific situation with a Certified Financial Planner or tax advisor to understand the tax implications and the correct procedure for TDS management.

FAQ

What is Form 121?

Form 121 is a new declaration form that will replace Forms 15G and 15H from April 1, 2026. It allows eligible taxpayers to declare that their total income for the financial year is below the basic exemption limit, requesting the payer not to deduct TDS on specified incomes.

Who can submit Form 121?

While specific eligibility criteria will be detailed by the CBDT, it is expected to be available to resident individuals (both below and above 60 years of age) and HUFs whose estimated total income for the financial year results in a nil tax liability.

When does Form 121 come into effect?

Form 121 will replace Forms 15G and 15H starting from April 1, 2026, for the financial year 2026-27 and onwards.

Does Form 121 make my EPF withdrawal tax-free?

No, Form 121 is a declaration to avoid TDS deduction at source, not an exemption from tax. If your EPF withdrawal is taxable as per income tax laws, you are still liable to pay the applicable tax when filing your Income Tax Return.

What happens if I don't submit Form 121 for an eligible EPF withdrawal?

If you are eligible but do not submit Form 121 (or the equivalent form at that time), the EPFO will deduct TDS as per the applicable rates on your taxable EPF withdrawal. You can then claim credit for this TDS when you file your income tax return.

How long is Form 121 valid?

Similar to Forms 15G and 15H, Form 121 is expected to be valid for one financial year. You would need to submit a new form for each financial year if you continue to meet the eligibility conditions.
