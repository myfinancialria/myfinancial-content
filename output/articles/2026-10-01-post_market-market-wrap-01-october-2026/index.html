Market Wrap — 01 October 2026

TL;DR

  • Indian equities witnessed a broad-based decline today, with benchmark indices Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing in the red.
  • Global cues, particularly rising US Treasury yields and a global bond rout, coupled with persistent FII selling, were the primary drivers of market weakness.
  • The IT sector emerged as the standout gainer, buoyed by positive global tech sentiment following strong earnings from a major IT services firm.
  • The Automobile sector was the biggest loser, with Bajaj Auto seeing a significant drop.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, offloading over ₹10,000 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided crucial support with substantial buying.
  • Futures & Options (F&O) build-up data is unavailable for this session and will be populated from the next wrap.

How the market behaved today

Indian markets experienced a negative session today, with key indices closing lower amidst broad-based selling pressure. The Nifty 50 opened with a gap down and trended downwards for most of the day, eventually closing at 22421.95, down 0.88%. The Bank Nifty also ended in negative territory at 54450.75, a decrease of 0.33%. Midcap and Smallcap indices mirrored the broader market sentiment, posting sharper declines.

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, surged by 7.19% to 14.46, indicating an increase in investor apprehension and expected market swings.

What moved the market — the factors

The Indian market's downturn today was largely influenced by a confluence of global headwinds and persistent foreign institutional selling. Global cues were distinctly negative, with US markets dipping as surging Treasury yields overshadowed software gains. This global bond rout was evident as UK 30-year gilt yields topped 6% for the first time since 1998, and the UK pound fell to three-month lows amid economic apprehensions over rates and oil. Domestically, this translated into a significant selloff in Indian government bonds, pushing the benchmark 10-year yield to a mid-2024 high.

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their aggressive selling, offloading a substantial ₹10,148 crore from the Indian equity market. This sustained outflow, a trend observed over the past five sessions, put significant pressure on the indices. While Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided a strong counter-balance by pumping in ₹11,272 crore, their buying was not enough to offset the FII selling and broader negative sentiment.

Sector-wise, the IT sector was a notable outlier, showing resilience and gaining over 2%. This uplift was primarily driven by positive global sentiment following upbeat Q4 results from Accenture, which boosted shares of major IT players like Cognizant, IBM, and Salesforce globally, and likely MphasiS domestically. Conversely, the Automobile sector was the biggest drag, declining over 3%, with Bajaj Auto being a significant contributor to this fall. The Metal and Media sectors also faced considerable selling pressure. The rising India VIX further underscored the heightened uncertainty and risk aversion among market participants.

News that moved stocks

  • RBI Eases Bank Stake Rules, Allows One-Time Approval for MFs, Insurers for Holdings up to 10%

    • The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has simplified bank shareholding rules, permitting eligible mutual funds, insurance companies, and pension funds to seek one-time approval for holdings up to 10%. This regulatory easing is positive for the Financial Services sector, particularly for banks, as it could attract more institutional investment and improve their capital structure.

  • US Markets Dip as Surging Treasury Yields Overshadow Software Gains; UK Pound Falls to Three-Month Lows

    • Wall Street experienced a downturn as rising Treasury yields impacted investor confidence, despite strong performance from the software sector. Concurrently, the UK pound hit a three-month low against the dollar due to economic apprehensions over soaring oil prices and persistent inflation. These global developments contributed to a negative sentiment across the Indian market, particularly impacting FII flows and increasing volatility.

  • Cognizant, IBM and Salesforce Gain up to 13% after Accenture’s Upbeat Q4 Results Lift Sentiment

    • Shares of global IT giants like Cognizant, Salesforce, and IBM surged following Accenture's strong fiscal quarter results, which exceeded forecasts. This positive sentiment spilled over to the Indian IT sector, leading to gains in companies like MphasiS, and making IT the top-performing sector today.

  • HDFC Bank ADRs Jump Over 5% After Anup Bagchi Appointed as MD & CEO for Three Years

    • HDFC Bank's American Depository Receipts (ADRs) rose significantly after the RBI approved Anup Bagchi as the new MD and CEO, effective October 27, 2026. This news is positive for HDFC Bank and the broader Financial Services sector, reflecting investor confidence in the leadership transition.

  • Global Bond Rout Hoists Benchmark Indian Yield to Mid-2024 High Before RBI Policy

    • A substantial selloff in Indian government bonds occurred as global yields rose and inflation pressures intensified, pushing the Indian benchmark 10-year yield to its highest level since April 2024. This development signals increased borrowing costs and tighter liquidity, which could impact interest-rate sensitive sectors across the Indian market ahead of the upcoming RBI policy review.

  • Sebi Revamps Document Number Verification System; Asks Stock Brokers to Display Investor Warnings

    • Sebi has revamped its Document Number Verification System, making the subject matter mandatory for authentication, and has also mandated stock brokers to display investor awareness messages on their websites and trading apps under 'Project Jagrook'. These regulatory moves aim to enhance transparency and investor protection, impacting stock brokers and the broader financial market infrastructure.

F&O build-up — where traders are positioning

OI build-up needs the previous session's OI — it will populate from the next run. Data for F&O build-up is unavailable for today's session.

Interpretation: Once available, F&O build-up data helps understand the conviction behind price movements. Long build-up (price up, Open Interest up) suggests fresh buying interest and a bullish outlook. Short build-up (price down, Open Interest up) indicates fresh selling pressure and a bearish sentiment. Short covering (price up, Open Interest down) implies that traders who had bet against the stock are now buying to close their positions, leading to a bounce. Long unwinding (price down, Open Interest down) suggests that traders who had bought the stock are now selling, either to book profits or cut losses, indicating weakness.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's session was predominantly driven by a combination of global macroeconomic factors and sustained institutional flows. The most significant event was the global bond rout, which saw US Treasury yields surge and UK 30-year gilt yields top 6%. This global trend directly impacted the Indian bond market, pushing the benchmark 10-year yield to a mid-2024 high. The rising yields globally and domestically signal higher borrowing costs and potential inflation concerns, which typically weigh on equity valuations and contribute to risk aversion.

The persistent selling by FIIs (₹10,148 crore today) was a direct consequence of these global headwinds and possibly profit-taking, putting significant pressure on the broader market. However, the strong buying by DIIs (₹11,272 crore) helped to mitigate a sharper fall.

In corporate news, Accenture's upbeat Q4 results had a positive ripple effect on the global and Indian IT sector. This led to a strong performance in Indian IT stocks, making it the only sector to close significantly in the green. Additionally, the RBI's easing of bank shareholding rules for MFs, insurers, and pension funds up to 10% is a positive regulatory development for the financial services sector, potentially attracting more long-term institutional investment into banks. The appointment of Anup Bagchi as MD & CEO of HDFC Bank also saw its ADRs jump, indicating positive market reception to leadership changes in a major financial institution.

Setup for tomorrow

Tomorrow's session will likely continue to be influenced by global bond market dynamics and FII flows. Overnight, US markets dipped due to surging Treasury yields, and the UK pound fell, indicating ongoing global economic concerns. While Brent Crude saw a dip, WTI Crude was up, and the Dollar Index strengthened, suggesting a cautious global environment. The US 10Y Yield, though slightly down today, remains a key monitor.

Investors will be watching for further cues from global markets, especially the movement in bond yields. The ongoing FII selling remains a critical factor, and DII buying will be essential for market stability. Key levels for Nifty 50 to watch would be today's low of 22217.30 as immediate support and 22610.60 as resistance. Sectors like IT, which showed resilience today, and the rate-sensitive financial sector, will be in focus. The upcoming RBI policy review and the start of the earnings season will also be crucial events to monitor in the near term.
