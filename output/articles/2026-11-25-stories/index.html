Story 1: New ESIC Rules Simplify Sickness and Maternity Claims

Topic signal: ESIC rules eased: No separate claims needed for sickness, maternity benefits; check payout deadlines

The Employees' State Insurance Corporation (ESIC) has introduced new regulations that aim to simplify how eligible workers claim sickness and maternity benefits. Under the Employees' State Insurance (General) Regulations, 2026, eligible claims can now be processed through direct benefit transfer (DBT) without requiring separate claim forms.

To qualify for this automatic processing, three conditions must be met: the medical certificate needs to be generated through ESIC's information system, the employer must submit the required monthly return via its portal, and the worker must have an Aadhaar-linked bank account with their insurance number also linked to Aadhaar.

These new rules, effective from November 21, 2026, also streamline claims for employment-related injuries. In such cases, the employer's accident report can now serve as the claim for temporary and permanent disablement benefits, as well as dependants' benefits in case of death. This change is designed to reduce paperwork and make it easier for insured employees to access their benefits.

Story 2: RBI Proposes New Digital Payment Safeguards

Topic signal: RBI proposes digital payment safeguards: Explained – How customers can control UPI, switch off cards, set payment limits

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has proposed new safeguards for digital payments, aiming to give customers more control over their online transactions. These draft rules suggest features that would allow you to disable various digital payment channels, such as UPI and mobile banking, and set your own transaction limits.

Under these proposals, you could set limits for individual payments, daily transactions, or even for specific beneficiaries. For example, you might choose to cap transfers to a single person at ₹20,000 per day. Additionally, new debit, credit, and prepaid cards would initially only work at ATMs and card machines in India, with online, contactless, and international use requiring you to activate them separately.

Another significant proposal is an account-wide 'stop switch' that would allow you to immediately block all outgoing payments if you suspect your account has been compromised. These measures are currently in draft form and open for public comments until December 14, 2026, with most proposed to start from July 1, 2027.

What to remember: The RBI's proposed digital payment safeguards aim to enhance user control and security through features like customizable transaction limits and a 'stop switch' for outgoing payments.

A small check for yourself: Do you actively review and manage the security settings for your digital payment methods?
