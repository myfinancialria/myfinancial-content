Pre-Market Brief — 18 September 2026

TL;DR

  • US markets closed significantly higher, while Asian markets are mostly positive this morning, setting a generally firm global tone.
  • GIFT Nifty is indicating a flat to slightly negative opening bias for the Indian benchmark Nifty 50.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong buying support.
  • The US SEC's five-year exemption for tokenized stock trading could pave the way for new digital asset market structures globally.
  • Nifty 50's CPR pivot at 23327.28 will be a crucial level to watch for intraday direction.
  • Ongoing FII selling pressure and geopolitical tensions keeping crude oil prices volatile remain key risks for the Indian market.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Global markets presented a mixed but largely positive picture overnight. US indices surged, with the tech-heavy Nasdaq leading the charge, reflecting optimism in growth sectors. Asian markets are also trading in the green this morning, buoyed by hopes of progress in US-China trade talks. This generally positive global sentiment, particularly from the US and key Asian peers, could provide a supportive backdrop for the Indian markets, though GIFT Nifty suggests a cautious start.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, with USD/INR declining by 0.33% to 95.82. The Dollar Index saw a marginal uptick of 0.09% to 100.31. Crude oil prices witnessed a notable decline, with Brent Crude falling 2.63% to $102.06 and WTI Crude down 2.30% to $99.57. Gold and Silver both moved higher, with Gold up 0.78% to $4433.80 and Silver surging 3.07% to $67.48. The US 10-Year Yield eased by 1.18% to 4.95%.

Interpretation:

  • USD/INR: A depreciating USD/INR (stronger Rupee) is generally positive for importers as their costs decrease, but it could be a slight headwind for IT and pharmaceutical exporters whose revenues are primarily in foreign currency.
  • Dollar Index: A stable Dollar Index suggests no major global currency shocks, providing a neutral signal for emerging markets like India.
  • Crude Oil: The significant drop in crude oil prices is a positive development for India, a major oil importer. This could ease inflationary pressures, reduce the import bill, and potentially benefit sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paints, and aviation.
  • Gold/Silver: Rising precious metal prices often indicate a flight to safety amidst global uncertainties, or a weakening of the dollar, though the Dollar Index was slightly up.
  • US 10Y Yield: A decline in the US 10-Year Yield makes emerging markets like India relatively more attractive for foreign capital, potentially reducing the risk of FII outflows.

FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree on Thursday, offloading Indian equities worth ₹-3,209 crore in the cash segment. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, remained strong buyers, injecting ₹3,618 crore into the market, largely offsetting the FII outflows.

Interpretation:

  • Nifty: High Call OI at 23400 and 23300 suggests that option sellers expect the Nifty to face strong resistance around these levels, acting as a ceiling. Conversely, the significant Put OI at 23300 and 23200 indicates that option buyers are defending these levels, providing strong support or a floor. The PCR of 0.83, being below 1, leans towards a slightly cautious or bearish-to-neutral sentiment.
  • Bank Nifty: The highest Call OI at 57500 and 57000 will act as immediate resistance. The highest Put OI at 57500 and 56000 suggests strong support. A PCR of 1.08, being above 1, indicates a supportive or bullish-to-neutral sentiment for Bank Nifty.
  • Sensex: The 74500 strike has both high Call and Put OI, indicating it's a battleground. Above 74500, 75000 will be a key resistance. Below 74500, 74400 and 74000 will provide support. A PCR of 1.0 suggests a neutral stance.
  • Combining technical and OI levels, for Nifty, the 23300-23400 range appears crucial, with 23300 acting as immediate support and 23400 as resistance. For Bank Nifty, 56000-57000 seems to be the range, with 56000 as strong support. For Sensex, 74400-74500 is a critical zone. Traders should respect these levels today, as a break on either side could dictate the intraday trend.

Stock & sector news that matters

Jefferies hikes Navin Fluorine share price target, forecasts 14% upside Jefferies has maintained a 'Buy' rating on Navin Fluorine International, raising its price target to ₹9,405, citing strong growth prospects in its CDMO, R32, and Chemours project segments. Why it matters: This positive brokerage report could lead to increased investor interest and potential upward movement in Navin Fluorine International shares today. It also highlights positive sentiment in the specialty chemicals sector.

US Market: SEC unveils five-year exemption for tokenized stock trading The US SEC has introduced a five-year exemption to allow certain blockchain-based platforms to trade tokenized stocks under limited regulatory relief. Why it matters: This is a significant development for the digital assets and financial technology space. While directly impacting US markets, it signals a potential shift in regulatory approach towards tokenized securities globally, which could eventually influence how financial instruments are traded in India and open up new avenues for innovation.

Global Market: China stocks rally 1% as Trump-Xi meeting raises trade hopes China's stock markets saw a strong rally, with technology, AI, and semiconductor stocks leading gains, as investors anticipate progress in upcoming US-China trade talks between Donald Trump and Xi Jinping. Why it matters: Positive developments in US-China trade relations are generally beneficial for global trade and economic stability. This could boost sentiment for export-oriented sectors in India and reduce geopolitical uncertainties that often weigh on emerging markets.

Motilal Oswal sees surging steel prices to offset cost inflation for metal majors Motilal Oswal expects firm steel prices, lean inventories, and resilient demand to support pricing- and cost-led earnings growth for domestic steelmakers, with stronger realisations. Why it matters: This positive outlook from a prominent brokerage could bring the metals sector, particularly steel stocks, into focus today. Companies like Tata Steel, JSW Steel, and Hindalco could see investor interest if this sentiment translates into market action.

GPT Infraprojects shares surge 9% after securing Rs 484 crore railway order from RVNL GPT Infraprojects shares rallied nearly 9% after the company secured a major railway bridge order worth ₹484 crore from Rail Vikas Nigam Limited (RVNL), strengthening its order book to ₹4,992 crore. Why it matters: This is a significant order win for GPT Infraprojects, indicating strong execution capabilities and a robust order pipeline. It could lead to a positive movement in the stock and potentially boost sentiment for other infrastructure and railway-related companies.

MCX traders complain of commodity position prices not reflecting on Zerodha, broker blames delay in exchange reports Zerodha users reported issues with average prices for MCX commodity positions showing as "NA/0" on the Kite app, with Zerodha attributing the problem to delays in exchange reports. Why it matters: This highlights operational challenges that can impact active traders in the commodity segment. While Zerodha has clarified the issue, such glitches can affect trading decisions and confidence, especially for those involved in intraday commodity trading.

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  • Indian Government Bond Auction: Traders will be closely watching the cutoff yields from today's Indian government bond auction. Markets typically react to these cutoffs, with higher-than-expected yields potentially leading to a cautious sentiment in the bond market and a slight negative spillover into equities, while lower yields are generally seen as positive.
  • US-China Trade Talks: While no specific outcome is expected today, the ongoing anticipation of progress in US-China trade talks (following reports of a Trump-Xi meeting) will be a key global sentiment driver. Markets tend to react positively to signs of de-escalation and negatively to increased trade tensions.
  • Geopolitical Developments (Middle East): JPMorgan's inability to predict oil's next move due to deepening Middle East tensions underscores the volatility risk. Any escalation or de-escalation in the region can cause sharp movements in crude oil prices, which directly impacts India's inflation outlook and current account deficit, leading to volatility in Indian markets.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Nifty 50's opening and CPR levels: Watch how Nifty reacts around its CPR pivot of 23327.28. A sustained move above 23367.97 or below 23294.02 could set the day's trend, especially given the narrow CPR.
  2. FII vs. DII flows: Monitor whether DII buying continues to absorb FII selling pressure. Sustained FII outflows could cap market upside.
  3. Crude Oil Prices: Any significant reversal in crude oil prices from yesterday's decline could quickly change market sentiment, particularly for oil-sensitive sectors.
  4. Metals Sector: Keep an eye on steel stocks following Motilal Oswal's positive outlook.
  5. Infrastructure/Railway Stocks: GPT Infraprojects' order win could bring focus to the broader infrastructure and railway construction space.
  6. Global Cues: The overall tone from Asian and European markets, and any fresh news regarding US-China trade, will continue to influence sentiment.

Bottom line

The Indian market is poised for a cautious to flat opening, as indicated by GIFT Nifty, despite positive overnight cues from US and most Asian markets. The persistent FII selling, largely offset by DII buying, remains a key dynamic. The Nifty's narrow CPR suggests a potentially trending day, with the 23300-23400 range being critical. Traders should remain vigilant of global geopolitical developments impacting crude oil and the ongoing FII flow patterns.
