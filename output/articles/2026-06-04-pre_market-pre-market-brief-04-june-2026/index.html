Pre-Market Brief — 04 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets closed in the red across the board, indicating a cautious start for Indian equities.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, offloading over ₹5,600 crore yesterday, though Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong counter-support.
  • The Indian Rupee weakened against the US Dollar, while gold prices saw an uptick, suggesting some safe-haven demand.
  • Concerns about persistent inflation in the US could lead to tighter monetary policy from the Federal Reserve, impacting global liquidity.
  • Key corporate actions today include interim dividends from TIIL and RALLIS, and a bonus issue from TRENT.

Where global markets closed

Global equity markets ended yesterday's session predominantly in negative territory, reflecting a cautious sentiment among investors. Major US indices, the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500, all registered declines. European markets, represented by the FTSE 100, also closed lower. Asian markets followed suit, with Japan's Nikkei 225, Hong Kong's Hang Seng, and China's Shanghai Composite all posting losses. This broad-based decline suggests a subdued opening is likely for Indian markets.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) depreciated by 0.51% to 95.75 against the US Dollar, indicating a weakening of the domestic currency. A weaker Rupee can make imports more expensive and potentially fuel inflation, which the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) monitors closely. The Dollar Index, which measures the US Dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies, saw a slight dip of 0.08% to 99.45.

In the commodities market, crude oil prices edged lower, with Brent Crude down 0.92% to $96.91 and WTI Crude falling 0.93% to $95.13. Lower crude prices are generally positive for India, as the country is a net importer of oil, potentially easing import bills and inflationary pressures. Gold prices, however, climbed 1.28% to $4493.30, suggesting increased safe-haven demand amidst global uncertainties. Silver saw a marginal decline of 0.23% to $73.31.

The US 10-Year Treasury Yield rose by 0.81% to 4.49%. A rising US yield can make dollar-denominated assets more attractive, potentially leading to capital outflows from emerging markets like India. It also signals concerns about inflation or expectations of tighter monetary policy by the US Federal Reserve.

Corporate actions today

Today marks the ex-date for several corporate actions that will impact shareholders:

  • TIIL: The stock will trade ex-interim dividend of ₹20 per share. Shareholders buying the stock today or later will not be eligible for this dividend.
  • RALLIS: The stock will trade ex-dividend of ₹3 per share. Investors must have held the shares before today to receive this dividend.
  • TRENT: The company has announced a bonus issue in the ratio of 1:2. This means for every two shares held, shareholders will receive one additional share. The stock will trade ex-bonus today, meaning the share price will adjust to reflect the bonus issue.

Recent FII/DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree yesterday, offloading Indian equities worth a net ₹5,617 crore. This marks a consistent trend of outflows from FIIs. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong support to the market, with net purchases of ₹5,741 crore, largely offsetting the FII selling.

Over the last five trading sessions, FIIs have been net sellers, with cumulative outflows exceeding ₹40,000 crore. DIIs, on the other hand, have consistently been net buyers, providing a crucial cushion against the foreign selling pressure.

Overnight news that matters

US Stock Market: Fed may need tighter policy as inflation risks persist Dallas Federal Reserve President Lorie Logan has indicated that US monetary policy might not be restrictive enough to bring inflation down to the 2% target, as inflation remains above the Fed's goal. This matters to Indian markets because a hawkish stance from the US Fed could lead to higher interest rates globally, strengthening the dollar, and potentially triggering further FII outflows from emerging markets like India.

Global firms exploit India's IPO boom to take profits back to home countries Foreign companies are increasingly listing their Indian units through secondary offerings to repatriate billions of dollars back to their home countries, driven by high stock valuations in India. This trend is significant for Indian markets as it represents a form of capital outflow, even if through IPOs, and could add to the supply of shares in the market, potentially influencing valuations.

Will India’s economy be in trouble if IMD’s monsoon forecast proves accurate? Economists are cautioning that if the weather office's prediction of the weakest rainfall in over a decade proves accurate, the threat to India's economic growth will hinge on specific regional risks, rather than just the headline numbers. The monsoon is critical for India's agriculture sector and rural demand, and a weak monsoon could lead to higher food inflation and impact corporate earnings, particularly for companies reliant on rural consumption.

Earnings slowdown in FY27? JM Financial lists 5 sectors which must do the heavy lifting JM Financial notes that Nifty 50 earnings growth fell short of expectations in FY25 and FY26, despite Q4 FY26 results leading to an upward revision for FY27E Nifty 50 EPS growth. The brokerage highlights automobiles, metals, and capital goods as sectors that need to perform strongly to drive overall earnings growth. This analysis provides a forward-looking perspective on sector performance and could guide investor allocation strategies.

Jefferies initiates coverage on Poonawalla Fincorp with Buy rating. Why are analysts bullish? Jefferies has initiated coverage on Poonawalla Fincorp with a "Buy" rating and a target price of ₹490, citing strong growth potential driven by a new leadership team and an expanded product portfolio. This news is relevant for investors tracking specific stock recommendations and the broader non-banking financial company (NBFC) sector, indicating potential upside for the stock.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Global Market Cues: Given the negative close across major global indices, monitor how Asian markets react in early trade and the overall sentiment, which could influence the Indian open.
  2. FII/DII Flow Dynamics: The persistent FII selling and strong DII buying will remain a key factor. Any significant shift in this trend could dictate market direction.
  3. Monsoon Developments: Keep an eye on any further updates or analyses regarding the monsoon forecast, as it has significant implications for inflation, agricultural output, and consumption-driven sectors.
  4. Corporate Actions: Stocks like TIIL, RALLIS, and TRENT will trade ex-dividend/bonus today. Their price movements and volumes will be worth watching.
  5. Sectoral Performance: Following expert commentary, observe the performance of sectors like Energy, Defence, and Healthcare for growth visibility, and the IT sector for selective opportunities rather than broad-based buying.

Bottom line

Indian markets are likely to open with a cautious bias today, influenced by the negative global cues and continued FII outflows. While DIIs have been providing strong support, concerns around US inflation and a potentially weak monsoon in India could keep investors on edge. Traders should monitor key global developments and FII/DII activity closely for directional cues.
