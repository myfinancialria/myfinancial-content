FAQ

What is a small-cap stock?

A small-cap stock refers to a company with a relatively small market capitalisation, typically between ₹5,000 crore and ₹20,000 crore in India. They are often seen as having higher growth potential but also carry higher risk compared to large-cap companies.

Why do corporate results matter for investors?

Corporate results provide a transparent view of a company's financial health, operational efficiency, and growth trajectory. They help investors assess past performance, understand current challenges, and make informed decisions about future investments.

What does "margin pressure" mean?

Margin pressure occurs when a company's profit margins (the percentage of revenue that turns into profit) decrease. This can happen due to rising costs of raw materials, increased operational expenses, or intense competition forcing lower selling prices.

What are "multibagger returns"?

Multibagger returns refer to an investment that has grown by multiple times its initial value. For example, a "ten-bagger" means an investment has increased tenfold. These are often associated with high-growth stocks, especially in the small-cap segment, over a long period.

How do I find management commentary?

Management commentary is typically included in the company's official earnings report, press releases, or transcripts of investor conference calls. These documents are usually available on the company's website under the 'Investor Relations' section and on stock exchange websites like BSE and NSE.

Should I invest in a stock based solely on strong revenue growth?

While strong revenue growth is a positive indicator of demand for a company's products or services, it should not be the sole criterion for investment. Sustainable profitability, healthy cash flows, strong management, and a clear competitive advantage are equally important for long-term value creation.
