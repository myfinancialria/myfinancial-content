Pre-Market Brief — 23 November 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets ended last week on a cautious note, with US tech indices seeing significant declines, while Asian markets were mixed this morning.
  • GIFT Nifty is signalling a notable gap-down open for the Indian benchmark indices today.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained strong net buyers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to book profits.
  • Indian markets saw profit booking after a sharp rally, with PSU banks particularly impacted, amidst a broader global tech sell-off.
  • Nifty 50's immediate support levels to watch are around 25300-25100, following yesterday's sharp correction.
  • The primary risk for today's session is the negative global sentiment, especially from the US tech sector, potentially leading to further profit booking in India.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Global markets closed last week with a mixed to negative tone. US indices, particularly the tech-heavy Nasdaq and S&P 500, saw significant declines, reflecting concerns over a semiconductor sell-off and broader profit booking in AI stocks. European markets, like the FTSE 100, managed a modest gain, while Asian markets this morning were largely negative, with the Nikkei 225 falling sharply. This sets a cautious to negative backdrop for the Indian market open.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) strengthened against the Dollar, closing at 92.91, down 0.41%. The Dollar Index, a measure of the dollar's value against a basket of major currencies, saw a marginal increase of 0.03% to 103.84. Crude oil prices witnessed a significant decline, with Brent Crude falling 4.04% to $88.83 and WTI Crude down 1.61% to $82.63. Gold and Silver, typically considered safe-haven assets, saw substantial gains of 3.16% and 3.64% respectively. The US 10-Year Treasury Yield also fell by 2.77% to 4.91%.

Interpretation:

  • USD/INR: A strengthening Rupee is generally positive for India, potentially easing import costs and making Indian assets more attractive to foreign investors. However, a retreat from earlier highs suggests some profit booking.
  • Dollar Index: A marginally stronger Dollar globally could limit the Rupee's upside, despite domestic factors.
  • Brent/WTI Crude: The significant fall in crude oil prices is a major positive for India, a net oil importer. This could ease inflationary pressures, reduce the current account deficit, and benefit sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paints, and aviation.
  • Gold/Silver: The sharp rise in precious metals indicates increased safe-haven demand globally, often a sign of underlying market uncertainty or risk aversion.
  • US 10Y Yield: A decline in US bond yields can make emerging markets like India relatively more attractive for FII investments, potentially supporting equity flows.

GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling

The GIFT Nifty is trading at 25351.00, down 1.53%. This indicates a significant gap-down opening for the Nifty 50 today, likely reflecting the negative global cues and yesterday's domestic profit booking.

Interpretation: The substantial fall in GIFT Nifty points towards a weak start for the Indian market, suggesting that the Nifty 50 could open considerably lower than its previous close.

FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers in the cash market yesterday, injecting ₹10,308 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, were net sellers, pulling out ₹4,860 crore. Over the last five sessions, FIIs have consistently been net buyers, while DIIs have largely been net sellers, indicating a divergence in their market stance.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Global Cues and GIFT Nifty: The significant gap-down indicated by GIFT Nifty suggests a weak opening. Monitor how Indian indices react to this initial shock.
  2. Nifty 50's Key Levels: Watch the 25300-25100 range for Nifty 50 as crucial support. A sustained break below S1 (25131) could signal further downside.
  3. FII vs. DII Stance: Observe if FIIs continue their buying spree despite the market correction, and if DIIs continue to book profits. Their net flows will be key to market direction.

  5. Rupee Movement: The rupee's stability amidst global volatility will be important. Any significant depreciation could add to market concerns.
  6. Global Tech Sentiment: Given the US tech sell-off, keep an eye on Indian IT and tech-related stocks for potential spillover effects.

Bottom line

The Indian market is poised for a gap-down open today, influenced by negative global cues, particularly from the US tech sector, and domestic profit booking after a strong rally. The Nifty's ability to hold the 25300-25100 support zone will be critical for determining the day's trajectory. While FIIs remain supportive, continued DII selling and global risk aversion warrant caution in today's session.
