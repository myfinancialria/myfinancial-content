Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options contracts provides a crucial insight into where market participants, particularly option writers (sellers), are positioning themselves. The strike price with the highest Call OI typically acts as a resistance level, meaning many believe the index may struggle to move above it. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI usually serves as a support level, indicating conviction that the index is unlikely to fall below this point. These levels are often referred to as "walls" because of the significant number of contracts accumulated there.

For the Nifty 50, the highest Call OI and highest Put OI are both concentrated at the 24000 strike. This suggests that option writers are strongly defending this level, expecting the index to largely remain around 24000. Today's fresh Call writing was heaviest near 24083, indicating that some call sellers expect the upside to be capped around this level. On the other hand, fresh Put writing was most significant near 23983, suggesting that put sellers are building a floor just below the current spot.

The Sensex saw today's heaviest fresh Call writing near 78166, implying that option sellers believe the index might face resistance around this higher level. In contrast, fresh Put writing was most concentrated near 76433, suggesting that put sellers are establishing a base of support below the current spot. The aggregate flow also showed a significantly higher net build-up in Put OI (20.4M) compared to Call OI (9.5M), reinforcing the idea of put writers being more active.

For Bank Nifty, while the option chain shows significant OI at 60000 for both calls and puts, the data on fresh writing was not explicitly provided for specific strikes. However, the overall flow (ΔPCR) was mixed, indicating a balanced activity between call and put writers today.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that compares the total Open Interest of put options to that of call options. A PCR above 1 generally suggests that more put options have been written than call options, which can be interpreted as a bullish signal by some, as put writers profit when the market stays above their strike. Conversely, a PCR below 1 might suggest a bearish sentiment.

For the Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 1.06, indicating a relatively balanced book with a slight tilt towards put writing. However, today's fresh flow in Open Interest (ΔPCR) was 1.58, which is significantly higher. This suggests that today, new put options were written much more aggressively than new call options, pointing to a fresh wave of put-heavy activity and a mildly bullish sentiment for the day's flow.

The Sensex also showed a balanced PCR(OI) at 1.23. More notably, its PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR) was a strong 2.16. This substantial fresh put writing indicates that option sellers were very confident today that the index would not fall significantly, contributing to a strong bullish signal from today's option flow.

The Bank Nifty had a PCR(OI) of 0.81, which is below 1, generally suggesting a slight bearish lean from the overall open interest. However, today's flow ΔPCR was 1.19, indicating a more balanced or even slightly put-heavy fresh writing activity for the day. This suggests that while the overall book might have a bearish tilt, today's new positioning was more neutral to mildly positive.

Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that specific level. It's often considered a "magnet" because as expiry approaches, the index sometimes tends to gravitate towards this point, as option writers try to minimise their losses.

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain point is at 24000, which is precisely where the index closed today. This suggests a strong gravitational pull towards the current levels, indicating that option writers are positioned for the index to expire very close to 24000.

The Sensex Max Pain point is at 76600, which is about 0.4% below its current spot of 76,922.64. This indicates a slight downward pull from the Max Pain perspective, suggesting that option writers might benefit if the index were to drift slightly lower towards 76600 as expiry nears.

The Bank Nifty Max Pain point is at 58600, which is approximately 1.0% above its current spot of 58,033.05. This suggests a potential upward pull, implying that option writers might see maximum benefit if the Bank Nifty moves higher towards 58600 by expiry.

Today, FIIs held a 10.0% long ratio in Index futures (32,476 long contracts versus 292,535 short contracts). This indicates a substantial net short position in index futures. Historically, a very low FII long ratio in index futures has often coincided with a cautious or bearish stance from this segment of participants, suggesting they are hedging or anticipating potential downside in the broader market.

In contrast, FIIs maintained a 53.5% long ratio in Stock futures (3,771,958 long contracts versus 3,275,109 short contracts). This shows a slightly net long position in individual stock futures, suggesting a more balanced or mildly positive outlook on specific companies, even while being net short on the broader indices.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these data points can empower you to interpret market sentiment daily. You can access options Open Interest data, including the total OI and daily changes (ΔOI), directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, or through most brokerage platforms.

To read this yourself, first, identify the strike prices with the highest Call OI and Put OI for the current expiry. These are your potential resistance and support levels. Next, look at the "change in OI" for calls and puts at various strikes; significant fresh writing (increase in OI) at a particular strike indicates where new positions are being taken. A high build-up in Call OI suggests resistance, while a high build-up in Put OI suggests support. You can also calculate the PCR by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI to gauge overall market sentiment. Finally, keep an eye on FII derivative statistics, also available on the NSE website, to understand how institutional money is positioning itself in futures contracts. Combining these insights helps you understand the market's underlying structure, rather than relying on speculative forecasts.
