India F&O Pulse — 18 Nov 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Indian Indices Show Bearish Tilt as Call Writers Dominate Today's Session

TL;DR

  • Nifty 50: Today's options data suggests a mildly bearish outlook, with significant call writing activity indicating potential resistance ahead.
  • SENSEX: Similar to Nifty, the SENSEX also displays a mildly bearish bias, driven by fresh call selling at higher strikes.
  • BANK NIFTY: The banking index shows a more pronounced bearish signal, with heavy call writing and a low Put-Call Ratio from today's activity.

What the indices did today

The Nifty 50 closed the day at 25,227.95, marking a decline of 0.63%. The index settled below its At-The-Money (ATM) strike of 25250, and also below the Max Pain point of 25400. While the broader option chain walls suggested a range between 24000 and 26000, today's activity saw the index moving towards the lower end of its immediate implied range.

The SENSEX followed a similar trajectory, ending at 82,031.81, down 0.61%. It closed just above its ATM strike of 82000, but significantly below its Max Pain level of 82500. The option chain implied a wider range of 80000 to 85000, with today's price action reflecting a move away from the upper resistance levels.

The BANK NIFTY saw a relatively milder decline of 0.38%, closing at 62,038.65. Interestingly, its Max Pain level was exactly at 62000, very close to its closing spot price. The index remained within its implied range of 60000 to 62000, with a notable concentration of Call Open Interest acting as a ceiling near the current levels.

Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) in options gives us a snapshot of outstanding contracts and can indicate where market participants, especially option writers, expect support and resistance. The highest Call Open Interest (Call OI) strike typically represents a strong resistance level, where many call options have been sold, indicating that writers believe the price is unlikely to rise above that point. Conversely, the highest Put Open Interest (Put OI) strike often acts as a significant support level, where put options have been sold, suggesting writers expect the price to stay above that level.

Today's fresh writing activity provides crucial insights. For the Nifty 50, the heaviest fresh Call (CE) writing was observed near the 25333 strike. This suggests that call sellers anticipate the index to face a cap around this level, reinforcing a bearish sentiment. The aggregate flow for Nifty showed a significant net Call OI build of 50.6 million contracts compared to a Put OI build of 17.9 million, indicating a clear preference for selling calls.

In the SENSEX, the heaviest fresh Call writing was seen near 83666, another sign of expected upside resistance. However, there was also fresh Put writing near 81166, suggesting some participants expect a floor around that level. Despite this, the aggregate flow leaned bearish, with net Call OI build of 17.0 million exceeding Put OI build of 8.0 million.

For the BANK NIFTY, the heaviest Call OI was prominently positioned at 62000, very close to the spot price, acting as an immediate ceiling. Fresh Call writing was concentrated near 62500, while fresh Put writing was seen near 61066. The aggregate flow was overwhelmingly call-heavy, with a net Call OI build of 2.0 million against a mere 0.2 million in Put OI, pointing to strong resistance expectations.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely watched indicator that compares the total open interest of put options to call options. A PCR(OI) above 1 typically suggests more put options are open, which can be seen as bullish (many put sellers expecting prices to rise). A PCR(OI) below 1, with more call options open, is generally considered bearish (many call sellers expecting prices to fall).

For the Nifty 50, the PCR(OI) stood at 0.75, indicating a balanced book overall, leaning slightly towards more call options outstanding. However, the PCR based on today's flow (ΔOI) was significantly lower at 0.35. This low figure suggests that today's fresh option writing was heavily skewed towards calls, implying that new positions taken today were predominantly bearish in nature.

The SENSEX showed a PCR(OI) of 0.78, again suggesting a relatively balanced overall book with a slight call bias. Its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was 0.47, also indicating that today's fresh writing was more focused on selling calls than puts, contributing to a bearish tilt for the session.

The BANK NIFTY had a PCR(OI) of 0.87, appearing relatively balanced. However, its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) was a very low 0.08. This extremely low ratio signifies an overwhelming dominance of call writing in today's session, strongly suggesting that option writers are positioning for limited upside or potential downside in the banking index.

Max pain and the magnet effect

Max Pain is the strike price at which option writers, as a group, would experience the least amount of financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that price. It's often referred to as a "magnet effect" because as expiry approaches, the underlying index sometimes tends to gravitate towards this level.

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain point is at 25400, which is approximately 0.7% above its spot close of 25,227.95. This suggests that option writers, on aggregate, would prefer the index to expire higher than its current level.

The SENSEX's Max Pain is at 82500, about 0.6% above its spot close of 82,031.81. Similar to Nifty, this indicates a slight upward pull from the perspective of option writers.

The BANK NIFTY, however, has its Max Pain exactly at 62000, which is almost at its spot close of 62,038.65. When Max Pain aligns closely with the spot price, it suggests that the market is already near the level where option writers are most comfortable, and the "magnet effect" might keep it anchored around this zone. As expiry draws nearer, the influence of Max Pain tends to become stronger.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning often provides insights into broader market sentiment.

Today, FIIs held an Index futures long ratio of 32.5%. This means that out of their total index futures positions, only 32.5% were long contracts, while 67.5% were short. Historically, such a low long ratio in index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish stance from these large institutional players, suggesting they are anticipating limited upside or potential downside in the broader market.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of 57.6%. This indicates a more balanced to slightly bullish stance in individual stock futures compared to their index futures positioning.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

When we synthesise today's various data points, a consistent theme emerges. The predominant fresh call writing across Nifty, SENSEX, and BANK NIFTY, coupled with low Put-Call Ratios from today's activity, collectively suggests that option writers are positioning for resistance and limited upside in the immediate term. The FIIs' cautious stance in index futures, indicated by their low long ratio, historically tends to coincide with a period of market consolidation or downward pressure. While Max Pain for Nifty and SENSEX shows a slight upward pull, the overwhelming call-heavy positioning from today's flow signals that market participants expect the indices to face headwinds. Historically, such a combination of signals has tended to coincide with sessions where indices either consolidate with a negative bias or experience further downside.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements more effectively. To read this yourself daily, you can start by visiting the NSE website (archives.nseindia.com) to download the end-of-day F&O Bhavcopy and participant-wise Open Interest data.

From the Bhavcopy, you can identify the highest Call OI and Put OI strikes for each index, which give you the immediate resistance and support levels. You can also calculate the PCR(OI) by dividing the total Put OI by total Call OI. For today's flow (ΔOI), compare the change in Call OI versus Put OI at different strikes. Many brokerage platforms also provide visual representations of option chains which make these levels easier to spot. Combining this with FII derivative positioning data, also available on the NSE website, helps you gauge the sentiment of large institutional players. By regularly observing these data points, you can develop a nuanced understanding of market positioning and potential biases for the upcoming sessions.
