ITR-3 & ITR-4: Key Schedules and Verification for AY 2026-27

TL;DR

  • Taxpayers with business or professional income must carefully choose between ITR-3 and ITR-4 based on their income type and turnover.
  • ITR-3 is for individuals and HUFs with income from business or profession, requiring detailed financial statements.
  • ITR-4 (Sugam) is for individuals, HUFs, and firms opting for presumptive taxation under Sections 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE.
  • Thoroughly review and reconcile key schedules like P&L, Balance Sheet (for ITR-3), and business income (for ITR-4) with your financial records.
  • Always verify your income and TDS details against Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement (AIS) to prevent discrepancies.

What this means in plain terms

As the August 31st deadline approaches for many individual taxpayers, understanding the nuances of Income Tax Return (ITR) forms becomes crucial, especially for those with business or professional income. If you're a salaried individual also earning from a proprietorship business, or a self-employed professional like a doctor or consultant, you'll likely be filing either ITR-3 or ITR-4. These forms are more detailed than the simpler ITR-1 or ITR-2, requiring you to report specific financial information related to your business or profession.

ITR-3 is designed for individuals and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) who have income from a proprietary business or a profession. This form requires comprehensive reporting, including profit and loss statements and balance sheets. On the other hand, ITR-4, also known as Sugam, is a simplified form for those who opt for the presumptive taxation scheme under specific sections of the Income Tax Act. This scheme allows eligible small businesses and professionals to declare income at a prescribed rate without maintaining detailed books of account. Regardless of which form you file, carefully reviewing the relevant schedules and reconciling your income and tax deducted at source (TDS) with official records is paramount to ensure accurate and error-free filing for Assessment Year (AY) 2026-27.

Understanding ITR-3: For Businesses and Professionals

ITR-3 is a comprehensive income tax return form designed for individuals and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) who derive income from a proprietary business or profession. This form is necessary when you have income under the head "Profits or Gains from Business or Profession" and are not eligible for the presumptive taxation scheme, or choose not to opt for it. It also covers income from capital gains, house property, and other sources, if applicable.

Who should file ITR-3?

You should file ITR-3 if you are an individual or a HUF and have income from:

  • A proprietary business.
  • A profession (e.g., doctor, lawyer, consultant, architect).
  • Partnership firm (as a partner, not the firm itself, which files ITR-5).
  • Income from house property, capital gains, or other sources, in addition to business/professional income.
  • Income from speculation business.

Key Schedules in ITR-3 to verify

Filing ITR-3 requires meticulous attention to several schedules that detail your financial performance. Here are some critical ones:

  • Schedule P&L (Profit and Loss Account): This schedule is fundamental, summarising your business's revenues, expenses, and ultimately, its net profit or loss. You must ensure that every income and expense item reported here matches your books of account. Discrepancies can lead to scrutiny.
  • Schedule BS (Balance Sheet): For businesses exceeding certain turnover limits or not opting for presumptive taxation, a Balance Sheet is mandatory. It provides a snapshot of your assets, liabilities, and capital at the end of the financial year. Verify that all figures, including fixed assets, current assets, loans, and capital, reconcile with your financial statements.
  • Schedule CG (Capital Gains): If you have sold any capital assets (like shares, mutual funds, property, gold) during the financial year, this schedule is crucial. Ensure all short-term and long-term capital gains or losses are accurately reported, including details of acquisition and sale.
  • Schedule BP (Computation of Income from Business or Profession): This schedule consolidates the net profit from your P&L account and makes adjustments as per income tax rules to arrive at your taxable business income. Cross-check all additions and deductions carefully.
  • Schedule TDS/TCS: This section details any Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) or Tax Collected at Source (TCS) on your income. It is vital to reconcile these figures with your Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement (AIS) to ensure you claim the correct tax credit. Any mismatch here can lead to demand notices from the Income Tax Department.

Navigating ITR-4 (Sugam): For Presumptive Taxation

ITR-4, also known as Sugam, is a simplified ITR form designed for individuals, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), and firms (other than Limited Liability Partnerships) who have opted for the presumptive taxation scheme. This scheme aims to ease the compliance burden for small taxpayers by allowing them to declare income at a prescribed rate without maintaining detailed books of account.

Who can opt for ITR-4?

You can file ITR-4 if you are an individual, HUF, or a partnership firm (excluding LLP) and have:

  • Income from business where turnover does not exceed ₹2 crores and you opt for presumptive taxation under Section 44AD.
  • Income from profession where gross receipts do not exceed ₹50 lakhs and you opt for presumptive taxation under Section 44ADA.
  • Income from plying, hiring, or leasing goods carriages, and you opt for presumptive taxation under Section 44AE.
  • Income from salary/pension, house property (one house property only), and other sources, in addition to the presumptive business/professional income.

However, you cannot file ITR-4 if you have:

  • Income from more than one house property (for AY 2026-27, you can report income from up to two house properties).
  • Income from capital gains.
  • Income from foreign assets or foreign income.
  • Income from winning lottery or horse races.
  • Losses brought forward or to be carried forward.

Important Schedules in ITR-4

While ITR-4 is simpler, certain schedules still require careful attention:

  • Schedule BP (Computation of Income from Business or Profession): This is the core schedule for ITR-4. Here, you declare your gross turnover/receipts and compute your presumptive income as per the applicable section (44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE). For Section 44AD, income is generally declared at 6% of digital receipts and 8% of cash receipts. For Section 44ADA, it's 50% of gross professional receipts. Ensure your reported turnover/receipts align with your bank statements and other records.
  • Schedule TDS/TCS: Similar to ITR-3, this schedule is for reporting any TDS or TCS on your income. It is critical to match these figures with your Form 26AS and AIS to claim accurate tax credits.

Common Verification Steps Across Forms

Regardless of whether you are filing ITR-3 or ITR-4, certain verification steps are universally important to ensure accuracy and avoid potential notices from the Income Tax Department.

Reconciling income with Form 26AS/AIS

This is arguably the most crucial step. Form 26AS provides a consolidated annual statement of tax deducted or collected at source, as well as advance tax and self-assessment tax paid. The Annual Information Statement (AIS) is even more comprehensive, providing a full view of your financial transactions, including interest income, dividend income, mutual fund transactions, and more.

  • Action: Download your Form 26AS and AIS from the Income Tax e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in).
  • Verification: Compare every income entry reported in your ITR (salary, interest, professional fees, business receipts) with what is reflected in Form 26AS and AIS. Pay close attention to TDS entries. Any mismatch must be investigated. If your income is higher in your records than in 26AS/AIS, ensure it's reported. If 26AS/AIS shows higher income than your records, you need to understand why and either update your records or contact the deductor/collector.

Matching turnover and expenses

For ITR-3, this involves a detailed comparison of your P&L and Balance Sheet figures with your accounting records. For ITR-4, it means ensuring the gross receipts/turnover declared for presumptive taxation matches your bank statements, payment gateway reports, and other income proofs.

  • Action: Compile all bank statements, cash books, invoices, and expense vouchers.
  • Verification: For ITR-3, cross-verify each line item in your P&L and Balance Sheet. For ITR-4, ensure your declared gross receipts are accurate and that any cash receipts are correctly accounted for when calculating presumptive income.

Verifying TDS/TCS entries

Incorrect or missing TDS/TCS credits are a common reason for tax demands.

  • Action: Check your Form 26AS for all entries of TDS and TCS.
  • Verification: Ensure that every TDS/TCS entry shown in Form 26AS is claimed in your ITR. If any entry is missing from 26AS but you have a TDS certificate, contact the deductor to get it updated. If you don't have a certificate but 26AS shows it, ensure you account for the corresponding income.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 42, a freelance graphic designer based in Mumbai, with gross professional receipts of ₹45 lakhs for FY 2025-26. She also has some interest income from fixed deposits.

Priya is eligible to opt for presumptive taxation under Section 44ADA as her gross receipts are below ₹50 lakhs. She decides to file ITR-4 (Sugam).

Step-by-step verification for Priya:

  1. Download Form 26AS and AIS: Priya first downloads her Form 26AS and AIS from the Income Tax portal.
  2. Verify Professional Receipts: Her bank statements and invoices show total professional receipts of ₹45 lakhs. She checks this against the 'Receipts from Business/Profession' section in her AIS. If there's any TDS deducted by clients, it will show up in Form 26AS. She ensures her declared gross receipts of ₹45 lakhs in Schedule BP of ITR-4 match her actual records and AIS.
  3. Compute Presumptive Income: Under Section 44ADA, she declares 50% of her gross receipts as her professional income.

    4. * Presumptive Income = 50% of ₹45,00,000 = ₹22,50,000. * This ₹22.50 lakhs will be entered in Schedule BP of ITR-4.

  4. Verify Interest Income: Her AIS shows ₹85,000 as interest income from fixed deposits, with ₹8,500 TDS deducted. She ensures this interest income is reported under 'Income from Other Sources' in ITR-4 and that the ₹8,500 TDS credit is correctly claimed.
  5. Reconcile TDS Credits: She cross-checks all TDS entries from her Form 26AS against the TDS claimed in her ITR-4. If a client deducted TDS but it's not reflecting in 26AS, she follows up with the client for correction.
  6. Final Review: Before submitting, Priya reviews the entire ITR-4 form, ensuring all personal details, bank account information for refund, and tax calculations are accurate.

By following these steps, Priya ensures her ITR-4 filing is accurate, avoiding potential issues with the Income Tax Department.

  2. Choose the Correct ITR Form: Determine whether you need to file ITR-3 or ITR-4 based on your income sources and eligibility for presumptive taxation. Refer to the Income Tax India website for detailed guidance.
  3. Reconcile Income & TDS: Download your Form 26AS and AIS from the e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in) and thoroughly compare all reported income and TDS entries with your personal financial records. Address any mismatches promptly.
  4. Review Key Schedules: If filing ITR-3, meticulously review your P&L, Balance Sheet, and Capital Gains schedules. For ITR-4, ensure your gross receipts and presumptive income calculations in Schedule BP are accurate and verifiable.
  5. Consult a Professional (If Needed): If your financial situation is complex or you are unsure about specific schedules or compliance requirements, consider consulting a Certified Financial Planner or a Chartered Accountant.

FAQ

Who is eligible to file ITR-3?

ITR-3 is for individuals and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) who have income from a proprietary business or profession, or are partners in a firm, along with other income sources like capital gains or house property.

What is the main difference between ITR-3 and ITR-4?

ITR-3 is for taxpayers with business or professional income who maintain detailed books of account or are not eligible for presumptive taxation. ITR-4 (Sugam) is a simplified form for small taxpayers opting for the presumptive taxation scheme under Sections 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE, avoiding the need for detailed bookkeeping.

How do I reconcile my income with Form 26AS and AIS?

Download both documents from the Income Tax e-filing portal. Compare all income entries (salary, interest, professional fees, etc.) and TDS/TCS entries in your ITR with those reflected in Form 26AS and AIS. Address any discrepancies by correcting your ITR or contacting the deductor.

Can I file ITR-4 if my business turnover is ₹3 crores?

No, you cannot. ITR-4 under Section 44AD is applicable only if your gross business turnover does not exceed ₹2 crores. For a turnover of ₹3 crores, you would typically need to file ITR-3 and maintain proper books of account.

What if I forgot to claim a TDS entry in my ITR?

If you forgot to claim a valid TDS entry that is reflected in your Form 26AS, you might receive a tax demand. You can file a revised return (if within the permissible time limit) to claim the TDS credit.

Is it mandatory to maintain books of account for ITR-4?

No, one of the primary benefits of opting for presumptive taxation under ITR-4 is that you are not required to maintain detailed books of account as specified under Section 44AA of the Income Tax Act.

What is the deadline for filing ITR for individuals with business income?

For individuals and HUFs whose accounts are not required to be audited, the deadline for filing ITR (including ITR-3 and ITR-4) for AY 2026-27 (FY 2025-26) is typically July 31st. However, the prompt mentions August 31st, which could be an extended deadline for the current year or specific cases. Always refer to the latest notifications from the Income Tax Department for the exact due date.
