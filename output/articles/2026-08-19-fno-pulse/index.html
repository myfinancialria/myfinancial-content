Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides insights into the market's liquidity and the conviction of participants. When we look at options, the highest Call OI strike often acts as a significant resistance level, meaning many Call option sellers (writers) believe the price is unlikely to rise above this point. Conversely, the highest Put OI strike typically acts as a strong support level, where Put option sellers expect the price to remain above.

For the NIFTY 50, the heaviest Call OI is observed around the 24,500 strike, indicating that option writers anticipate the Nifty might find it challenging to move beyond this level. On the Put side, the 24,000 strike holds the heaviest Put OI, suggesting it could act as a crucial support. Today's fresh Call writing was most concentrated near the 24,200 level, reinforcing the idea that call sellers expect upside to be capped. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 24,050, suggesting that put sellers are establishing a floor around current levels. The aggregate flow showed a net Call OI build of 55.8 million contracts, significantly higher than the 21.7 million Put OI build, which points to a stronger conviction among Call writers.

The SENSEX saw its heaviest Call OI wall at 78,000, while the 76,000 strike held the most Put OI. Today, the heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near the 77,000 strike, indicating that option sellers believe this level could act as a resistance. Fresh Put writing was most significant around 76,733, suggesting some support building below current spot.

For BANK NIFTY, the 58,000 strike had the highest Call OI, potentially acting as a ceiling, while 57,000 held the most Put OI, forming a base. Today's fresh Call writing was concentrated near 57,366, suggesting some resistance around that point. However, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 56,900, indicating that option sellers are actively building support at lower levels.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used sentiment indicator calculated by dividing the total Put Open Interest by the total Call Open Interest. A PCR value above 1 generally suggests a bullish bias, as more Put options are being written (sellers expect prices to rise), while a value below 1 indicates a bearish bias, due to a dominance of Call writing (sellers expect prices to fall). We also look at the PCR based on today's change in OI (ΔOI) to gauge fresh sentiment.

For the NIFTY 50, the PCR(OI) stood at 0.68, which is below 0.7, indicating a dominance of Call options in the overall open interest and suggesting a bearish lean with potential upside capping. Today's flow, represented by PCR(today flow ΔOI) at 0.39, further reinforces this, showing that fresh writing was heavily skewed towards Calls.

The SENSEX exhibited a similar trend, with a PCR(OI) of 0.66, also below 0.7, implying a bearish sentiment from the overall options book. The PCR(today flow ΔOI) at 0.53 suggests that today's fresh option writing was also predominantly in Calls, reinforcing the view of potential resistance.

The BANK NIFTY presented a slightly different picture. Its PCR(OI) was 0.71, which is closer to a balanced book, indicating a more neutral stance from the overall options data. However, the PCR(today flow ΔOI) was 1.25, suggesting that today's fresh option writing was significantly skewed towards Puts, which typically implies that Put sellers are expecting the index to find support or move higher.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" is a concept in options theory that refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the minimum financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that specific price. As expiry approaches, the underlying index often tends to gravitate towards this Max Pain strike, acting like a 'magnet' as option writers adjust their positions.

For the NIFTY 50, the Max Pain level is currently at 24,500. This is approximately 1.8% above the current spot price of 24,078.30. This suggests that option writers are collectively positioned for the Nifty to potentially move higher towards this level by expiry, although the overall bearish bias from PCR and Call writing might temper this pull.

The SENSEX has its Max Pain point at 77,600, which is about 0.9% above its current spot of 76,909.68. Similar to Nifty, this indicates a potential upward pull from option writers' positioning, even as other signals suggest caution.

For the BANK NIFTY, the Max Pain is at 58,000, sitting approximately 1.3% above its spot price of 57,239.75. This aligns with the mildly bullish signals from its PCR and fresh Put writing, suggesting a potential move towards this level.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) are significant participants in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment.

Today's data shows FIIs holding a relatively low Index futures long ratio of 9.6%. This means that out of their total open positions in Index futures, only 9.6% are long contracts, while the vast majority are short. Specifically, FIIs held 24,870 long contracts against 234,677 short contracts. Historically, such a low long ratio in FII Index futures has tended to coincide with a cautious or bearish sentiment from these institutional players towards the broader market.

In Stock futures, FIIs maintained a long ratio of 54.7%, with 3,580,969 long contracts versus 2,963,832 short contracts. This indicates a slightly positive bias in individual stocks, suggesting that while they may be cautious on the overall index, they are selectively bullish on specific counters.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

The strong Call writing observed in both Nifty and Sensex, coupled with their respective PCR values below 0.7 and heavily Call-dominated fresh writing, suggests that option writers are positioned for potential resistance and limited upside. Historically, when such a dominance of Call writing is evident, the next session has tended to see either consolidation or a downward bias, with upside moves being met with selling pressure.

Bank Nifty, on the other hand, shows a mildly bullish tilt, supported by significant fresh Put writing and its Max Pain level sitting above the current spot. When Bank Nifty's option data shows such strength, it has historically tended to coincide with some resilience or even an upward move, potentially acting as a counter-balance to broader market weakness.

The FIIs' significantly net short positioning in Index futures, with a long ratio of just 9.6%, is a notable factor. Historically, such a pronounced short stance from institutional players has tended to coincide with a cautious outlook for the overall market, potentially leading to continued pressure or volatility. While their stock futures positioning is slightly positive, the index-level caution from FIIs often plays a more dominant role in overall market sentiment.

Therefore, when these signals align in this direction, the next session has historically tended to see the Nifty and Sensex facing resistance from Call writers, while Bank Nifty might find some support. The broad market sentiment could remain cautious, influenced by the FIIs' index futures positioning.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals daily can empower you to better interpret market movements. You can start by visiting the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website, specifically the "Derivatives" section. Here, you'll find the "Option Chain" for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and other indices. Look for the "Open Interest" column for both Calls and Puts at various strike prices. The strikes with the highest OI indicate significant support (for Puts) and resistance (for Calls) levels. Also, pay attention to the "Change in OI" column to see where fresh money is being deployed today.

Another crucial piece of data is the "Participant-wise Open Interest" report, also available on the NSE website under the "Reports" section for derivatives. This CSV file provides a breakdown of FII, DII, Client, and Pro activity in futures and options. Focus on the FII data for Index Futures and Stock Futures to gauge their net long or short positions, which can offer a valuable perspective on institutional sentiment. Combining these observations daily can help you build a clearer picture of market positioning and potential biases for the upcoming sessions.
