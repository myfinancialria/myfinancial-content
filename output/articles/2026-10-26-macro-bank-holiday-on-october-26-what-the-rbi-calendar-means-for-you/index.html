Bank Holiday on October 26: What the RBI Calendar Means for You

TL;DR

  • Many bank branches across India will observe a holiday on October 26, 2026, for various regional festivals and events.
  • The specific holidays, such as Laxmi Puja, Accession Day, or Maharishi Valmiki's Birthday, lead to closures in certain states and union territories.
  • While physical branches will be closed, digital banking services like UPI, net banking, and mobile banking will remain fully operational.
  • It is crucial for individuals and businesses to check the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) official holiday calendar for their specific region.
  • Planning urgent transactions in advance or utilising digital alternatives can help avoid inconvenience during these non-working days.

What this means in plain terms

Today, October 26, 2026, many bank branches across India will be closed. This isn't a nationwide holiday for all banks, but rather a series of state-specific closures as per the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) official holiday calendar. The reasons for these closures vary by region and include important festivals like Laxmi Puja, Accession Day, and Maharishi Valmiki's Birthday.

For customers of major public and private lenders such as State Bank of India (SBI), HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and Punjab National Bank (PNB), this means that physical branch services like cash deposits, withdrawals, cheque clearances, and loan applications will not be available in the affected regions. However, it's important to remember that the digital infrastructure of banking remains fully operational, ensuring continuity for most routine financial transactions.

Understanding the RBI Bank Holiday Calendar

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) categorises bank holidays under three main heads:

  1. Negotiable Instruments Act Holidays: These are the most common holidays, declared under Section 25 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881. This category includes national festivals like Republic Day, Independence Day, and Gandhi Jayanti, as well as state-specific festivals.
  2. Negotiable Instruments Act and Real Time Gross Settlement Holiday: This category includes holidays that impact not just physical banking but also real-time large-value fund transfers like RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement).
  3. Banks' Closing of Accounts: This is an annual holiday observed on April 1st for banks to close their yearly accounts.

The RBI publishes a comprehensive list of bank holidays at the beginning of each calendar year, which is accessible on its official website. This calendar is crucial because bank holidays are not uniform across the country. A festival celebrated prominently in one state might not be a holiday in another. For instance, while Laxmi Puja is a significant festival leading to bank closures in many parts of India, its observance and the specific date can vary regionally, as can other events like Accession Day in Jammu & Kashmir or Maharishi Valmiki's Birthday in certain northern states.

October 26: Specific Holidays and Locations

On October 26, 2026, bank branches in various states will observe holidays for specific events. The primary reason for closures today is Laxmi Puja, a significant Hindu festival, which is observed in states like Maharashtra, Gujarat, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, and others where Diwali festivities are prominent.

Additionally, Accession Day is observed as a bank holiday in Jammu & Kashmir. This day commemorates the signing of the Instrument of Accession by Maharaja Hari Singh in 1947. Maharishi Valmiki's Birthday, honouring the author of the epic Ramayana, is also a bank holiday in some northern states, including Punjab, Haryana, and Himachal Pradesh. These regional observances underscore the diverse cultural fabric of India and how it translates into varying bank holiday schedules across different states. Customers are strongly advised to refer to the RBI's official calendar or check with their local bank branch for precise information relevant to their location.

Impact on Banking Services

While the closure of physical bank branches on October 26 might seem inconvenient, it's essential to understand that the modern banking ecosystem is designed to minimise disruption.

What will be unavailable:

  • Cash Transactions: Deposits and withdrawals requiring a teller will not be possible.
  • Cheque Clearances: Physical cheque deposits and withdrawals will be processed only on the next working day. The cheque clearing cycle will be affected.
  • Loan Applications and Other Services: Any service requiring in-person interaction, document submission, or consultation with bank staff will be on hold.

What remains fully operational:

  • Digital Payment Systems: Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions, Immediate Payment Service (IMPS), and National Electronic Funds Transfer (NEFT) will function 24/7.
  • Net Banking and Mobile Banking: All online services, including bill payments, fund transfers, fixed deposit (FD) renewals, and statement downloads, will be accessible.
  • ATMs: Cash withdrawals and deposits (where available) can be performed at Automated Teller Machines (ATMs). However, cash replenishment might be slower in some areas due to the holiday.
  • Credit and Debit Card Transactions: Point-of-Sale (POS) transactions and online purchases using credit and debit cards will work as usual.

This distinction is particularly important for salaried individuals and self-employed professionals who rely heavily on digital channels for their day-to-day financial management.

Planning Your Financial Transactions

Given the regional nature of bank holidays, proactive planning can save you from last-minute hassles.

Check the RBI Calendar

Always consult the official RBI website for the most accurate and up-to-date information on bank holidays in your specific state or union territory. This should be your first step before planning any branch-dependent transaction.

Prioritise Digital Channels

For routine transactions like fund transfers, bill payments, or checking account balances, make full use of net banking, mobile banking apps, and UPI. These services are available 24/7, irrespective of bank holidays.

Cash Management

If you anticipate needing a significant amount of cash for expenses or business transactions, withdraw it a day or two in advance. While ATMs are available, they can sometimes run out of cash during extended holiday periods.

Cheque Deposits and Clearances

If you have cheques to deposit or are expecting a cheque payment, factor in the holiday. Cheques deposited on a holiday will only be processed on the next working day, potentially delaying the credit to your account. For urgent payments, consider using NEFT or IMPS.

Business Transactions

For self-employed individuals and businesses, especially those dealing with suppliers or customers who might also be observing the holiday, communicate payment schedules clearly. Utilise bulk payment options via net banking where possible to manage payroll or vendor payments efficiently.

A real example

Consider Rohan, 38, earning ₹28 lakhs CTC, based in Bengaluru. He runs a small consulting firm and needs to pay his two employees their monthly salaries, ₹75,000 each, and also deposit a client cheque of ₹2.5 lakhs into his business account. He usually does this by visiting his bank branch.

On October 26, 2026, Bengaluru banks are open as Laxmi Puja is not a bank holiday in Karnataka, nor are Accession Day or Maharishi Valmiki's Birthday observed there. However, Rohan's client is based in Ahmedabad, Gujarat, where banks are closed today for Laxmi Puja.

Scenario 1: Rohan is unaware of the regional holiday. Rohan deposits the ₹2.5 lakh cheque in Bengaluru. His bank processes it. However, the client's bank in Ahmedabad is closed. The cheque clearance will be delayed until the next working day when the Ahmedabad bank reopens. This means the funds won't be credited to Rohan's account as quickly as he expected, potentially impacting his cash flow.

For his employees' salaries, Rohan typically transfers funds via NEFT. Since NEFT operates 24/7 regardless of physical branch closures, his employees will receive their ₹75,000 salaries on time, as long as he initiates the transfer through net banking.

Scenario 2: Rohan checks the RBI calendar. Realising his client's bank is in a state with a holiday, Rohan contacts his client to request an NEFT or IMPS transfer instead of a cheque. The client can initiate this digitally, and the ₹2.5 lakhs would be credited to Rohan's account much faster, often within minutes for IMPS or a few hours for NEFT, even on a holiday. For his employees, he proceeds with NEFT as planned, ensuring timely salary disbursal.

This example highlights that while Rohan's local bank might be open, the regional nature of holidays can still impact transactions involving other states, making digital alternatives and proactive checking of the RBI calendar indispensable.

What to do this week

  1. Check Your Local Bank Holiday Status: Spend 5 minutes visiting the RBI website (rbi.org.in) and navigate to their holiday calendar to confirm if your specific bank branch or any counterparty banks are closed today or in the coming days.
  2. Review Upcoming Payments: Take 15 minutes to look at your planned payments for the next few days. If you have any large cash withdrawals, cheque deposits, or in-person bank requirements, adjust your schedule or consider digital alternatives.
  3. Set Up Digital Payment Options: If you haven't already, ensure your net banking, mobile banking app, and UPI are fully set up and functional. This 10-minute check can save significant hassle during holidays.

FAQ

Are all banks in India closed on October 26, 2026?

No, bank holidays on October 26, 2026, are regional. While some states will observe closures for festivals like Laxmi Puja, Accession Day, or Maharishi Valmiki's Birthday, banks in other states will remain open.

Can I still use online banking services during a bank holiday?

Yes, digital banking services such as net banking, mobile banking, UPI, IMPS, and NEFT operate 24/7, even on bank holidays. You can perform most transactions online without any interruption.

Will ATMs be functional on a bank holiday?

ATMs are generally functional for cash withdrawals and deposits. However, cash replenishment services might be slower in some areas due to the holiday, so it's advisable to plan urgent cash needs in advance.

What happens to cheque clearances on a bank holiday?

Cheques deposited on a bank holiday will typically be processed on the next working day. This means the credit to your account will be delayed until the bank reopens for business.

How can I find out the exact bank holidays for my state?

You can find the official list of bank holidays for all states and union territories on the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) official website under their holiday calendar section.
