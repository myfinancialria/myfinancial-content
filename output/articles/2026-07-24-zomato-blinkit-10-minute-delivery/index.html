Why Deepinder Goyal Bet Zomato on 10-Minute Grocery Delivery When Everyone Said It Was Impossible

The year was 2022. India's quick commerce sector, promising groceries delivered in minutes, was burning cash at an alarming rate. Valuations were plummeting, and skepticism hung heavy in the air. Yet, amidst this financial inferno, Deepinder Goyal, the founder and CEO of Zomato, made a move that many called reckless: he announced Zomato would acquire Blinkit (formerly Grofers) for $568 million in an all-stock deal. This wasn't just an investment; it was a deep dive into a segment notorious for its high operational costs and elusive profitability. Investors panicked, sending Zomato's stock down 10% in a single day. Analysts questioned the sanity of buying a company reportedly bleeding 100 crore rupees monthly, with no clear path to breaking even. It looked like a bailout, an act of desperation for a food delivery giant already striving for profitability. But Goyal saw something others missed, a strategic imperative hidden beneath the red ink.

The World Before

Before Zomato's audacious bet, the quick commerce landscape in India was a chaotic battlefield. Companies like Grofers, Swiggy Instamart, and a nascent Zepto were locked in an intense race to deliver everything from fresh produce to electronics in under 15-30 minutes, with some, like Grofers (which rebranded to Blinkit in December 2021), pushing for an ambitious 10-minute target. This speed came at a colossal cost. The business model relied on a dense network of "dark stores"—small, local warehouses designed for rapid fulfillment—and a vast fleet of delivery partners. Maintaining these dark stores, managing inventory, and heavily subsidizing deliveries led to massive cash burn across the industry.

Grofers, founded in 2013, had pivoted from scheduled grocery deliveries to express service during the pandemic, sensing a shift in consumer demand for instant gratification. By November 2021, it was clocking 125,000 orders daily and had raised significant capital, including $630 million from investors like SoftBank and Tiger Global. However, by early 2022, Blinkit found itself cash-strapped, reportedly laying off 1,600 employees and seeking external support. Zomato, already a minority investor with a $100 million stake acquired in June 2021, extended a $150 million loan to Blinkit in March 2022, signaling a deeper involvement.

The general sentiment was that quick commerce was a "money pit," an unsustainable venture where heavy discounting and logistical complexities made profitability a distant dream. Goyal himself had previously experimented with groceries on Zomato and pulled back, making his renewed, and much larger, commitment to Blinkit seem even more perplexing.

The Crack

Deepinder Goyal's decision to fully acquire Blinkit for $568 million on June 24, 2022, was not merely an investment; it was a strategic integration. Goyal articulated his belief that a well-entrenched quick commerce player could pose a long-term threat to Zomato's core food delivery business. He saw quick commerce as "very close to what Zomato is as a business," sharing much of the same last-mile hyperlocal delivery mechanics. The acquisition was a move to leverage Zomato's existing logistics expertise and customer ecosystem, strengthening its position in the broader hyperlocal delivery market.

The initial integration wasn't without its challenges. Goyal openly admitted to a turbulent phase post-acquisition, even twice asking Blinkit founder Albinder Dhindsa to step down, believing he "will not be able to cut it" in the new, fast-scaling quick commerce model. However, Dhindsa ultimately adapted, and the companies focused on a disciplined approach to turnaround Blinkit's fortunes.

Zomato's strategy for Blinkit involved several key pillars:

  1. Operational Efficiency over Discounts: Unlike many competitors burning cash through heavy promotions, Blinkit, under Zomato, deliberately limited deep discounting. Instead, the focus shifted to streamlining the supply chain and delivery networks, improving delivery times, and optimizing order fulfillment rates.
  2. Unit Economics and Dark Store Optimization: The core of quick commerce profitability lies in efficient dark stores. Blinkit refined its dark store model, aiming for optimal inventory management and faster turnaround. By March 2024, Blinkit had achieved adjusted EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortisation) positive status, a significant milestone demonstrating improved unit economics.
  3. Assortment Expansion: Rather than just expanding geographically, Blinkit focused on increasing its product range in existing locations. In the National Capital Region (NCR), for instance, its assortment grew from 35,000-40,000 items to around 80,000 within a year, enhancing customer loyalty and creating a competitive moat.
  4. Strategic Capital Allocation: Zomato continued to invest in Blinkit, understanding the capital-intensive nature of the business, but with a clear eye on sustainable growth and returns. This included plans to expand its dark store count to 1,000 by March 2025.

What Happened Next

The turnaround was nothing short of spectacular. By 2024, Blinkit had not only become Zomato's fastest-growing segment but was also driving Zomato's overall valuation.

  • Profitability: Zomato reported its first-ever consolidated net profit of ₹351 crore for the full fiscal year 2024, a dramatic swing from a loss of ₹971 crore the previous year. Blinkit played a crucial role, turning adjusted EBITDA positive in March 2024.
  • Revenue Growth: Blinkit's revenue from operations nearly tripled, soaring from ₹806 crore in FY23 to ₹2,301 crore in FY24. By FY25, Blinkit's full-year revenue more than doubled again to ₹5,206 crore. In Q1 FY27 (June 2026), Blinkit's adjusted EBITDA rose to ₹102 crore, marking its fifth consecutive quarter of improvement.
  • Market Leadership: Blinkit rapidly outpaced its competitors. By late 2024, Blinkit held approximately 46% of the organized quick commerce market share in India, followed by Zepto at 29% and Swiggy Instamart at 24%. By 2025, Reuters cited Datum Intelligence data showing Blinkit with a 48% market share, with Swiggy Instamart at 24% and Zepto at 22%. In FY26, Blinkit further solidified its lead, processing 916.6 million orders, almost matching Zepto (640.2 million) and Swiggy Instamart (412.2 million) combined.
  • Expansion: Blinkit plans to expand its dark store count to 1,000 by March 2025 and even further to 2,000 by the end of 2026. By Q1 FY27, it had already reached 2,443 dark stores across more than 300 cities.
  • Zomato's Overall Performance: Zomato's overall revenue from operations jumped 71% year-on-year to ₹12,114 crore in FY24. The company's Gross Order Value (GOV) for its B2C segments (food delivery, Blinkit, and dining out) was ₹13,536 crore in Q4 FY24, a 51% increase year-on-year, with Blinkit's GOV nearly doubling to ₹4,027 crore.

Deepinder Goyal's once-criticized bet transformed Blinkit from a cash-burning liability into Zomato's primary growth engine, proving that sometimes, what looks impossible is merely an opportunity misunderstood.

Lessons to Take Away

  1. Strategic Fit Trumps Short-Term Losses: The market initially punished Zomato for acquiring a loss-making entity. However, Goyal understood that quick commerce was a strategic imperative, leveraging existing logistics infrastructure and preventing a future competitive threat. Sometimes, a seemingly expensive acquisition of a struggling asset can be justified if it aligns with a larger, long-term strategic vision and prevents competitive disadvantage.
  2. Execution and Unit Economics are Paramount: Quick commerce's high cash burn was a major concern. Blinkit’s turnaround wasn't just about Zomato's capital infusion, but a disciplined focus on operational efficiency, optimizing dark stores, and improving unit economics. Deep discounting was avoided in favor of sustainable growth, demonstrating that profitability in high-burn sectors is achievable with rigorous execution.
  3. Founder Vision and Resilience are Crucial: Deepinder Goyal's conviction in Blinkit, even to the point of challenging its founder to adapt, highlights the importance of strong leadership and a willingness to make tough decisions post-acquisition. The ability to "penetrate the armor" and push for fundamental changes was key to unlocking Blinkit's potential.
  4. Market Leadership Justifies Investment: While the acquisition price was steep, securing a dominant position in a rapidly expanding market like quick commerce proved invaluable. Entering the market later or trying to build from scratch would have been far costlier and risked missing the growth window entirely. Blinkit's current market share demonstrates the power of early strategic positioning.
