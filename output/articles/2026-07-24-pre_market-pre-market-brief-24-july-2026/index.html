Pre-Market Brief — 24 July 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets saw broad-based selling overnight, with US and Asian indices closing notably lower.
  • GIFT Nifty is signalling a flat to slightly negative open for the Indian benchmark Nifty 50.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers yesterday, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support.
  • Rising crude oil prices above $100 and increasing US 10-year Treasury yields are key concerns.
  • Nifty 50's CPR pivot at 23667.3 will be a crucial level, with resistance around 23728 and support near 23571.
  • The day's main risk stems from persistent global market weakness, elevated crude prices, and continued FII outflows.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Global markets witnessed a significant downturn yesterday, with major US indices closing in the red. The Nasdaq led the losses, shedding over 2%, while the Dow Jones and S&P 500 also declined. Asian markets followed suit this morning, with the Nikkei 225 falling sharply by 2.75% and the Hang Seng and Shanghai also trading lower. This broad-based global weakness, coupled with concerns over rising oil prices and bond yields, sets a cautious tone for the Indian markets at open.

Currency, commodities and yields

The USD/INR pair closed at ₹96.53, showing a slight depreciation of 0.03% for the Rupee. The Dollar Index also saw a minor dip of 0.02% to 101.41. In the commodities space, Brent Crude was at $100.43, down 0.26%, while WTI Crude traded at $91.79, down 0.43%. Despite these minor overnight dips, crude prices remain elevated above the $100 mark. Gold was at $4029.10, down 0.43%, and Silver at $57.76, down 0.06%. A significant move was seen in the US 10-Year Yield, which rose by 0.99% to 4.70%.

Interpretation: A stable USD/INR, despite RBI intervention, indicates ongoing pressure on the Rupee from global factors like higher oil prices. Elevated crude oil prices above $100 per barrel are a concern for India, potentially increasing import bills and inflation, which could impact oil marketing companies, paint, and aviation sectors. The notable rise in the US 10-Year Yield makes US bonds more attractive, potentially leading to capital outflows from emerging markets like India, posing a risk to FII flows.

GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling

The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 23649.00, down 0.12%. This suggests a flat to slightly negative opening for the Nifty 50 on the NSE today.

FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape

Yesterday, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the Indian cash equity segment, offloading equities worth ₹-2,999 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, continued to provide support, with net purchases of ₹2,947 crore.

Interpretation: The continued FII selling, especially the substantial outflow yesterday, indicates a cautious stance from foreign investors, likely influenced by global factors. DIIs have been crucial in absorbing this selling pressure, providing a floor to the market, but sustained FII outflows could weigh on market sentiment.

Stock & sector news that matters

Oil shock, Treasury yield spike put Indian bonds under pressure before debt sale Indian government bonds experienced a decline as oil prices surpassed one hundred dollars per barrel, pushing the benchmark bond yield to its highest level in five weeks. Why it matters: Rising bond yields in India increase borrowing costs for the government and corporations, potentially impacting economic growth and corporate earnings. This also makes bonds more attractive relative to equities, which could divert FII flows.

RBI intervention blunts oil surge, shores up rupee The Reserve Bank of India intervened by selling dollars to prevent the rupee from reaching a record low, as surging oil prices and declining stock markets put pressure on the Indian currency. Why it matters: RBI intervention provides temporary stability to the Rupee but highlights underlying pressures from high crude prices and global market weakness. A weaker Rupee could make imports costlier and fuel inflation.

Why Swiggy shares fell over 5% today: Foreign ownership cap sparks $460 million passive outflow fears Swiggy shares declined after the company proposed reducing its foreign ownership limit to 49.5% from 100%, raising concerns about its eligibility for major global indices. Why it matters: This move could lead to Swiggy's exclusion from global indices, potentially triggering passive outflows of around $460 million. This specific news is negative for Swiggy stock and could impact investor sentiment towards other companies with high foreign ownership.

HDFC Bank, 3 other bank stocks wipe out Rs 1.5 lakh crore of investors' wealth after Q1. Time to buy the dip? Shares of HDFC Bank, Axis Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank, and Yes Bank collectively erased over ₹1.5 lakh crore in market value this week following their June-quarter earnings. Why it matters: The significant wealth erosion in major banking stocks after Q1 results indicates investor disappointment. The banking sector is a heavyweight in Indian indices, and continued weakness here could drag down the broader market.

Trump's tariff policy: Abbott India, Ajanta Pharma to Alembic — pharma stocks crash up to 4% Pharma stocks dipped after President Trump announced a phased tariff plan for imported generic medicines, raising concerns for Indian exporters. Abbott India led losses at 3.69%. Why it matters: This news directly impacts Indian pharmaceutical exporters, as new tariffs could reduce their competitiveness and profitability in the US market, which is a major destination for Indian generics. This could create headwinds for the entire pharma sector.

IndiGo shares sink 3% after Rs 238 crore Q1 loss, but why are Citi and Nuvama raising target prices? InterGlobe Aviation (IndiGo) reported a Q1FY27 net loss of ₹238 crore, as soaring fuel costs pushed expenses ahead of revenue growth. Despite the earnings miss, brokerages like Citi and Nuvama raised target prices. Why it matters: IndiGo's loss highlights the pressure on aviation companies from high fuel costs. While brokerages remain optimistic, the immediate earnings miss could keep the stock under pressure.

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  • Indian Government Bond Sale: The pressure on Indian bonds due to rising oil prices and US Treasury yields ahead of a debt sale could lead to volatility in bond markets and impact banking sector liquidity. Markets typically react with caution to rising yields, as it increases borrowing costs.
  • RBI's Stance on Rupee: The Reserve Bank of India's continued intervention to support the Rupee will be closely watched. While intervention can stabilize the currency in the short term, persistent pressure could signal broader economic concerns.
  • Global Central Bank Commentary (US urging BOJ): The US Treasury Department's call for the Bank of Japan to continue raising interest rates to control inflation and yen volatility indicates ongoing global monetary policy tightening discussions. Such commentary can influence global capital flows and risk appetite, impacting FII sentiment towards emerging markets.
  • F&O Expiry (next week): While not today, the upcoming F&O expiry often brings increased volatility and short covering/unwinding as participants roll over or close their positions.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Global Market Cues: The sustained weakness in US and Asian markets will likely dictate the initial sentiment for Indian equities.
  2. Crude Oil Prices: Any further upward movement in Brent Crude above $100 will remain a key concern, impacting inflation expectations and corporate costs.
  3. FII Flows: The trend of FII selling will be crucial to monitor. Continued outflows could put further pressure on the market.
  4. Key Index Levels: For Nifty, watch the CPR pivot at 23667.3, with immediate support at 23571 and resistance at 23728. A break of these levels could signal the day's direction.
  5. Banking Sector Performance: Given the recent wealth erosion in major bank stocks, their performance today will be important for the broader market.
  6. Pharma Sector Reaction: The impact of potential US tariffs on Indian pharma exporters will be a key focus for stocks like Abbott India and other pharma majors.

Bottom line

Indian markets are poised for a cautious opening, influenced by negative global cues, elevated crude oil prices, and rising US bond yields. While DIIs have been providing support, sustained FII selling remains a concern. The Nifty 50's ability to hold above its immediate support levels will be critical in determining the day's trend, with global risk aversion and domestic sector-specific news likely to keep volatility high.
