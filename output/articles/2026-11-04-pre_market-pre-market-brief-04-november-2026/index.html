Pre-Market Brief — 04 November 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight, with US indices closing higher (Nasdaq leading) while Asian markets were mixed, influenced by US midterm election outcomes.
  • GIFT Nifty is signalling a positive start for the Indian market, pointing to a potential gap-up open.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were significant net buyers yesterday, continuing their buying trend over the last few sessions, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers.
  • The US midterm election results, with a Democratic victory, are a key global driver, impacting commodity prices (silver surge, crude slide) and raising concerns for certain tech sectors.
  • For Nifty, the 24000 level appears to be a crucial resistance point, while 23500 could act as strong support based on Option Chain data.
  • The main risk for today could be the continued global market reaction to the US election outcome, particularly its implications for technology and AI-related stocks, alongside a slowdown in China's services PMI.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Overnight, US markets closed largely positive, with the tech-heavy Nasdaq leading the gains, while the S&P 500 also ended in the green. The Dow Jones, however, saw a slight decline. In Asia this morning, the picture is mixed; Japan's Nikkei 225 is down, but Hong Kong's Hang Seng and China's Shanghai Composite are trading with significant gains. This mixed global sentiment, particularly the positive cues from the US and parts of Asia, sets a cautiously optimistic tone for the Indian markets.

Currency, commodities and yields

The USD/INR pair saw a marginal decline to ₹94.66, indicating a slight strengthening of the Rupee against the Dollar. The Dollar Index also fell by 0.51% to 101.35, suggesting broader Dollar weakness globally. Crude oil prices (Brent and WTI) softened, with Brent trading at $102.85 and WTI at $95.74, both down from yesterday's close. Gold prices surged by 2.91% to $4055.00, while Silver saw an even sharper jump of 4.80% to $51.97. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose by 1.19% to 4.95%.

Interpretation: A stronger Rupee (USD/INR down) is generally positive for Indian importers and could ease imported inflation pressures. Falling crude oil prices are a significant tailwind for India, benefiting oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, aviation, and logistics sectors by reducing input costs. The sharp rise in gold and silver, partly attributed to a weaker dollar and US midterm election results, might attract investor interest in precious metals. The uptick in US 10-year yields could potentially make US assets more attractive, posing a slight risk to FII flows into emerging markets like India.

Interpretation: This indicates a positive bias for the Indian market open, suggesting a potential gap-up for the Nifty 50 today.

FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers yesterday, injecting a substantial ₹6,837 crore into the Indian equity market. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), on the other hand, were net sellers, pulling out ₹3,679 crore. Over the last five sessions, FIIs have largely been net buyers, providing significant support to the market, while DIIs have shown a mixed trend with net selling in three of the last five sessions.

This could also indirectly support equity markets by improving corporate margins and consumer spending power.

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  • Global Reaction to US Midterm Elections: The ongoing analysis and implications of the Democratic victory in the US midterms will continue to shape global market sentiment. Markets typically react with volatility as they digest policy implications, especially for sectors like technology, energy, and commodities.
  • Crude Oil Price Movement: Continued softening of crude oil prices, as seen overnight, will be a key factor. A sustained decline is generally positive for India, easing inflationary pressures and improving trade balances. Conversely, a rebound could introduce headwinds.
  • FII/DII Flow Trends: The direction and magnitude of FII and DII flows will be closely watched. Consistent FII buying provides strong market support, while sustained DII selling could indicate profit-booking or reallocation.
  • Corporate Actions: Today's ex-dividend and ex-bonus dates for several companies, including GODREJCP, COALINDIA, and ASIANPAINT, will lead to price adjustments in these stocks. Investors typically factor these in, but high-volume trading around these dates can occur.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Global Cues: Monitor how global markets, particularly US and other Asian indices, react further to the US midterm election results and any new developments.
  2. GIFT Nifty's Open: Confirm if the positive bias indicated by GIFT Nifty translates into a strong gap-up open for the Nifty 50.
  3. Key Nifty Levels: Watch Nifty's ability to sustain above its CPR pivot (23853.98) and challenge the 24000 Call OI resistance. On the downside, 23500 Put OI support is crucial.
  4. FII Flows: Observe if FIIs continue their buying spree, as their sustained participation is vital for market momentum.
  5. Crude Oil Prices: Any significant movement in Brent crude prices will be important for sectors sensitive to oil, such as OMCs, aviation, and paints.
  6. Corporate Actions Impact: Keep an eye on stocks like COALINDIA and ASIANPAINT as they trade ex-dividend, and CONCORDBIO for its ex-bonus.

Bottom line

The Indian market is poised for a potentially positive open, largely driven by favourable global cues from the US and a strong signal from GIFT Nifty. The continued robust buying by FIIs provides a strong underlying support. However, traders should remain cautious of the 24000 level on Nifty, which acts as a significant resistance, and monitor global reactions to the US midterm elections, especially for tech and AI-related sectors, which could introduce volatility.
