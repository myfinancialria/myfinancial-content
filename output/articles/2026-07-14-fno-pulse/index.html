Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. It provides a snapshot of the total number of positions currently held by market participants.

The strike with the highest Call Open Interest (OI) often acts as a potential resistance level. This is because a large number of call options have been sold (or "written") at this strike, indicating that option writers generally do not expect the underlying index to rise significantly above this level. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put Open Interest (OI) typically functions as a potential support level. Here, many put options have been written, suggesting that option writers do not anticipate the index falling below this point.

For Nifty 50, the heaviest Call OI is observed at 24,100, which can be seen as a potential ceiling. The heaviest Put OI, acting as a potential floor, is at 24,050. Today's fresh Call writing was concentrated near 24,100, suggesting that call sellers expect the index to remain capped around this level. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing was notably seen near 24,000, indicating that put sellers anticipate this level to act as a strong base.

On the Sensex, today's heaviest fresh Call writing was observed near 78,200, implying that call sellers are positioning for the index to face resistance around this mark. The aggregate flow for the day showed a significantly larger build-up in Call OI (10.7 million contracts) compared to Put OI (0.8 million contracts), reinforcing the view of stronger resistance being built on the upside.

For Bank Nifty, fresh Call writing was most significant near 58,166, suggesting that option writers expect this level to act as a potential cap. In contrast, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 56,433, indicating that put sellers are establishing a potential floor for the index around this area. The heaviest Put OI wall is further up at 59,000, which could provide strong support.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that compares the total number of outstanding put options to call options. It can be calculated based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) or based on the change in Open Interest for the day (PCR(today flow ΔOI)). Generally, a PCR above 1 suggests a higher number of put options compared to call options, often interpreted as a bullish sentiment by option writers who expect prices to rise (as they sell puts). A PCR below 1, conversely, suggests more call options, often interpreted as a bearish sentiment. A PCR around 1 indicates a relatively balanced market.

For Nifty 50, the PCR(OI) stands at 1.0, indicating a balanced options book overall. Today's flow ΔPCR, at -0.19, suggests a mixed or balanced fresh writing activity for the day, without a strong directional bias from new positions.

The Sensex has a PCR(OI) of 0.72, which leans towards more call writing and is typically seen as a mildly bearish signal. Today's flow ΔPCR is 0.07, further indicating that fresh writing activity during the day was predominantly call-heavy, reinforcing the cautious outlook.

For Bank Nifty, the PCR(OI) is 0.81, also suggesting a slight tilt towards more call writing. However, today's flow ΔPCR is -0.33, which points to a mixed or balanced fresh writing activity for the day, not strongly reinforcing the overall OI bias.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (sellers) would collectively experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that particular price. It's often viewed as a magnetic point towards which the index might gravitate, especially as the expiry date approaches, as option writers collectively try to minimise their overall losses.

For Nifty 50, the Max Pain point is at 24,050, which is almost exactly where the index closed today. This suggests that option writers are currently positioned for the index to expire very close to its current levels, implying a tight expiry range.

The Sensex Max Pain is at 77,600, which is approximately 0.7% above today's spot close. This indicates that the "pin pull" or magnetic effect for Sensex is currently pointing upwards, suggesting a potential move towards this level.

Bank Nifty has its Max Pain at 58,500, which is a more significant 1.8% above today's spot close. This substantial difference suggests a notable upward pull for Bank Nifty towards its Max Pain point as expiry nears.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret market movements more effectively. To begin, you can regularly check the Open Interest data, particularly the option chain, available on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website or through your broker's platform. Look for the strike prices with the highest Call Open Interest and highest Put Open Interest, as these often indicate potential resistance and support levels, respectively. Also, pay attention to where fresh Call and Put writing is occurring, as this shows where new positions are being established and where option sellers expect prices to be capped or supported.

Additionally, calculate or find the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) based on both total Open Interest and today's change in Open Interest. This helps gauge overall market sentiment and the immediate flow of new positions. Keep an eye on the "Max Pain" level, as this can act as a magnetic point for the index, especially as expiry approaches. Finally, tracking the Foreign Institutional Investor (FII) derivative positioning, which is often available in daily participant-wise Open Interest reports on the NSE website, can offer insights into the sentiment of large institutional money. By combining these different data points, you can develop a more holistic understanding of the market's underlying structure and potential short-term biases.
