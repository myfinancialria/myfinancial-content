What moved the market — the factors

The Indian market witnessed a strong upward trajectory today, primarily driven by a confluence of positive factors. A significant drop in crude oil prices provided a tailwind, easing inflation concerns and boosting sentiment. Strong corporate earnings reported for the second quarter further bolstered investor confidence, suggesting a healthy underlying economic performance. Positive global cues also contributed to the bullish mood. Crucially, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned significant net buyers, injecting ₹3,599 crore into the market, which provided substantial liquidity and buying pressure. The broad market rally was evident in the strong market breadth, with advances significantly outnumbering declines. Heavyweight stocks like Reliance Industries Ltd. also saw substantial gains, contributing to the overall index surge.

Market breadth

The market breadth today was overwhelmingly positive, with 319 advances against just 76 declines out of a total of 395 traded stocks. This indicates a broad-based rally across the market, suggesting strong underlying buying sentiment.

FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were significant net buyers today, infusing ₹3,599 crore into the Indian equity market. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers, pulling out ₹818 crore.

Looking at the 5-day trend, FIIs have shown renewed interest in Indian equities, being net buyers for three out of the last five sessions, including today's substantial inflow. This suggests a potential shift towards increased foreign investment, which is a positive sign for the market. DII flows have been more mixed, balancing out some of the FII activity.

Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's session was primarily driven by broad positive flows and favourable global cues rather than a single defining event. The significant fall in crude oil prices provided a strong boost to market sentiment, easing concerns about inflation and corporate input costs. This was further amplified by positive global market signals and robust corporate earnings reports for the second quarter, which painted an optimistic picture of the domestic economy. The substantial net buying by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) also played a crucial role in propelling the indices higher across the board.

Setup for tomorrow

Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will continue to be a key determinant. The overnight session will see attention on the US midterm election outcomes and their potential impact on sectors like defence, healthcare, crypto, and AI stocks. Commodity prices, especially crude, will remain in focus after today's significant drop (Brent Crude at $104.15, WTI Crude at $96.09). The Dollar Index closed lower at 101.75, while Gold and Silver saw gains, indicating some risk-off sentiment or inflation hedging. The US 10-year yield also softened to 4.89%. Domestically, investors will continue to monitor corporate earnings announcements and the sustainability of FII inflows. Key levels and broader market sentiment will likely be influenced by these global and domestic factors.
