What this means in plain terms

Tata Consultancy Services (TCS), one of India's largest IT services companies, has kicked off the financial year 2025 on a positive note. The company announced its results for the April-June quarter, showing a healthy increase in both its earnings and profitability. This performance indicates a resilient demand for IT services despite global economic uncertainties.

A key highlight for investors is the interim dividend of ₹12 per share declared by the company's board. This means that for every share of TCS an investor owns, they will receive ₹12 as a payout from the company's profits. Such dividends are a way for companies to share their success with shareholders and can be an important component of an investor's total returns. Investors should also be aware of the tax implications of dividend income, which is now fully taxable in the hands of the recipient as per their income tax slab rates.

Q1 FY25 Performance: Exceeding Expectations

TCS reported a strong financial performance for the first quarter of FY25, surpassing analyst expectations on key metrics. The company's consolidated revenue for the quarter stood at ₹62,613 crore, reflecting a year-on-year (YoY) growth of 5.4%. In constant currency terms, the revenue growth was 4.4% YoY. This growth was driven by broad-based demand across various industries and markets.

The net profit for the quarter saw an even healthier rise, increasing by 7.7% YoY to ₹12,040 crore. This demonstrates the company's ability to maintain profitability amidst a dynamic business environment. The operating margin for Q1 FY25 was reported at 24.7%, indicating efficient operations and cost management. The net margin stood at 19.2%. These figures underscore TCS's operational excellence, even with the impact of annual wage increments during the quarter.

Segmental and Geographical Highlights

The growth in Q1 FY25 was notably broad-based, with emerging markets leading the charge. India, Latin America, and the MEA (Middle East and Africa) region registered double-digit growth. India, in particular, showcased remarkable growth of 61.8%. All major markets returned to sequential growth, signaling a positive shift in client spending.

From an industry perspective, the Manufacturing sector led the growth with a 9.4% constant currency growth year-on-year. This was followed by Energy, Resources & Utilities (5.7%) and Life Sciences & Healthcare (4%). While some segments like BFSI, Communication & Media, and Technology & Services contracted in the June quarter, the overall picture remained positive due to strong performance in other areas. Services related to AI, Cloud, and Cybersecurity continued to be key growth drivers, with TCS actively expanding its capabilities and client engagements in these areas. The company's AI deal pipeline grew significantly to $1.5 billion, up from $900 million in the previous quarter.

Dividend Declaration and Tax Implications

The Board of Directors of TCS recommended an interim dividend of ₹12 per equity share. The record date for this dividend has been fixed as July 20, 2024, and the payment date is August 5, 2024. This interim dividend reflects the company's healthy financial position and its commitment to returning value to shareholders.

From a tax perspective, dividend income declared, distributed, or paid on or after April 1, 2020, is fully taxable in the hands of the shareholders. This means that the ₹12 per share dividend received will be added to your total income and taxed according to your applicable income tax slab rates. For resident shareholders, if the total dividend income from a company exceeds ₹5,000 in a financial year, the company is required to deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) at a rate of 10%. If a valid PAN is not provided, the TDS rate increases to 20%. Non-resident Indians (NRIs) typically face a TDS rate of 20% (plus surcharge and cess), unless a lower rate is specified under a Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA). Shareholders can claim a deduction for interest expenditure incurred to earn dividend income, limited to 20% of the total dividend income. No other expenses, such as brokerage or commission, are deductible.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 42, ₹35L CTC, Mumbai, who holds 500 shares of TCS. With the interim dividend declared at ₹12 per share, Priya will receive a total dividend of: 500 shares * ₹12/share = ₹6,000.

Since her dividend income of ₹6,000 exceeds ₹5,000 in the financial year, TCS will deduct TDS at 10% on this amount. TDS deducted = 10% of ₹6,000 = ₹600. Priya will receive ₹6,000 - ₹600 = ₹5,400 in her bank account.

When Priya files her Income Tax Return, this ₹6,000 dividend income will be added to her total income and taxed at her applicable slab rate. Assuming Priya falls into the 30% tax bracket (excluding cess and surcharge for simplicity), her tax liability on this dividend would be: Tax on dividend = 30% of ₹6,000 = ₹1,800. Since ₹600 has already been deducted as TDS, Priya will have to pay an additional ₹1,200 (₹1,800 - ₹600) as tax on this dividend income. The TDS amount will be reflected in her Form 26AS or Annual Information Statement (AIS) and can be adjusted against her final tax liability.

FAQ

Is dividend income taxable in India?

Yes, dividend income declared, distributed, or paid on or after April 1, 2020, is fully taxable in the hands of the shareholders as per their applicable income tax slab rates.

What is the record date for a dividend?

The record date is the specific date set by a company to determine which shareholders are eligible to receive the declared dividend. Only those holding shares on or before the record date will receive the dividend.

Is TDS applicable on dividend income?

Yes, for resident shareholders, TDS at 10% is applicable if the total dividend income from a company exceeds ₹5,000 in a financial year. For non-residents, a 20% TDS rate applies, subject to DTAA provisions.

Can I claim expenses against dividend income?

You can claim a deduction for interest expenditure incurred to earn dividend income, limited to 20% of the total dividend income. No other expenses like brokerage or commission are allowed as deductions.

How do corporate results impact my investments?

Corporate results provide insights into a company's financial health, growth prospects, and management efficiency. Strong results often lead to positive market sentiment and potential stock price appreciation, while weak results can have the opposite effect. They help investors make informed decisions.

What is constant currency growth?

Constant currency growth measures a company's revenue growth by excluding the impact of currency fluctuations. This provides a clearer picture of the underlying business performance, unaffected by exchange rate movements.
