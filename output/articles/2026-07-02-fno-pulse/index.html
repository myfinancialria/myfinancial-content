India F&O Pulse — 02 Jul 2026: what the day's options & futures positioning is whispering

Options Data Signals Broadly Bullish Undercurrent for Nifty and Sensex

TL;DR

  • Nifty 50: Today's options data, particularly fresh Put writing and aggregate Put Open Interest build-up, suggests a bullish bias for tomorrow's session, with strong support forming below current levels.
  • Sensex: The options positioning indicates a mildly bullish outlook, with Put writers establishing a floor, though Call writers are also present, suggesting a potential cap on immediate upside.
  • Bank Nifty: While some signals like Max Pain point upwards, a notable increase in Call writing and a call-heavy fresh Put-Call Ratio suggest a mixed to mildly cautious sentiment for the banking index.

What the indices did today

The Nifty 50 closed the day at 24,175.70, marking a gain of 0.71%. The index ended comfortably above its heaviest Put Open Interest (OI) wall at 24,000, suggesting this level could act as a strong support.

The Sensex advanced by 0.75%, settling at 77,502.12. It closed right at its heaviest Call OI wall of 77,500, indicating that this level might pose immediate resistance, while a strong Put base at 77,400 provided support.

The Bank Nifty remained largely unchanged, closing at 58,031.65. It ended just above its heaviest Put OI at 58,000, which could serve as a floor, but significantly below the major Call wall at 60,000, leaving room for movement within this broader range.

Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) refers to the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options, that have not yet been settled. When we look at the option chain, the strike price with the highest Call OI represents a level where many Call options have been written (sold). Call writers typically expect the market to stay below this strike, making it a potential resistance level. Conversely, the strike with the highest Put OI indicates a level where many Put options have been written. Put writers generally anticipate the market will not fall below this strike, thus forming a potential support level.

Today's data provides interesting insights into these levels. For the Nifty 50, the heaviest Put OI is observed at 24,000, acting as a robust floor, while the highest Call OI stands at 25,000, indicating a significant ceiling for the current expiry. Fresh Put writing, meaning new Put options being sold, was notably concentrated near 24,150, suggesting that Put sellers believe this area will hold as support. This was further reinforced by an aggregate net Put OI build of 32.3 million contracts, significantly higher than the 5.6 million Call OI build, pointing to a strong bullish sentiment from option writers.

On the Sensex, the heaviest Put OI is at 77,400, providing a close support, with the heaviest Call OI at 77,500, acting as an immediate resistance. Fresh Put writing was seen near 77,400, indicating conviction from Put sellers at this level. However, fresh Call writing also emerged near 77,500, suggesting that Call sellers are attempting to cap the upside.

For the Bank Nifty, the 58,000 strike holds the heaviest Put OI, establishing a near-term support. The most significant Call OI is much higher at 60,000, suggesting a broader range. Today, fresh Call writing was observed near 58,533, indicating that Call sellers anticipate a cap around this level. Meanwhile, fresh Put writing near 57,633 suggests some support building lower down.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a crucial sentiment indicator calculated by dividing the total Open Interest of Put options by the total Open Interest of Call options. A PCR value above 1 typically suggests that more Put options are being written than Call options, which is often interpreted as a bullish signal because Put writers expect the market to rise or stay stable. Conversely, a PCR below 1 indicates more Call writing, which can be seen as a bearish signal.

For the Nifty 50, the PCR based on total Open Interest (PCR(OI)) stands at 1.2, which suggests a balanced to mildly bullish book overall. More significantly, today's flow of fresh Open Interest (ΔOI) resulted in a PCR of 5.78, indicating a substantial amount of fresh Put writing compared to Call writing. This strong put-heavy flow suggests that option writers are predominantly betting on the Nifty finding support and potentially moving higher.

The Sensex recorded a PCR(OI) of 1.16, also pointing to a balanced book with a slight bullish tilt. Its PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR) was 1.33, signifying that fresh writing was skewed towards Puts. This indicates that option writers are establishing support and anticipate the index to remain stable or ascend.

In contrast, the Bank Nifty's PCR(OI) is 0.79, which is below 1, suggesting a slightly bearish bias from the overall outstanding positions. The PCR based on today's flow (ΔPCR) was even lower at 0.54, indicating that fresh writing for the day was heavily concentrated in Call options. This call-heavy flow suggests that Call writers are actively capping the upside for the Bank Nifty.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers collectively face the least amount of financial loss if the underlying index were to expire at that specific price. It's often considered a "magnet" because the market sometimes tends to gravitate towards this level as expiry approaches, as option writers may try to influence the price towards this point to minimise their potential payouts.

For the Nifty 50, the Max Pain point is currently at 24,000, which is about 0.7% below today's spot price. This suggests a potential downward pull towards this level if the expiry were imminent.

The Sensex's Max Pain is at 77,400, which is very close to its current spot price. This indicates that option writers are positioned for the index to consolidate around these levels.

For the Bank Nifty, the Max Pain point is significantly higher at 58,600, about 1.0% above its current spot price. This suggests a potential upward pull or a target for option writers if the expiry were drawing near. It's worth noting that the magnetic effect of Max Pain typically strengthens as the expiry date gets closer.

FII positioning — what the big money did today

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) play a significant role in the Indian derivatives market, and their positioning can offer insights into broader market sentiment. Today, FIIs held a very low long ratio in Index futures, standing at just 9.3% (29,443 long contracts versus 286,343 short contracts). Historically, such a low long ratio in index futures has often coincided with a cautious or bearish outlook from FIIs.

In Stock futures, their long ratio was 53.8% (3,804,985 long contracts versus 3,270,416 short contracts), indicating a more balanced to mildly bullish stance on individual stocks compared to the indices.

Stocks where money moved today

In the F&O watchlist, several stocks saw notable movements today. Among the top gainers, Infosys (INFY) surged by 5.64% to ₹1040.90, accompanied by significantly high trading volume of over 1.87 crore shares. TCS also saw a strong gain of 4.31%, closing at ₹2068.10 with a volume of over 60 lakh shares. Other notable gainers included Titan (+1.88%), ICICI Bank (+1.46%), and Hindustan Unilever (+1.31%).

On the losing side, L&T (LT) dipped by 0.81% to ₹4059.40. Axis Bank declined by 0.45%, while Reliance Industries saw a marginal drop of 0.34%. Maruti and Kotak Bank also ended the day slightly lower, with declines of 0.33% and 0.29% respectively. These movements reflect shifts in participant interest within specific F&O stocks.

What this combination historically tends to mean for tomorrow

Synthesising today's options data and FII positioning presents a nuanced picture. For the Nifty 50, the strong put-heavy fresh writing, robust Put base, and overall bullish bias from the 8-vote check suggest that option writers are positioning for support to hold and potentially for upward momentum. Historically, when such strong Put writing activity is observed, it has tended to coincide with the index finding a floor and attempting to move higher in the subsequent session.

The Sensex also shows a mildly bullish inclination from the options data, with fresh Put writing establishing support. However, the presence of fresh Call writing at 77,500 suggests that immediate upside might be capped. Historically, this kind of setup has often led to a range-bound movement or a struggle for the index to break decisively above immediate resistance levels.

Bank Nifty, on the other hand, presents a more cautious outlook. While Max Pain points upwards, the call-heavy fresh writing and a PCR(OI) below 1 indicate that Call writers are actively trying to cap any upward moves. Historically, when fresh Call writing dominates, the index has tended to face resistance and may consolidate or see some downward pressure.

A key counter-signal comes from the FIIs, whose significantly low long ratio in index futures historically tends to coincide with a cautious or bearish stance from these large participants. When strong bullish signals from index options are met with such a bearish FII index futures positioning, the market has historically tended to experience periods of consolidation or a struggle to sustain strong directional moves, as the "big money" may be betting against the broader option writer sentiment.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals daily can empower you to interpret market sentiment. To read this yourself, you would typically start by looking at the Open Interest (OI) data for Call and Put options, which is readily available on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Option Chain" section. Your brokerage platform may also provide this data.

Focus on identifying the strike prices with the highest Call OI and highest Put OI, as these represent potential resistance and support levels, respectively. Next, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing the total Put OI by the total Call OI; a PCR above 1 suggests bullishness, while below 1 indicates bearishness. Also, observe the "Max Pain" strike, which some platforms calculate, as it can act as a magnetic point. Finally, track the FII derivative positioning data, also available on the NSE website (under "Participant-wise Open Interest"), to gauge how institutional investors are positioned in index and stock futures. Combining these data points can provide a holistic view of the market's underlying sentiment and potential movement.
