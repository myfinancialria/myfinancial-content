NSC for NRIs: Can Non-Resident Indians Invest and Claim Tax Benefits?

TL;DR

  • Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are generally not eligible to open new National Savings Certificate (NSC) accounts.
  • If an individual holds an NSC account and subsequently becomes an NRI, they cannot renew it or open a new one.
  • For existing NSCs held by individuals who became NRIs, the account can be continued until maturity.
  • However, NRIs cannot claim Section 80C tax deductions for investments made in NSCs after acquiring NRI status.
  • The interest earned on NSC for NRIs is fully taxable in India and is generally subject to TDS.

What this means in plain terms

The National Savings Certificate (NSC) is a popular small savings scheme backed by the Government of India, primarily designed for resident Indians. It offers a fixed interest rate and qualifies for tax deductions under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act for resident investors. Many NRIs, especially those looking for safe investment avenues in India, often wonder if they can also invest in NSCs and avail similar benefits.

The clear answer is that NRIs are not permitted to open new NSC accounts. The scheme is specifically structured for resident individuals. If you held an NSC while you were a resident Indian and then moved abroad, your existing certificate remains valid until its maturity. However, the tax benefits associated with it change significantly once you become a Non-Resident Indian.

Understanding National Savings Certificates (NSC)

The National Savings Certificate (NSC) is a fixed-income investment scheme that encourages small savings and offers tax benefits. Issued by the Department of Posts, it is considered a secure investment option as it is backed by the Government of India.

Key Features of NSC

  • Minimum Investment: Investments in NSC start at ₹1,000, with no upper limit.
  • Tenure: NSCs typically have a fixed maturity period, often 5 years.
  • Interest Rate: The interest rate is fixed by the Ministry of Finance quarterly and compounded annually, but paid at maturity.
  • Tax Benefits (for Residents): For resident investors, the amount invested in NSC qualifies for a deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, up to the prescribed limit. The interest accrued annually is also deemed reinvested and thus eligible for Section 80C deduction, except for the interest in the final year.
  • Premature Encashment: Premature encashment of an NSC is generally not allowed before maturity, except under specific circumstances such as the death of the account holder, forfeiture by a pledgee, or a court order.

Can NRIs Invest in National Savings Certificates?

A common query among Non-Resident Indians is regarding their eligibility to invest in various Indian savings schemes. For National Savings Certificates, the rules are quite specific.

New Investments by NRIs

NRIs are explicitly not allowed to open new National Savings Certificate (NSC) accounts. The scheme is designed for resident individuals in India. This restriction applies whether the NRI intends to invest using NRE (Non-Resident External) or NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) funds.

Existing NSCs Held by Individuals Who Became NRIs

If an individual held an NSC account while they were a resident Indian and subsequently changed their residential status to NRI, the existing NSC can continue until its maturity. Upon maturity, the proceeds can be credited to their NRO account. However, such an individual cannot open a new NSC account or renew the existing one upon maturity.

This distinction is crucial: while existing investments are protected, the opportunity for fresh investment in NSC ceases upon acquiring NRI status.

Tax Implications for NRIs Holding Existing NSCs

For NRIs who hold existing NSC accounts opened before they became non-resident, the tax treatment differs significantly from that for resident Indians.

Loss of Section 80C Benefits

One of the primary attractions of NSC for resident Indians is the tax deduction available under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act. However, once an individual becomes an NRI, they are no longer eligible to claim this deduction for the NSC investment. This applies even if the investment was made when they were a resident. The benefit of Section 80C is tied to the residential status at the time of claiming the deduction, and NRIs generally cannot claim most deductions available to residents.

Taxation of Interest Income

The interest earned on an NSC held by an NRI is fully taxable in India. This interest income is typically treated as "income from other sources" and is subject to the applicable income tax slab rates for NRIs. Furthermore, the interest income is generally subject to Tax Deducted at Source (TDS). The bank or post office where the NSC is held will deduct TDS at the prevailing rates before crediting the interest or maturity amount. NRIs can claim credit for this TDS when filing their income tax return in India.

It's important for NRIs to understand their tax obligations both in India and in their country of residence, as they may also be liable to pay tax on this income in their country of residence, depending on the tax laws and any Double Taxation Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) between India and that country.

A real example

Meet Suresh, 42, an IT professional earning ₹60 lakhs per annum, based in Dubai since 2020. Before moving to Dubai, in 2019, while he was a resident Indian in Bengaluru, Suresh invested ₹5 lakhs in a 5-year NSC. He claimed the ₹5 lakh deduction under Section 80C in his Income Tax Return for the Assessment Year 2020-21.

Now, in 2024, his NSC is maturing. Let's assume the interest rate was 7.7% compounded annually.

Original Investment: ₹5,00,000 Maturity Value (approx. for 5 years at 7.7%): ₹7,25,000 (roughly, actual calculation would be precise)

When Suresh invested in 2019, he was a resident, so he claimed the Section 80C deduction. However, for the subsequent years (2020-21, 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24) when he was an NRI, he cannot claim any further Section 80C deduction for the accrued interest that is deemed reinvested.

Upon maturity in 2024, the entire maturity amount, including the accumulated interest, will be paid out. The interest component of this maturity amount will be fully taxable in India. For instance, if the total interest earned over 5 years is ₹2,25,000, this entire amount will be added to Suresh's taxable income in India for the financial year 2023-24 (when it matures). The post office will deduct TDS on this interest income at the applicable rates before transferring the funds to Suresh's NRO account.

Suresh also cannot open a new NSC account now that he is an NRI, nor can he renew his existing one.

What to do this week

  1. Verify your NRI status: Confirm your residential status as per the Income Tax Act to understand your eligibility for various investment products.
  2. Review existing Indian investments: Check if you hold any NSCs or similar small savings schemes that you opened as a resident Indian.
  3. Understand maturity procedures: If you have an existing NSC nearing maturity, contact your post office or bank to understand the process for encashment and crediting funds to your NRO account.
  4. Consult a tax advisor: If you hold existing NSCs as an NRI, speak with a financial planner or tax consultant familiar with NRI taxation to understand your specific tax liabilities in India and your country of residence.
  5. Explore alternative NRI-eligible investments: Research other investment options available for NRIs in India, such as NRE/NRO Fixed Deposits, NCDs, or mutual funds, which are specifically designed for non-residents.

FAQ

Can an NRI open a new NSC account?

No, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are generally not eligible to open new National Savings Certificate (NSC) accounts. The scheme is designed for resident individuals.

What happens to an NSC if I become an NRI after investing?

If you invested in an NSC as a resident Indian and then became an NRI, your existing NSC will continue until its maturity. You cannot open a new one or renew it upon maturity.

Can NRIs claim Section 80C benefits for NSC?

No, NRIs cannot claim tax deductions under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act for investments in NSC, even if the investment was made when they were a resident. The deduction is available only to resident Indians.

Is the interest earned on NSC taxable for NRIs?

Yes, the interest earned on an NSC held by an NRI is fully taxable in India. It is usually subject to Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) at the prevailing rates.

Where can the maturity proceeds of an NRI's NSC be credited?

Upon maturity, the proceeds from an NRI's NSC can typically be credited to their Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) account in India.

Are there any specific rules for premature encashment of NSC for NRIs?

The rules for premature encashment are generally the same for NRIs as for residents, allowed only under specific circumstances like the death of the account holder or a court order.
