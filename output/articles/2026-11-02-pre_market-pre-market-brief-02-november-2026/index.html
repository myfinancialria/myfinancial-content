Pre-Market Brief — 02 November 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets saw a significant tech sell-off in the US, while Asian markets were mixed.
  • GIFT Nifty is signalling a strong gap-up open for the Indian benchmark indices.
  • FIIs were strong net buyers yesterday, injecting ₹3,599 crore, while DIIs were net sellers.
  • India's IPO market achieved a record ₹84,400 crore fundraising in H1 FY27, indicating robust investor appetite.
  • Nifty's immediate resistance to watch is around the 23400 level, with strong support at 23000.
  • The primary risk for today could be potential profit-booking after a strong open, especially if global tech weakness persists.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Overnight, US markets closed sharply lower, led by a significant sell-off in technology stocks, with the Nasdaq declining nearly 3%. European markets remained largely flat. In Asia this morning, indices are showing a mixed trend, with Shanghai and Hang Seng in positive territory, while Nikkei dipped slightly.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, with USD/INR trading at 94.93, down 0.23%. The Dollar Index also saw a slight dip of 0.29% to 101.58. In commodities, crude oil prices witnessed a significant fall, with Brent Crude down 7.70% to $108.58 and WTI Crude falling 4.25% to $99.69. Gold prices edged up 0.21% to $3907.20, while Silver surged 2.59% to $50.37. The US 10-Year Treasury Yield decreased by 1.20% to 4.93%.

Interpretation: A stronger Rupee is generally positive for the Indian economy, easing import costs. The sharp decline in crude oil prices is a significant tailwind for India, a major oil importer, potentially benefiting sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paints, and aviation by reducing input costs. A falling US 10-year yield typically makes emerging markets, including India, more attractive for foreign capital.

GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling

The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 23364.50, up 1.21% from its previous close. Interpretation: This indicates a likely gap-up open for the Nifty 50 today, suggesting a positive start to the trading session.

Yesterday, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers in the Indian cash market, infusing ₹3,599 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, were net sellers, pulling out ₹818 crore.

  • Highest PUT OI (support): 23000 (OI 15,726,815), 23200 (OI 13,646,425), 23300 (OI 13,083,265)

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  • Bank of England (BoE) Interest Rate Expectations: Goldman Sachs expects the BoE to potentially raise rates again in February.

    • How markets usually react: Global central bank actions, especially rate hikes, can influence global liquidity and FII flows into emerging markets. Higher rates in developed economies can sometimes make them more attractive, potentially drawing capital away from markets like India, though the immediate impact depends on the quantum and forward guidance.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Nifty's Open and Initial Price Action: Given the strong GIFT Nifty signal, watch if Nifty can sustain the gap-up open and hold above the 23300-23400 resistance zone.
  2. Crude Oil Impact: Monitor stocks in sectors sensitive to crude oil prices, such as OMCs (HPCL, BPCL, IOC), paint companies (Asian Paints, Berger Paints), and aviation (IndiGo, SpiceJet), for positive reactions to the sharp fall in crude.
  3. FII Flow Momentum: Observe if FIIs continue their net buying streak, which has been a key support for the market.
  4. Global Tech Sentiment: Keep an eye on Indian IT stocks, as they might react to the overnight tech sell-off in the US, despite a positive domestic open.
  5. Corporate Action Stocks: Stocks like TANLA, ROUTE, PITTIENG, and COLPAL will trade ex-dividend; their prices will adjust, and traders might see increased activity.
  6. IPO Market Buzz: The record IPO fundraising news could keep primary market sentiment buoyant, potentially benefiting recently listed companies or those in the IPO pipeline like South Eastern Coalfields.

Bottom line

Indian markets are poised for a gap-up open, largely driven by positive domestic cues and a strong GIFT Nifty, despite a cautious global backdrop from the US tech sell-off. The sharp decline in crude oil prices is a significant positive for India. The ability of Nifty to hold above the 23300-23400 zone will be crucial for sustaining momentum. Investors should remain watchful of global tech trends and FII flows throughout the session.
