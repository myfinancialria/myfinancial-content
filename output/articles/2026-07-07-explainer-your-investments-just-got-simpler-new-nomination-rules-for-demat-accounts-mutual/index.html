Your Investments Just Got Simpler: New Nomination Rules for Demat Accounts & Mutual Funds

TL;DR

  • SEBI has updated rules for nominating beneficiaries for your Demat accounts and mutual funds.
  • From September 1, 2026, if you open a new single-holder account, you must nominate someone or formally opt out.
  • This makes sure your money goes to your chosen family easily, reducing unclaimed funds.
  • The process is now simpler: less paperwork, fewer details needed, and easier online options.
  • Joint accounts still have optional nomination, but single accounts will now have it as a default.

What's this about?

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), our market regulator, has recently made some important changes to how you nominate beneficiaries for your Demat accounts and mutual fund investments. A Demat account is where your shares, bonds, and other securities are held electronically, much like a bank account holds your money. Mutual funds are investment schemes that pool money from many investors to invest in stocks, bonds, or other assets.

These new rules are all about making it easier for your loved ones to get your investments if something happens to you. It helps ensure your hard-earned money doesn't get stuck or become "unclaimed" after you're gone.

Why it's in the news right now

SEBI issued a circular on May 29, 2026, outlining these revised nomination rules. The most crucial part is that these new rules will come into effect from September 1, 2026. This means that financial institutions like depository participants (where you open Demat accounts) and mutual fund houses are now preparing to implement these changes, and investors need to be aware of what's coming. The changes simplify a previous framework that faced operational challenges.

How it actually works

Here’s a breakdown of the key changes:

  1. Mandatory Nomination for New Single Accounts: If you open a new Demat account or mutual fund folio (an account for your mutual fund investments) as a single holder on or after September 1, 2026, you must either provide nomination details or formally declare that you choose not to nominate anyone. You can't just leave it blank anymore.
  2. Optional for Joint Accounts: For accounts held jointly by two or more people, nomination will continue to be optional.
  3. Simplified Process: SEBI has made the nomination process much simpler. You now need to provide fewer mandatory details for your nominee, typically just their name and relationship with you. If the nominee is a minor, their date of birth is also required. Other details like contact information or KYC particulars are now optional.
  4. No Witness for Physical Forms: If you submit a physical nomination form with your regular signature, you no longer need a witness signature. A witness is only required if you use a thumb impression instead of a signature.
  5. Online Options Expanded: You can now submit nominations online using various methods like a Digital Signature Certificate (DSC), Aadhaar-based e-sign, or two-factor authentication (like an OTP).
  6. Up to Three Nominees: You can nominate up to three individuals for your Demat account or mutual fund folio. If you name multiple nominees but don't specify their share, the investments will be divided equally among them by default.
  7. Regular Reminders: Depository participants and mutual fund registrars will send you bi-annual reminders (twice a year) via email and SMS if you haven't nominated anyone or formally opted out. They will also show pop-up messages on their apps or websites.
  8. Supersedes Old Rules: This new circular replaces all previous SEBI circulars related to nominations in Demat accounts and mutual fund folios, creating a single, clearer framework.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine your Demat account or mutual fund folio is like your personal locker at a bank, holding your valuable documents (investments). Previously, you could choose to leave a note inside saying who should get the contents if something happened to you (nomination), or you could just leave it empty.

Now, with the new rules, when you get a new locker (open a new single account) from September 1, 2026, the bank manager will hand you a form and say, "Please tell us who should get the contents if you're not around, or sign here to confirm you don't want to name anyone." You can't just ignore the form anymore. This makes sure your chosen person knows exactly where to go and what to do, without any confusion or delay.

Why it matters to you

  1. Easier Transfer of Wealth: The biggest benefit is that your investments can be transferred smoothly to your chosen nominees after you're gone. This avoids lengthy legal processes and ensures your family gets access to your assets quickly.
  2. Reduces Unclaimed Assets: Many investments end up unclaimed because nominees aren't named or the process is unclear. These new rules aim to significantly reduce the amount of money lying idle with financial institutions.
  3. Simplified Process: The reduced paperwork and expanded digital options make it much easier and less time-consuming to add or update nominees.
  4. Clarity for Single Holders: If you invest alone, you now have a clear path to either nominate or explicitly opt out, removing any ambiguity about your wishes.
  5. Flexibility to Change: You can add, modify, or cancel your nominations any number of times.

Common confusions cleared up

  • Myth: Nomination is now mandatory for all my existing Demat accounts and mutual funds.

    • Fact: The mandatory nomination (or opt-out) applies to new* single-holder Demat accounts and mutual fund folios opened on or after September 1, 2026. For your existing accounts, while it's highly recommended to nominate, the explicit mandatory rule with an opt-out declaration is primarily for new accounts. However, regulated entities will send reminders for existing accounts without nominations.

  • Myth: I need a lawyer to help me with the nomination process.

    • * Fact: The process has been simplified. You can easily do it yourself, either online or by filling out a simple form provided by your depository participant or mutual fund house. You only need basic details of your nominee.

  • Myth: If I nominate someone, they automatically become the owner of my investments.

    • * Fact: A nominee is a custodian or trustee of your assets until the legal heirs are determined. While nomination makes the transfer process much smoother, the nominee holds the assets for the benefit of the legal heirs according to succession laws.

FAQ

What happens if I don't nominate and don't opt out for a new single account after September 1, 2026?

If you open a new single-holder Demat account or mutual fund folio after September 1, 2026, you will be required to either provide nomination details or submit an explicit opt-out declaration. You cannot proceed without choosing one of these options.

Can I nominate a minor?

Yes, you can nominate a minor. In such a case, you will also need to provide the minor nominee's date of birth and details of a guardian who will manage the investments until the minor becomes an adult.

How many nominees can I add?

You can add up to three nominees for your Demat account or mutual fund folio.

Do I need to provide the percentage share for each nominee?

It's advisable to specify the percentage share for each nominee. If you don't, the investments will be divided equally among all named nominees by default.

Where can I find the official SEBI circular?

The official SEBI circular (SEBI/HO/OIAE/OIAE_IAD-3/P/CIR/2026/12676) dated May 29, 2026, is usually available on the SEBI website under the "Circulars" section.
