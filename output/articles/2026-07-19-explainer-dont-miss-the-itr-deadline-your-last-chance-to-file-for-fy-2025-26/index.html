Don't Miss the ITR Deadline: Your Last Chance to File for FY 2025-26!

TL;DR

  • The deadline to file your Income Tax Return (ITR) for Financial Year 2025-26 (Assessment Year 2026-27) is fast approaching.
  • Most salaried individuals and those with income from house property or other sources need to file by July 31, 2026.
  • Business owners and professionals (non-audit cases) have until August 31, 2026, this year.
  • Filing on time helps you avoid late fees, interest, and losing benefits like carrying forward losses.
  • This year's ITR is still under the old Income Tax Act, 1961, for income earned in FY 2025-26.

What's this about?

It's that time of the year again! Many of us need to file our Income Tax Return (ITR) for the financial year that ended on March 31, 2026 (this is called Assessment Year 2026-27). An ITR is simply a form you submit to the Income Tax Department, telling them about your income, the taxes you've already paid, and any deductions you're claiming. It's how you make sure you've paid the correct amount of tax to the government.

Why it's in the news right now

The main reason ITR filing is in the news is because the deadline for many individual taxpayers is July 31, 2026. With just a few weeks left, the Income Tax Department and financial experts are reminding everyone to file their returns on time. There have also been important updates regarding different filing deadlines for various taxpayer categories and new reporting requirements in the ITR forms for this year. Plus, while a new Income Tax Act, 2025, has come into effect from April 1, 2026, the ITR you're filing now (for income earned in FY 2025-26) is still governed by the older Income Tax Act, 1961.

How it actually works

  1. Gather Your Documents: Before you start, collect all necessary documents. This includes Form 16 (from your employer), Form 26AS (tax credit statement), Annual Information Statement (AIS), Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS), bank statements, investment proofs, and details of any other income.
  2. Choose the Right ITR Form: The Income Tax Department has different forms (ITR-1, ITR-2, ITR-3, ITR-4, etc.) depending on your income sources and total income.

    3. * ITR-1 (Sahaj): For resident individuals with total income up to ₹50 lakh from salary, one house property, other sources (like interest), and agricultural income up to ₹5,000. * ITR-2: For individuals and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) who don't have business or professional income but might have income from capital gains, more than one house property, foreign assets, or agricultural income above ₹5,000. * ITR-3: For individuals and HUFs who have income from business or profession. * ITR-4 (Sugam): For individuals, HUFs, and firms (not LLPs) with total income up to ₹50 lakh and income from business and profession, especially those opting for the presumptive taxation scheme.

  3. Calculate Your Income and Tax: Add up all your income, subtract eligible deductions (if you choose the old tax regime) or claim the standard deduction (under the new tax regime), and calculate your final tax liability. Remember, the new tax regime is now the default option.
  4. File Online: Most taxpayers file their ITR electronically through the Income Tax Department's e-filing portal. You'll need to log in, fill in the details, and submit the form.
  5. Verify Your Return: After filing, it's crucial to verify your ITR. This can be done online using Aadhaar OTP, net banking, or by sending a signed physical copy of the ITR-V (acknowledgement) to the Income Tax Department's CPC in Bengaluru. Your filing is not complete until it's verified.

A simple way to think about it

Imagine your household budget for the year. You keep track of all the money that comes in (your salary, interest from savings, maybe some rent from a spare room) and all the important expenses you have (like school fees, home loan repayments, or medical bills). At the end of the year, you sit down and put all this information on a form to show your family head (the government, in this case). This form helps them understand your financial picture and confirm that you've contributed your fair share to the family pot. That annual budget report is essentially your ITR!

Why it matters to you

  1. Avoid Penalties and Interest: Missing the deadline can lead to a late filing fee. If your total income is up to ₹5 lakh, the fee is ₹1,000. For incomes above ₹5 lakh, it's ₹5,000. You might also have to pay interest (1% per month) on any unpaid tax.
  2. Claim Refunds: If you've paid more tax than you owe (e.g., through TDS deductions), you'll only get your refund after filing your ITR.
  3. Carry Forward Losses: If you have capital losses (e.g., from selling shares) or business losses, you can carry them forward to future years to offset future gains, but only if you file your ITR on time.
  4. Loan and Visa Applications: Banks and embassies often ask for ITR copies as proof of income and financial stability when you apply for loans, credit cards, or visas.
  5. Compliance and Peace of Mind: Filing on time ensures you are tax compliant and avoids potential notices or scrutiny from the Income Tax Department.

Common confusions cleared up

  • Myth: The new Income Tax Act, 2025, means all tax rules have changed for this year's filing.

    • Fact: The new Income Tax Act, 2025, came into effect from April 1, 2026. However, the ITR you are filing now (for Financial Year 2025-26, which ended on March 31, 2026) is still governed by the provisions of the older Income Tax Act, 1961. The new Act will apply to income earned from FY 2026-27 onwards.

  • Myth: July 31st is the deadline for everyone.

    • Fact: While July 31, 2026, is the deadline for most salaried individuals and those filing ITR-1 and ITR-2, business owners and professionals (non-audit cases filing ITR-3 or ITR-4) have an extended deadline of August 31, 2026, this year. Taxpayers whose accounts need to be audited have even later deadlines.

  • Myth: If I miss the July 31st deadline, I can't file my ITR at all.

    • Fact: You can still file a "belated return" until December 31, 2026, but you will have to pay a late filing fee and interest. You also lose certain benefits like carrying forward losses.

FAQ

What is the new tax regime and is it mandatory?

The new tax regime, which offers lower tax rates without most deductions, is now the default option. However, you still have the choice to opt for the old tax regime if you prefer to claim various deductions like Section 80C, HRA, etc.

What is the tax-free income limit under the new tax regime for FY 2025-26?

Under the new tax regime for FY 2025-26, income up to ₹12 lakh can be effectively tax-free for resident individuals due to an enhanced rebate under Section 87A. For salaried individuals, with a standard deduction of ₹75,000, the effective tax-free income can go up to ₹12.75 lakh.

What are Form 26AS and AIS, and why are they important?

Form 26AS is your tax credit statement, showing all taxes deducted (TDS) or collected (TCS) against your PAN. The Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS) provide a more comprehensive view of your financial transactions, including interest, dividends, and mutual fund transactions. It's crucial to cross-check these with your ITR to ensure accuracy and avoid discrepancies.

Can I revise my ITR if I make a mistake?

Yes, if you discover an error after filing your original ITR, you can file a "revised return" up to March 31, 2027, for AY 2026-27.

What if I have income from futures and options (F&O) trading?

For AY 2026-27, the ITR forms require specific reporting of turnover and income from futures and options (F&O) trading. Ensure you accurately disclose these details.
