PPF for Children: Contributions & Tax Benefits for Parents

TL;DR

  • Parents can open a Public Provident Fund (PPF) account for their minor child, offering long-term savings and tax benefits.
  • The combined annual contribution from both parents to a minor's PPF account cannot exceed ₹1.5 lakh.
  • This ₹1.5 lakh limit includes any contributions made by the parent to their own PPF account for Section 80C deduction purposes.
  • Only the parent contributing the funds can claim the tax deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act.
  • The income generated from a minor's PPF account is clubbed with the parent's income, but the interest itself is tax-exempt.

What this means in plain terms

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a popular long-term savings scheme in India, known for its attractive interest rates, tax-free returns, and sovereign guarantee. Many parents consider opening a PPF account for their children to build a corpus for their future needs, such as higher education or marriage. This is permitted by the government, allowing a parent or a legal guardian to open and operate a PPF account on behalf of a minor.

A key aspect often misunderstood is how contributions and tax deductions work when both parents are involved. While both parents can contribute money to their child's PPF account, the total amount deposited in a single minor's account in a financial year cannot exceed ₹1.5 lakh. This ceiling is crucial because it also impacts how each parent can claim tax benefits under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act. The deduction is available to the parent who makes the contribution, but the overall limit for the minor's account remains ₹1.5 lakh.

Understanding Minor PPF Accounts

A PPF account for a minor is essentially a long-term savings vehicle opened by a parent or legal guardian for a child under 18 years of age. It operates under the same rules as a regular PPF account, including its 15-year maturity period and tax-exempt interest. The primary objective is to accumulate a significant corpus over time, leveraging the power of compounding and tax benefits.

Who can open and operate it?

Only one PPF account can be opened per minor child. This account must be opened by either the father or the mother, or a legal guardian, but not both parents for the same child. For instance, if the father opens an account for the child, the mother cannot open another PPF account for the same child. The parent or guardian operating the account is responsible for all transactions until the child turns 18. Once the child becomes a major, they take over the operation of their account.

Number of accounts

A parent can open a PPF account for themselves, and one for each minor child they have. For example, a father can have his own PPF account and separate PPF accounts for his two minor children. However, a parent cannot open multiple PPF accounts for a single minor child.

Contribution Limits and Clubbing Provisions

The rules governing contributions to a minor's PPF account are critical, especially when both parents wish to contribute.

The ₹1.5 lakh annual limit

The most important rule is that the combined annual contribution to a minor's PPF account cannot exceed ₹1.5 lakh in a financial year. This limit applies to the sum of all deposits made by the parent/guardian and any other person into that specific minor's account. For example, if the father contributes ₹1 lakh to the child's PPF account, the mother can only contribute up to an additional ₹50,000 in the same financial year to that same account. Any amount exceeding ₹1.5 lakh will not earn interest and will be refunded without interest.

Impact of Section 80C limit

The contributions made to a minor's PPF account are eligible for deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, up to the overall limit of ₹1.5 lakh. However, this deduction is available to the parent who makes the contribution. Crucially, the amount contributed to the minor's PPF account is included within the parent's own ₹1.5 lakh Section 80C limit. For instance, if a father contributes ₹80,000 to his own PPF account and ₹70,000 to his minor child's PPF account, he can claim a total deduction of ₹1.5 lakh under Section 80C.

Income clubbing rules

According to Section 64(1A) of the Income Tax Act, any income arising to a minor child is clubbed with the income of the parent whose income (excluding the minor's income) is greater. While the interest earned on a PPF account is tax-exempt, it's important to understand this clubbing provision for other types of income a minor might generate. In the context of PPF, the tax-exempt status of the interest means that even if clubbed, it does not lead to additional tax liability for the parent.

Claiming Tax Benefits under Section 80C

Both parents can contribute to a minor's PPF account, but the tax deduction under Section 80C is tied to the individual who makes the contribution.

Who claims the deduction?

Only the parent who deposits the money into the minor's PPF account can claim the deduction under Section 80C. If the father deposits ₹1 lakh and the mother deposits ₹50,000 into their child's PPF account, the father can claim a deduction for ₹1 lakh and the mother for ₹50,000, provided these amounts fall within their respective individual Section 80C limits of ₹1.5 lakh. The combined contribution to the minor's account, however, cannot exceed ₹1.5 lakh.

Deduction within the individual limit

It's vital to remember that the contribution to the minor's PPF account is not an additional deduction. It forms part of the contributor's overall ₹1.5 lakh limit for Section 80C deductions, which also includes other eligible investments like ELSS, life insurance premiums, EPF, etc.. Therefore, parents need to plan their investments carefully to maximise their tax savings while staying within the prescribed limits.

Maturity and Withdrawal Rules for Minor PPF

A minor's PPF account matures after 15 full financial years from the end of the financial year in which the initial subscription was made. For example, an account opened in July 2023 will mature after March 31, 2039.

Conversion to major account

Once the minor turns 18 years old, the account status needs to be changed from 'minor' to 'major'. The erstwhile minor, now an adult, will need to submit an application along with age proof to the bank or post office where the account is held. From this point, the individual operates their own PPF account.

Partial withdrawals

Partial withdrawals are permitted from the 7th financial year from the year of opening the account. The maximum withdrawal allowed is 50% of the balance at the end of the 4th financial year preceding the year of withdrawal or 50% of the balance at the end of the immediately preceding financial year, whichever is lower. These withdrawals are permitted for specific purposes like higher education or serious illness, even for minor accounts, with the parent/guardian applying for it.

Premature closure

Premature closure of a PPF account is generally not allowed before 15 years. However, it is permitted in specific circumstances after 5 years, such as for the treatment of life-threatening diseases of the account holder, spouse, dependent children or parents, or for the higher education of the account holder or dependent children. A penalty of 1% reduction in interest earned is applicable in such cases.

A real example

Karan (40, ₹35L CTC, Mumbai) and Priya (38, ₹25L CTC, Mumbai) have a 5-year-old daughter, Sia. They want to start saving for Sia's higher education using a PPF account.

Scenario 1: One parent contributes Karan opens a PPF account for Sia. In the financial year 2024-25, Karan contributes ₹1.5 lakh to Sia's PPF account.

  • Sia's PPF Account: Receives ₹1.5 lakh.
  • Karan's Tax Deduction: Karan can claim a full deduction of ₹1.5 lakh under Section 80C for this contribution, provided he has sufficient space within his overall ₹1.5 lakh limit (e.g., if he has no other 80C investments or only ₹0.5 lakh in other 80C investments, he can claim the full ₹1.5 lakh for Sia's PPF).
  • Priya's Tax Deduction: Priya cannot claim any deduction for this contribution as she did not make it.

Scenario 2: Both parents contribute In the financial year 2024-25, Karan contributes ₹90,000 to Sia's PPF account, and Priya contributes ₹60,000 to the same account.

  • Sia's PPF Account: Total contribution is ₹90,000 + ₹60,000 = ₹1.5 lakh. This is within the annual limit.
  • Karan's Tax Deduction: Karan can claim a deduction of ₹90,000 under Section 80C.
  • Priya's Tax Deduction: Priya can claim a deduction of ₹60,000 under Section 80C.
  • Combined Impact: Both Karan and Priya utilise part of their individual ₹1.5 lakh Section 80C limit for Sia's PPF. The interest earned will be tax-free and clubbed with the higher-earning parent's income, but since PPF interest is exempt, there's no additional tax implication.

Scenario 3: Exceeding the limit (Incorrect) If Karan contributes ₹1 lakh and Priya contributes ₹80,000 to Sia's PPF account in the same financial year.

  • Sia's PPF Account: Total contribution is ₹1 lakh + ₹80,000 = ₹1.8 lakh.
  • Outcome: Only ₹1.5 lakh will be accepted and earn interest. The excess ₹30,000 will be returned to the parents without any interest. Neither parent can claim a tax deduction for the excess amount.

This example clearly shows that while both parents can contribute, the ₹1.5 lakh limit for the minor's account is a hard cap, and the deduction is tied to the individual contributor within their own 80C limits.

What to do this week

  1. Check existing PPF accounts: Verify if you or your spouse already have a PPF account open for your minor child. Remember, only one account per child is allowed.
  2. Review contribution history: If you're contributing to a minor's PPF, check the total contributions made in the current financial year to ensure you stay within the ₹1.5 lakh limit.
  3. Coordinate with spouse: Discuss and decide with your spouse on how to split contributions to your child's PPF account, if applicable, to optimally utilise your individual Section 80C limits.
  4. Gather documents: If opening a new account, prepare necessary documents like the child's birth certificate, parent's KYC documents (ID proof, address proof), and photographs.
  5. Visit your bank/post office: If you plan to open a new minor PPF account, visit your bank or post office to understand their specific application process and forms.

FAQ

Can both parents open separate PPF accounts for the same child?

No, only one PPF account can be opened for a minor child, and it must be operated by one parent or a legal guardian.

What if contributions exceed ₹1.5 lakh in a minor's PPF account?

Any amount contributed beyond ₹1.5 lakh in a financial year to a minor's PPF account will not earn interest and will be refunded to the contributor.

When does a minor's PPF account become a major account?

The account becomes a major account when the child turns 18. The individual then needs to apply to change the account status and will operate it themselves.

Can NRIs open a PPF account for their children?

No, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are not eligible to open new PPF accounts. However, if a resident Indian opens a PPF account and subsequently becomes an NRI, they can continue to hold the account until maturity but cannot extend it.

Is interest from a minor's PPF account taxable?

No, the interest earned on a PPF account, whether for a minor or an adult, is completely tax-exempt under Section 10(11) of the Income Tax Act.

Can I withdraw money from my child's PPF account before maturity?

Partial withdrawals are allowed from the 7th financial year for specific purposes like higher education or medical treatment, subject to certain limits.
