Decoding today's open interest

Open Interest (OI) represents the total number of outstanding derivative contracts, such as options or futures, that have not yet been settled. In the options market, the strike price with the highest Call OI is often seen as a significant resistance level, as many Call option sellers (writers) believe the index is unlikely to rise above this point. Conversely, the strike price with the highest Put OI is typically viewed as a strong support level, as Put option sellers expect the index to remain above it.

For the NIFTY 50, the heaviest Call OI is observed at 24,300, which is just 0.2% above the spot price. This indicates that many Call writers are positioned for the index to stay below this level, creating a potential ceiling. On the Put side, fresh writing was heaviest near 24,250, suggesting Put sellers are establishing a floor around the current spot. Today's heaviest fresh Call writing, where new Call sellers entered the market, was seen near 24,366, further reinforcing the expectation of a cap on upward movement.

In SENSEX, the heaviest Call OI sits at 77,600, a mere 0.1% above the spot price, acting as a close ceiling. The heaviest Put OI is at 77,500, also close to the spot, suggesting a strong floor. Fresh Call writing was most prominent near 78,100, while fresh Put writing concentrated around 77,600, indicating that option writers are establishing positions for a relatively tight range.

For BANK NIFTY, the heaviest Call OI is at 58,000, which is 0.4% above the spot, presenting a potential resistance. Fresh Call writing was significant near 58,733, implying that Call sellers anticipate the index to remain below this level. Conversely, fresh Put writing was heaviest near 57,733, suggesting Put sellers are creating a support base around the current levels.

PCR — what the put-call ratio is whispering

The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) is a widely used indicator that helps gauge market sentiment by comparing the number of Put options traded or open to the number of Call options. A PCR(OI) value above 1 generally suggests more Put options open than Calls, which can be interpreted as a bullish sentiment or strong support. A value below 1, with more Call options open, often suggests a bearish sentiment or strong resistance. PCR(today's flow ΔOI) looks at the change in Open Interest during the day, giving insight into fresh activity.

For NIFTY 50, the PCR(OI) stands at 1.01, indicating a balanced options book with roughly equal Put and Call Open Interest. Today's flow ΔPCR, which measures the change in the ratio based on new positions, is 0.9. This mixed flow suggests that while the overall book is balanced, fresh activity leans slightly towards Call writing.

SENSEX also shows a balanced book with a PCR(OI) of 1.04. Its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) is also 1.04, signifying a relatively balanced fresh flow of Put and Call writing, reinforcing the idea of a range-bound setup.

BANK NIFTY has a PCR(OI) of 0.82, which is slightly below 1, indicating a marginally higher Call Open Interest compared to Put Open Interest. Its PCR(today's flow ΔOI) is -3.56, suggesting a significant increase in Call writing relative to Put writing today. While the overall book is somewhat balanced, the daily flow indicates a stronger inclination from Call writers.

Max pain and the magnet effect

"Max Pain" refers to the strike price at which option writers (those who sell options) would experience the least financial loss if the underlying asset were to expire at that specific price. It's often viewed as a magnetic point, suggesting that the index tends to gravitate towards this level as expiry approaches, as option writers try to minimise their collective losses.

For NIFTY 50, the Max Pain point is at 24,250, which is precisely at the spot price. This indicates that option writers are positioned for the index to expire around its current levels.

SENSEX has its Max Pain at 77,600, which is very close to its spot price. This suggests that the index could be drawn towards this level by expiry.

BANK NIFTY's Max Pain is at 58,000, which is 0.4% above its spot price. This suggests a slight upward pull for the index towards this level as expiry nears. The closer an expiry gets, the stronger this "magnet effect" of Max Pain tends to be.

How to read this yourself daily

Understanding these market signals can empower you to interpret daily market movements more effectively. You can access Open Interest data directly from the National Stock Exchange (NSE) website under the "Derivatives" section, where daily option chain data is published. Many brokerage platforms also provide this information in an easily digestible format.

To read this yourself, first, look for the highest Call Open Interest and highest Put Open Interest strikes for the nearest expiry. These strikes often act as immediate resistance and support levels. Next, observe the "change in Open Interest" for the day; significant increases in Call OI at higher strikes or Put OI at lower strikes can indicate fresh positioning by option writers. Finally, calculate the Put-Call Ratio (PCR) by dividing total Put OI by total Call OI, and also look at the PCR based on today's change in OI to gauge fresh sentiment. Combining these data points can help you form your own understanding of the market's immediate technical landscape.
