Pre-Market Brief — 21 August 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets ended mixed, with US indices closing lower while Asian markets showed a mixed trend this morning.
  • GIFT Nifty indicates a slightly positive opening bias for the Indian market.
  • FIIs were net sellers yesterday, while DIIs continued to provide strong support, absorbing the selling pressure.
  • Crude oil prices surged, nearing $95 per barrel, as hopes for an Iran peace deal faded, posing inflation concerns.
  • Nifty's key pivot for the day is at 24227, with immediate resistance at 24269 and support at 24189.
  • The primary risk for today remains elevated crude oil prices and sustained FII outflows.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight. US indices closed lower, with the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all registering declines, indicating a cautious sentiment from Wall Street. In Asia, the Nikkei 225 also saw a slight dip, while the Hang Seng managed to post gains and the Shanghai Composite traded marginally lower. The FTSE 100 in the UK ended flat.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) depreciated slightly against the US Dollar, closing at ₹95.69, up 0.20%. The Dollar Index, which measures the dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies, saw a minor dip of 0.11% to 98.72.

In commodities, crude oil prices surged significantly. Brent Crude jumped 2.08% to $93.53 per barrel, while WTI Crude rose 0.78% to $86.50. Gold prices also saw a notable increase of 2.22% to $4589.20, and Silver rallied sharply by 4.85% to $68.93. The US 10-Year Treasury Yield climbed 0.92% to 4.70%.

Interpretation:

  • USD/INR: A rising USD/INR is generally a positive for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters as their dollar earnings translate to more rupees. However, it puts pressure on importers and can contribute to imported inflation.
  • Dollar Index: A slight dip in the Dollar Index could offer some relief to emerging market currencies, but the Rupee's depreciation against the dollar suggests domestic factors might be at play.
  • Crude Oil (Brent/WTI): The significant jump in crude oil prices is a major concern for India, a net oil importer. This could lead to higher inflation, increased current account deficit, and put pressure on sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paints, and aviation.
  • Gold/Silver: The sharp rise in precious metals indicates increased safe-haven demand amidst global uncertainties, potentially reflecting investor caution.
  • US 10Y Yield: The uptick in US bond yields could make US assets more attractive, potentially leading to continued FII outflows from emerging markets like India.

GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling

The GIFT Nifty is trading at 24319.50, up 0.10%. This suggests a slightly positive to flat opening for the Nifty 50 today, likely a minor gap-up or opening around yesterday's close.

FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the Indian cash equity market yesterday, offloading equities worth ₹583 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, continued to provide strong support, buying equities worth ₹3,538 crore.

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  • Crude Oil Price Movements: The ongoing geopolitical situation affecting crude oil prices, particularly the fading hopes of an Iran peace deal, will be a key factor. Markets typically react negatively to rising crude prices due to inflation concerns and potential impact on corporate earnings for oil-sensitive sectors.
  • FII/DII Flow Trends: The continued divergence between FII selling and DII buying will be closely watched. Sustained DII support can cushion market falls, while aggressive FII selling could lead to increased volatility.
  • Global Market Cues: The performance of US and Asian markets throughout the day will continue to influence sentiment. Negative global cues can often trigger risk-off sentiment in Indian markets, leading to declines.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Nifty's 24200-24300 range: Watch for a decisive break above 24300 or below 24200, as the narrow CPR suggests a trending move could follow.
  2. Crude Oil Impact: Keep an eye on oil-sensitive stocks like OMCs (HPCL, BPCL, IOC), aviation (IndiGo, SpiceJet), and paint companies (Asian Paints, Berger Paints) due to surging crude prices.
  3. FII vs. DII dynamics: Monitor the institutional flow data. Continued strong DII buying could provide a floor, while persistent FII selling might cap upside moves.
  4. Corporate Actions: Stocks going ex-dividend or ex-bonus today, particularly GOODLUCK (Bonus 2:1) and LICHSGFIN (Dividend Rs 10), may see price adjustments and increased trading activity.
  5. Sectoral Rotation: Look for potential shifts towards value stocks or sectors highlighted as growth leaders (electronics, solar, defence) as suggested by market experts.

Bottom line

The Indian market is likely to open with a slightly positive bias, as indicated by GIFT Nifty, but global cues from the US are cautious. The surge in crude oil prices is the most significant concern, potentially weighing on sentiment and specific sectors. The Nifty's ability to hold above its immediate support levels, particularly 24200, will be crucial, with DII buying expected to provide some resilience against FII outflows.
