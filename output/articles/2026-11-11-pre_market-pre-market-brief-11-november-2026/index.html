Pre-Market Brief — 11 November 2026

TL;DR

  • Mixed Global Cues: US markets closed lower, while Asian markets were mixed, and Europe saw gains.
  • GIFT Nifty Signals Positive Start: Trading higher, indicating a potential gap-up open for Indian indices.
  • FIIs Remain Net Buyers: Foreign Institutional Investors continued to pump money into Indian equities, while DIIs were net sellers.
  • Bajaj Finance Share Sale: A significant ₹11,700 crore share sale by Bajaj Finance is expected to be a key focus.
  • Nifty's CPR Pivot Crucial: The Nifty 50's Central Pivot Range (CPR) pivot at 25213.75 will be a critical level to watch for intraday direction.
  • Rising US Yields & Crude: Increasing US 10-year Treasury yields and higher crude oil prices pose potential headwinds for the market.

How global markets traded — and what it means for India

Overnight, global markets presented a mixed picture. US indices closed in the red, with the Nasdaq leading the declines, reflecting some pressure on technology stocks. European markets, however, ended positively. This morning, Asian markets are mixed, with Japan's Nikkei 225 showing strength while Hong Kong's Hang Seng declined.

Currency, commodities and yields

The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, with USD/INR trading at 93.68, down 0.28%. The Dollar Index, however, saw a slight uptick of 0.23% to 102.47. Crude oil prices continued their upward trajectory, with Brent Crude rising 1.37% to $92.74 and WTI Crude up 0.88% to $86.80. Gold and Silver also gained, with Gold up 0.64% to $4055.90 and Silver surging 3.59% to $52.94. A significant move was seen in the US 10-year Treasury yield, which jumped 2.45% to 4.98%.

Interpretation: A strengthening Rupee is generally positive for importers and helps in managing imported inflation, but it could be a slight headwind for IT and pharma exporters. Rising crude oil prices are a concern for India, as it is a major oil importer, potentially impacting oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors. The sharp rise in US 10-year yields could make emerging markets like India less attractive for foreign investors, potentially leading to FII outflows or increased volatility in bond markets.

GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling

The GIFT Nifty is trading at 25171.50, up by 0.51%. This indicates a positive bias and suggests a potential gap-up opening for the Nifty 50 today.

FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were significant net buyers yesterday, infusing ₹7,212 crore into the Indian equity market. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), on the other hand, were net sellers, pulling out ₹7,173 crore.

Combining with technicals, Nifty's 25500 Call OI aligns closely with the R2 level, reinforcing it as a significant resistance point to respect today. For Bank Nifty, 60500 has both high Call and Put OI, indicating a battleground for bulls and bears around that strike.

Stock & sector news that matters

Bajaj Finance to launch share sale worth up to Rs 11,700 cr this week: Report Bajaj Finance Ltd. is reportedly planning a significant share sale to raise up to ₹11,700 crore, targeting institutional investors. Why it matters: This is a substantial capital raise for a prominent financial services company. While it brings fresh funds for growth, it could also lead to some short-term stock price volatility due to potential dilution or supply of shares in the market. Investors will watch for details on pricing and investor participation.

Nvidia Stock Sell-Off Deepens: Spy scandal, price target cuts put AI giant under pressure Nvidia shares plunged nearly 10% amid a broader technology sell-off, leadership controversy, and concerns over AI valuation. Why it matters: Nvidia is a bellwether for the global AI and semiconductor sectors. Its sharp decline could dampen sentiment for technology and AI-related stocks globally, including in India, potentially impacting IT and tech-heavy funds.

India bonds slip as Treasury rout offsets oil slide Indian government bonds declined as a sharp rise in US Treasury yields, driven by inflation concerns and hawkish signals, overshadowed any relief from lower crude oil prices. Why it matters: This highlights the significant influence of global factors, particularly US bond yields, on India's debt market. Rising yields make borrowing more expensive for the government and corporations, and can impact FII flows into Indian debt.

Rupee rises 7 paise to 93.70 against US dollar in early trade The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, supported by sustained foreign fund inflows, easing crude oil prices (though crude is up today), and improving geopolitical sentiment. Why it matters: A stronger rupee is generally positive for the broader economy by making imports cheaper and helping to control inflation. It can provide a tailwind for import-dependent sectors and could be a slight headwind for export-oriented sectors like IT and pharmaceuticals.

Up 70% in 6 months, Kissht parent OnEMI raises ₹832 cr from preferential issue; check who invested, price, performance OnEMI Technology Solutions, parent company of Kissht, raised ₹832.20 crore through a preferential share issue from 34 investors including Axis Mutual Fund schemes, Citigroup, and MIT. Why it matters: This news indicates strong investor confidence in the fintech space and specific companies like OnEMI. It could bring focus to other listed fintech players and the broader digital lending sector.

Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

  • US 10-Year Treasury Yields: The significant jump in US 10Y yields yesterday is a critical factor. Markets typically react to rising global yields with caution, as it can lead to capital outflows from emerging markets and increase borrowing costs.
  • FII Flow Momentum: The continued strong buying by FIIs has been a key market driver. Any significant shift in this trend could impact market direction. Markets generally react positively to sustained FII inflows and negatively to outflows.
  • Global Technology Sector Performance: Following Nvidia's sell-off, the performance of global tech giants will influence sentiment for Indian IT stocks. A continued downturn in global tech often leads to a cautious approach towards domestic IT.
  • Bajaj Finance Share Sale Details: The specifics of the ₹11,700 crore share sale by Bajaj Finance, including pricing and institutional participation, will be closely watched. Large share sales can sometimes create short-term supply pressure on the stock.

What to watch in today's session

  1. GIFT Nifty's Open: Confirm if the positive bias indicated by GIFT Nifty translates into a gap-up open for Nifty 50.
  2. Nifty's Key Levels: Monitor Nifty's CPR pivot at 25213.75. A sustained move above it could signal strength, while a break below could invite selling. The 25500 Call OI strike is a crucial resistance.
  3. FII vs. DII Stance: Observe if FIIs continue their buying spree and if DIIs persist with profit booking. This tug-of-war will be key for market direction.
  4. Bajaj Finance Stock Movement: Watch how Bajaj Finance shares react to the news of the large share sale.
  5. Crude Oil Prices: Any further significant rise in Brent crude above $92 could put pressure on inflation expectations and specific sectors.
  6. Global Yields & Rupee: Keep an eye on the US 10-year yield for any further upward movement and the USD/INR pair for stability or further rupee appreciation.

Bottom line

Indian markets are likely to open with a positive bias, as suggested by GIFT Nifty, but will face mixed global cues and rising US bond yields. The Nifty's ability to sustain above its CPR pivot of 25213.75 will be crucial for today's direction, with 25500 acting as a strong resistance. Investors should remain cautious of the impact of higher crude oil prices and the potential for FII flows to shift if global yields continue to climb.
