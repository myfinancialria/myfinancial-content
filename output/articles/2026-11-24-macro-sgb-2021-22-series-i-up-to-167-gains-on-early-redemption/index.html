SGB 2021-22 Series I: Up to 167% Gains on Early Redemption

TL;DR

  • Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) 2021-22 Series I has opened its premature redemption window, marking five years since its issuance.
  • Investors opting to exit now can realise significant capital gains, potentially up to 167% over their initial investment.
  • The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has fixed the premature redemption price at ₹12,746 per gram for this series.
  • In addition to capital appreciation, investors have also earned a fixed interest rate of 2.5% per annum, paid semi-annually.
  • Understanding the tax implications for both capital gains and interest income is crucial before making a redemption decision.

What this means in plain terms

Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) are government securities denominated in grams of gold, offering an alternative to physical gold investment. They come with an 8-year maturity period, but also provide an option for investors to exit prematurely after the fifth year. For those who invested in the SGB 2021-22 Series I, that five-year mark has now been reached. This means that investors holding these bonds can choose to redeem them before the full 8-year term, taking advantage of the current high gold prices. The RBI sets the redemption price based on the average gold price of the preceding three business days, and for this series, it offers substantial capital gains, alongside the regular interest already received.

Understanding Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs)

Sovereign Gold Bonds are issued by the Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Government of India. They are designed to offer investors a safe and convenient way to invest in gold without the hassles of physical gold storage, purity concerns, or making charges. Each SGB unit represents one gram of 999 purity gold.

Key features of SGBs include:

  • Assured Purity: The bonds are backed by the government, guaranteeing the purity of gold.
  • Interest Income: Investors receive a fixed interest rate of 2.50% per annum on their initial investment, paid semi-annually. This is a unique advantage over physical gold, which does not yield interest.
  • No Storage Costs: Unlike physical gold, SGBs are held in dematerialised form, eliminating storage costs and the risk of theft.
  • Tax Benefits: While interest income is taxable, capital gains on redemption at maturity are exempt for individual investors, making them tax-efficient for long-term holding.
  • Maturity Period: The bonds have a maturity period of eight years. However, a premature redemption option is available after the fifth year from the date of issue.

The 5-Year Premature Redemption Window

The SGB scheme allows investors to exit their investment after the fifth year from the date of issue. This feature provides liquidity and flexibility, especially if an investor needs funds or wishes to book profits before the full 8-year maturity. The SGB 2021-22 Series I was issued with an application period from October 29 to November 2, 2021, at an issue price of ₹4,777 per gram. Consequently, the five-year lock-in period for this series has concluded, opening the window for premature redemption.

When an investor opts for premature redemption, the redemption price is determined by the RBI. This price is based on the simple average of the closing price of 999 purity gold, published by the India Bullion and Jewellers Association (IBJA) Ltd, for the last three business days of the week preceding the date of redemption. For the SGB 2021-22 Series I, the RBI has fixed this premature redemption price at ₹12,746 per gram. Investors wishing to exit must do so during the specific window announced by the RBI for this series.

Calculating Your Returns: Capital Gains and Interest

Investors in SGB 2021-22 Series I have seen a substantial appreciation in the value of their investment. With an issue price of ₹4,777 per gram and a premature redemption price of ₹12,746 per gram, the capital gain per gram is ₹7,969 (₹12,746 - ₹4,777).

To calculate the percentage gain: (₹7,969 / ₹4,777) * 100 = 166.82%

This translates to an impressive capital appreciation of approximately 167% over the five-year holding period.

In addition to these capital gains, investors have also received a fixed interest of 2.50% per annum on their initial investment. This interest is paid semi-annually. Over five years, the total simple interest earned would be 12.5% of the initial investment value. For example, on an investment of ₹4,777 per gram, the total interest earned over five years would be ₹597.125 per gram (₹4,777 2.5% 5 years). This regular income stream further enhances the overall returns from SGBs.

Taxation on SGB Redemption

Understanding the tax implications is crucial when considering SGB redemption. There are two components to consider: the interest earned and the capital gains.

  1. Interest Income: The 2.50% annual interest received on SGBs is taxable as 'income from other sources'. This income is added to your total income and taxed as per your applicable income tax slab rates. Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is not applicable on SGB interest, but investors are responsible for declaring this income and paying the appropriate tax.
  1. Capital Gains:

    2. * Redemption at Maturity (8 years): For individual investors, capital gains arising from the redemption of SGBs at maturity (after 8 years) are fully exempt from Income Tax. This is a significant tax advantage, making SGBs highly attractive for long-term gold investments. * Premature Redemption (after 5 years): If you opt for premature redemption (after 5 years but before the 8-year maturity), the capital gains are subject to taxation. Since the holding period is more than 36 months, these gains are treated as Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG). LTCG from SGBs are taxed at a rate of 20% with the benefit of indexation. Indexation adjusts the purchase price for inflation, thereby reducing the taxable gain. Investors should consult the Income Tax Department's guidelines or a tax advisor for precise calculations.

A real example

Let's consider Priya, 42, ₹32L CTC, Gurugram, who invested in 10 grams of SGB 2021-22 Series I.

  • Initial Investment: Priya purchased 10 grams of SGB at the issue price of ₹4,777 per gram.

    • Total Initial Investment = 10 grams ₹4,777/gram = ₹47,770

  • Interest Earned (over 5 years): The annual interest rate is 2.50% on the initial investment.

    • * Annual Interest = 2.50% of ₹47,770 = ₹1,194.25 Total Interest over 5 years = ₹1,194.25 5 = ₹5,971.25 * This interest was paid semi-annually over the five years and is taxable as per Priya's income slab.

  • Premature Redemption Value: Priya decides to redeem her SGBs prematurely at the RBI-fixed price of ₹12,746 per gram.

    • Redemption Value = 10 grams ₹12,746/gram = ₹1,27,460

  • Capital Gain Calculation:

    • * Capital Gain = Redemption Value - Initial Investment * Capital Gain = ₹1,27,460 - ₹47,770 = ₹79,690

  • Tax on Capital Gains (LTCG): Since Priya is redeeming prematurely after 5 years, the capital gains are long-term and eligible for indexation.

    • * Assuming a Cost Inflation Index (CII) for the purchase year (FY 2021-22) of 317 and for the redemption year (FY 2026-27) of 370 (hypothetical for illustration, actual CII needs to be checked from official sources): Indexed Cost of Acquisition = Original Cost (CII of Redemption Year / CII of Purchase Year) Indexed Cost = ₹47,770 (370 / 317) = ₹47,770 * 1.16719 = ₹55,758 (approx) * Taxable Long-Term Capital Gain = Capital Gain - Indexed Cost * Taxable LTCG = ₹79,690 - ₹55,758 = ₹23,932 * LTCG Tax @ 20% = 20% of ₹23,932 = ₹4,786.40

Priya's total return includes the ₹5,971.25 in interest (taxed annually) and a net capital gain after tax of ₹74,903.60 (₹79,690 - ₹4,786.40).

FAQ

What is the premature redemption period for SGBs?

SGBs have an 8-year maturity period, but investors can opt for premature redemption after the fifth year from the date of issue. This window opens for a specific period announced by the RBI.

How is the premature redemption price determined?

The RBI determines the premature redemption price based on the simple average of the closing price of 999 purity gold, published by the IBJA Ltd, for the last three business days of the week preceding the date of redemption.

Are capital gains from SGBs taxable?

Capital gains from SGBs redeemed at maturity (after 8 years) are exempt from tax for individuals. However, capital gains from premature redemption (after 5 years) are treated as Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) and taxed at 20% with indexation benefit.

Is the interest earned on SGBs taxable?

Yes, the 2.50% annual interest paid semi-annually on SGBs is taxable. It is added to your total income and taxed as per your applicable income tax slab rates.

How do I apply for premature redemption?

Investors holding SGBs in dematerialised form can apply for premature redemption through their respective banks or depository participants. For physical certificate holders, the application is typically made through the receiving office where the bond was initially purchased.
