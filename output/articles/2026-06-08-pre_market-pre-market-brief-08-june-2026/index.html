Pre-Market Brief — 08 June 2026

TL;DR

  • Global markets witnessed a significant sell-off, particularly in technology stocks, with US indices down sharply.
  • Crude oil prices surged over 5% due to escalating US-Iran tensions, a key concern for India.
  • Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, offloading ₹8,776 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support with net buying of ₹9,134 crore.
  • US 10-year bond yields rose, and Goldman Sachs pushed its Fed rate-cut forecast to 2027, indicating a longer period of higher interest rates.
  • Indian IT stocks are expected to face pressure following the global tech sector downturn.

Where global markets closed

Global equity markets closed significantly lower on Friday, with a broad-based sell-off, particularly impacting technology stocks. Concerns over the "AI trade trap" and concentration risks in tech-heavy indices contributed to the downturn.

The overall global tone is distinctly negative, marked by a significant risk-off sentiment, especially in the technology sector.

Currency, commodities and yields

The USD/INR pair saw a slight dip of 0.15% to close at ₹95.65, indicating a marginal strengthening of the Rupee against the Dollar. The Dollar Index edged up by 0.05% to 100.12, suggesting the US Dollar maintained its strength against a basket of major currencies.

In commodities, Brent Crude surged by a substantial 5.06% to $97.80 per barrel, while WTI Crude also climbed 4.71% to $94.80 per barrel. This sharp rise in crude oil prices is a significant concern for India, a net oil importer, as it could fuel inflation and impact trade balances.

Gold prices saw a modest decline of 0.37% to $4321.20, and Silver fell by 2.78% to $67.03, suggesting some profit-booking amidst the broader market volatility.

The US 10-Year Yield rose by 1.32% to 4.54%. An increase in US bond yields typically makes equities less attractive and can lead to capital outflows from emerging markets like India.

Recent FII/DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree on June 5, 2026, offloading Indian equities worth ₹8,776 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, provided strong counter-support, buying equities worth ₹9,134 crore.

| Date | FII Net (₹ crore) | DII Net (₹ crore) | | :----------- | :---------------- | :---------------- | | 2026-06-05 | -8,776 | +9,134 |

Over the last five trading sessions, FIIs have been consistent net sellers, while DIIs have consistently been net buyers, absorbing the foreign outflows. This trend indicates sustained domestic buying interest offsetting foreign selling pressure.

Overnight news that matters

Global Tech Selloff Triggers Market Meltdown in Asia, Impacts Indian IT A sharp selloff in global technology and AI stocks, particularly in the US, has exposed concentration risks in Asian markets. This downturn has already impacted Indian IT giants like TCS, which saw shares tumble 2% Monday, extending a four-day loss to 12% amidst fears of further Federal Reserve rate hikes and AI disruption concerns. Sterlite Technologies also hit the lower circuit, sliding 5% after a significant rally. This global tech correction is likely to weigh heavily on the Nifty IT index and related stocks today.

Escalating US-Iran Tensions Push Crude Oil Higher, Pressuring Indian Bonds Geopolitical events in West Asia, specifically escalating US-Iran tensions, have pushed crude oil prices significantly higher. Brent Crude surged over 5% overnight. This rise in oil prices is a major concern for India, as it could lead to increased import bills and inflationary pressures. Indian government bonds already faced pressure on Monday, with the rise in oil prices overshadowing recent supportive measures from the Reserve Bank of India.

Goldman Sachs Delays Fed Rate-Cut Call to 2027 Amid Strong US Jobs Data Goldman Sachs now anticipates the U.S. Federal Reserve will maintain current interest rates through 2026, delaying any rate cuts until 2027. This shift, driven by robust economic and job growth in the US, suggests a longer period of higher interest rates globally. This development could further fuel rising US bond yields and potentially impact capital flows to emerging markets like India, adding to the cautious sentiment.

CBI Conducts Searches in IDFC First Bank Fraud Case IDFC First Bank shares dipped after the CBI conducted searches in connection with a ₹646 crore fraud case. A forensic review by KPMG confirmed collusion involving bank employees and government officials. This news could lead to increased scrutiny and potential volatility for IDFC First Bank shares in today's session.

What to watch in today's session

  1. Global Tech Sentiment: The significant sell-off in US and Asian tech stocks will likely dictate the mood for Indian IT companies. Monitor the Nifty IT index closely for further weakness.
  2. Crude Oil Prices: The sharp rise in Brent and WTI crude due to geopolitical tensions is a critical factor. Any further escalation or sustained high prices could impact inflation expectations and bond yields in India.
  3. FII/DII Flows: The persistent FII selling trend versus DII buying will be crucial to watch. Continued DII support might cushion the market, but heavy FII outflows could lead to broader declines.
  4. US Bond Yields and Fed Outlook: The rise in US 10-year yields and Goldman Sachs' revised Fed rate-cut timeline suggest a 'higher for longer' interest rate environment. This could influence global capital allocation.
  5. Specific Stock Reactions: Keep an eye on IT majors like TCS and Sterlite Tech following the global tech rout. IDFC First Bank will be under scrutiny due to the CBI investigation. RELTD will trade ex-rights today.

Bottom line

Indian markets are likely to open with a cautious to negative bias, primarily influenced by the significant global market sell-off, especially in the technology sector, and the sharp rise in crude oil prices. While domestic institutional investors have been providing strong support, persistent foreign outflows and rising US bond yields could keep the market under pressure. Investors should remain vigilant regarding global cues and commodity price movements.
