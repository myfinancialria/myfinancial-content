IPO Watch

Educational only. This page explains how IPOs work and shows publicly-available numbers. It contains no recommendations — nothing here is a suggestion to apply for, buy, sell, or avoid any IPO or stock. myfinancial is not a SEBI-registered investment adviser.

An IPO (Initial Public Offering) is when a private company sells its shares to the public for the first time and gets listed on the stock exchange. This page tracks two things in plain English: the IPOs currently open or lined up, and a report card on how recent IPOs have done about a month after listing.

How to read these numbers

  • Price band — the fixed price range within which you place your bid in an IPO.
  • Lot size — the minimum number of shares you can apply for in one go; you bid in multiples of a lot.
  • Issue size — the total amount of money the company is raising through the IPO.
  • Listing gain — the change from the IPO (issue) price to the price at which the stock opened on its first trading day.
  • Vs IPO price — where the stock trades today compared with the price at which shares were originally issued.

Green means the current price is above the reference price and red means below — these are simple factual comparisons, not a view on the company.
