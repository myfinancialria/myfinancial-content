IPO Watch

Educational only. This page explains how IPOs work and shows publicly-available numbers. It contains no recommendations — nothing here is a suggestion to apply for, buy, sell, or avoid any IPO or stock. myfinancial is not a SEBI-registered investment adviser.

An IPO (Initial Public Offering) is when a private company sells its shares to the public for the first time and gets listed on the stock exchange. This page tracks two things in plain English: the IPOs currently open or lined up, and a report card on how recent IPOs have performed 30 to 60 days after listing.

How to read these numbers

  • Price band — the fixed price range within which you place your bid in an IPO.
  • Lot size — the minimum number of shares you can apply for in one go; you bid in multiples of a lot.
  • Issue size — the total amount of money the company is raising through the IPO.
  • IPO price — the per-share price at which shares were allotted in the IPO (the issue price).
  • Listing close — the price the stock closed at on its very first trading day.
  • Listing gain — the change from the IPO price to that first-day close. A positive listing gain means the stock rose on debut.
  • Change vs IPO price — where the stock trades today compared with the IPO price. Read next to listing gain, it shows whether the debut move has held, grown, or reversed.
  • Type — Mainboard (large, NSE/BSE main platform) or SME (smaller companies on the SME platform).

Green means above the IPO price, red means below — these are simple factual comparisons, not a view on the company.
