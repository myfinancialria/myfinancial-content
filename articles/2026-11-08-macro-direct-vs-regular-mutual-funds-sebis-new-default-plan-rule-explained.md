# Direct vs. Regular Mutual Funds: SEBI's New Default Plan Rule Explained

**TL;DR**
- From November 1, 2026, SEBI has mandated that direct mutual fund plans be the default option on Asset Management Companies' (AMCs) digital platforms.
- This means when you invest in mutual funds online via an AMC, the direct plan will be pre-selected, promoting cost-efficiency.
- Should you choose a regular plan, AMCs are now required to explicitly inform you about its comparatively higher expense ratio.
- The key distinction between direct and regular plans lies in the expense ratio, with direct plans being cheaper as they exclude distributor commissions.
- This regulatory change aims to increase transparency and empower investors to make more informed decisions about their mutual fund investments.

## What this means in plain terms

When you invest in a mutual fund, you typically have two options: a 'direct plan' or a 'regular plan'. Both plans invest in the exact same set of underlying securities and are managed by the same fund manager, but they come with different costs. A regular plan includes commissions paid to distributors or intermediaries who assist you with your investment. These commissions are embedded within the fund's annual charges, known as the expense ratio, making the regular plan slightly more expensive. A direct plan, on the other hand, is purchased directly from the Asset Management Company (AMC) without any intermediary, thus eliminating these commissions and resulting in a lower expense ratio.

Recognising the significant impact of these cost differences on long-term wealth creation, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has introduced a new rule. Effective November 1, 2026, when you access an AMC's digital platform (like their website or app) to invest in a mutual fund, the direct plan will now be the default choice presented to you. If you still prefer a regular plan, the AMC is obliged to highlight that it carries a higher expense ratio. This move is designed to bring greater transparency to investment costs and encourage investors to consider the more cost-efficient direct plans.

## Understanding Expense Ratios: The Hidden Cost

The Total Expense Ratio (TER) is an annual percentage fee charged by a mutual fund to cover its operational and management costs. These costs include fund management fees, registrar and transfer agent fees, audit fees, and marketing expenses. For regular plans, the TER also incorporates the commission paid to distributors or intermediaries for their services, which can range from 0.5% to 1.5% annually.

This seemingly small percentage difference in the expense ratio between a direct plan and a regular plan can have a substantial impact on your accumulated wealth over the long term, thanks to the power of compounding. Since the expense ratio is deducted from the fund's assets daily, it directly affects the Net Asset Value (NAV) and, consequently, your overall returns. A lower expense ratio means a larger portion of your investment remains invested and continues to grow. SEBI, through its regulations, specifies the maximum expense ratios that different categories of mutual funds can charge, which also decline as the fund's Assets Under Management (AUM) grow.

## SEBI's New Default Rule: What Changes for You?

From November 1, 2026, SEBI has implemented a significant change to how mutual funds are presented on Asset Management Companies' (AMCs) digital platforms. As announced by SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey, direct mutual fund plans will now be the default selection when investors make transactions through AMC websites and mobile applications. This means that when you initiate a new investment or a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) on an AMC's digital channel, the direct plan option will be pre-selected.

The core intent behind this regulation is to enhance transparency regarding mutual fund costs. If an investor chooses to opt for a regular plan instead of the default direct plan, AMCs are now mandated to clearly inform them about the comparatively higher expense ratios associated with regular plans. This disclosure aims to make the cost difference more apparent, enabling investors to make a conscious choice after understanding the financial implications.

It is important to note what this rule *does not* change:
*   **No Discontinuation of Regular Plans:** Regular plans will continue to be available for investors who prefer to invest through distributors and seek their assistance.
*   **No Automatic Conversion:** The new rule does not automatically convert your existing regular plan investments into direct plans. Your current SIPs and lump sum investments in regular plans will continue as they are.
*   **Scope:** The default rule primarily applies to transactions made through the digital platforms directly operated by AMCs. It does not necessarily extend to every bank app, distributor portal, or independent investment platform.

Essentially, SEBI's move is a step towards empowering investors by putting investment costs at the forefront of the mutual fund selection process, fostering greater awareness and potentially leading to more cost-efficient investment choices.

## The Long-Term Impact of Expense Ratios

The difference in expense ratios between direct and regular plans, though seemingly small on an annual basis (often between 0.5% to 1.5%), can translate into a substantial difference in your wealth accumulation over a long investment horizon. This is primarily due to the power of compounding. When a fund has a lower expense ratio, more of your investment capital remains invested and continues to earn returns, which then also get reinvested, leading to exponential growth.

For instance, an additional 1% expense ratio annually means that 1% less of your capital is compounding each year. Over 10, 15, or even 20 years, this seemingly minor difference can amount to several lakhs of rupees. This is particularly crucial for long-term goals like retirement planning or children's education, where the investment tenure is typically decades. The money saved on commissions in a direct plan directly adds to your investment corpus, allowing it to grow larger over time. While distributors provide valuable services, understanding this long-term cost impact allows investors to weigh the value of advisory services against the potential for higher returns from a direct plan.

## Switching from Regular to Direct: Considerations

If you currently hold mutual fund units in a regular plan and are considering switching to a direct plan of the same scheme, it's essential to understand the implications. A switch from a regular plan to a direct plan within the same scheme is generally treated as a redemption of your existing units from the regular plan, followed by a fresh investment in the direct plan.

This "redemption and reinvestment" process can trigger two main considerations:
1.  **Capital Gains Tax:** The redemption of your regular plan units may lead to capital gains. Depending on the type of mutual fund (equity or debt) and your holding period, these gains will be subject to either Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) or Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax as per the Income Tax Act. For instance, equity funds held for less than one year attract STCG tax, while those held for over one year attract LTCG tax (with certain exemptions). You can find detailed tax regulations on the Income Tax India website.
2.  **Exit Load:** Some mutual fund schemes levy an 'exit load' if units are redeemed before a specified period (e.g., one year from the investment date). This is a charge deducted from your redemption amount. You should check the scheme's offer document for applicable exit loads before initiating a switch.

Before making any switch, it is advisable to calculate the potential capital gains tax liability and any applicable exit loads. Compare these costs against the expected savings from the lower expense ratio of the direct plan over your remaining investment horizon. For a comprehensive understanding tailored to your financial situation, consulting a Certified Financial Planner is recommended.

## A real example

Let's consider **Rahul, 38, ₹28L CTC, Bengaluru**, who plans to invest in mutual funds for his retirement. He decides to start a monthly SIP of ₹25,000 and aims to continue this for 22 years until he turns 60.

He is evaluating an equity mutual fund scheme that he expects to generate an average annual return of 10.5% before expenses.

*   **Direct Plan:** This plan has an assumed Total Expense Ratio (TER) of **0.75%** per annum.
*   **Regular Plan:** This plan has an assumed Total Expense Ratio (TER) of **1.75%** per annum (including distributor commissions).

Let's calculate the approximate corpus Rahul would accumulate in both scenarios:

**Scenario 1: Investing via Direct Plan (TER 0.75%)**
*   Effective annual return: 10.5% - 0.75% = 9.75%
*   Monthly SIP: ₹25,000
*   Investment Tenure: 22 years (264 months)
*   Total amount invested: ₹25,000 x 264 = ₹66,00,000

Using a SIP calculator for 22 years at an effective annual return of 9.75%:
Approximate accumulated corpus: **₹2,21,30,000** (Two Crore Twenty-One Lakh Thirty Thousand Rupees)

**Scenario 2: Investing via Regular Plan (TER 1.75%)**
*   Effective annual return: 10.5% - 1.75% = 8.75%
*   Monthly SIP: ₹25,000
*   Investment Tenure: 22 years (264 months)
*   Total amount invested: ₹25,000 x 264 = ₹66,00,000

Using a SIP calculator for 22 years at an effective annual return of 8.75%:
Approximate accumulated corpus: **₹1,91,50,000** (One Crore Ninety-One Lakh Fifty Thousand Rupees)

**The Difference:**
By choosing the direct plan, Rahul would accumulate approximately **₹29,80,000** (Twenty-Nine Lakh Eighty Thousand Rupees) more over 22 years, simply due to a 1% lower annual expense ratio. This example highlights how even a seemingly small difference in costs can have a significant impact on long-term wealth creation.

## FAQ

### What is the core difference between direct and regular mutual funds?
The core difference lies in the expense ratio. Direct plans are purchased directly from the AMC and do not include distributor commissions, resulting in a lower expense ratio. Regular plans are bought through intermediaries and include these commissions, leading to a higher expense ratio.

### How does SEBI's new rule affect existing mutual fund investments?
SEBI's new rule, effective November 1, 2026, makes direct plans the default option on AMC digital platforms for *new* investments. It does not automatically convert your existing regular plan holdings into direct plans, nor does it discontinue regular plans.

### Will I still be able to invest in regular plans?
Yes, you can still invest in regular plans. The new SEBI rule only changes the default selection on AMC digital platforms. If you wish to choose a regular plan, you will be informed about its higher expense ratio.

### What is an expense ratio and why does it matter?
The expense ratio is the annual fee charged by a mutual fund to cover its operating and management costs, expressed as a percentage of your investment. It matters because it is deducted from the fund's returns, directly impacting your net gains and overall wealth accumulation over time.

### Are there any costs involved in switching from a regular to a direct plan?
Yes, switching from a regular to a direct plan is treated as a redemption and reinvestment. This can trigger capital gains tax on any profits made from your regular plan units and may also incur an exit load if you redeem before a specified holding period.

### Who should opt for a direct plan?
Direct plans are ideal for investors who are comfortable doing their own research, managing their investments independently, and prefer a cost-efficient approach. Experienced investors who do not require intermediary assistance often benefit most from direct plans.
