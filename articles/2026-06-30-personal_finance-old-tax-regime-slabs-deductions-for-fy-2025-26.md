# Old Tax Regime: Slabs & Deductions for FY 2025-26

**TL;DR**
- The old tax regime remains an option for taxpayers in FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27), despite the new regime becoming the default.
- It allows a wide array of deductions and exemptions, which can significantly reduce your taxable income.
- Key deductions include Section 80C, 80D, HRA, and interest on home loans under Section 24(b).
- Taxpayers with substantial investments, insurance premiums, and home loan EMIs often find the old regime more beneficial.
- A careful comparison of your potential tax liability under both regimes is crucial before making your choice for the financial year.

## What this means in plain terms

For many years, the Indian income tax system primarily operated under what is now known as the "old tax regime." This system allowed taxpayers to reduce their taxable income by claiming various deductions and exemptions for expenses like investments, insurance premiums, home loan interest, and rent paid. It was the standard way of calculating income tax.

However, from FY 2023-24, a "new tax regime" was introduced and made the default option. This new regime offers simpler, lower tax slab rates but significantly limits the deductions and exemptions you can claim. While the new regime is now the default, the old tax regime has not been abolished. For Financial Year 2025-26 (Assessment Year 2026-27), you still have the option to choose the old regime if it proves more beneficial for you. Its continued relevance lies in the substantial tax savings it can offer to individuals who make considerable use of the available deductions.

## Understanding the Old Tax Slabs for FY 2025-26

The old tax regime categorises taxpayers based on their age, offering different slab rates for each group. These slabs determine the percentage of tax you pay on different income levels. It's important to understand these categories to calculate your tax liability accurately.

### For Individuals Below 60 Years of Age (Resident & Non-Resident)
This category includes most salaried and self-employed individuals.
*   **Up to ₹2,50,000:** Nil
*   **₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000:** 5%
*   **₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000:** 20%
*   **Above ₹10,00,000:** 30%

### For Senior Citizens (60 Years to Less Than 80 Years of Age)
Senior citizens get a higher basic exemption limit.
*   **Up to ₹3,00,000:** Nil
*   **₹3,00,001 to ₹5,00,000:** 5%
*   **₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000:** 20%
*   **Above ₹10,00,000:** 30%

### For Super Senior Citizens (80 Years of Age and Above)
Super senior citizens enjoy an even higher exemption threshold.
*   **Up to ₹5,00,000:** Nil
*   **₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000:** 20%
*   **Above ₹10,00,000:** 30%

Additionally, a 4% Health and Education Cess is levied on the total income tax calculated, regardless of the slab. Surcharge may also apply for very high incomes: 10% for income between ₹50 lakhs and ₹1 crore, 15% for income between ₹1 crore and ₹2 crores, 25% for income between ₹2 crores and ₹5 crores, and 37% for income above ₹5 crores.

## Key Deductions and Exemptions in the Old Tax Regime

The primary advantage of the old tax regime lies in the extensive list of deductions and exemptions it permits, allowing you to significantly lower your taxable income. Understanding these can help you maximise your tax savings.

### Section 80C, 80CCC, and 80CCD(1)
This is one of the most popular sections, allowing a combined deduction of up to ₹1.50 lakhs. It covers a wide range of investments and expenses:
*   **Provident Fund (PF) contributions:** Both Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) and Public Provident Fund (PPF).
*   **Life insurance premiums:** Paid for self, spouse, or children.
*   **Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS):** Tax-saving mutual funds.
*   **Home loan principal repayment:** The principal component of your housing loan EMI.
*   **Children's tuition fees:** For up to two children, paid to any university, college, school, or educational institution in India.
*   **National Savings Certificates (NSC).**
*   **Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY).**
*   **Senior Citizens' Savings Scheme (SCSS).**
*   **Certain fixed deposits (tax-saver FDs) for 5 years.**
*   **80CCC:** Contributions to certain pension funds.
*   **80CCD(1):** Employee contributions to the National Pension System (NPS).

### Section 80D: Health Insurance Premiums
You can claim deductions for health insurance premiums paid for yourself, your family (spouse and dependent children), and your parents.
*   **For self, spouse, and dependent children:** Up to ₹25,000 (if below 60 years) or ₹50,000 (if 60 years or above).
*   **For parents:** An additional deduction of up to ₹25,000 (if below 60 years) or ₹50,000 (if 60 years or above).
*   Preventive health check-ups up to ₹5,000 are also covered within these limits.

### Section 24(b): Interest on Home Loan
Interest paid on a home loan for a self-occupied property can be claimed up to ₹2 lakhs per financial year. For let-out or deemed let-out properties, there is no upper limit on the interest deduction, though the total loss from house property that can be set off against other income in a year is capped at ₹2 lakhs.

### House Rent Allowance (HRA) Exemption
Salaried individuals receiving HRA as part of their salary can claim exemption under Section 10(13A) if they pay rent. The exemption is the least of:
1.  Actual HRA received.
2.  50% of basic salary + Dearness Allowance (for metro cities like Mumbai, Delhi, Chennai, Kolkata) or 40% for non-metro cities.
3.  Actual rent paid minus 10% of basic salary + Dearness Allowance.

### Standard Deduction for Salaried Employees
Salaried individuals are eligible for a standard deduction of ₹50,000 from their gross salary, which directly reduces their taxable income.

### Other Important Deductions
*   **Section 80E:** Interest on education loan. No upper limit, but only interest component is deductible.
*   **Section 80G:** Donations to certain approved charities. The deduction amount can be 50% or 100% of the donated amount, subject to certain limits.
*   **Section 80TTA/80TTB:** Interest income from savings accounts. For individuals below 60 years, up to ₹10,000 (80TTA). For senior citizens, up to ₹50,000 on interest from savings and fixed deposits (80TTB).
*   **Professional Tax:** Deductible from gross salary.

## Who Benefits Most from the Old Tax Regime?

The old tax regime typically offers greater tax efficiency for individuals who make significant use of the various deductions and exemptions available. These usually include:

*   **Individuals with Home Loans:** Those paying substantial interest on a housing loan (up to ₹2 lakhs for self-occupied property under Section 24(b)) and principal repayment (under Section 80C) find the old regime highly beneficial.
*   **Active Investors:** Taxpayers who invest in instruments like EPF, PPF, ELSS, NPS, or pay life insurance premiums, thereby maxing out their Section 80C limit of ₹1.50 lakhs, stand to gain.
*   **Families with Health Insurance:** Individuals paying health insurance premiums for themselves, their spouse, children, and parents can claim significant deductions under Section 80D.
*   **Rent Payers:** Salaried employees living in rented accommodation and receiving HRA can claim a substantial exemption, which is not available in the new regime.
*   **Parents with Education Loans:** Those repaying interest on an education loan under Section 80E can benefit from the unlimited deduction.

Essentially, if your total deductions (including standard deduction, HRA exemption, 80C, 80D, 24(b), etc.) amount to a significant portion of your income, often exceeding ₹2.5 lakhs to ₹3.5 lakhs, the old regime is likely to result in a lower tax outgo compared to the new regime. It requires a more active approach to financial planning and investment, but the rewards can be substantial.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 35, ₹22L CTC, Gurugram**, a salaried employee. She is evaluating her tax options for FY 2025-26.

**Priya's Income and Deductions:**
*   Gross Salary: ₹22,00,000
*   HRA Component: ₹2,50,000
*   EPF Contribution (employee share): ₹1,00,000 (covered under 80C)
*   ELSS Investment: ₹50,000 (covered under 80C)
*   Health Insurance Premium (self & family): ₹20,000 (covered under 80D)
*   Health Insurance Premium (parents, non-senior citizens): ₹30,000 (covered under 80D)
*   Home Loan Interest: ₹2,00,000 (covered under 24(b))
*   Professional Tax: ₹2,500

**Calculation under Old Tax Regime:**

1.  **Gross Salary:** ₹22,00,000
2.  **Less: Standard Deduction:** ₹50,000
3.  **Less: Professional Tax:** ₹2,500
4.  **Less: HRA Exemption:**
    *   Assuming Basic Salary + DA is ₹10,00,000.
    *   Actual HRA received: ₹2,50,000
    *   50% of Basic + DA (Gurugram is non-metro for this calculation, so 40%): ₹4,00,000 (40% of ₹10,00,000)
    *   Rent Paid (let's assume ₹25,000/month, so ₹3,00,000 annually) - 10% of Basic + DA: ₹3,00,000 - ₹1,00,000 = ₹2,00,000
    *   Least of the three: ₹2,00,000.
    *   *HRA Exemption:* ₹2,00,000

5.  **Gross Taxable Income after HRA & Standard Deduction:** ₹22,00,000 - ₹50,000 - ₹2,500 - ₹2,00,000 = ₹19,47,500

6.  **Less: Deductions under Chapter VI-A:**
    *   **Section 80C:**
        *   EPF: ₹1,00,000
        *   ELSS: ₹50,000
        *   Total 80C: ₹1,50,000 (Maxed out)
    *   **Section 80D:**
        *   Self & Family: ₹20,000 (within ₹25,000 limit)
        *   Parents: ₹30,000 (within ₹25,000 limit for non-senior citizens)
        *   Total 80D: ₹50,000
    *   **Section 24(b) (Home Loan Interest):** ₹2,00,000 (within ₹2,00,000 limit)
    *   **Total Chapter VI-A Deductions:** ₹1,50,000 + ₹50,000 + ₹2,00,000 = ₹4,00,000

7.  **Net Taxable Income:** ₹19,47,500 - ₹4,00,000 = ₹15,47,500

8.  **Tax Calculation:**
    *   Up to ₹2,50,000: Nil
    *   ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000 (₹2,50,000 @ 5%): ₹12,500
    *   ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 (₹5,00,000 @ 20%): ₹1,00,000
    *   ₹10,00,001 to ₹15,47,500 (₹5,47,500 @ 30%): ₹1,64,250
    *   **Total Income Tax:** ₹12,500 + ₹1,00,000 + ₹1,64,250 = ₹2,76,750

9.  **Add: Health & Education Cess (4%):** 4% of ₹2,76,750 = ₹11,070

10. **Total Tax Liability:** ₹2,76,750 + ₹11,070 = **₹2,87,820**

In this scenario, Priya's substantial deductions for home loan interest, 80C investments, and health insurance make the old tax regime a favourable choice.

## What to do this week

1.  **Gather Your Financial Documents:** Collect all investment proofs (EPF statements, ELSS certificates, life insurance premium receipts), health insurance premium receipts, home loan statements (principal and interest breakup), rent receipts, and any other documents related to potential deductions for FY 2025-26.
2.  **Estimate Your Deductions:** Based on your planned or ongoing investments and expenses, make a realistic estimate of the total deductions you expect to claim under the old tax regime for the current financial year.
3.  **Compare Tax Liabilities:** Use an online tax calculator (available on the Income Tax India website or financial portals) or a simple spreadsheet to calculate your estimated tax liability under both the old and new tax regimes. This will give you a clear picture of which regime saves you more.
4.  **Consult Your Employer:** If you are a salaried employee, inform your employer about your chosen tax regime (old or new) for the current financial year. This ensures that your TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) is calculated correctly. You usually do this by submitting an investment declaration.
5.  **Review Your Financial Plan:** If the old regime is more beneficial due to certain deductions, consider if your current investments align with your long-term financial goals. If not, adjust your plan to optimise both tax savings and wealth creation.

## FAQ

### Is the old tax regime still available for FY 2025-26?
Yes, the old tax regime is still an option for taxpayers for Financial Year 2025-26 (Assessment Year 2026-27), alongside the new tax regime.

### What is the standard deduction in the old tax regime?
Salaried individuals can claim a standard deduction of ₹50,000 from their gross salary under the old tax regime.

### Can I switch between the old and new tax regimes every year?
Yes, salaried individuals and those not having business income can choose between the old and new tax regimes each financial year when filing their Income Tax Return.

### Are NRIs eligible for the old tax regime deductions?
Yes, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) can also opt for the old tax regime and claim eligible deductions and exemptions, provided they meet the criteria for those deductions.

### What is the maximum deduction under Section 80C?
The maximum deduction allowed under Section 80C (including 80CCC and 80CCD(1) for employee's NPS contribution) is ₹1.50 lakhs per financial year.

### Is HRA exemption available in the new tax regime?
No, the HRA exemption is not available under the new tax regime. It is one of the key exemptions unique to the old tax regime.

### How does home loan interest deduction work in the old regime?
Under Section 24(b) of the old regime, you can claim a deduction of up to ₹2 lakhs for interest paid on a home loan for a self-occupied property. For let-out properties, the full interest can be claimed, though the set-off against other income is capped at ₹2 lakhs.
