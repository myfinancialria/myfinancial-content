# Hitachi Energy's Growth Outlook: Have Valuations Run Ahead?

**TL;DR**
- Hitachi Energy India reported strong Q2 FY27 results with significant growth in revenue and profit.
- The company's revenue surged by 63.5% year-on-year to ₹2,997 crore, while net profit rose 20.5% to ₹318 crore.
- Order inflows jumped an impressive 85.9% to ₹4,120.8 crore, leading to a record-high order backlog of ₹33,335.4 crore.
- This robust performance is driven by high demand in power transmission infrastructure, including HVDC projects and grid modernisation.
- While the strong outlook has powered a rally in shares, some analysts suggest valuations might be stretched, warranting careful consideration.

## What this means in plain terms

Hitachi Energy India, a key player in the power transmission sector, has delivered an exceptionally strong financial performance for the second quarter of the current fiscal year (Q2 FY27). The company saw a significant increase in its sales and profits, indicating robust operational execution and a healthy demand environment. More importantly, the value of new orders it received jumped substantially, pushing its total pending orders to an all-time high.

This strong momentum is largely attributed to India's ongoing investments in upgrading its power infrastructure, particularly in high-voltage direct current (HVDC) transmission systems, which are crucial for integrating renewable energy and ensuring grid stability. The company's management is optimistic about sustained growth, driven by these long-term industry tailwinds. However, the market has reacted very positively, causing the company's share price to surge. This raises the question for investors: does the current share price fully reflect future growth, or has it perhaps climbed too quickly, making it potentially overvalued for now?

## Robust Q2 FY27 Performance Outshines Expectations

Hitachi Energy India showcased a stellar performance in the second quarter of Fiscal Year 2027 (July-September 2026), with key financial metrics demonstrating significant year-on-year growth. The company reported a standalone revenue from operations of ₹2,996.5 crore, marking a substantial 63.5% increase compared to ₹1,832.6 crore in the same period last year. This top-line expansion was a clear indicator of strong project execution and robust market demand.

The net profit after tax (PAT) also saw a healthy rise, climbing 20.5% year-on-year to ₹318.5 crore, up from ₹264.4 crore in Q2 FY26. While the revenue growth outpaced the profit growth, the absolute profit figure still represents a solid bottom-line expansion. The company's earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, and amortisation (EBITDA) increased by 69.5% to ₹494.4 crore, compared to ₹291.6 crore in the prior year. This suggests improved operational efficiency.

### Order Inflows and Backlog Reach Record Highs

Perhaps the most compelling aspect of Hitachi Energy's Q2 results was the exceptional growth in its order book. New order inflows soared by an impressive 85.9% year-on-year, reaching ₹4,120.8 crore. This significant influx of new business pushed the company's total order backlog to a record-high of ₹33,335.4 crore as of September 2026. This substantial backlog provides strong revenue visibility for the coming quarters, assuring investors of sustained business momentum. Transmission orders from utilities, particularly for power transformers, STATCOM, cooling systems, and various switchgear, spearheaded this growth. The data centre segment also emerged as a significant contributor to the order pipeline, reflecting increasing demand for reliable electricity infrastructure to support expanding digital facilities.

## Margin Performance and Operational Efficiency

While the top-line and order book growth were stellar, the company's margin performance presented a nuanced picture. The operational EBITDA margin improved to 16.5% in Q2 FY27, up from 15.9% in the corresponding quarter last year. However, some reports using a different EBITDA calculation (which includes other income) indicated a moderation in EBITDA margins to 13.56% from 16.32% in the prior-year period, attributing this to project cost mix and execution ramp-up overheads. It's important for investors to distinguish between different margin definitions (e.g., operational EBITDA vs. reported EBITDA) when evaluating profitability. The company's management emphasised that operational EBITDA provides a clearer view of underlying business trends. The growth in operational EBITDA was notably higher than revenue growth, suggesting an improvement in operating efficiency and disciplined project execution.

## Management Commentary and Growth Drivers

N Venu, Managing Director and CEO of Hitachi Energy India Limited, highlighted that the robust Q2 results reflect the company's anticipation of underlying opportunities and its investments in new infrastructure and strengthening existing capabilities. The management expressed confidence in the long-term demand drivers for power transmission infrastructure in India.

Key growth catalysts identified include:
*   **HVDC Projects**: Continued investments in High Voltage Direct Current (HVDC) projects are expected to be a major growth propeller. Hitachi Energy has been actively involved in significant HVDC projects in India, such as the Bhadla-Fatehpur link and the Khavda-Nagpur link, crucial for transmitting renewable energy over long distances.
*   **Grid Modernisation**: India's expanding electricity demand and the push for renewable energy integration necessitate substantial investments in grid modernisation and expansion. The country added over 3,000 circuit kilometres of high-voltage transmission lines during Q2 FY27.
*   **Digitalisation and Battery Energy Storage Systems**: The company also anticipates growth from digitalisation initiatives within the power sector and the increasing adoption of battery energy storage systems.
*   **Exports and Services**: The services business saw significant momentum, with orders rising 77% year-on-year in Q2 FY27. Export orders also grew by 41% year-on-year, contributing to the overall order book.

## Peer and Industry Context

The power transmission sector in India is currently experiencing a significant upcycle, driven by the country's ambitious renewable energy targets and the need to modernise its grid infrastructure. India's electricity generation in Q2 FY27 was up 11% year-on-year, and peak demand met saw an 18% increase. This strong demand environment benefits players like Hitachi Energy India, which specialise in critical components and solutions for power transmission. The government's initiatives, including the PM Dhara GEC Phase III, with approved investments of ₹1.86 lakh crore to connect 135 GW of renewable capacity, provide a structural tailwind for the industry.

While the overall industry outlook is positive, competition remains a factor. However, Hitachi Energy's strong technological expertise, particularly in HVDC, and its established market presence give it a competitive edge. The record order backlog across the industry underscores the robust demand, and companies with strong execution capabilities and advanced technology are well-positioned to capitalise on this investment cycle.

## A real example

Consider Anjali, a 42-year-old software architect in Hyderabad earning ₹35 lakhs CTC. She has been tracking Hitachi Energy India for a while, impressed by its strong market position in India's growing power sector. After the Q2 FY27 results, she sees the stock rally significantly.

Anjali notes the robust revenue growth (63.5% YoY) and the impressive 85.9% jump in order inflows, leading to a record backlog of ₹33,335.4 crore. This indicates excellent revenue visibility for several years. She also sees the operational EBITDA margin at 16.5%, showing good profitability.

However, Anjali also considers the valuation aspect. Let's assume, hypothetically, that before the results, Hitachi Energy India was trading at a Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio of 60x, and after the rally, it jumped to 75x. If the company's annualised earnings per share (EPS) based on Q2 were, say, ₹40, the share price would have been ₹2,400 earlier (40 * 60). After the rally, with the same EPS, the price could be ₹3,000 (40 * 75).

Anjali understands that while the growth story is compelling, a P/E of 75x is quite high, even for a growth stock. She thinks: "The company's future growth is already significantly priced into the current stock value. While the long-term prospects due to HVDC and grid modernisation are strong, I need to evaluate if this growth can sustain at a pace that justifies such a premium valuation. A small dip or any negative news could lead to a sharp correction."

Instead of rushing to buy at the peak, Anjali decides to wait for a potential correction or accumulate shares gradually on dips, keeping a long-term perspective. She prefers to invest when the valuations offer a better margin of safety, despite the strong underlying business performance.

## FAQ

### What is an order backlog and why is it important for a company like Hitachi Energy?
An order backlog represents the total value of orders received by a company that are yet to be fulfilled. For capital goods and infrastructure companies like Hitachi Energy, a large and growing order backlog (like their record ₹33,335.4 crore) provides strong revenue visibility and indicates future business certainty.

### How do HVDC projects contribute to Hitachi Energy's growth?
High Voltage Direct Current (HVDC) technology is crucial for efficient, long-distance transmission of large amounts of electricity, especially from renewable energy sources. India's push for green energy and grid modernisation means a high demand for HVDC systems, positioning Hitachi Energy as a key beneficiary.

### What does "valuations running ahead" mean in stock market terms?
It means that a company's share price has increased significantly, often faster than its underlying earnings growth or future prospects might justify. While the business performance might be strong, the stock's current price might be at a premium, potentially limiting future upside or increasing downside risk.

### How does EBITDA margin contraction impact a company's outlook?
EBITDA margin contraction, even with rising revenue, can indicate increasing operational costs relative to sales. While Hitachi Energy's operational EBITDA margin improved, some reports pointed to a moderation in overall EBITDA margins due to project cost mix. Investors should monitor if this is a temporary factor or a sustained trend impacting profitability.

### Is it always risky to invest in a stock with high valuations?
Not necessarily. High valuations can be justified for companies with exceptional growth prospects, strong competitive advantages, and consistent earnings delivery. However, they also imply that significant future growth is already priced in, making the stock more sensitive to any negative news or missed expectations.
