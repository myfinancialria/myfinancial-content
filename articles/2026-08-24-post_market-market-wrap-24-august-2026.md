# Market Wrap — 24 August 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed in negative territory today, while the Midcap 100 showed resilience with a slight gain.
*   Global cues, particularly concerns over potential US sanctions on Iran impacting oil prices and anticipation of Nvidia's earnings, largely dictated market sentiment.
*   Vishal Mega Mart Ltd. emerged as the top gainer among Nifty 500 stocks, surging nearly 10%.
*   BLS International Services Ltd. was the biggest laggard in the Nifty 500, dropping over 10%.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers for the day, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong buying support.
*   F&O positioning data for today is unavailable and will populate from the next session.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equities witnessed a subdued session, with benchmark indices Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing lower amidst global uncertainties. The Nifty 50 ended at 24219.05, down 0.14%, after trading in a narrow range for most of the day. The Bank Nifty also saw declines, closing at 57525.95, a drop of 0.41%. In contrast, the Nifty Midcap 100 managed to eke out a gain of 0.13%, while the Nifty Smallcap 100 closed in the red.

| Midcap 100 | 63817.25 | +0.13% | 63990.35 | 63683.35 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, rose by 2.95% to 11.53, indicating a slight uptick in investor apprehension.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market's performance today was largely influenced by a confluence of global factors and persistent FII outflows. Global markets, particularly in Europe and Japan, edged lower as investors awaited details on potential US sanctions on Iran and key earnings from Nvidia. The threat of US sanctions on Iran kept crude oil prices in focus, with traders anticipating significant impacts on global crude prices, which could, in turn, fuel inflation and widen India's trade deficit. Adding to the cautious sentiment, markets are increasingly pricing in a more hawkish stance from the European Central Bank due to elevated energy costs and geopolitical tensions.

Domestically, while FIIs continued their selling spree with a net outflow of ₹543 crore, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided crucial support, pumping in ₹2,124 crore. This DII buying helped cushion the fall in the broader indices. Sectorally, the Metal and Realty sectors showed strength, while PSU Banks and broader banking indices faced selling pressure. Individual stock movements, such as the surge in Shanthi Gears after Tube Investments increased its stake, also captured investor attention.

## Market breadth
The market breadth leaned towards declines today, with 290 stocks falling compared to 207 advances out of a total of 497 traded stocks, indicating a slightly negative sentiment across the broader market.

## FII / DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the Indian cash market today, offloading equities worth ₹543 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide strong support, making net purchases of ₹2,124 crore.

| Investor Type | Net Flow (₹ crore) |
| :------------ | :----------------- |
| FII | -543 |
| DII | 2,124 |

Over the last five trading sessions, FIIs have largely been net sellers, with significant outflows on three days, totaling ₹3,153 crore. DIIs, however, have consistently been net buyers, injecting a substantial ₹13,698 crore into the market over the same period, thereby absorbing the FII selling pressure. This consistent DII buying indicates underlying domestic confidence despite foreign outflows.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was primarily driven by broad global flows and geopolitical developments rather than a single defining domestic event. The anticipation of potential US sanctions on Iran, which could significantly impact global crude oil prices, kept traders on edge and influenced sectors like Energy and Oil & Gas. Concerns over a potentially more hawkish European Central Bank due to persistent inflation risks also contributed to the cautious global sentiment, which spilled over into Indian markets. Additionally, the looming Nvidia earnings and their potential impact on AI-related stocks globally, as seen in Japan's Nikkei, added to the overall uncertainty. The consistent FII outflows were a key domestic factor, though largely offset by robust DII buying.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will continue to be crucial. Investors will closely monitor developments regarding US sanctions on Iran and any further statements from central banks. The performance of US and Asian markets overnight, particularly in response to Nvidia's earnings, will set the tone for the Indian opening. Key levels to watch for Nifty 50 will be around today's low of 24144 and high of 24313. The movement in crude oil prices (Brent Crude closed at $92.93, down 1.55%) and the Dollar Index (up 0.21% to 99.00) will also be significant.
