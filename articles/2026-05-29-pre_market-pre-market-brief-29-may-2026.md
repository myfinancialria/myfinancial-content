# Pre-Market Brief — 29 May 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets presented a mixed picture, with Asian indices generally positive, while European markets and some US indices closed lower.
*   The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, and crude oil prices saw a significant dip, potentially easing domestic inflation concerns.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers for the fourth session out of the last five, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide strong buying support.
*   Several prominent companies, including Bajaj Auto, LICI, and Glaxo, are trading ex-dividend or ex-bonus today, impacting their share prices.
*   The Supreme Court's decision to uphold the 28% GST levy on online gaming companies is expected to weigh on the gaming sector.

## Where global markets closed
Global markets closed with a mixed performance on Thursday. In the US, the Nasdaq led gains, rising by 0.91%, followed by the S&P 500 which was up 0.58%, and the Dow Jones which edged up 0.05%. Asian markets showed strength, with the Nikkei 225 surging 2.58% and the Hang Seng gaining 0.86%. However, Europe's FTSE 100 declined by 0.75%, and China's Shanghai Composite closed down 0.94%.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, with the USD/INR pair trading at 95.36, down 0.71%. This appreciation of the Rupee is generally positive for India's import bill and can help in managing inflation. The Dollar Index saw a marginal dip of 0.01% to 99.01.

In commodities, crude oil prices experienced a notable decline. Brent Crude fell by 2.48% to $91.39 per barrel, and WTI Crude was down 1.80% at $87.30 per barrel. This significant dip in oil prices is a positive development for India, a major oil importer, as it can alleviate pressure on the current account deficit and potentially ease inflationary concerns.

Gold prices rose by 1.15% to $4551.00, while Silver also saw an increase of 0.40% to $75.95. This could indicate some safe-haven demand or a response to broader market movements.

The US 10-Year Treasury Yield decreased by 0.58% to 4.45%. A falling US yield can sometimes attract foreign investment into emerging markets like India, and also suggests easing inflation expectations or a flight to quality.

## Overnight news that matters
**Delta Corp shares tank 16%. What’s triggering the sharp selloff?**
Shares of Delta Corp tumbled 16% after the Supreme Court upheld the government’s retrospective 28% GST levy on online gaming companies, ruling that online gaming platforms qualify as activities subject to this tax. This news is significant for the entire online gaming sector, as it confirms a substantial tax burden that could impact profitability and future growth.

**Indian bonds nudge up on oil dip; debt sale weighs**
Indian government bonds saw a slight increase, mirroring U.S. Treasuries, as falling oil prices eased inflation concerns. However, gains were tempered by an upcoming debt auction. The dip in crude oil prices is a positive for India's economy, potentially leading to lower inflation and interest rate stability, which generally supports bond markets.

**Infosys, Wipro, TCS, other IT stocks jump up to 4%: What’s behind the bullish sentiment in a muted market?**
IT stocks, including Infosys and TCS, surged up to 4% on Friday, driven by Wipro's expanded partnership with ServiceNow to scale AI workflows. This collaboration aims to integrate Wipro Intelligence with ServiceNow's platform. This development signals continued investment and growth opportunities in the IT sector, particularly in AI-driven solutions, and could provide a positive sentiment for Indian IT majors.

**West Asia conflict a stagflationary shock; world economy not ready for a long war: Bank of America**
The West Asia conflict has triggered a stagflationary shock, impacting global growth and inflation, with Bank of America forecasting a lower global growth projection due to disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz. This geopolitical risk remains a key concern, as prolonged conflict could lead to higher crude oil prices and broader economic instability, affecting global and Indian markets.

**HDFC Bank shares fall 5% in two sessions; lender responds to recent Rs 45 crore differential payment report**
HDFC Bank shares dropped for a second day following a report of an internal investigation into ₹45 crore in payments disguised as marketing expenses, though the bank clarified its internal audit function is robust. This news could lead to increased scrutiny on the banking sector and specifically HDFC Bank, potentially impacting investor sentiment in the short term.

While FIIs have shown a selling trend, robust DII buying has been a consistent support. Investors should remain cautious and watch for sector-specific movements driven by corporate actions and news, particularly in the gaming and IT sectors.
