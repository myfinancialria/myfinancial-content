# Pre-Market Brief — 21 July 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight, with US indices closing lower while Asian markets showed varied performance.
*   GIFT Nifty is signalling a positive start for the Indian benchmark indices, pointing to a potential gap-up open.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide strong buying support.
*   IndiGo's massive engine order with CFM International is a significant corporate development that could impact the aviation sector.
*   Nifty 50's key technical levels suggest a tight range around its CPR pivot of 24233, with 24200 and 24250 as immediate support and resistance from option chain data.
*   The day's main risk factors include sustained FII outflows, global crude oil price volatility, and the overall cautious sentiment from Western markets.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Overnight, US markets closed with a negative bias, with the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all registering declines. European markets also ended lower. However, Asian markets this morning are showing a mixed trend, with Japan's Nikkei 225 posting significant gains, while Hang Seng and Shanghai Composite are trading with minor movements. This mixed global sentiment suggests a cautious but potentially positive start for Indian equities, largely influenced by the positive signal from GIFT Nifty.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) depreciated slightly against the US Dollar, closing at ₹96.37. The Dollar Index strengthened, indicating broader dollar demand. Brent crude oil prices rose, while WTI crude saw a minor dip. Gold and Silver both registered notable gains. The US 10-year Treasury yield increased, reflecting higher borrowing costs and potentially impacting global capital flows.

*   **USD/INR:** 96.37 (+0.09%)
    **Interpretation:** A rising USD/INR indicates a weakening rupee. This typically offers a tailwind for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters, as their dollar earnings translate to more rupees. Conversely, it increases the cost of imports, potentially impacting sectors reliant on imported raw materials and making foreign travel more expensive.
*   **Dollar Index:** 100.98 (+0.23%)
    **Interpretation:** A stronger Dollar Index suggests global investors are favouring the US dollar, which can sometimes lead to capital outflows from emerging markets like India, especially if coupled with rising US yields.
*   **Brent Crude:** 88.61 (+0.58%)
    **Interpretation:** Higher Brent crude prices are generally a concern for India, a major oil importer. This can lead to increased import bills, higher inflation, and potential pressure on the current account deficit. Sectors like aviation, paints, and oil marketing companies (OMCs) typically face margin pressure with rising crude.
*   **WTI Crude:** 82.22 (-0.33%)
    **Interpretation:** While Brent rose, WTI saw a slight decline. The overall crude movement remains a key factor for Indian markets, with any sustained upward trend being a headwind.
*   **Gold:** 4048.20 (+0.88%)
    **Interpretation:** Rising gold prices often indicate increased safe-haven demand amidst global uncertainties or inflation concerns. This could suggest a cautious sentiment among some investors.
*   **Silver:** 57.95 (+3.41%)
    **Interpretation:** A significant jump in silver prices, often seen as both a precious metal and an industrial commodity, could reflect a combination of safe-haven demand and optimism about industrial activity.
*   **US 10Y Yield:** 4.60 (+1.26%)
    **Interpretation:** An increase in US Treasury yields makes US dollar-denominated assets more attractive. This can potentially lead to FIIs reallocating capital away from emerging markets, posing a risk to Indian equity flows.

## FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers in the Indian equity market yesterday, offloading equities worth ₹376 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their strong buying streak, injecting ₹1,018 crore into the market. Over the last five sessions, FIIs have consistently been net sellers, while DIIs have provided robust support, absorbing the selling pressure.
