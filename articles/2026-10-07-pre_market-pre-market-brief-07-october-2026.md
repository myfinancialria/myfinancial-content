# Pre-Market Brief — 07 October 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets present a mixed picture, with US indices positive overnight but Asian and European markets showing caution.
- GIFT Nifty is signalling a negative open for the Indian benchmark, pointing to a potential gap-down.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remain net sellers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continue to provide strong support.
- The RBI's recent rate hike and shift to "calibrated tightening" are key domestic drivers, impacting auto stocks negatively and fintech positively.
- Nifty 50's immediate range to watch is between 22600 (support) and 22800 (resistance).
- The persistent FII selling and global economic uncertainties, particularly in Europe, pose the main risks for today's session.

Overnight, US markets closed higher, with the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all posting gains. However, European markets ended in the red, and Asian markets this morning are mixed, with Nikkei and Hang Seng down, while Shanghai shows a slight uptick. This indicates a lack of clear global direction, suggesting a cautious start for Indian equities.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee weakened against the US Dollar, with USD/INR rising by 0.47% to 96.80. The Dollar Index also strengthened by 0.41% to 102.25. Crude oil prices saw a modest increase, with Brent Crude up 0.49% to $101.07 and WTI Crude up 0.13% to $89.56. Gold and Silver prices declined, falling by 0.87% and 0.90% respectively. The US 10-Year Yield dipped slightly by 0.79% to 5.27%.

**Interpretation:** A rising USD/INR could provide a tailwind for export-oriented sectors like IT and pharmaceuticals, but it puts pressure on importers and could fuel inflationary concerns. A stronger Dollar Index often indicates global risk aversion, which can lead to FII outflows from emerging markets. Higher crude oil prices are a negative for India, potentially increasing import bills and putting pressure on oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors. The slight dip in US 10-year yields could offer some relief to global equity markets, but the overall yield level remains elevated.

## GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling
The GIFT Nifty is trading at 22628.00, down 0.75%. This indicates a potential gap-down open for the Nifty 50 today, suggesting a cautious or negative start to the trading session.

## FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape
Yesterday, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the Indian equity market, offloading shares worth ₹-2,961 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, continued to provide strong support, buying shares worth ₹5,089 crore. This trend of FII selling being largely absorbed by DII buying has been consistent over the last five sessions.

**Interpretation:** The sustained selling by FIIs indicates a cautious stance from foreign investors, potentially due to global factors or domestic policy outlook. However, the consistent and robust buying by DIIs is acting as a crucial counter-balance, preventing sharper market declines and providing a floor for the indices. The market direction will largely depend on whether DII buying can continue to offset FII outflows.

## Option-chain signals (OI) — where support & resistance sit
**NIFTY:**
The highest Call Open Interest (OI) is observed at the 22800 strike (10,452,130 contracts), followed by 22700 (9,952,410) and 23000 (9,752,860). These strikes are likely to act as immediate resistance levels. On the Put side, the highest OI is at 22600 (8,623,615 contracts), followed by 22000 (7,533,175) and 22500 (7,241,195), indicating strong support at these levels. The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) stands at 0.78, which leans towards a cautious-to-bearish sentiment.

**BANK NIFTY:**
For Bank Nifty, 56000 (1,181,310 OI) and 55000 (919,920 OI) are the key Call OI resistance levels. On the Put side, 55000 (1,167,030 OI) and 54000 (741,900 OI) show significant support. The PCR is 0.96, suggesting a neutral-to-slightly cautious outlook.

**SENSEX:**
Sensex sees highest Call OI at 73000 (3,850,540 OI) and 73500 (3,246,940 OI), acting as resistance. Strong Put OI is seen at 72000 (3,504,320 OI) and 72500 (3,313,380 OI), providing support. The PCR is 0.91, indicating a neutral-to-slightly cautious sentiment.

**Interpretation:** For Nifty, the 22800 level appears to be a strong ceiling where sellers are active, while 22600 and 22500 are crucial floors that buyers are defending. The PCR below 1 suggests that option writers are expecting the market to either consolidate or move lower. For Bank Nifty, 55000 is a contested zone, acting as both resistance (Call OI) and support (Put OI), while 56000 is a clear resistance. Sensex has resistance at 73000 and support at 72000. Combined with technicals, traders should respect these levels today: Nifty 22600-22800, Bank Nifty 54800-55500, and Sensex 72400-73000 will be key ranges to monitor.

## Stock & sector news that matters
-   **RBI signals rate cuts are ‘off the table’; Auto stocks drop up to 3%**
    Auto stocks like Tata Motors PV, Maruti Suzuki, and Ashok Leyland declined after the RBI raised the repo rate and shifted to 'calibrated tightening.' This indicates that the central bank is not looking at rate cuts in the near term, which could impact demand for interest-rate sensitive sectors like automobiles.
    **Why it matters:** This news directly impacts the auto sector, which is highly sensitive to interest rates. Higher borrowing costs for consumers could dampen vehicle sales, putting pressure on auto manufacturers and ancillary companies.
-   **Indian rupee FX premiums jump after RBI policy leaves traders bracing for more sell/buy swaps**
    Dollar-rupee forward premiums surged post-RBI policy, marking the highest implied 1-year dollar/rupee forward premium in six months.
    **Why it matters:** This indicates increased hedging costs for importers and potentially higher returns for exporters who hedge their dollar receivables. It reflects market expectations of continued rupee volatility and RBI intervention.
-   **Pine Labs, CAMS, Moneyview, other fintech stocks rally up to 11% after RBI MPC move**
    Shares of fintech firms rose after the RBI announced interoperability among NBFC account aggregators, allowing customers to share financial information across different platforms.
    **Why it matters:** This regulatory move is a significant positive for the fintech sector, promoting data sharing and potentially expanding the reach and services of these companies. It could lead to increased innovation and customer adoption in digital financial services.
-   **Nifty earnings seen rising 27%, strongest growth in 17 quarters in Q2: Motilal Oswal**
    Analysts project Nifty 50 earnings to grow 27% year-on-year in Q2FY27, marking the highest growth in 17 quarters.
    **Why it matters:** Strong corporate earnings are a fundamental driver for market upside. This positive outlook for the upcoming earnings season could provide a significant boost to market sentiment, especially if companies deliver on these expectations.
-   **Green Infra stock hits 5% upper circuit after ₹615 crore order**
    Green Infra received an order of ₹615.76 crore from MSEDCL.
    **Why it matters:** This is positive news for Green Infra, indicating strong order book visibility and revenue potential. It could also provide a sentiment boost for other infrastructure-related stocks.
-   **HDFC Bank shares drop 15% in 3 months since Q1 results. Will Q2 earnings bring redemption as leadership cloud clears?**
    HDFC Bank shares have seen a significant decline post Q1 results. With new CEO Anup Bagchi, Q2 earnings are keenly awaited.
    **Why it matters:** HDFC Bank is a heavyweight in the banking sector and the broader market. Its performance significantly influences banking indices. The upcoming Q2 results will be crucial for its stock trajectory and could impact overall market sentiment, especially given its recent underperformance.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
-   **Aftermath of RBI Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) Meeting:** While the rate hike and "calibrated tightening" stance are already announced, the market will continue to digest its implications. Markets typically react with volatility around policy announcements, and the current stance suggests a prolonged period of higher interest rates, which can impact credit-sensitive sectors.
-   **Start of Q2FY27 Earnings Season:** Although no Nifty 500 results are scheduled today, the broader Q2 earnings season is expected to pick up pace. Markets tend to become stock-specific during earnings season, with individual company results driving price movements and setting sectoral moods. Strong results can lead to rallies, while misses can cause sharp corrections.
-   **Global Macroeconomic Developments:** News regarding France's public finances and the Bank of England's repo reforms highlight ongoing global economic uncertainties. Such developments can influence FII flows and overall risk appetite, leading to increased market volatility.

## What to watch in today's session
1.  **Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty Levels:** Keep a close eye on Nifty's 22600 support and 22800 resistance, and Bank Nifty's 54800 support and 55500 resistance. A break on either side could dictate the day's trend.
2.  **FII vs. DII Flows:** The persistent FII selling and DII buying trend will be crucial. Any significant shift in DII buying intensity or a moderation in FII selling could influence market direction.
3.  **Auto Sector Performance:** Following the RBI's hawkish stance, watch how auto stocks react. Continued pressure could weigh on the broader market.
4.  **Fintech Sector Momentum:** The positive regulatory news for fintech firms could see continued interest in stocks like Pine Labs, CAMS, and Moneyview.
5.  **Crude Oil Prices:** Any sharp movement in Brent crude will be important for sectors like OMCs, aviation, and paints, and for overall inflation expectations.
6.  **Global Cues:** Monitor Asian market performance through the day and any fresh developments from European economies.

## Bottom line
The Indian market is likely to open with a cautious bias, indicated by the negative signal from GIFT Nifty and persistent FII selling. While DIIs continue to provide a strong domestic cushion, the RBI's hawkish stance on interest rates and mixed global cues will keep sentiment in check. The Nifty's ability to hold above 22600 will be critical, with 22800 acting as a key resistance. Traders should remain vigilant for volatility, especially given the narrow CPR for Nifty and Sensex, which hints at a potentially trending day.
