# Pre-Market Brief — 19 November 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight, with US tech showing strength while broader indices faced pressure.
- GIFT Nifty is signalling a strong positive opening for the Indian benchmark indices today.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their robust buying streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net sellers.
- Fintech major Razorpay is reportedly preparing to file for its India IPO as early as next week, indicating strong primary market activity.
- Nifty 50's 25500 level will be crucial, with immediate resistance around 26000 and strong support at 25000.
- The sustained DII selling trend, despite FII inflows, remains a key risk factor for market breadth.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, with USD/INR trading lower at ₹93.29. The Dollar Index also saw a marginal dip. Crude oil prices, both Brent and WTI, declined significantly, which is a positive development for India, a major oil importer. Gold prices edged lower, while Silver saw a slight uptick. The US 10-year Treasury yield also moved lower, which can be supportive for emerging market flows.

**Interpretation:** A stronger Rupee and falling crude oil prices are generally favourable for the Indian economy, potentially easing import bills and inflationary pressures. This could provide a tailwind for sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paints, and aviation. A declining US 10-year yield typically makes emerging markets, including India, more attractive for foreign capital.

Yesterday, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were strong net buyers, injecting ₹5,867 crore into the Indian equity market. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, remained net sellers, offloading equities worth ₹3,160 crore. This trend of FII buying and DII selling has been consistent over the last five sessions.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
- **Mahanadi Coalfields IPO opens today (November 19-23, 2026):** Large-cap IPOs typically generate significant interest and can divert liquidity from the secondary market. The subscription levels and grey market premium (GMP) will be closely watched. Markets often see some churn around major IPOs as funds are reallocated.
- **Crude Oil and US Treasury Yield Movements:** As highlighted in the bond market news, global crude oil prices and US Treasury yields remain critical external triggers. A sharp rise in crude can fuel inflation concerns and pressure sectors like OMCs and airlines, while a significant increase in US yields can make emerging markets less attractive for FIIs, potentially leading to outflows. Conversely, falling crude and yields are generally positive.
- **FII Flow Trend:** The consistent strong FII buying is a key driver. Any significant change in this trend could impact market direction. Markets tend to react positively to sustained FII inflows and cautiously to outflows.
