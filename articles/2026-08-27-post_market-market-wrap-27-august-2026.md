# Market Wrap — 27 August 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed lower today, marking a negative end to the session.
- Expiry-day volatility, coupled with global cues surrounding the US Federal Reserve's stance on inflation, were the primary drivers of market sentiment.
- Bombay Burmah Trading Corporation Ltd. emerged as the top gainer among Nifty 500 stocks, surging over 13%.
- The Ramco Cements Ltd. was the biggest laggard in the Nifty 500, dropping over 5%.
- Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their strong buying spree, while Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) also turned net buyers for the day.
- F&O build-up data is unavailable for today, so no clear positioning signal can be derived from it.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equities ended sharply lower on Thursday, with both Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty registering declines, as expiry-day volatility intensified following the first monthly expiry under the Closing Auction Session. The Nifty 50 closed at 24090.85, down 0.48%, while the Bank Nifty settled at 57509.95, a decrease of 0.47%. Both indices touched their day's lows at closing, indicating a negative trend throughout the latter half of the session.

## What moved the market — the factors
Today's market movement was largely influenced by a confluence of domestic expiry-day volatility and cautious global sentiment. Indian equities ended sharply lower as expiry-day volatility intensified after the first monthly expiry under the Closing Auction Session. Globally, Wall Street's focus quickly shifted to Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech, with Federal Reserve officials expressing concerns about stubbornly high inflation. Some policymakers believe further interest rate increases may be necessary soon, which weighed on investor sentiment.

Despite a positive lead from US markets, where Nvidia's strong revenue forecast revived the AI rally and lifted the Nasdaq and S&P 500, the Indian market reacted more to the domestic expiry dynamics and the cautious tone from the US Fed. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide strong support, injecting ₹6,425 crore into the market, while Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) also turned net buyers with a modest inflow of ₹503 crore. However, this strong domestic buying was not enough to offset the selling pressure and volatility. The PSU Bank, Media, and Metal sectors were among the top losers, contributing to the overall market decline.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's market session was primarily driven by expiry-day volatility, which intensified after the first monthly expiry under the Closing Auction Session, leading to a sharp decline in Indian equities. This domestic factor was compounded by global cues, particularly the cautious stance emerging from the US Federal Reserve's Jackson Hole symposium. Federal Reserve officials expressed concerns about stubbornly high inflation and hinted at the possibility of further interest rate hikes, which created an atmosphere of uncertainty and risk aversion among investors.

While US markets saw a boost from Nvidia's strong earnings and a revived AI rally, the Indian market's reaction was more aligned with the Fed's hawkish undertones and the local expiry dynamics. Sectors like PSU Bank, Media, and Metal bore the brunt of the selling pressure, while Pharma showed some resilience. The overall market sentiment remained subdued, with the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing at their day's lows.
