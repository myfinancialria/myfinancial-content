# Market Wrap — 15 September 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian markets witnessed a broad-based sell-off today, with benchmark indices closing significantly lower.
- Rising US Treasury yields and strengthening expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike globally weighed heavily on investor sentiment.
- Tata Chemicals Ltd. emerged as the top gainer in the Nifty 500, surging nearly 20%.
- Solar Industries India Ltd. was the biggest loser, plummeting over 13%.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support.
- F&O positioning data for today is unavailable and will populate from the next session.
- A sharp, momentary plunge in Nifty due to a CAS system crash added to intra-day volatility.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equities concluded the trading session deep in the red, marking a day of significant declines across the board. The Nifty 50 ended at 23118.60, down 1.19%, while the Bank Nifty closed at 55794.75, shedding 1.43%. Both benchmark indices saw a sharp sell-off into the close, with their closing prices matching their respective day's lows, indicating strong selling pressure throughout the latter half of the session. Midcap and Smallcap indices also bore the brunt, falling over 2% each.

| Index | Close | Change | High | Low |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nifty 50 | 23118.60 | -1.19% | 23592.85 | 23118.60 |
| Bank Nifty | 55794.75 | -1.43% | 56996.35 | 55794.75 |
| Midcap 100 | 60878.25 | -2.12% | 62516.95 | 60875.00 |
| Smallcap 100 | 19422.45 | -2.43% | 20025.20 | 19380.90 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, surged by 9.28% to 13.43, reflecting increased uncertainty and fear among investors.

## What moved the market — the factors
The primary driver behind today's market downturn was a confluence of negative global cues, particularly the relentless rise in US Treasury yields and heightened expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike. The US 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.041%, its highest since July 2007, fueled by rising crude oil prices and a hawkish outlook from the Fed. This led to a cautious mood on Wall Street, with US stocks trading lower.

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued to pull money out of Indian equities, registering net sales of ₹931 crore today. This persistent selling by FIIs, coupled with their withdrawal of over ₹5,109 crore from Indian government bonds in the last three days amid global uncertainty, contributed significantly to the bearish sentiment. While Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided some counter-balance with net purchases of ₹1,968 crore, their buying was not enough to offset the broader selling pressure.

Adding to the intra-day volatility, the Nifty experienced a sharp 2% plunge in indicative price within seconds during the closing auction session, attributed to a "CAS crash" before recovering some losses. This technical glitch further unnerved market participants. Sectorally, heavyweight sectors like Realty, Metal, and PSU Banks witnessed significant declines, dragging down the broader market.

## Sectors — winners and losers
Only the IT sector managed to close in the green today, while most other sectors faced heavy selling pressure.

## News that moved stocks
**Global Economic Concerns & US Fed Outlook**
- **US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Rising Bond Yields**: US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent attributed rising bond yields to 'global issues' during comments before a congressional hearing, highlighting the international backdrop impacting financial markets.
- **US Stocks Subdued Ahead of Fed Decision**: US stocks, including the Nasdaq and S&P 500, traded lower as AI shares steadied and investors remained cautious due to rising oil prices and Treasury yields, with focus shifting to an anticipated Federal Reserve rate hike.
- **US 10-year Treasury Yield Hits 2007 High**: The US 10-year Treasury yield surged to 5.041%, its highest level since July 2007, driven by increasing crude oil prices and strengthening expectations for a Federal Reserve rate hike, raising concerns about higher borrowing costs.
*Impact*: These global developments created a risk-off sentiment, leading to broad selling across the Indian market, particularly impacting FII flows.

**Regulatory & Market Infrastructure Updates**
- **SEBI Proposes Shorter Disaster Recovery Drills**: SEBI has proposed tighter business continuity and disaster recovery norms for market infrastructure institutions, including reducing mock drill durations to a minimum of four hours and strengthening data centre stress testing.
- **New Charges on UPI Payments for Stock Market Investments**: UPI payments for capital market transactions will now incur a nominal 0.02% Merchant Discount Rate, capped at ₹300, affecting mutual funds, securities, brokers, and dealers.
- **Nifty Plunges Due to CAS Crash**: The Nifty witnessed a sharp 2% plunge in indicative price within seconds during Tuesday’s closing auction session before recovering, raising concerns over the new CAS system and prompting a SEBI review.
- **NSE May Seek SEBI Nod to Trade on Own Platform**: NSE is exploring the possibility of seeking SEBI's approval to allow its shares to trade on its own platform after its IPO, despite being formally listed only on BSE.
*Impact*: While some regulatory changes aim to strengthen market infrastructure, the Nifty's CAS crash today created intra-day volatility and highlighted operational risks. New UPI charges could slightly increase transaction costs for investors.

**IPO Market Developments**
- **Digital Lending Platform Fibe Gets SEBI Nod for IPO**: Social Worth Technologies, the parent company of digital lending platform Fibe, has received SEBI approval for its Initial Public Offering, aiming to raise ₹750 crore through a fresh share issue.
- **Valmiki Leela Capital Eyes ₹3,000 Crore IPO Pipeline**: Valmiki Leela Capital has built a ₹3,000 crore IPO pipeline for the next 12-18 months, spanning renewable energy, power, pharmaceuticals, packaging, and electrical goods sectors.
*Impact*: These news items indicate a healthy pipeline for the primary market, potentially providing new investment avenues for investors.

**Real Estate Sector**
- **Embassy Developments Aims ₹4,500 Crore Revenue from Bengaluru Projects**: Embassy Developments is set to launch two new housing projects in Bengaluru, part of an 85-acre township, with an anticipated revenue of ₹4,500 crore.
*Impact*: Positive news for the real estate sector, but the sector as a whole was among the top losers today, suggesting broader market sentiment outweighed specific company news.

## Market breadth
The market breadth was overwhelmingly negative today, indicating a broad-based sell-off. Out of 498 stocks traded, only 62 advanced, while a significant 436 declined. This suggests that the selling pressure was not confined to a few large-cap stocks but was widespread across the market.

## FII / DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree in the Indian equity market today, recording net sales of ₹931 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net buyers, injecting ₹1,968 crore into the market.

| Investor Type | Net Flow (₹ crore) |
|---|---|
| FII | -931 |
| DII | +1,968 |

Looking at the 5-day history, FIIs have been consistent net sellers for the past four sessions, accumulating net outflows. This trend of FII selling, coupled with DIIs providing consistent buying support, suggests that while domestic investors are finding value, foreign investors are de-risking from Indian equities amidst global uncertainties.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was predominantly driven by negative global cues and a significant intra-day technical event. The relentless rise in US 10-year Treasury yields to a 17-year high of 5.041%, coupled with strengthening expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike, created a risk-off environment globally. This directly impacted FII flows into India, leading to sustained selling in both equities and government bonds.

Domestically, the Nifty experienced a sharp, momentary plunge of 462 points during the closing auction due to a "CAS crash," raising concerns about market infrastructure and adding to the overall volatility. While the index recovered some losses, the incident highlighted technical vulnerabilities. The combined effect of global headwinds and intra-day technical issues resulted in a broad-based market decline, with Realty, Metal, and PSU Bank sectors being the hardest hit.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will continue to be paramount. The ongoing concerns around rising US Treasury yields and the Federal Reserve's monetary policy stance will likely dictate market direction. US stocks closed lower today, and Asian markets will react to these developments overnight. Investors will also monitor crude oil prices, with Brent crude closing at $103.11 and WTI crude at $104.75. The USD/INR pair closed higher at 95.94, which could put further pressure on imports and FII flows. Key levels for Nifty 50 to watch would be today's low of 23118.60 as immediate support.
