# Pre-Market Brief — 21 September 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight, with US indices flat to positive, while Asian markets largely traded in the green.
*   GIFT Nifty indicates a positive opening bias for the Indian benchmark Nifty 50, pointing to a potential gap-up.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned net buyers yesterday after a streak of selling, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their buying support.
*   Crude oil prices witnessed a sharp decline of over 6% overnight, which is a significant positive for India as a major oil importer.
*   Nifty 50's CPR pivot sits at 23395.68, with 23476.57 as the immediate resistance (R1) and 23353.67 as the immediate support (S1).
*   Key risks for the day include rising US 10-year Treasury yields and ongoing concerns regarding global inflation and central bank policies.

Overnight, US markets closed with a mixed performance, with the Dow Jones seeing a slight dip, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq and the broader S&P 500 managed marginal gains. This morning, Asian markets are largely positive, led by a strong rally in the Nikkei 225 and healthy gains in the Hang Seng and Shanghai indices. This mixed global sentiment, leaning positive from Asia, sets a cautiously optimistic tone for the Indian markets.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) saw a marginal depreciation against the US Dollar, closing at ₹95.81. The Dollar Index also strengthened slightly by 0.10% to 100.32. In commodities, Brent Crude and WTI Crude oil prices plunged significantly by over 6% each, a major development. Gold prices also declined by nearly 1%, while Silver saw a minor dip. The US 10-Year Treasury Yield rose by over 1% to hit 5.00%, indicating a hawkish stance from the US Federal Reserve.

**Interpretation:**
*   **USD/INR:** A stable to slightly depreciating Rupee is generally positive for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters as their dollar earnings translate to more rupees, but it could put pressure on importers.
*   **Dollar Index:** A strengthening Dollar Index suggests global risk aversion or expectations of tighter US monetary policy, which can sometimes lead to FII outflows from emerging markets like India.
*   **Brent/WTI Crude:** The sharp drop in crude oil prices is a significant positive for India, which imports over 80% of its oil. This could ease inflationary pressures, reduce the current account deficit, and benefit sectors like aviation, paints, and oil marketing companies (OMCs).
*   **Gold:** Declining gold prices often reflect a shift away from safe-haven assets, possibly indicating increased risk appetite or pressure from a stronger dollar and rising yields.
*   **US 10Y Yield:** The rise in US 10-Year Treasury yields to 5.00% is a key concern. Higher US yields make dollar-denominated assets more attractive, potentially drawing capital away from emerging markets and increasing the cost of borrowing globally. This could pose a risk to FII flows into India.

**Interpretation:**
*   **Nifty:** The highest Call OI at 23500 suggests that option sellers expect the Nifty to face a strong ceiling around this level. Conversely, the highest Put OI at 23400 and 23300 indicates that option buyers are defending these levels as strong support. A PCR of 1.17 is above 1, leaning towards a supportive or bullish-to-neutral sentiment, suggesting more put writing than call writing. Combining with technicals, Nifty's immediate range could be between 23300-23500.
*   **Bank Nifty:** The highest Call OI at 58000 and 57500 suggests significant resistance at these higher levels. The highest Put OI at 57500 and 56000 indicates these as crucial support zones. A PCR of 0.93 is below 1, indicating a slightly cautious or bearish-to-neutral sentiment, with more call writing than put writing. Bank Nifty's immediate range could be between 56000-57500.
*   **Sensex:** The highest Call OI at 75000 and 76000 acts as strong resistance. The highest Put OI at 74500 and 74700 suggests solid support. A PCR of 1.22 is supportive, indicating bullish-to-neutral sentiment. Sensex's immediate range could be between 74500-75000.

Overall, the option chain data, combined with technical levels, suggests that Nifty has immediate support around 23300-23400 and resistance around 23500. Bank Nifty faces resistance near 57500-58000 and finds support near 56000-56500. Sensex has strong support around 74500-74700 and resistance around 75000-75200. These are the levels to respect today.

## Stock & sector news that matters

*   **Tata Group Boardroom Battle:** Amidst the ongoing dispute between Tata Trusts and Tata Sons over N Chandrasekaran's reappointment, Tata Group stocks like TCS and Tata Chemicals could remain volatile. Technical analysts have highlighted key resistance levels and bearish signals for some of these stocks.
    **Why it matters:** This news could lead to increased volatility and price swings in major Tata Group companies, affecting investor sentiment across the conglomerate's listed entities.
*   **Crude Oil Plunge:** Brent and WTI crude oil prices fell by over 6% overnight. This significant drop is a major positive for India, a net oil importer.
    **Why it matters:** Lower crude oil prices can ease inflation, reduce the government's import bill, and improve profit margins for sectors heavily reliant on crude oil, such as aviation (e.g., IndiGo, SpiceJet), paints (e.g., Asian Paints, Berger Paints), and oil marketing companies (e.g., IOC, BPCL, HPCL). This could provide a tailwind for these sectors.
*   **IT Stocks Under Pressure (OFSS, Wipro, Infosys, Coforge):** Shares of Oracle Financial Services Software (OFSS) tumbled over 7%, while other major IT stocks like Infosys, Wipro, and Coforge also saw declines of up to 2%. This pressure follows reports of Oracle's $18 billion data centre debt pressure.
    **Why it matters:** This news could weigh on the broader IT sector, especially companies with direct or indirect exposure to Oracle's business or similar debt-related concerns. Investors will be watching for any contagion effect on other large-cap IT players.
*   **NMDC Shares Rally on Net-Zero Target:** NMDC shares rallied up to 2% after the company announced a target to achieve net-zero operational emissions by 2047, covering Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions.
    **Why it matters:** This is positive news for NMDC, potentially attracting ESG-focused investors and improving its long-term sustainability outlook. It could also set a precedent for other public sector undertakings (PSUs) in the mining sector.
*   **HAL Shares Rise on Platform Handover:** Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd (HAL) shares rose 2% after the company handed over three indigenously developed platforms, including the Dhruv-NG helicopter, to the Indian Air Force (IAF) and Pawan Hans.
    **Why it matters:** This highlights HAL's execution capabilities and its role in India's defence indigenisation efforts, which is positive for the stock and the broader defence manufacturing sector.
*   **GAIL Gas IPO Process Begins:** GAIL Gas, a wholly-owned subsidiary of GAIL (India) Ltd, has initiated the process to appoint bankers for an IPO of up to ₹3,100 crore.
    **Why it matters:** This indicates a potential future listing in the city gas distribution space, which could create interest in the sector and potentially unlock value for GAIL (India) Ltd shareholders.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
*   **US 10-Year Treasury Yields:** The continued rise in US 10-year yields to 5.00% is a critical factor. Markets typically react with caution to rising US yields, as they can signal tighter global financial conditions and potentially lead to FII outflows from emerging markets.
*   **US Fed's Kashkari Comments:** Minneapolis Fed President Neel Kashkari's statement that US inflation is still too high despite rate hikes reinforces a hawkish stance. Such comments from central bank officials can increase volatility as markets price in future rate hike expectations.
*   **Global Market Watch (US-China talks, UN General Assembly, AI risks):** Investors will be tracking these geopolitical and technological developments. Geopolitical tensions or significant policy announcements from major global events can introduce uncertainty and volatility into markets.
*   **RBI Bond Sales:** The Reserve Bank of India's active bond sales are influencing the domestic bond market. While Indian bonds remained calm yesterday due to falling oil prices, RBI actions can impact liquidity and domestic interest rates, which in turn affect equity market sentiment.
*   **Micron Earnings:** Micron's upcoming September 30 earnings will be closely watched for insights into AI-driven memory demand. Strong or weak results from major global tech players can influence the sentiment towards the broader technology sector, including Indian IT stocks.
*   **F&O Expiry:** While not explicitly stated for today, the option chain data implies an upcoming F&O expiry. Expiry weeks typically see increased volatility and position adjustments as traders roll over or close their derivative contracts.

Indian markets are poised for a potentially positive opening, largely buoyed by favourable Asian cues and a significant drop in crude oil prices. However, the rising US 10-year Treasury yields and ongoing FII selling trend over the past week (despite yesterday's buying) warrant caution.
