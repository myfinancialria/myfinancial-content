# Pre-Market Brief — 08 September 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets displayed a mixed-to-negative tone, with US indices closing lower and Asian markets seeing varied performance.
*   GIFT Nifty is signalling a cautious start for Indian equities, pointing to a potential gap-down.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers yesterday, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their supportive stance.
*   Rising crude oil prices and copper hitting record highs are significant headwinds for the Indian economy and specific sectors.
*   Nifty's immediate resistance is seen around 23700, with strong support at 23600.
*   The day's main risk stems from elevated global commodity prices and their potential impact on inflation and corporate margins.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight. Major US indices like the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 closed in negative territory, reflecting a cautious sentiment among investors. In Asia, the Nikkei 225 saw a significant decline, while the Hang Seng also edged lower. The Shanghai Composite, however, managed to close with modest gains. The FTSE 100 in Europe also registered a slight positive close.

While some Asian markets showed resilience, the lack of strong positive cues globally could lead to profit booking or a subdued start for domestic indices.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) weakened against the US Dollar, trading at ₹94.75, a rise of 0.34%. The Dollar Index data was unavailable. In commodities, both Brent Crude and WTI Crude saw significant jumps, with Brent rising 2.50% to $98.69 and WTI up 2.93% to $94.16. Gold prices also moved higher by 0.47% to $4450.70, and Silver gained 1.52% to $67.05. The US 10-Year Yield increased by 0.46% to 4.78%.

**Interpretation:** A rising USD/INR is generally a positive for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters as their dollar earnings translate to more rupees, but it puts pressure on importers. The sharp increase in crude oil prices is a significant concern for India, a major oil importer, as it could fuel inflation, impact current account deficit, and put pressure on oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors. Rising US 10-year yields could make emerging markets like India less attractive for foreign capital, posing a risk to FII flows. Higher gold and silver prices suggest some safe-haven demand or inflationary concerns.

## Stock & sector news that matters

**Copper hits record high, UltraTech’s Ultravolt on the offensive: A double whammy for cable makers in FY27?**
Copper prices have reached unprecedented levels globally, impacting manufacturing costs. Simultaneously, UltraTech's Ultravolt is aggressively entering the Indian wires and cables sector.
**Why it matters:** This news signals a potential double whammy for Indian cable and wire manufacturers. Rising copper prices will directly increase their raw material costs, potentially squeezing margins. Increased competition from a large player like UltraTech could further pressure pricing and market share in the sector, affecting companies like Polycab India, Havells India, and KEI Industries.

**PVR Inox’s ₹300-crore buyback opens on Sept 10: Check buyback price, key dates, entitlement ratio**
PVR Inox's share buyback offer of ₹300 crore opens on September 10 and closes on September 17, with a buyback price of ₹1,450 per share, which is over 25% above the current market price.
**Why it matters:** This is a significant corporate action for PVR Inox shareholders. A buyback at a premium typically signals management confidence and can provide a floor for the stock price. Eligible shareholders might see an opportunity to tender their shares at a higher price, potentially leading to short-term price volatility around the buyback dates.

**FCNR(B) windfall for banks explained: Who got highest inflows and what it means for investors**
FCNR(B) deposits offer Indian banks a cheaper and balance-sheet-efficient source of funding, though their finite tenor and initial deployment challenges could pressure margins. ICICI Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank reportedly saw high inflows.
**Why it matters:** This news is relevant for the banking sector. While FCNR(B) deposits provide a cost-effective funding source, banks need to manage their deployment effectively to avoid margin pressure. Banks that have successfully attracted these deposits, like ICICI Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank, might see some benefits in their funding costs, but the overall impact on profitability will depend on their asset-liability management.

**FPIs see autos, consumer durables race past staples during festive season**
Foreign investors are increasing their holdings in auto and consumer durable sectors, while reducing exposure to food, beverages, and tobacco stocks, anticipating strong festive season demand.
**Why it matters:** This indicates a clear sectorial preference by FPIs, driven by expectations of a robust festive season. Companies in the auto sector (e.g., Maruti Suzuki, Tata Motors, M&M) and consumer durables (e.g., Voltas, Blue Star) could see increased buying interest and positive sentiment. Conversely, companies in the food, beverages, and tobacco sectors might experience some selling pressure.

**Biocon shares rise 2% on 10-year Pertuzumab supply deal for breast cancer therapy in Brazil**
Biocon shares gained after securing a 10-year Pertuzumab supply contract in Brazil, with a consortium receiving full allocation under Brazil’s PDP programme.
**Why it matters:** This is positive news for Biocon, highlighting its strong position in the biosimilars market and expanding its international footprint. A long-term supply deal provides revenue visibility and could contribute positively to the company's financials, potentially boosting investor confidence in the stock.

**HFCL, Sterlite Tech shares jump 5%: What’s driving up to 705% multibagger run?**
HFCL and Sterlite Technologies hit their upper circuits, extending their significant rallies in 2026, driven by strong business updates.
**Why it matters:** This highlights strong momentum in specific telecom and optical fiber related stocks. Positive business updates are clearly resonating with investors, leading to sharp price appreciation. These stocks could remain in focus, and their performance might indicate broader sentiment towards the digital infrastructure sector.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
*   **Crude Oil Price Movement:** The sharp rise in Brent and WTI crude oil prices is a critical factor to watch today. Markets typically react negatively to sustained increases in crude oil, as it fuels inflation, impacts corporate margins (especially for OMCs, airlines, and paint companies), and can lead to a widening current account deficit.
*   **RBI Liquidity Management:** As per news, the Reserve Bank of India is tightening liquidity to combat rising inflation. Markets generally become cautious when the RBI tightens liquidity, as it can lead to higher borrowing costs for businesses and impact economic growth. Bond markets are particularly sensitive to such moves.
*   **FPI Sectoral Flows:** The observed shift by FPIs towards auto and consumer durables and away from staples ahead of the festive season will be important to monitor. Such clear sectoral preferences can drive significant price movements in the identified sectors.
*   **Global Market Cues:** Given the mixed global sentiment, especially the weakness in US and some Asian markets, Indian markets will closely watch how global indices perform through the day. Negative global cues can often trigger profit booking in domestic markets.

2.  Nifty's ability to hold the 23600 support level and its attempt to cross the 23700 resistance.

5.  Specific stock movements in PVR Inox (due to buyback), Biocon (Brazil deal), and cable/wire makers (due to copper prices and new competition).

## Bottom line
Indian markets are likely to open with a cautious to negative bias today, influenced by subdued global cues and a negative signal from GIFT Nifty. The key levels of 23600 for Nifty support and 23700 for resistance will be crucial in determining the day's trading range. Investors should remain vigilant about rising crude oil and other commodity prices, which pose a significant risk to the broader economy and corporate earnings.
