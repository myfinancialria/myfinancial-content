# Choosing Your ITR Form: ITR-1, ITR-2, or ITR-4?

**TL;DR**
- Selecting the correct Income Tax Return (ITR) form is crucial for accurate tax filing and avoiding penalties.
- ITR-1 (Sahaj) is for individuals with simple income sources like salary, one house property, and other basic income.
- ITR-4 (Sugam) is designed for individuals, HUFs, and firms (other than LLPs) opting for presumptive taxation.
- ITR-2 is for individuals and HUFs who do not have income from business or profession but have more complex income like capital gains.
- Misfiling an ITR form can lead to the return being treated as defective, requiring rectification.

## What this means in plain terms

Every year, as a taxpayer in India, you need to inform the Income Tax Department about your earnings and the taxes you've paid. This is done by filing an Income Tax Return (ITR). The department provides various forms, each tailored for different types of taxpayers and income sources. Choosing the right form is not just a formality; it's essential for accurately reporting your income and ensuring your tax compliance.

If you pick the wrong form, your return might be considered invalid, leading to complications or even penalties. This article will help you understand the primary differences between ITR-1, ITR-2, and ITR-4, which are the most commonly used forms for individual taxpayers, so you can confidently select the correct one for the Assessment Year (AY) 2026-27, pertaining to the Financial Year (FY) 2025-26. The Income Tax Department has already enabled online filing and Excel utilities for these forms, with filing having commenced from May 15.

## ITR-1 (Sahaj): For Simple Income Sources

ITR-1, also known as Sahaj, is the simplest of the income tax return forms and is meant for resident individuals with straightforward income profiles. If your financial life is relatively uncomplicated, this is likely the form for you.

### Who can use ITR-1?
You are eligible to file ITR-1 if you are a resident individual (not a Resident Not Ordinarily Resident - RNOR, or a Non-Resident Indian - NRI) and your total income for the financial year 2025-26 does not exceed ₹50 lakhs. Your income sources must be limited to:
*   Income from Salary or Pension.
*   Income from One House Property (excluding cases where loss brought forward from previous years, or loss to be carried forward).
*   Income from Other Sources (like interest from savings accounts, fixed deposits, family pension, etc., excluding winnings from lottery or horse races).
*   Agricultural income up to ₹5,000.

### Who cannot use ITR-1?
You cannot use ITR-1 if you:
*   Are a Director in a company.
*   Have held unlisted equity shares at any time during the financial year.
*   Are an NRI or RNOR.
*   Have total income exceeding ₹50 lakhs.
*   Have income from more than one house property.
*   Have income from Capital Gains (e.g., selling shares, mutual funds, property).
*   Have income from Business or Profession.
*   Have foreign assets or foreign income.
*   Have agricultural income exceeding ₹5,000.
*   Have income from winnings from lottery or horse races.
*   Are filing for an individual having income under section 115BBDA (dividend income exceeding ₹10 lakhs) or section 115BTC (long-term capital gains on certain specified securities).

## ITR-4 (Sugam): For Presumptive Taxation

ITR-4, or Sugam, is designed for individuals, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), and firms (other than Limited Liability Partnerships or LLPs) who are residents and opt for the presumptive income scheme under Section 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE of the Income Tax Act. This scheme simplifies tax filing by allowing eligible taxpayers to declare income at a prescribed rate, rather than maintaining detailed books of accounts.

### Who can use ITR-4?
You can use ITR-4 if you are a resident individual, HUF, or firm (other than LLP) and your total income does not exceed ₹50 lakhs, and you have income from:
*   Business income computed under the presumptive taxation scheme of Section 44AD (for eligible businesses with turnover up to ₹2 crores).
*   Professional income computed under the presumptive taxation scheme of Section 44ADA (for eligible professionals with gross receipts up to ₹75 lakhs).
*   Income from plying, hiring, or leasing goods carriages computed under the presumptive taxation scheme of Section 44AE.
*   Salary/Pension.
*   One House Property (excluding cases where loss brought forward from previous years, or loss to be carried forward).
*   Other Sources (excluding winnings from lottery or horse races).
*   Agricultural income up to ₹5,000.

### Who cannot use ITR-4?
You cannot use ITR-4 if you:
*   Have total income exceeding ₹50 lakhs.
*   Are a Director in a company.
*   Have held unlisted equity shares at any time during the financial year.
*   Are an NRI or RNOR.
*   Have income from more than one house property.
*   Have income from Capital Gains.
*   Have foreign assets or foreign income.
*   Are not opting for presumptive taxation under Section 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE.
*   Have agricultural income exceeding ₹5,000.

## ITR-2: For Individuals with Complex Income (No Business/Profession)

ITR-2 is a more comprehensive form for individuals and HUFs who do not have income from business or profession but have income from sources that make them ineligible for ITR-1. This form caters to a wider array of income types.

### Who can use ITR-2?
You should file ITR-2 if you are an individual or HUF (including NRIs and RNORs) and do not have income from business or profession, but your income sources include:
*   Income from Salary or Pension.
*   Income from House Property (can be more than one property, or include brought forward/carried forward losses).
*   Income from Capital Gains (e.g., from sale of shares, mutual funds, property, etc.).
*   Income from Other Sources (including winnings from lottery, horse races, foreign income, etc.).
*   Foreign assets/income.
*   Are a Director in a company.
*   Have held unlisted equity shares at any time during the financial year.
*   Agricultural income exceeding ₹5,000.
*   Total income exceeding ₹50 lakhs.

### Who cannot use ITR-2?
You cannot use ITR-2 if you have:
*   Income from Business or Profession (even if it's a small amount). In such cases, you would typically need to file ITR-3 or ITR-4 (if opting for presumptive taxation).

## A real example

Let's consider **Meena, 42, ₹35L CTC, Mumbai**. Meena is a salaried employee with a Cost to Company (CTC) of ₹35 lakhs per annum. She also owns two residential properties, one of which is rented out, generating ₹3 lakhs in rental income annually. The other property is self-occupied. Additionally, she sold some equity mutual funds during the year, resulting in a long-term capital gain of ₹2.5 lakhs. She also has interest income from fixed deposits totalling ₹80,000.

Let's break down her income sources:
1.  **Salary Income:** ₹35 lakhs (eligible for ITR-1, ITR-2, ITR-4 if other conditions met).
2.  **Income from House Property:** Two properties, one rented out. This makes her ineligible for ITR-1 (which allows only one house property) and ITR-4 (same restriction).
3.  **Capital Gains:** ₹2.5 lakhs from equity mutual funds. This immediately makes her ineligible for ITR-1 and ITR-4.
4.  **Income from Other Sources:** ₹80,000 interest from FDs (eligible for all forms, but other factors restrict her).

Based on her income profile, specifically having income from more than one house property and, more critically, income from capital gains, Meena is **ineligible to file ITR-1 or ITR-4**. She must file **ITR-2**.

If Meena had also run a small proprietorship business with a turnover of ₹40 lakhs and opted for presumptive taxation, she would then be ineligible for ITR-2 and would need to file ITR-4. However, if that business did not opt for presumptive taxation, she would need to file ITR-3.

## FAQ

### What happens if I file the wrong ITR form?
If you file an incorrect ITR form, the Income Tax Department may treat your return as "defective". You will receive a notice under Section 139(9) of the Income Tax Act, asking you to rectify the defect by filing a revised return with the correct form. Failure to do so within the specified timeframe can lead to your return being treated as not filed.

### Can NRIs use ITR-1 or ITR-4?
No, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are specifically excluded from using ITR-1 (Sahaj) and ITR-4 (Sugam). NRIs typically need to file ITR-2 if they don't have business income, or ITR-3 if they do.

### What is presumptive taxation?
Presumptive taxation is a scheme under the Income Tax Act that allows eligible small businesses and professionals to declare income at a prescribed percentage of their gross receipts or turnover, without maintaining detailed books of accounts. This simplifies compliance for them.

### I have both salary and capital gains. Which form should I use?
If you have both salary income and income from capital gains, you cannot use ITR-1 or ITR-4. You would typically need to file ITR-2, provided you do not have income from a business or profession.

### What if I have foreign assets or foreign income?
If you are a resident individual and have foreign assets or any income from a source outside India, you are not eligible to file ITR-1 or ITR-4. You would generally need to file ITR-2 (if no business income) or ITR-3 (if you have business income).

### What is the deadline for filing ITR-1, ITR-2, and ITR-4?
For most individual taxpayers, including those filing ITR-1, ITR-2, and ITR-4, the deadline for filing income tax returns for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) is typically July 31st of the assessment year. However, it is always advisable to check the official Income Tax Department website (incometax.gov.in) for any specific notifications or changes to deadlines.
