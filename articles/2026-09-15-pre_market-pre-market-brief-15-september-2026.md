# Pre-Market Brief — 15 September 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets closed mostly lower, with US and European indices seeing declines, indicating a cautious global tone.
- GIFT Nifty is signalling a significant gap-down open for the Indian benchmark indices.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued to be net sellers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained consistent net buyers.
- The NSE IPO is set to open on September 17, with potential windfalls for PSU shareholders and strong grey market premium signals.
- Nifty 50's key resistance sits at 23400-23500 (Call OI) and 23496 (R1), with support around 23300 (Put OI) and 23183 (S1).
- The upcoming US Federal Reserve rate decision and global bond market turmoil are key risks to watch.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Global markets largely ended in negative territory yesterday, reflecting a cautious sentiment across major economies. US indices like the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all closed lower, with tech stocks showing particular weakness. European markets also saw declines, with the FTSE 100 falling almost 1%. In Asia this morning, the Nikkei 225 was largely flat, while the Hang Seng and Shanghai Composite also traded lower.

**Interpretation:** The overall negative sentiment across major global indices, especially the US and Europe, suggests a cautious to negative start for Indian markets. Asian markets also largely followed suit, reinforcing the weak global tone.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) depreciated significantly against the US Dollar, closing at ₹95.93, up 0.95%. The Dollar Index also strengthened slightly by 0.12% to 99.58. In commodities, Brent Crude saw a notable drop of 3.13% to $102.37, while WTI Crude rose 1.87% to $103.29. Gold prices edged lower by 0.52% to $4329.40, and Silver was marginally down by 0.01% to $63.51. The US 10-Year Yield dipped by 0.28% to 4.96%.

**Interpretation:** A rising USD/INR is a headwind for Indian importers and could fuel imported inflation, while providing a tailwind for IT and pharma exporters. The drop in Brent crude is a positive for India, potentially easing concerns on import bills and inflation. The slight dip in US 10Y yields might offer some relief, but the underlying "bond market turmoil" mentioned in global news suggests broader uncertainty.

## GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling
The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 23295.00, down 0.92% from the previous Nifty 50 close.

**Interpretation:** This indicates a significant gap-down opening for the Nifty 50 when the Indian market opens today.

## FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, offloading equities worth ₹931 crore in the cash segment yesterday. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, remained strong buyers, pumping in ₹1,968 crore.

| Date         | FII Net (₹ crore) | DII Net (₹ crore) |
| :----------- | :---------------- | :---------------- |
| 2026-09-11   | -931              | +1,968            |

Over the last five sessions, FIIs have been net sellers in four out of five sessions, while DIIs have consistently been net buyers, providing a crucial domestic cushion to the market.

**Interpretation:** The continued FII selling indicates foreign investors are pulling out funds, while DIIs are consistently stepping in to absorb this selling pressure, providing a domestic cushion. This dynamic suggests that while foreign sentiment is negative, domestic buying is preventing a sharper decline.

## Stock & sector news that matters

**NSE IPO jackpot: 10 PSUs could pocket Rs 12,802 crore from stake sale**
The upcoming NSE IPO could provide a significant windfall of around ₹12,802 crore to 10 public-sector shareholders by selling their shares at the upper price band of ₹1,785.
**Why it matters:** This news highlights the substantial value unlocking potential of the NSE IPO for public sector undertakings, which could lead to increased liquidity or investment capacity for these PSUs. It also underscores the market's interest in the IPO.

**NSE IPO set to open on September 17; GMP signals 11% listing gain**
The ₹22,561.57-crore NSE IPO is scheduled to open for subscription from September 17 to September 21, with its current grey-market premium (GMP) suggesting an 11% listing gain.
**Why it matters:** This is a major market event. A strong IPO opening and listing could boost overall market sentiment, especially for the broader financial services sector. The GMP indicates robust investor interest.

**Cochin Shipyard shares tumble 12% over 2 sessions**
Shares of Cochin Shipyard have fallen sharply for two consecutive sessions after the management indicated a lower EBITDA margin target for the next two financial years.
**Why it matters:** This is negative news for Cochin Shipyard and could put pressure on other defence or shipbuilding stocks. Investors will be watching for clarity on future margin outlooks.

**Solar Industries shares crash 9% after acquisition of South Africa’s Omnia Holdings for Rs 12,951 crore**
Solar Industries shares fell sharply after the company announced an all-cash acquisition of South Africa's Omnia Holdings for ₹12,951 crore.
**Why it matters:** This significant acquisition, especially an all-cash deal, can raise concerns about funding and potential dilution or debt for Solar Industries. The stock's reaction suggests investor apprehension about the deal's immediate impact.

**Hexaware Technologies stock gains 5% on Project NANDA Fellowship collaboration to advance AI agents**
Hexaware Technologies shares rose after the company partnered with Project NANDA Fellowship, a global program focused on developing open-source infrastructure for AI agents.
**Why it matters:** This indicates positive sentiment towards IT companies making strides in Artificial Intelligence (AI). It could provide a tailwind for Hexaware and potentially other mid-cap IT stocks focused on AI solutions.

**Paytm shares drop 3% after govt shields UPI payments only up to Rs 2,000 from charges**
Paytm shares declined after the government directed banks and payment providers not to charge for UPI transactions up to ₹2,000, raising uncertainty over charges for higher-value transactions.
**Why it matters:** This policy decision could impact the revenue models of payment service providers like Paytm, particularly if it extends to higher-value transactions or affects their ability to monetise UPI services. It creates uncertainty for the fintech sector.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
-   **US Federal Reserve Rate Decision:** A global bond market selloff has put the Fed's rate decision under the spotlight. Markets typically react with heightened volatility around central bank policy announcements, especially when there is uncertainty regarding rate hikes or pauses. A hawkish stance could lead to global equity outflows, while a dovish tone might provide relief.
-   **NSE IPO Opening (September 17):** The IPO of India's largest stock exchange is a significant event. Large IPOs often generate considerable market buzz and can attract liquidity, potentially impacting other segments in the short term. A strong listing can boost overall market sentiment.

1.  **Global Cues and GIFT Nifty Signal:** The negative global market closes and a significant gap-down indicated by GIFT Nifty will set a cautious tone for the open.

## Bottom line
Indian markets are poised for a cautious to negative open today, largely influenced by weak global cues and a significant gap-down signal from GIFT Nifty. The continued FII selling, though cushioned by DII buying, suggests underlying foreign investor apprehension. Key levels for Nifty around 23300-23400 will be crucial to watch, with a narrow CPR suggesting potential for a trending day. Investors should remain vigilant about global developments, particularly the upcoming US Fed rate decision, and monitor stock-specific news.
