# Market Wrap — 30 September 2026

**TL;DR**
- The Indian benchmark Nifty 50 ended lower for the third consecutive session, while the Bank Nifty managed to close in the green, indicating a mixed day for the broader market.
- Persistent and heavy selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) was a significant drag on the market, overshadowing positive global cues from softer US inflation data.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equities witnessed a mixed trading session today, with the benchmark Nifty 50 extending its losses for the third straight day, while the Bank Nifty showed resilience. The Nifty 50 opened lower and remained under pressure throughout the day, closing near its day's low. In contrast, the Bank Nifty started with a slight gain and maintained its positive momentum, closing higher. The broader markets, represented by the Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100, managed to eke out marginal gains, suggesting some underlying strength despite the Nifty's decline.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market today presented a dichotomy, with the Nifty 50 succumbing to selling pressure while the Bank Nifty and broader markets showed some strength. The primary factor weighing down the Nifty was the relentless selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs), who offloaded a substantial ₹9,980 crore in the cash segment. This marked the fourth consecutive session of significant FII outflows, with foreign investors pulling out over ₹26,000 crore from Indian equities in just three sessions, intensifying September's market correction.

Despite this domestic headwind, global cues offered a glimmer of hope. Softer-than-expected inflation data from the US led to a retreat in the US dollar and a pull-back in US Treasury yields, easing fears of aggressive interest rate hikes by the Federal Reserve. This positive global sentiment helped US stocks surge, but its impact on the Indian benchmark Nifty was largely offset by the heavy FII selling.

Domestically, there was clear sector rotation. While sectors like Pharma, Metal, and FMCG witnessed significant declines, the Media, Realty, and Banking sectors posted healthy gains. The Healthcare sector, in particular, saw a sharp correction, with major hospital chains like Fortis Healthcare, Apollo Hospitals, Max Healthcare, and Aster DM Quality Care featuring prominently among the top losers. Conversely, construction-related stocks like IRCON and RITES, along with media major Sun TV Network, saw strong buying interest. The consistent buying by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), who infused ₹6,953 crore, provided crucial support, preventing a steeper fall in the broader market.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was largely influenced by a combination of global cues and persistent domestic institutional flows. The most significant global event was the release of softer-than-expected inflation data from the US, specifically the Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index. This data eased concerns about aggressive interest rate hikes by the US Federal Reserve, leading to a retreat in the US dollar and a pull-back in US Treasury yields. Consequently, US stocks surged higher, providing a positive backdrop for global markets.

However, the Indian market, particularly the Nifty 50, could not fully capitalize on these positive global cues due to the continued heavy selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs). Their substantial outflows of nearly ₹10,000 crore overshadowed the global optimism and kept the benchmark index under pressure. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) stepped in with significant buying, which helped the broader market and specific sectors like Banking, Media, and Realty to perform well. The Healthcare sector, however, saw a sharp correction across major hospital stocks like Fortis and Apollo Hospitals, indicating sector-specific weakness. Additionally, a block deal in Avalon Technologies, where promoters divested a 6% stake worth ₹871 crore, was a specific stock-moving event.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues appear mixed but with a positive tilt from the US market's rally on easing inflation fears and falling bond yields. However, the persistent FII selling remains a key concern for the Indian market. Investors will be closely watching the FII flow data. Key sectors to watch include IT, as major companies like TCS, Infosys, and HCL Tech are set to announce their Q2 FY27 results in October, which could trigger significant movements. On the technical front, analysts suggest that the Nifty 50 index would need a decisive breach above the 23,100 zone to establish conviction and clarity.
