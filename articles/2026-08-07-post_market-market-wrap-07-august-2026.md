# Market Wrap — 07 August 2026

## How the market behaved today
Indian equities witnessed a day of cautious trading, with frontline indices Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing lower. The Nifty 50 ended at 24570.65, down 0.27%, after trading in a narrow range. The Bank Nifty also saw declines, closing at 57746.45, down 0.55%. In contrast, the broader market showed a mixed trend, with the Nifty Midcap 100 posting gains, while the Nifty Smallcap 100 ended slightly in the red.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market today was largely influenced by a combination of factors, including foreign institutional investor (FII) outflows and concerns stemming from global geopolitical developments. FIIs turned net sellers in the cash market, offloading shares worth ₹943 crore, which contributed to the negative sentiment in large-cap indices. Domestic institutional investors (DIIs), however, provided strong support, pumping in ₹2,883 crore, helping to cushion the fall and providing buoyancy to the midcap segment.

Concerns over a potential peace deal in the Middle East and perceived weaknesses in the financial sector also weighed on investor sentiment. This was evident in the underperformance of the Banking sector, which was the top sectoral loser. Heavyweight stock Bajaj Finance Ltd. saw a significant decline of 5.84%, impacting the broader financial services space. Conversely, the Automobile and IT sectors showed strength, with stocks like Tata Technologies Ltd. and Samvardhana Motherson International Ltd. among the top gainers, indicating some sector-specific buying interest.

## Sectors — winners and losers
The market saw a clear divergence in sectoral performance today, with Auto and IT leading the gains, while Banking and Realty faced selling pressure.

**Top 3 Loser Sectors:**

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was primarily driven by a combination of FII outflows and global cues, particularly concerns surrounding a potential peace deal in the Middle East and weaknesses observed in the financial sector. The news of the "West Asia crisis" also impacted corporate earnings, as seen in JK Tyre & Industries reporting a significant profit decline due to soaring raw material prices. The Banking sector, in particular, felt the pressure, with Bank Nifty closing down and heavyweight Bajaj Finance Ltd. being a top loser. On the other hand, the RBI's decision to hold the repo rate steady continued to underpin the residential real estate sector, as evidenced by strong luxury home sales in Mumbai.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead, global cues will continue to play a significant role. The US 10-year yield saw an increase, while crude oil prices (Brent and WTI) were down. Gold and Silver saw notable gains. The USD/INR closed slightly higher. Investors will monitor these global indicators for direction. Domestically, the ongoing earnings season will continue to provide stock-specific action. Key levels for Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty will be watched closely, with any further FII selling potentially putting pressure on large caps, while DII buying could continue to support mid and small-cap segments.
