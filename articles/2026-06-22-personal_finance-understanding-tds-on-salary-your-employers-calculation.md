# Understanding TDS on Salary: Your Employer's Calculation

**TL;DR**
-   From April 1st, 2026, TDS on salary is governed by the new Income Tax Act 2025, which replaced the 1961 Act.
-   Your employer estimates your total annual income, including salary, perquisites, and other taxable components.
-   They consider all eligible deductions under sections like 80C, 80D, and HRA exemptions to arrive at your net taxable income.
-   The applicable income tax slabs are then applied to this net taxable income to determine your total annual tax liability.
-   This annual tax liability is divided by 12 to calculate the monthly TDS amount to be deducted from your salary.

## What this means in plain terms

Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on salary is essentially a prepayment of your income tax. Your employer, as the deductor, is legally obligated to deduct a certain portion of your salary each month and deposit it with the government on your behalf. This mechanism ensures a steady flow of tax revenue for the government and simplifies tax collection.

With the introduction of the Income Tax Act 2025, effective April 1st, 2026, the framework for this deduction has been updated. While the core tax rates and slabs remain largely consistent with previous provisions, the procedural aspects, relevant forms, and specific section numbers have been streamlined. Understanding how your employer calculates this monthly deduction is crucial for effective financial planning and ensuring your tax compliance.

## The Income Tax Act 2025 and Your Salary

The Income Tax Act 2025 marks a significant legislative update, succeeding the Income Tax Act 1961, which had been in force for over six decades. This new Act, effective from April 1st, 2026, aims to modernise and simplify India's direct tax regime. For salaried individuals, the most pertinent aspect is its impact on how TDS is calculated and reported by employers.

### Key Changes and Continuities

While the Act introduces updated section numbers and forms, it's important to note that the fundamental income tax rates and slab structures for individual taxpayers have largely been retained from the previous regime. This means that the actual tax burden, assuming the same income and deductions, should not drastically change for most individuals. The primary shift lies in the administrative and procedural aspects, which employers must now adhere to. These changes are detailed in notifications issued by the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT).

### Employer's Role in Compliance

Under the new Act, employers continue to play a pivotal role as tax deductors. They are responsible for accurately estimating your annual taxable income, applying the correct tax slabs, and deducting TDS monthly. They must also file quarterly TDS returns (Form 24Q) and issue Form 16 to employees, which provides a summary of salary paid and tax deducted. Adherence to the new Act's provisions is mandatory for all employers to avoid penalties.

## Components of Your Taxable Salary

To calculate TDS, your employer first assesses your total estimated annual income. This isn't just your basic salary; it includes various components as per your Cost-to-Company (CTC) structure. Understanding these components is the first step in comprehending your TDS calculation.

### Basic Salary and Allowances

Your basic salary is the fixed portion of your remuneration and is fully taxable. Beyond this, various allowances contribute to your gross salary:
*   **House Rent Allowance (HRA):** A portion of HRA can be exempt from tax, subject to certain conditions and actual rent paid.
*   **Special Allowances:** These are generally fully taxable unless specifically exempted under certain sections of the Income Tax Act 2025.
*   **Dearness Allowance (DA):** This allowance is usually fully taxable and is often linked to the cost of living index.
*   **Conveyance Allowance/Transport Allowance:** While earlier a specific exemption was available, its treatment can vary and might be fully taxable depending on current regulations.
*   **Leave Travel Allowance (LTA):** Exemption is available for the cost of travel incurred by an employee and their family for a holiday in India, subject to conditions.

### Perquisites and Other Incomes

Perquisites are non-cash benefits provided by the employer, such as company cars, rent-free accommodation, or club memberships. These are valued as per Income Tax rules and added to your taxable income. Additionally, if you declare other incomes to your employer (e.g., interest income from fixed deposits), they might also factor this into your total taxable income for TDS purposes.

## Deductions and Exemptions Employers Consider

Once your gross taxable income is estimated, your employer then reduces this amount by considering various deductions and exemptions available under the Income Tax Act 2025. This step is crucial as it directly impacts your net taxable income and, consequently, your monthly TDS.

### Section 80C, 80CCC, and 80CCD (1B)

This is one of the most widely used sections for tax savings. The employer will consider your declared investments and expenses under:
*   **Section 80C:** Covers investments like Employees' Provident Fund (EPF), Public Provident Fund (PPF), Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS), life insurance premiums, home loan principal repayment, and children's tuition fees. The maximum deduction under this section is ₹1.5 lakhs.
*   **Section 80CCC:** For contributions to certain pension funds.
*   **Section 80CCD (1B):** Allows an additional deduction of up to ₹50,000 for contributions to the National Pension System (NPS), over and above the 80C limit.

### Other Major Deductions

Employers also consider other significant deductions that you declare:
*   **Section 80D:** For health insurance premiums paid for yourself, your family, and parents. The limits vary based on age and whether the insured are senior citizens.
*   **Section 24(b):** Interest paid on a home loan for self-occupied property, up to ₹2 lakhs annually.
*   **Section 80E:** Interest paid on education loans.
*   **Section 80G:** Donations to certain approved charitable institutions.

It is important to provide proof of these investments and expenses to your employer, typically by specific deadlines, so they can accurately factor them into your TDS calculation. Without adequate proof, your employer may not consider these deductions, leading to a higher TDS.

## The TDS Calculation Process

Your employer follows a systematic process to arrive at your monthly TDS amount. This involves several steps, from estimating your annual income to applying the tax rates.

### Step-by-Step Methodology

1.  **Estimate Gross Annual Salary:** The employer projects your total salary for the entire financial year (April to March), including basic pay, allowances, perquisites, and any other taxable benefits.
2.  **Subtract Exemptions:** Exempt portions of allowances like HRA and LTA (if applicable and declared) are subtracted from the gross salary.
3.  **Deduct Professional Tax (if applicable):** Any professional tax paid by the employee is also deductible.
4.  **Consider Declared Deductions:** The employer takes into account all eligible deductions declared by you under various sections like 80C, 80D, 80CCD(1B), 24(b) (for home loan interest), etc., provided you submit the necessary proofs.
5.  **Arrive at Net Taxable Income:** After subtracting all applicable exemptions and deductions from the gross annual salary, the employer arrives at your estimated net taxable income.
6.  **Apply Income Tax Slabs:** The applicable income tax slab rates (as per the Income Tax Act 2025) are applied to this net taxable income to determine your total annual tax liability.
7.  **Add Surcharge and Cess:** If your income exceeds certain thresholds, a surcharge might be added. A Health and Education Cess (currently 4%) is applied to the total tax liability (including surcharge, if any).
8.  **Subtract Rebate (if applicable):** If your net taxable income is below a certain threshold (currently ₹7 lakhs under the new tax regime, or ₹5 lakhs under the old regime), you might be eligible for a tax rebate under Section 87A, reducing your tax liability to zero.
9.  **Calculate Monthly TDS:** The final annual tax liability is then divided by 12 (for 12 months in a financial year) to arrive at the monthly TDS amount to be deducted from your salary.

This process is dynamic; if there are changes in your salary, declared investments, or other factors during the year, your employer will re-calculate your TDS for the remaining months to ensure the correct amount is deducted by year-end.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 32, ₹22L CTC, Gurugram**, who is employed at a tech firm. Her annual salary breakdown is:
*   Basic Salary: ₹10,00,000
*   HRA: ₹4,00,000
*   Special Allowance: ₹6,00,000
*   Other Allowances: ₹2,00,000
*   Professional Tax: ₹2,500 (annual)

Priya has declared the following investments/expenses:
*   EPF Contribution (80C): ₹1,00,000
*   ELSS Investment (80C): ₹50,000
*   NPS Contribution (80CCD(1B)): ₹50,000
*   Health Insurance Premium (80D): ₹25,000
*   Rent Paid: ₹1,80,000 per annum

Let's calculate her estimated annual TDS (assuming she opts for the old tax regime, which allows for deductions):

**1. Gross Annual Salary:**
Basic + HRA + Special Allowance + Other Allowances = ₹10,00,000 + ₹4,00,000 + ₹6,00,000 + ₹2,00,000 = ₹22,00,000

**2. HRA Exemption Calculation:**
Least of the following is exempt:
a. Actual HRA received: ₹4,00,000
b. Actual rent paid less 10% of Basic Salary: ₹1,80,000 - (10% of ₹10,00,000) = ₹1,80,000 - ₹1,00,000 = ₹80,000
c. 50% of Basic Salary (for metro cities): 50% of ₹10,00,000 = ₹5,00,000
**Exempt HRA = ₹80,000**

**3. Income after HRA Exemption:**
₹22,00,000 - ₹80,000 = ₹21,20,000

**4. Deductions:**
*   **Section 80C:** EPF (₹1,00,000) + ELSS (₹50,000) = ₹1,50,000 (Max limit)
*   **Section 80CCD(1B):** NPS = ₹50,000 (Additional deduction)
*   **Section 80D:** Health Insurance = ₹25,000
*   **Professional Tax:** ₹2,500
**Total Deductions = ₹1,50,000 + ₹50,000 + ₹25,000 + ₹2,500 = ₹2,27,500**

**5. Net Taxable Income:**
₹21,20,000 - ₹2,27,500 = ₹18,92,500

**6. Tax Calculation (Old Regime Slabs for FY 2026-27 assumed to be similar to FY 2023-24 for illustration):**
*   Up to ₹2,50,000: Nil
*   ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000: 5% of (₹5,00,000 - ₹2,50,000) = 5% of ₹2,50,000 = ₹12,500
*   ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000: 20% of (₹10,00,000 - ₹5,00,000) = 20% of ₹5,00,000 = ₹1,00,000
*   ₹10,00,001 to ₹18,92,500: 30% of (₹18,92,500 - ₹10,00,000) = 30% of ₹8,92,500 = ₹2,67,750

**Total Tax = ₹12,500 + ₹1,00,000 + ₹2,67,750 = ₹3,80,250**

**7. Add Health and Education Cess (4%):**
4% of ₹3,80,250 = ₹15,210

**8. Total Annual Tax Liability:**
₹3,80,250 + ₹15,210 = ₹3,95,460

**9. Monthly TDS:**
₹3,95,460 / 12 = ₹32,955

So, Priya's employer would deduct approximately ₹32,955 as TDS from her salary each month.

## FAQ

### What is the Income Tax Act 2025?
The Income Tax Act 2025 is the new legislation governing income tax in India, effective April 1st, 2026. It replaces the Income Tax Act 1961, updating the legal framework for taxation.

### Have tax rates changed under the new Act?
No, the primary income tax rates and slab structures for individual taxpayers have largely remained unchanged with the introduction of the Income Tax Act 2025. The changes are primarily procedural and administrative.

### Can my employer deduct more TDS than my actual tax liability?
Your employer estimates your annual tax liability based on your declared income and investments. If you haven't declared all your tax-saving investments, they might deduct more. You can claim a refund for any excess TDS when filing your income tax return.

### What if I switch jobs during the financial year?
If you switch jobs, your new employer will calculate TDS based on the income earned from them and any previous income declared by you. It's crucial to provide details of your previous employment's income and TDS to your new employer to avoid under or over-deduction.

### How does HRA exemption work for TDS calculation?
Your employer will exempt the least of the actual HRA received, actual rent paid minus 10% of basic salary, or 40%/50% of basic salary (depending on the city), from your taxable income for TDS purposes.

### Is professional tax considered for TDS calculation?
Yes, any professional tax paid by an employee is deductible from their gross salary before calculating the net taxable income, thereby reducing the overall tax liability.

### Where can I check the TDS deducted from my salary?
You can check the TDS deducted by your employer in your Form 26AS, which is available on the Income Tax India e-filing portal. This form provides a consolidated statement of taxes deducted at source.
