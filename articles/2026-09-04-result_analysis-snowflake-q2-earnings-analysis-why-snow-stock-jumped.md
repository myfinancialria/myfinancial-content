# Snowflake Q2 Earnings Analysis: Why SNOW Stock Jumped

**TL;DR**
- Snowflake reported stellar Q2 FY2027 results, significantly beating analyst expectations for both revenue and earnings.
- Product revenue soared 37% year-over-year to $1.49 billion, marking a third consecutive quarter of acceleration, largely driven by AI adoption.
- The company raised its full-year product revenue and adjusted operating margin guidance, signalling strong confidence in future performance.
- New AI products like CoWork and CoCo are gaining significant traction, expanding platform usage and attracting new customers.
- Following the announcement, Snowflake's stock surged by over 20% in extended trading, reflecting robust investor confidence.

## What this means in plain terms

When a company like Snowflake, a global leader in cloud data platforms, announces its quarterly financial results, investors and analysts keenly watch for key figures such as revenue, earnings per share (EPS), and future projections (guidance). These numbers indicate the company's health and growth trajectory. A "beat" means the company performed better than what financial experts had predicted, while "raise" signifies that the company expects to do even better in the future than its previous forecasts.

Snowflake's latest Q2 FY2027 earnings report showed a strong "double beat" – meaning both its revenue and adjusted earnings surpassed market expectations. More importantly, the company's management expressed optimism by increasing their financial outlook for the entire fiscal year. This positive performance was primarily fuelled by the growing adoption of its data platform, particularly its new Artificial Intelligence (AI) offerings. For investors, especially those in India looking at global equities, such results suggest a company with strong operational momentum and a promising position in a high-growth sector.

## Strong Beat on Key Metrics and Raised Outlook

Snowflake delivered an impressive performance for its second quarter of fiscal year 2027, ending July 31, 2026, significantly surpassing Wall Street's expectations. The company reported total revenue of $1.55 billion, marking a substantial 35.1% year-over-year increase. This figure comfortably beat the average analyst estimate of approximately $1.48 billion.

The core of Snowflake's business, product revenue, which is a crucial metric for the company, reached $1.49 billion, demonstrating a robust 37% year-over-year growth. This marked the third consecutive quarter where product revenue growth accelerated, indicating sustained business momentum. Adjusted earnings per share (EPS) also came in strong at $0.62, significantly higher than the consensus estimate of $0.45 per share. On a GAAP basis, the company reported a net loss of $191.7 million, or $0.55 per share, which was an improvement from the prior year.

Looking ahead, Snowflake demonstrated strong confidence in its future by raising its guidance for both the upcoming quarter and the full fiscal year. For Q3 FY2027, the company anticipates product revenue to be between $1.588 billion and $1.593 billion, implying a 37% to 38% year-over-year growth, exceeding analyst expectations of around $1.50 billion. Furthermore, Snowflake lifted its full-year FY2027 product revenue guidance to $6.07 billion, up from its previous forecast of $5.84 billion. This revised outlook represents a 36% year-over-year growth, an increase from the earlier projected 31%. The company also raised its full-year adjusted operating margin outlook to 14.5%, up from 13.5%, signalling improved profitability.

## AI Momentum and Customer Expansion Drive Growth

A significant driver behind Snowflake's exceptional Q2 performance was the accelerating adoption of its AI capabilities and the continuous expansion of its customer base. CEO Sridhar Ramaswamy highlighted that AI continues to "compound our advantages, creating a flywheel effect across the business." The company's new AI products, CoWork (an enterprise chatbot) and CoCo (a coding assistant), are gaining substantial traction, driving transformational outcomes for customers and fueling rapid adoption. CoCo, for instance, now boasts over 9,100 accounts, while CoWork has expanded to 5,800 accounts.

Snowflake continues to attract and retain high-value customers. The company added 692 net new customers during the quarter, bringing its total customer count to over 14,500 globally. Notably, the number of customers generating over $1 million in trailing 12-month product revenue grew by 27% year-over-year to 828. The net revenue retention rate, a key indicator of existing customer spending, remained strong at 126%, suggesting that customers are not only staying with Snowflake but also increasing their usage of the platform. Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO), which represent contracted future revenue, grew 30% year-over-year to $9 billion, providing a solid foundation for future revenue recognition. This robust customer growth and increased consumption, particularly for AI-related workloads, underscore the strong demand for Snowflake's cloud-native data platform.

## Profitability and Operational Efficiency

Beyond top-line growth, Snowflake also demonstrated improvements in profitability and operational efficiency during Q2 FY2027. The company's non-GAAP operating margin expanded to 15%, exceeding its own guidance. This expansion reflects a concerted effort to balance rapid growth with financial discipline, a priority emphasized by CFO Brian Robins. The management's decision to raise the full-year adjusted operating margin guidance to 14.5% further reinforces this commitment to improving the bottom line while continuing strategic investments.

Non-GAAP product gross profit for the quarter was reported at $1.11 billion. While there was a slight dip in non-GAAP product gross margin to 76.4% from 78% in Q2 FY2024, this was attributed to GPU-related costs incurred to meet customer demand for newer product features, especially those related to AI. Despite these investments in innovation, the company's ability to expand its operating margin indicates effective cost management across other areas. Snowflake's strong free cash flow generation and the authorization of an additional $2.5 billion for its stock repurchase program through March 2027 also highlight its financial strength and commitment to managing shareholder dilution. This focus on both growth and efficiency is crucial for long-term value creation, especially in the competitive cloud software market.

## Implications for Indian Investors

For Indian investors, whether salaried professionals with disposable income or Non-Resident Indians seeking global diversification, Snowflake's Q2 results offer several insights. Investing in global technology companies like Snowflake provides exposure to high-growth sectors and innovative technologies like AI, which may not be fully represented in the Indian equity markets.

### Long-Term Perspective
Snowflake's strong growth, driven by AI adoption and consistent customer expansion, paints a positive long-term picture. The company's position as a foundational platform for enterprise AI suggests sustained demand for its services. Indian investors with a long-term horizon, aiming for capital appreciation and portfolio diversification, might view SNOW as a potential growth stock. However, it's crucial to acknowledge that such high-growth stocks often come with premium valuations. Snowflake's current valuation, with a high price-to-sales ratio, implies that much of this future growth is already priced into the stock. Long-term investors should evaluate if the company's innovation pipeline and market leadership justify this premium, considering their overall risk appetite and investment goals.

### Short-Term Considerations
In the short term, the significant jump in SNOW's stock price post-earnings reflects immediate investor enthusiasm for the strong beat and raised guidance. While this can be tempting, chasing such rapid price movements can be risky. Market sentiment can shift quickly, and even fundamentally strong companies can experience volatility. Indian investors should avoid making impulsive decisions based solely on immediate post-earnings surges. Instead, they might consider a staggered investment approach (like Systematic Investment Plans, or SIPs, for global equities if offered by their broker) to average out entry costs, rather than trying to time the market. Understanding the underlying business drivers, like product revenue acceleration and AI integration, is more valuable than reacting to daily price fluctuations.

## FAQ

### What is product revenue for Snowflake?
Product revenue is the primary revenue stream for Snowflake, derived from customer consumption of its cloud data platform. It reflects how much customers are using Snowflake's services, including data storage, compute, and cloud services.

### Why did Snowflake's stock jump after earnings?
Snowflake's stock jumped because the company reported financial results (revenue and adjusted EPS) that significantly exceeded analyst expectations and raised its future guidance, indicating strong business momentum, particularly driven by its AI offerings.

### What are Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO)?
Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO) represent the contracted future revenue that Snowflake expects to recognize from its customers. It's an indicator of the company's future revenue visibility and the health of its long-term customer commitments.

### How does AI impact Snowflake's business?
AI significantly impacts Snowflake by driving increased platform usage as customers leverage its data cloud for AI application development. Snowflake's own AI products like CoWork and CoCo are also directly contributing to revenue and customer engagement.

### Is Snowflake profitable on a GAAP basis?
No, Snowflake reported a GAAP net loss of $191.7 million for Q2 FY2027. While the company is growing rapidly and improving its non-GAAP operating margin, it is still investing heavily for growth, which results in GAAP losses.

### Should Indian investors directly invest in US stocks like Snowflake?
Indian investors can invest in US stocks directly through brokerage platforms that offer international investing. It allows for diversification and access to global growth opportunities, but involves currency risk and understanding foreign tax implications.
