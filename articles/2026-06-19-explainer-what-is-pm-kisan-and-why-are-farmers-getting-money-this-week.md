# What is PM-KISAN and Why Are Farmers Getting Money This Week?

**TL;DR**
- The Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi (PM-KISAN) scheme gives financial support to eligible farmer families in India.
- Each eligible family receives ₹6,000 per year, paid in three equal instalments of ₹2,000 each.
- The money is sent directly to farmers' Aadhaar-linked bank accounts through Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT).
- This week, the 23rd instalment, worth over ₹18,880 crore, is being released to more than 9.44 crore farmers across the country.
- This scheme aims to help farmers with their agricultural expenses and daily needs.

## What's this about?
The Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi, or PM-KISAN, is a special scheme by the Indian government. Its main goal is to provide income support to landholding farmer families across the country. Think of it as a helping hand for farmers to manage their expenses. Under this scheme, eligible farmer families receive ₹6,000 every year. This amount is given in three easy payments of ₹2,000 each, spread throughout the year.

## Why it's in the news right now
The PM-KISAN scheme is in the news because the **23rd instalment** is being released this week. On June 20, 2026, Prime Minister Narendra Modi is set to transfer over ₹18,880 crore directly into the bank accounts of more than 9.44 crore farmers. This includes over 2.18 crore women farmers who will also benefit. The event for releasing this instalment is taking place in Tarakeswar, Hooghly district, West Bengal. This timely release is important, especially as farmers prepare for the upcoming agricultural season.

## How it actually works
The PM-KISAN scheme is designed to be straightforward and efficient, using technology to reach farmers directly. Here’s how it works:

1.  **Central Government Scheme:** PM-KISAN is fully funded by the Central Government. This means the entire cost of the scheme is borne by the Union government.
2.  **Eligibility:** The scheme is for all landholding farmer families in India. However, there are some exclusion criteria. For example, individuals who are government employees, professionals (like doctors, engineers, lawyers), pensioners receiving more than ₹10,000 per month, or those who paid income tax in the last assessment year are generally not eligible.
3.  **Financial Benefit:** Each eligible farmer family receives ₹6,000 per year. This is paid in three equal instalments of ₹2,000 each. These instalments are typically released every four months (April-July, August-November, and December-March).
4.  **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT):** The money is transferred directly into the bank accounts of the beneficiaries. This is done through a system called Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT). For this to work, the farmer's bank account must be linked with their Aadhaar number and also be seeded with NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India). This helps ensure the money reaches the right person without any middlemen.
5.  **eKYC is Mandatory:** To receive the instalments, farmers must complete their eKYC (Electronic Know Your Customer) process. This can often be done online through the PM-KISAN portal or at nearby Common Service Centres (CSCs).
6.  **Registration and Verification:** Farmers can register for the scheme through the official PM-KISAN portal or at Common Service Centres. State and Union Territory governments are responsible for identifying the eligible farmer families and verifying their land records and other details.

Since its launch on February 24, 2019 (with effect from December 1, 2018), the scheme has disbursed over ₹4.46 lakh crore to farmers across the nation.

## A simple way to think about it
Imagine your family runs a small kirana (grocery) shop. Sometimes, you need a little extra money to buy new stock, pay for electricity, or even just manage daily household expenses until the next big sale. The government, through PM-KISAN, is like a well-wishing relative who sends your family ₹2,000 every few months. This small, regular amount helps you buy essential supplies for the shop (like agricultural inputs for farmers) or take care of immediate family needs, without having to borrow from others at high interest. It's a steady, predictable income boost that helps keep things running smoothly.

## Why it matters to you
Even if you are not a farmer, the PM-KISAN scheme has several impacts on the broader economy and indirectly on your life:

1.  **Supports Rural Economy:** A large part of India's population lives in rural areas and depends on agriculture. By providing direct income support, PM-KISAN helps boost spending in rural markets, which can benefit local businesses and the overall economy.
2.  **Stabilises Food Prices:** When farmers have financial stability, they can invest better in their farms, which can lead to better harvests. This can contribute to a stable supply of food grains and vegetables, potentially helping to keep food prices in check for all consumers.
3.  **Reduces Farmer Distress:** The scheme aims to reduce the financial stress on farmers, who often face challenges like unpredictable weather, fluctuating market prices, and debt. This support can help them avoid falling into debt traps.
4.  **Promotes Digital Transactions:** The use of Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) encourages farmers to have bank accounts and links them with Aadhaar. This pushes financial inclusion and digital literacy in rural areas, making it easier for people to access other financial services.
5.  **Government Spending and Taxes:** The scheme involves a significant outlay of public funds (over ₹18,880 crore for this instalment alone). This money comes from taxes, and its effective use for farmer welfare reflects a government priority in resource allocation.

## Common confusions cleared up

**Myth:** PM-KISAN is a loan for farmers.
**Fact:** PM-KISAN is a direct income support scheme, not a loan. The money given to farmers does not need to be repaid. It is a grant from the government.

**Myth:** All farmers, regardless of land size or income, get PM-KISAN benefits.
**Fact:** While initially for small and marginal farmers, it was later extended to all landholding farmer families. However, there are specific exclusion criteria. For instance, institutional landholders, government employees, professionals, and income tax payees are excluded.

**Myth:** The money is given in cash or through intermediaries.
**Fact:** The funds are transferred directly into the Aadhaar-linked bank accounts of beneficiaries using Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT). This ensures transparency and reduces the chances of corruption or delays.

## FAQ

### Who is eligible for PM-KISAN?
All landholding farmer families with cultivable land are eligible, subject to certain exclusion criteria. These include institutional landholders, government employees, professionals, and those who pay income tax.

### How often do farmers receive the PM-KISAN amount?
Farmers receive ₹2,000 every four months, making it three instalments of ₹2,000 each, totalling ₹6,000 per year.

### What documents are needed to apply for PM-KISAN?
Farmers need their Aadhaar card, bank account details (linked to Aadhaar), and land records to apply and receive benefits. eKYC completion is also mandatory.

### What if a farmer does not receive an instalment?
If an eligible farmer doesn't receive an instalment, they should check their beneficiary status on the PM-KISAN portal (pmkisan.gov.in) or contact the scheme's helpline numbers (155261 / 011-24300606 / 1800-11-5526). Issues often arise due to incomplete eKYC, unlinked Aadhaar-bank accounts, or incorrect land records.

### Can a husband and wife both receive PM-KISAN benefits?
No, only one member from a farmer family (defined as husband, wife, and minor children) is eligible to receive the benefits. If both husband and wife receive it, one might be asked to refund the amount.
