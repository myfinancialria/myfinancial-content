# ITR Filed & Verified? How the Income Tax Department Spots Mismatches

**TL;DR**
- The Income Tax Department (ITD) uses technology to cross-verify information declared in your ITR with data from various sources like Form 26AS, Annual Information Statement (AIS), and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS).
- Mismatches often arise from discrepancies in reported income, deductions, or tax credits, leading to potential scrutiny.
- Common reasons for notices include incorrect ITR form usage, calculation errors, or undeclared income like interest or capital gains.
- Receiving a notice, such as a defective return notice under Section 139(9) or a scrutiny notice under Section 143(2), requires a timely and accurate response.
- Proactively reconciling your financial data with Form 26AS, AIS, and TIS before filing can significantly reduce the chances of receiving an ITD notice.

## What this means in plain terms

Once you've filed your Income Tax Return (ITR) and completed the verification process, many taxpayers assume their tax obligations are settled. However, the Income Tax Department (ITD) doesn't just take your word for it. They have a sophisticated system to cross-check the information you've provided against a vast database of financial transactions reported by banks, employers, mutual fund houses, and other entities.

This automated process aims to identify any discrepancies or "mismatches" between what you've declared and what their records show. For instance, if your employer reported a certain salary to the ITD, but your ITR shows a different amount, that's a mismatch. Similarly, if your bank reported interest income to the ITD, but you didn't include it in your ITR, that's another red flag. When these mismatches are significant, the ITD can issue a notice, asking for clarification or further action from your side.

## How the Income Tax Department Processes Returns

The Centralised Processing Centre (CPC) of the Income Tax Department plays a pivotal role in handling the millions of ITRs filed annually. Its primary function is to process returns quickly and efficiently, using technology to identify potential issues.

### Preliminary Scrutiny by CPC
Upon receiving your ITR, the CPC performs an initial, automated check. This involves verifying several key aspects:
*   **Correct ITR Form:** Ensuring you've used the appropriate ITR form (e.g., ITR-1, ITR-2, ITR-3, etc.) based on your income sources and taxpayer category. Using the wrong form can immediately flag your return as defective.
*   **Mandatory Schedules:** Checking if all compulsory schedules in the ITR form have been filled. Leaving essential sections blank can lead to a defective return.
*   **Calculation Errors:** Identifying any arithmetical errors in the computation of income, deductions, or tax liability. Even simple mistakes can lead to discrepancies.
*   **Verification Status:** Confirming that the ITR has been duly verified, either electronically (e-verification) or by sending a signed ITR-V to Bengaluru. An unverified return is considered invalid.

If the CPC finds major issues during this preliminary check, you may receive a "defective return" notice under Section 139(9) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, asking you to rectify the errors.

### Data Matching with Form 26AS, AIS, and TIS
Beyond the initial checks, the ITD's most powerful tool for spotting mismatches is the cross-referencing of your ITR data with information available from third parties. This primarily involves:

*   **Form 26AS:** This annual statement provides a consolidated view of tax deducted at source (TDS) and tax collected at source (TCS) against your Permanent Account Number (PAN). It also shows details of certain high-value financial transactions. The ITD compares the TDS/TCS claimed in your ITR with what's reported in your 26AS. Any discrepancy here is a common trigger for notices. You can access your Form 26AS on the Income Tax e-filing portal.
*   **Annual Information Statement (AIS):** Introduced to provide a more comprehensive view, AIS includes all information available with the ITD related to a taxpayer. This encompasses not just TDS/TCS but also interest income, dividend income, mutual fund transactions, securities transactions, foreign remittances, and more. The AIS aims to give taxpayers a complete picture of their financial activities reported to the ITD, allowing them to reconcile it with their records before filing.
*   **Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS):** The TIS is a summarised category-wise information statement derived from AIS, showing processed value (derived after deduplication and grouping) and reported value. It helps taxpayers get an overview of their financial data.

The ITD's system automatically matches the income, deductions, and tax credits declared in your ITR with the data in Form 26AS, AIS, and TIS. Significant variances often lead to further investigation and potentially a notice.

## Common Reasons for Receiving an ITD Notice

Receiving a notice from the Income Tax Department can be unsettling, but understanding the common triggers can help you avoid them. Mismatches are at the heart of most notices.

### Discrepancies in Income Reporting
*   **Salary Income:** If the salary declared in your ITR (as per Form 16) does not match the information reported by your employer to the ITD.
*   **Interest Income:** Undeclared interest income from savings accounts, fixed deposits, recurring deposits, or even tax-saving bonds. Banks report this income to the ITD, and if it's missing from your ITR, it will be flagged.
*   **Capital Gains:** Failure to report capital gains from the sale of shares, mutual funds, property, or other assets. Brokerages and registrars report these transactions.
*   **Dividend Income:** While dividend income up to ₹10 lakhs was exempt for individuals until FY 2019-20, it is now fully taxable in the hands of the recipient. Missing this from your ITR, especially if it's significant, can lead to a notice.
*   **Rental Income:** Under-reporting or non-reporting of rental income from properties.

### Mismatches in Deductions and Exemptions
*   **Incorrect Deductions:** Claiming deductions under Sections 80C, 80D, 80G, etc., that are not supported by proper documentation or exceed permissible limits.
*   **Exempt Income:** While certain incomes are exempt from tax (e.g., agricultural income), they still need to be reported in the ITR, especially if they are substantial. Not reporting them can raise questions.

### Tax Credits and High-Value Transactions
*   **TDS/TCS Mismatch:** The most frequent mismatch. If the TDS/TCS claimed in your ITR is higher or lower than what is reflected in your Form 26AS.
*   **Advance Tax/Self-Assessment Tax:** Discrepancies in advance tax or self-assessment tax paid versus what is recorded by the ITD.
*   **High-Value Transactions:** The ITD receives information on various high-value transactions, such as cash deposits/withdrawals, credit card payments, property purchases/sales, and mutual fund investments. If these transactions are disproportionate to your declared income, it can trigger scrutiny.

## Types of Notices and How to Respond

The Income Tax Department issues various types of notices, each serving a different purpose and requiring a specific response. Understanding them is crucial for compliance.

### Defective Return Notice (Section 139(9))
*   **Purpose:** Issued when your ITR is deemed "defective" due to errors like using the wrong form, missing mandatory information, or calculation errors.
*   **Action Required:** You need to rectify the defects within the specified timeframe (usually 15 days from receiving the notice, though extensions can be requested). You can revise your return on the e-filing portal. Failure to respond makes your original return invalid.

### Scrutiny Notice (Section 143(2))
*   **Purpose:** This is a more serious notice, indicating that your return has been selected for detailed scrutiny to ensure all claims and declarations are accurate. It's usually issued within six months from the end of the financial year in which the return was filed.
*   **Action Required:** You will be required to provide documents and explanations to support your income, deductions, and claims. This may involve attending hearings or submitting responses online. It's advisable to consult a tax professional.

### Notice for Income Escaping Assessment (Section 148)
*   **Purpose:** Issued when the Assessing Officer believes that some income chargeable to tax has escaped assessment. This can happen if the ITD discovers undeclared income or assets after your return has already been processed.
*   **Action Required:** You will be asked to file an updated return or provide reasons why income has not been assessed. This notice typically has a longer validity period, up to 3 years from the end of the relevant assessment year, and up to 10 years in cases of income escaping assessment exceeding ₹50 lakhs.

### Other Common Notices
*   **Notice under Section 143(1):** This is an intimation after preliminary processing of your return. It indicates if there's any tax payable or refundable based on the ITD's calculations. It's not strictly a "notice for mismatch" but an outcome of initial processing.
*   **Notice under Section 245 for Adjustment of Refund:** If you have an outstanding tax demand from a previous year, the ITD may adjust your current year's refund against that demand and send a notice.
*   **Notice under Section 142(1):** Issued to call for documents or information, even if no return has been filed or if the Assessing Officer needs further details.

When responding to any notice, ensure your replies are accurate, well-documented, and submitted within the stipulated deadlines. Always keep copies of all communications.

## A real example

Let's consider **Meena, 35, ₹22L CTC, working in Bengaluru.**

Meena diligently filed her ITR-1 for the Financial Year 2025-26 (Assessment Year 2026-27) in July 2026. Her Form 16 from her employer showed a gross salary of ₹22,00,000 and TDS of ₹2,80,000. She also claimed deductions under Section 80C for ₹1,50,000 (EPF, ELSS) and Section 80D for ₹25,000 (health insurance premium).

However, Meena had forgotten about a fixed deposit (FD) she had opened with her bank a few years ago, which matured in FY 2025-26, earning her ₹35,000 in interest. The bank had deducted TDS of ₹3,500 on this interest and reported it to the Income Tax Department.

**The Mismatch:**
When the ITD processed Meena's ITR, their system cross-referenced her declared income with the Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS linked to her PAN.
*   Her ITR showed total income (after deductions) as ₹20,25,000 (₹22,00,000 - ₹1,75,000).
*   Her AIS/TIS, however, showed her salary income, plus the ₹35,000 interest income from the FD, along with the corresponding TDS of ₹3,500.

The ITD's system identified a mismatch of ₹35,000 in her reported income.

**Outcome:**
Meena received an intimation under Section 143(1) from the ITD, highlighting the discrepancy. The intimation mentioned that the interest income of ₹35,000 was not included in her ITR, and consequently, her total income was understated, leading to a higher tax liability of approximately ₹10,500 (30% tax bracket + cess on ₹35,000) and a potential interest charge under Section 234B/C for delayed tax payment. It also showed that she had claimed ₹3,500 less TDS than available in her 26AS.

**Meena's Action:**
Meena realised her oversight. She promptly filed a revised return, including the ₹35,000 interest income. This corrected her total income and tax liability. She then paid the additional tax of ₹10,500 plus any applicable interest online. By rectifying the error, she avoided further scrutiny or a more severe notice.

This example highlights how even a small, forgotten income source can lead to a notice due to the ITD's robust data matching capabilities.

## What to do this week

1.  **Download Your AIS and TIS:** Log in to the Income Tax e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in) and navigate to "Annual Information Statement (AIS)" under the "Services" tab. Download both your AIS and TIS for the relevant financial year.
2.  **Reconcile with Form 26AS:** Access your Form 26AS (also available on the e-filing portal via "My Account" -> "View Form 26AS") and compare it with the TDS/TCS details in your AIS and TIS. Ensure all tax credits match.
3.  **Cross-Verify All Income Sources:** Carefully compare all income details (salary, interest, dividends, capital gains, etc.) listed in your AIS/TIS with your personal records (bank statements, Form 16, investment statements). Note down any discrepancies.
4.  **Review Your Filed ITR:** If you've already filed your ITR, review a copy of it against your reconciled AIS/TIS and personal records. Identify any potential mismatches in income, deductions, or tax credits.
5.  **Consult a Professional (if needed):** If you find significant discrepancies or are unsure about how to interpret your AIS/TIS data, consider consulting a Certified Financial Planner or a tax advisor. They can help you understand the implications and guide you on filing a revised return if necessary.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between AIS and Form 26AS?
AIS (Annual Information Statement) is a comprehensive statement covering all financial transactions reported to the ITD, including interest, dividends, securities transactions, and more. Form 26AS primarily focuses on TDS/TCS and certain high-value transactions. AIS is broader in scope.

### How long does the Income Tax Department have to send a notice?
The time limits for sending notices vary by section. For instance, a defective return notice (Section 139(9)) is typically issued shortly after filing. A scrutiny notice (Section 143(2)) can be issued within six months from the end of the financial year in which the return was filed. Notices for income escaping assessment (Section 148) have longer periods, up to 3 or 10 years depending on the amount.

### What happens if I ignore an ITD notice?
Ignoring an ITD notice can lead to serious consequences, including penalties, higher tax demands, initiation of assessment proceedings ex-parte (without your input), and even prosecution in severe cases. Always respond promptly and accurately.

### Can I revise my ITR after receiving a notice?
Yes, in many cases, especially for notices under Section 139(9) (defective return) or intimation under Section 143(1) that highlight a mismatch, you can file a revised return to correct the errors. However, for scrutiny notices (Section 143(2)) or income escaping assessment notices (Section 148), you need to respond to the specific queries raised.

### What if the information in my AIS is incorrect?
If you find incorrect information in your AIS, you can submit feedback on the e-filing portal. The ITD will then forward this feedback to the reporting entity (e.g., bank, employer) for verification and correction. It's crucial to ensure your AIS is accurate.

### Is it mandatory to report exempt income in my ITR?
Yes, while exempt income is not taxable, it is generally mandatory to report it in your ITR, especially if it exceeds certain thresholds. This helps the ITD get a complete picture of your financial standing and avoids potential mismatches.

### What is the deadline to respond to an ITD notice?
The deadline to respond is specified in the notice itself, typically ranging from 15 to 30 days. It is crucial to adhere to these deadlines. If you need more time, you can often request an extension from the Assessing Officer, stating valid reasons.
