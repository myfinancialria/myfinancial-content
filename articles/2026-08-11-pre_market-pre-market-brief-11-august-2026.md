# Pre-Market Brief — 11 August 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets presented a mixed picture, with US and European indices closing slightly negative, while Asian markets showed divergence.
*   GIFT Nifty indicates a slightly negative opening bias for the Indian benchmark Nifty 50.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers yesterday, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) turned net sellers.
*   Crude oil prices surged significantly overnight, posing a potential headwind for oil-importing nations like India.
*   Nifty 50's CPR is narrow, suggesting a potentially trending day, with 24500-24600 as key levels from options data.
*   The sharp rise in crude oil prices and the strengthening US Dollar Index remain the primary risks for today's session.

Overnight, US markets closed marginally lower, with the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all registering minor declines. European indices like the FTSE 100 also ended in negative territory. However, Asian markets presented a mixed picture this morning, with Japan's Nikkei 225 showing a strong rally, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng and China's Shanghai Composite saw slight dips. This mixed global sentiment suggests a cautious start for Indian equities, with no clear strong directional cues from major international markets.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee depreciated against the US Dollar, with USD/INR rising by 0.17% to 95.38. The Dollar Index also strengthened by 0.21% to 99.81. Crude oil prices saw a significant jump, with Brent Crude surging by 4.99% to $87.72 and WTI Crude by 5.05% to $82.13. Gold prices rose by 0.49% to $4361.80, and Silver by 2.80% to $65.11. The US 10-Year Yield increased by 0.84% to 4.70%.

**Interpretation:**
*   **USD/INR & Dollar Index:** A rising USD/INR and strengthening Dollar Index indicate pressure on the Rupee. This could provide a tailwind for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters but may increase import costs and put pressure on companies with significant foreign currency debt.
*   **Crude Oil:** The sharp surge in Brent and WTI crude prices is a significant concern for India, a major oil importer. Higher crude oil prices could lead to increased inflation, higher current account deficit, and put pressure on sectors like aviation, paints, and oil marketing companies (OMCs).
*   **Gold & Silver:** The rise in precious metals suggests some safe-haven buying amidst global uncertainties, possibly linked to geopolitical tensions (Middle East, US-Iran) mentioned in news.
*   **US 10Y Yield:** An increase in US bond yields typically makes emerging markets less attractive for foreign investors, potentially leading to FII outflows or reduced inflows into Indian equities.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
*   **Crude Oil Price Movement:** The significant surge in Brent and WTI crude prices overnight will be a major factor. Markets typically react negatively to sharp increases in crude, especially for net oil-importing economies like India, due to inflation concerns and potential impact on corporate margins.
*   **Ongoing IPOs:** Several IPOs (LEAP India, Technocraft Ventures, Dhoot Transmission) are in various stages of bidding. Strong subscription numbers and grey market premiums often reflect overall market liquidity and investor appetite, which can influence broader market sentiment.
*   **Global Inflation Focus:** As mentioned in commodity news, Middle East tensions and US inflation remain in focus. Any new developments on these fronts could trigger volatility in global and Indian markets, with inflation concerns typically leading to cautious investor sentiment.
*   **FII/DII Flow Trends:** The direction and magnitude of FII and DII flows will continue to be closely watched. Consistent FII buying generally supports market rallies, while sustained DII selling can indicate profit booking or reallocation.
