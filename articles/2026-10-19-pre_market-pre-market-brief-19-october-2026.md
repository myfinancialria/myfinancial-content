# Pre-Market Brief — 19 October 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets presented a mixed picture, with US indices closing significantly lower, European markets also down, while some Asian counterparts saw modest gains.
*   GIFT Nifty is signalling a negative opening bias for the Indian benchmark indices, pointing to a potential gap-down.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers yesterday after several sessions of outflows, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) turned net sellers.
*   A global bond selloff is tightening financial conditions in the Eurozone, a warning from ECB President Christine Lagarde that could impact global growth sentiment.
*   Nifty 50's key resistance sits around the 23000 mark, with immediate support at 22800.
*   The day's main risk factors include continued global bond market volatility, sustained FII outflows over the past week, and a rising USD/INR.

Global markets displayed a cautious tone overnight, largely influenced by a significant sell-off in US technology stocks. The Nasdaq led the declines in the US, falling over 2%, while the Dow Jones and S&P 500 also closed in negative territory. European markets, represented by the FTSE 100, followed suit with losses. In Asia this morning, the Nikkei 225 was down, but Chinese markets, including the Hang Seng and Shanghai, managed to post modest gains.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee weakened against the US Dollar, with USD/INR rising to 94.93, a gain of 0.43%. The Dollar Index, measuring the greenback against a basket of major currencies, also edged up by 0.09% to 101.94. Crude oil prices saw a notable decline, with Brent Crude falling 2.42% to $107.20 and WTI Crude dropping 4.41% to $96.94. Gold prices moved higher by 0.49% to $4201.50, and Silver also gained 1.23% to $57.69. The US 10-Year Treasury Yield increased by 0.18% to 5.10%.

**Interpretation:** A rising USD/INR is generally a positive for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters as their dollar earnings translate to more rupees, but it increases import costs for the broader economy. A stronger Dollar Index can put pressure on emerging market currencies. The significant fall in crude oil prices is a positive for India, a major oil importer, potentially easing inflationary pressures and benefiting sectors like aviation, paints, and oil marketing companies (OMCs). Rising gold and silver prices indicate a flight to safety amidst global uncertainties. An uptick in the US 10-year yield could make US assets more attractive, potentially impacting FII flows into emerging markets like India.

## GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling
The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 22879.50, down 0.93%. This indicates a negative bias and points to a potential gap-down opening for the Nifty 50 when the Indian market opens today.

## FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape
After several sessions of significant outflows, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned net buyers on Friday, injecting ₹1,166 crore into the Indian equity market. Conversely, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers, pulling out ₹3,661 crore. Over the last five sessions, FIIs have been net sellers, while DIIs have largely provided support, except for yesterday's session.

| Date         | FII Net (₹ crore) | DII Net (₹ crore) |
| :----------- | :---------------- | :---------------- |
| 2026-10-16   | +1,166            | -3,661            |

**Interpretation:** While FIIs showed a positive flow yesterday, the trend over the last five sessions remains one of net selling by foreign investors, which can exert downward pressure on the market. DIIs, who have been net buyers for most of the past week, turned sellers yesterday, suggesting a cautious stance from domestic institutions as well. This divergence in flows and the overall FII selling trend could lead to continued volatility.

## Stock & sector news that matters

*   **Global Market: Eurozone bond selloff to weigh on growth, curb inflation, Lagarde says**
    ECB President Christine Lagarde warned that a global bond selloff is tightening euro zone financial conditions, raising borrowing costs and weighing on growth.
    **Why it matters:** This is a significant macro development. Rising borrowing costs in a major global economy like the Eurozone can dampen global growth prospects and investor sentiment, potentially leading to risk aversion and impacting FII flows into emerging markets like India.

*   **Global Market: Indonesia stock exchange to meet MSCI after downgrade warning triggers market rout**
    Indonesia’s stock exchange will meet MSCI today to seek clarity over a warning that could strip the country of its emerging-market status, a threat that triggered a sharp selloff wiping $65 billion.
    **Why it matters:** While specific to Indonesia, such a warning from MSCI can create ripples across other emerging markets, including India. It highlights the sensitivity of markets to index inclusion/exclusion and could lead to broader caution among investors regarding emerging market allocations.

*   **PSU bank stocks soar up to 8%, but HDFC, Axis and other private lenders fall up to 5% after Q2 results. Why the sharp divide?**
    PSU bank stocks surged up to 8% on Monday, led by Punjab National Bank after its Q2FY27 earnings beat estimates, while private lenders like Axis Bank and HDFC Bank fell up to 5% despite reporting results.
    **Why it matters:** This news highlights a clear divergence in performance within the banking sector. PSU banks, particularly PNB, are seeing strong positive momentum due to better-than-expected earnings, while private banks are facing selling pressure. This could lead to sector-specific rotation, with traders potentially favouring PSU banks over private banks in the near term.

*   **HFCL Q2 Results: Profit zooms 448% YoY to Rs 394 crore; stock hits 5% upper circuit**
    HFCL reported a massive 448% year-on-year jump in Q2FY27 net profit to ₹394 crore, with revenue surging 144% to ₹2,542 crore.
    **Why it matters:** This is a strong earnings beat for HFCL, indicating robust growth in the telecom infrastructure sector. The stock hitting an upper circuit suggests strong investor confidence, and this positive sentiment could potentially spill over to other companies in the telecom equipment or infrastructure space.

*   **Jio Platforms IPO GMP signals 22% potential listing gain; issue opens on October 21**
    Jio Platforms’ ₹30,213-crore IPO will open for subscription on October 21 and close on October 23, 2026.
    **Why it matters:** This is a major upcoming IPO from a prominent Indian conglomerate. A large IPO like Jio Platforms can attract significant liquidity from the market, potentially diverting funds from existing listed equities. The grey market premium (GMP) signalling a strong listing gain could generate considerable interest among retail investors.

*   **Global Market Volatility (Earnings Season & Central Bank Policies):** Global markets are facing fresh volatility as the earnings season gains pace and central bank policy decisions loom. Markets typically react with increased volatility around earnings announcements, especially from major US corporations, as these can set the tone for global economic health. Central bank policy decisions, particularly from the US Fed or ECB, often lead to significant market movements as they impact interest rate expectations and liquidity.
