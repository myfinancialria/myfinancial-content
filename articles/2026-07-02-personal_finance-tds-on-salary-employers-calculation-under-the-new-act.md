# TDS on Salary: Employer's Calculation Under the New Act

**TL;DR**
-   From April 1st, 2026, TDS on salary is governed by the new Income Tax Act 2025, replacing the 1961 Act.
-   Your employer deducts Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) from your salary based on your estimated annual income and declared investments.
-   The new Act retains the same income tax rates and slabs, but introduces changes to forms and section numbers for calculation.
-   Accurate investment declarations via Form 12BB are crucial to ensure your employer deducts the correct amount of TDS.
-   Form 16, issued by your employer, provides a summary of your gross salary, declared deductions, and the TDS deducted and deposited.

## What this means in plain terms

Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on salary is essentially an advance income tax that your employer deducts from your monthly paycheque and deposits with the government on your behalf. It's not an additional tax; it's simply a mechanism to collect tax throughout the year rather than as a lump sum at year-end. This system helps individuals manage their tax liability by spreading it across the financial year.

With the introduction of the Income Tax Act 2025, effective April 1st, 2026, the framework governing these deductions has been updated. While the actual tax rates and income slabs remain consistent with the previous regime, there are procedural changes concerning the forms used and the specific sections under which these deductions are processed. Your employer acts as a facilitator, estimating your total annual income, factoring in any eligible exemptions and deductions you declare, and then calculating the monthly TDS amount to ensure you're paying your taxes progressively.

## Understanding Gross Salary for TDS Calculation

Your employer begins the TDS calculation by first assessing your "gross salary." This isn't just your basic pay; it encompasses all monetary and non-monetary benefits you receive from your employer during the financial year. Key components typically include your basic salary, dearness allowance (DA), house rent allowance (HRA), special allowances, performance bonuses, and the monetary value of perquisites like company-provided accommodation or a car.

However, not every component of your gross salary is fully taxable. Certain allowances, like HRA (subject to specific conditions and limits), transport allowance (for certain categories), and leave travel concession (LTC), can be partially or fully exempt from tax under the provisions of the Income Tax Act 2025. Your employer will consider these exemptions based on the declarations and proofs you submit at the beginning of the financial year and periodically thereafter. Understanding what constitutes your gross salary and which parts are exempt is the foundational step in accurate TDS computation.

### Components of Taxable Salary
For TDS purposes, your employer consolidates various salary components. This includes your basic pay, dearness allowance, and other fixed allowances. Variable components like commissions and bonuses are also factored in when they are paid or become due. Perquisites, which are non-cash benefits provided by the employer, such as rent-free accommodation or medical facilities, are also assigned a monetary value and added to your taxable income.

### Exemptions to Consider
The Income Tax Act 2025 provides for certain exemptions that reduce your gross salary to arrive at the taxable salary. The most common one is the House Rent Allowance (HRA) exemption, which is calculated based on factors like rent paid, HRA received, and salary, subject to specific limits. Other exemptions might include leave travel allowance (LTA) for actual travel expenses, and certain allowances for specific duties. It's crucial for employees to provide proof for these exemptions to their employer.

## Leveraging Deductions to Reduce Taxable Income

Once your employer has determined your gross taxable salary after accounting for applicable exemptions, the next step involves factoring in various deductions available under the Income Tax Act 2025. These deductions allow you to reduce your overall taxable income, thereby lowering your TDS liability. The most commonly utilised deductions fall under Chapter VI-A of the Act, which includes sections like 80C, 80D, 80G, and others.

To benefit from these deductions, you must declare your planned investments and expenses to your employer, typically using Form 12BB. This form allows you to provide details of your contributions to Public Provident Fund (PPF), Employees' Provident Fund (EPF), life insurance premiums, housing loan principal repayment (under Section 80C), health insurance premiums (under Section 80D), donations (under Section 80G), and interest on education loans, among others. Your employer relies on these declarations to project your total deductible amount for the financial year.

### Common Deductions Under Chapter VI-A
Section 80C is perhaps the most popular, allowing deductions for investments in PPF, EPF, ELSS mutual funds, life insurance premiums, home loan principal repayment, and children's tuition fees, up to a specified limit. Section 80D covers health insurance premiums for yourself, your family, and your parents. Section 80E provides for deduction on interest paid on education loans, while Section 80G allows deductions for specified donations. There are other sections as well, catering to various investments and expenses, all aimed at encouraging savings and specific spending.

### Importance of Form 12BB
Form 12BB is a statement that salaried individuals submit to their employer to declare their claims for various deductions and exemptions. This includes details of HRA claims, LTA claims, interest payable on home loans, and investments eligible for deduction under Chapter VI-A. Submitting this form accurately and on time, along with supporting proofs, is critical. If you fail to submit Form 12BB or the required proofs, your employer will not consider these deductions, leading to a higher TDS deduction from your salary.

## The Monthly TDS Calculation Process

After considering your gross taxable salary, exemptions, and declared deductions, your employer arrives at your estimated "net taxable income" for the entire financial year. This is the income on which your tax liability will be calculated. The Income Tax Act 2025, while new in its structure, explicitly states that the tax rates and slabs remain unchanged from the previous regime. This means the standard progressive tax slabs (e.g., income up to ₹2.5 lakhs exempt, ₹2.5 lakhs to ₹5 lakhs at 5%, etc.) are applied to determine your total annual tax payable.

Once the total annual tax liability is computed, any applicable cess (like Health and Education Cess) is added. Your employer then reduces this total tax liability by any relief available under Section 89 (if applicable) and any TDS already deducted by a previous employer (if you joined mid-year and provided Form 16 from your prior employer). The resulting net annual tax liability is then divided by the number of remaining months in the financial year to arrive at the monthly TDS amount to be deducted from your salary. This ensures that your tax is spread evenly throughout the year.

### Understanding Tax Slabs and Rates
The tax slabs for individuals, as per the Income Tax Act 2025, are the same as before. For instance, individuals below 60 years typically have income up to ₹2.5 lakhs exempt from tax. Income between ₹2.5 lakhs and ₹5 lakhs is taxed at 5%, ₹5 lakhs to ₹10 lakhs at 20%, and above ₹10 lakhs at 30%. These rates are applied to your estimated net taxable income. Additionally, a Health and Education Cess of 4% is levied on the income tax amount.

### Adjustments and Revisions
Your employer doesn't just calculate TDS once and forget it. They are mandated to review and adjust the TDS calculation throughout the year. If you provide additional investment proofs, change your declarations, or if there are changes in your salary structure, your employer will re-calculate your estimated annual income and tax liability. This can lead to adjustments in your monthly TDS deduction, either increasing or decreasing it, to ensure that the correct amount of tax is deducted by the end of the financial year.

## Your Role: Declarations and Form 16

As an employee, your active participation is crucial for accurate TDS deduction. The onus is on you to provide timely and accurate information to your employer. This includes submitting your investment declarations through Form 12BB at the beginning of the financial year and then providing actual proofs of these investments and expenses towards the end of the financial year. Failure to do so means your employer will not consider these deductions, potentially leading to a higher TDS deduction than necessary.

At the end of the financial year, or upon leaving employment, your employer will issue Form 16. This is a vital document for you. Form 16 Part A contains details of the TDS deducted and deposited by your employer, along with their TAN (Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number). Part B provides a detailed break-up of your salary, perquisites, allowances, and the deductions allowed under Chapter VI-A. This form is essential for filing your income tax return, as it serves as proof of income and tax paid.

### Verifying TDS Deductions
It's always a good practice to cross-check the TDS deducted by your employer with the amount reflected in your Form 26AS, which can be accessed from the Income Tax Department's portal. Form 26AS is an annual consolidated tax statement that shows details of tax deducted at source, tax collected at source, advance tax paid, and self-assessment tax paid. Any discrepancy between your Form 16 and Form 26AS should be immediately brought to your employer's attention for rectification.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 32, a Senior Software Engineer in Bengaluru, with an annual CTC of ₹22 lakhs.**

Her salary breakup for the Financial Year 2026-27 (under Income Tax Act 2025) is:
*   Basic Salary: ₹10,00,000
*   House Rent Allowance (HRA): ₹4,00,000
*   Special Allowance: ₹6,00,000
*   Conveyance Allowance: ₹1,80,000
*   Employer's PF Contribution: ₹1,20,000 (not part of taxable salary, but employee's contribution is part of 80C)

Priya's declared investments and expenses:
*   Rent paid per month: ₹30,000 (₹3,60,000 annually)
*   Employee's PF Contribution: ₹1,20,000 (under 80C)
*   ELSS Mutual Funds: ₹30,000 (under 80C)
*   Health Insurance Premium: ₹25,000 (for self, under 80D)
*   NPS Contribution (self, beyond 80C): ₹50,000 (under 80CCD(1B))

**Step-by-step TDS Calculation by her employer:**

1.  **Gross Salary:** ₹10,00,000 (Basic) + ₹4,00,000 (HRA) + ₹6,00,000 (Special) + ₹1,80,000 (Conveyance) = **₹21,80,000**
    *(Note: CTC is ₹22 lakhs, but employer PF is not part of taxable salary. Conveyance allowance may have specific exemption limits, but for simplicity, we'll assume it's fully taxable here unless specific exemption details are provided by the employee.)*

2.  **Exemptions:**
    *   **HRA Exemption:** Least of:
        *   Actual HRA received: ₹4,00,000
        *   Rent paid less 10% of Basic Salary: ₹3,60,000 - (10% of ₹10,00,000) = ₹3,60,000 - ₹1,00,000 = ₹2,60,000
        *   50% of Basic Salary (Bengaluru is a metro): 50% of ₹10,00,000 = ₹5,00,000
        *   **HRA Exemption = ₹2,60,000**

3.  **Standard Deduction:** ₹50,000 (fixed for salaried individuals).

4.  **Gross Taxable Income (after exemptions & standard deduction):**
    ₹21,80,000 (Gross Salary) - ₹2,60,000 (HRA Exemption) - ₹50,000 (Standard Deduction) = **₹18,70,000**

5.  **Deductions under Chapter VI-A:**
    *   **Section 80C:** Employee PF (₹1,20,000) + ELSS (₹30,000) = ₹1,50,000.
        *(Maximum limit for 80C is ₹1,50,000, so full amount is allowed.)*
    *   **Section 80D:** Health Insurance Premium = ₹25,000.
    *   **Section 80CCD(1B):** NPS contribution = ₹50,000.
        *(Additional deduction over and above 80C limit.)*
    *   **Total Deductions = ₹1,50,000 + ₹25,000 + ₹50,000 = ₹2,25,000**

6.  **Net Taxable Income:**
    ₹18,70,000 (Gross Taxable Income) - ₹2,25,000 (Total Deductions) = **₹16,45,000**

7.  **Annual Tax Calculation (using unchanged tax slabs):**
    *   Up to ₹2,50,000: ₹0
    *   ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000 (₹2,50,000 @ 5%): ₹12,500
    *   ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000 (₹5,00,000 @ 20%): ₹1,00,000
    *   ₹10,00,001 to ₹16,45,000 (₹6,45,000 @ 30%): ₹1,93,500
    *   **Total Income Tax = ₹12,500 + ₹1,00,000 + ₹1,93,500 = ₹3,06,000**

8.  **Add Health and Education Cess (4%):**
    4% of ₹3,06,000 = ₹12,240

9.  **Total Annual Tax Liability:**
    ₹3,06,000 + ₹12,240 = **₹3,18,240**

10. **Monthly TDS:**
    ₹3,18,240 / 12 months = **₹26,520**

Priya's employer will deduct approximately ₹26,520 as TDS from her salary each month.

## FAQ

### What is the new Income Tax Act 2025?
The Income Tax Act 2025 is a new legislation effective from April 1st, 2026, which replaces the long-standing Income Tax Act 1961. It governs all aspects of income tax, including TDS on salary, with updated forms and section numbers, though tax rates and slabs remain unchanged.

### How does the new Act affect my TDS calculation?
While the tax rates and slabs for individuals remain the same, the new Act introduces changes to the specific forms and section numbers used for claiming exemptions and deductions. This means the procedural aspects of how your employer calculates and reports TDS have been updated.

### What if I have multiple employers in a financial year?
If you switch jobs, your new employer will consider your income from the previous employer and the TDS already deducted. You must provide Form 16 from your previous employer to your new employer to ensure accurate TDS calculation and avoid over-deduction.

### Can my employer deduct more TDS than required?
Yes, if you do not submit your investment declarations or proofs on time, your employer might deduct higher TDS as they won't consider your eligible deductions. You can claim a refund for any excess TDS when filing your income tax return.

### What is Form 16 and why is it important?
Form 16 is a certificate issued by your employer detailing the salary paid to you and the TDS deducted and deposited on your behalf. It's a crucial document for filing your income tax return as it summarises your income and tax payments.

### How can I ensure my employer deducts the correct TDS?
The most effective way is to accurately declare all your planned investments and expenses through Form 12BB at the start of the financial year and submit actual proofs of these investments and expenses periodically or by the deadlines set by your employer.

### What if I forget to submit investment proofs?
If you miss the deadline for submitting investment proofs, your employer will likely not consider those deductions, leading to higher TDS. You can still claim these deductions when you file your income tax return directly with the Income Tax Department.

## FAQ

### What is the new Income Tax Act 2025?
The Income Tax Act 2025 is a new legislation effective from April 1st, 2026, which replaces the long-standing Income Tax Act 1961. It aims to simplify and modernise India's direct tax system, with updated forms and section numbers.

### How does the new Act affect my TDS calculation?
The new Act makes the New Tax Regime the default for TDS calculation, featuring revised tax slabs and a standard deduction of ₹75,000. Most Chapter VI-A deductions are not available under this default regime, but the Old Tax Regime remains an option for those who choose it.

### What if I have multiple employers in a financial year?
If you switch jobs, your new employer will consider your income from the previous employer and the TDS already deducted. You must provide Form 130 (the new Form 16) from your previous employer to your new employer to ensure accurate TDS calculation.

### Can my employer deduct more TDS than required?
Yes, if you do not submit your tax regime choice or investment declarations/proofs on time, your employer might deduct higher TDS. You can claim a refund for any excess TDS when filing your income tax return.

### What is Form 130 and why is it important?
Form 130 is the new certificate issued by your employer (replacing Form 16) detailing the salary paid to you and the TDS deducted and deposited on your behalf under the Income Tax Act 2025. It is crucial for filing your income tax return.

### How can I ensure my employer deducts the correct TDS?
The most effective way is to formally declare your preferred tax regime (New or Old) to your employer. If you choose the Old Regime, accurately declare all your planned investments and expenses through Form 12BB and submit actual proofs by the employer's deadlines.

### What if I forget to submit investment proofs for the Old Regime?
If you miss the deadline for submitting investment proofs for the Old Tax Regime, your employer will likely not consider those deductions, leading to higher TDS. You can still claim these deductions when you file your income tax return directly with the Income Tax Department.
