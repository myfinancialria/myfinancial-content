# Market Wrap — 26 June 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed marginally in the green, showing resilience.
- Broader markets, represented by Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100, ended the day in negative territory.
- Auto and FMCG sectors led the gains, while Metal and IT sectors were the biggest laggards.
- Market breadth remained negative, with more declining stocks than advancing ones.
- Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their strong buying spree, while Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned net buyers for the day.

## How the market closed

The Indian equity markets witnessed a largely flat to positive close today, with the Nifty 50 managing to eke out marginal gains, closing above the 24,050 mark. The Bank Nifty also ended slightly in the green, while the broader market indices, Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100, faced selling pressure throughout the session.

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, cooled off by 2.54% to settle at 13.05, indicating a slight easing of market apprehension.

## Sectors — winners and losers

Today's session saw a clear divergence in sectoral performance, with defensive and consumption-oriented sectors leading the charge, while cyclicals and growth sectors faced headwinds.

**Top 3 Gainer Sectors:**
- **AUTO (+2.25%)**: The Automobile sector was the top performer today, likely benefiting from the positive sentiment around easing economic headwinds and falling crude oil prices, which can reduce input costs and boost consumer spending.
- **FMCG (+0.68%)**: Fast-Moving Consumer Goods stocks also saw buying interest, often considered a defensive play in volatile markets.
- **REALTY (+0.33%)**: The Realty sector managed to close in positive territory, albeit with modest gains.

**Top 3 Loser Sectors:**
- **METAL (-1.37%)**: The Metal sector was the biggest drag on the market, experiencing significant selling pressure.
- **IT (-0.86%)**: Information Technology stocks also faced declines, possibly influenced by the broader global tech sell-off seen in Asian and US markets.
- **ENERGY (-0.68%)**: The Energy sector ended lower, despite falling crude oil prices, which could impact upstream companies negatively.

## Market breadth

The market breadth was decidedly negative today, with 174 advances against 324 declines out of a total of 498 stocks traded on the NSE. This indicates that despite the Nifty and Bank Nifty closing marginally higher, the underlying sentiment across the broader market was weak, with more stocks losing ground than gaining.

## What moved markets today

Several key developments influenced market sentiment today, both domestically and globally.

**Geopolitical Easing and Economic Optimism**
A significant driver of positive sentiment was the **US-Iran peace deal**, which has led to expectations of lower oil prices and an improved global economic outlook. Goldman Sachs has already upgraded India's 2026 real GDP growth forecast to 6.8% from 6.5%, citing lower oil prices, easing supply disruptions, and resilient domestic demand. Crisil Ratings also reported that falling crude oil prices could limit India Inc.'s profitability hit to about 100 basis points this fiscal year, significantly less than previous estimates. This overall easing of geopolitical tensions and the associated economic benefits provided a tailwind for the Indian markets.

**Falling Crude Oil Prices**
The anticipation of **crude oil prices dropping to around $65 per barrel** was a major positive for India, a net oil importer. Lower crude prices are expected to ease India's economic headwinds, stabilize the Rupee, and boost market sentiment by reducing import bills and inflationary pressures. This directly contributed to the strength seen in sectors like Auto, which benefit from reduced input costs.

**Robust IPO Pipeline**
The Indian IPO market is gearing up for a strong second half of 2026, with **major listings like Jio Platforms, NSE, and SBI Mutual Fund** in the pipeline. SBI Mutual Fund's unlisted shares have already delivered nearly 3.8x returns over the past three years, highlighting investor enthusiasm. This robust pipeline indicates strong investor confidence and liquidity in the market.

**Global Tech Sell-off**
On the global front, a **renewed global technology sell-off** impacted market sentiment, particularly in the IT sector. Japan's Nikkei index dropped 4% due to a sharp decline in SoftBank Group following reports of a potential delay in OpenAI's IPO. US stock futures also declined, with Nasdaq futures dropping over 1%, influenced by concerns over high valuations and rising market volatility. This global trend likely contributed to the weakness observed in India's IT sector today.

## Setup for tomorrow

Tomorrow's session will likely continue to be influenced by global cues and the ongoing domestic institutional buying. While the Nifty managed to close positive today, the negative market breadth suggests underlying caution. Investors will be watching for further developments on crude oil prices and any fresh global economic data.

Globally, US stock futures are pointing lower due to the tech sell-off, and Asian markets like Japan's Nikkei also saw significant declines today. Key data releases overnight and the performance of US markets will provide crucial direction. Sectors like Auto and FMCG might continue to show resilience, while the Metal and IT sectors could remain under pressure.
