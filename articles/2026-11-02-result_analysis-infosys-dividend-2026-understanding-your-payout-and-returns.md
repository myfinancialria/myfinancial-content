## What this means in plain terms

When a company like Infosys declares a dividend, it's essentially sharing a portion of its profits with its shareholders. Think of it as a reward for owning a piece of the company. An "interim dividend" means it's paid out during the financial year, rather than waiting for the full year's results. For Infosys, they announced an interim dividend of ₹26 for every share you own, specifically for the financial year 2027 (FY27). This announcement came on October 23, along with their September quarter results.

The "record date" is crucial here. It's the cut-off date to determine which shareholders are eligible to receive the dividend. If you own Infosys shares by the end of tomorrow, November 3, 2026, your name will be recorded as a shareholder, and you'll receive the ₹26 dividend for each share you hold. Shares typically trade "ex-dividend" on or before the record date, meaning new buyers on or after this date will not be entitled to this particular dividend payment. This mechanism ensures clarity on who gets paid.

## Taxation of Dividends: What Indian Investors Need to Know

For investors in India, dividend income is taxable in the hands of the recipient. This regime came into effect from April 1, 2020, following the Union Budget 2020. Prior to this, companies paid Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT), and dividends were exempt for shareholders up to a certain limit. Now, dividend income is added to the investor's total income and taxed at their applicable income tax slab rates.

For resident Indian individuals, the dividend income received from Infosys will be clubbed with their other income sources and taxed as per the slab rates ranging from 5% to 30% (plus cess and surcharge, if applicable). For Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), dividend income is also taxable in India. Generally, a Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is applied by the company at the time of dividend payment, typically at a rate of 20% for NRIs, though this can vary based on Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAA) between India and the NRI's country of residence. NRIs should consult their tax advisors to understand the specific implications under their DTAA and explore options for claiming tax credits in their country of residence. It is crucial for all investors to accurately report dividend income in their income tax returns.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 42, ₹35L CTC, Mumbai**, a salaried professional who has invested in Infosys shares.

Priya holds 500 shares of Infosys in her demat account.
The company announced an interim dividend of ₹26 per equity share for FY27.
The record date for this dividend is tomorrow, November 3, 2026.

1.  **Eligibility Check:** Since Priya holds 500 shares by the record date, she is eligible for the dividend.
2.  **Total Dividend Income:** Priya will receive ₹26 per share for her 500 shares.
    Total Dividend = Number of Shares × Dividend per Share
    Total Dividend = 500 shares × ₹26/share = ₹13,000.
3.  **Taxation:** Assuming Priya falls into the 30% income tax bracket (plus 4% health and education cess), her effective tax rate would be 31.2%.
    Taxable Dividend Income = ₹13,000.
    Income Tax on Dividend = ₹13,000 × 31.2% = ₹4,056.
4.  **Net Dividend Received:**
    Net Dividend = Total Dividend – Income Tax on Dividend
    Net Dividend = ₹13,000 – ₹4,056 = ₹8,944.

So, Priya will receive a net amount of ₹8,944 from this interim dividend, after accounting for her applicable income tax. This example highlights the direct impact of dividend income and its taxation on an investor's overall returns.

## FAQ

### What is an interim dividend?
An interim dividend is a dividend paid by a company to its shareholders during the course of a financial year, before the final annual results are declared and the annual general meeting is held. It's usually based on the company's performance in the initial quarters.

### How is the record date different from the ex-dividend date?
The record date is the cut-off date by which you must be listed as a shareholder to receive the dividend. The ex-dividend date is typically one business day before the record date. If you buy shares on or after the ex-dividend date, you will not be entitled to the upcoming dividend payment.

### How are Infosys dividends taxed for resident Indians?
For resident Indians, dividend income is added to your total income and taxed at your applicable income tax slab rates. There is no Dividend Distribution Tax (DDT) paid by the company anymore, and the entire dividend amount is taxable in the recipient's hands.

### Do NRIs pay tax on Infosys dividends?
Yes, NRIs are also subject to tax on dividend income in India. Generally, TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) is applied by the company at a rate of 20%, though this can be lower based on applicable Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAA) between India and the NRI's country of residence.

### What is dividend yield, and why is it important?
Dividend yield is a financial ratio that shows how much a company pays out in dividends each year relative to its stock price. It's calculated as annual dividend per share divided by the share price. It's important for income-focused investors as it indicates the return on investment purely from dividends.

### Can I reinvest my Infosys dividend automatically?
While some companies offer Dividend Reinvestment Plans (DRIPs), most Indian companies credit the dividend directly to your bank account. You would then need to manually use these funds to purchase more shares if you wish to reinvest.
