# Pre-Market Brief — 31 August 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets presented a mixed picture, with US indices closing lower and Asian markets showing varied performance.
*   GIFT Nifty is signalling a slightly negative open for the Indian benchmark indices.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong support on Friday.
*   Concerns over potential US Fed rate hikes and new Chinese property regulations are key global triggers this week.
*   Nifty's immediate support is seen around 24000, with resistance at 24200.
*   Rising US 10-year yields and global central bank rate decisions are the main risks to monitor today.

Overnight, US markets closed in the red, with the Nasdaq leading the declines, indicating a cautious sentiment among investors. Asian markets this morning are largely mixed, with China's Shanghai Composite showing gains while Nikkei and Hang Seng are slightly down. This mixed global backdrop suggests a subdued start for Indian equities.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee strengthened slightly against the US Dollar, with USD/INR at 95.22, down 0.26%. The Dollar Index also saw a minor dip of 0.16% to 99.54. In commodities, Brent Crude was down 0.81% to $88.59, while WTI Crude rose significantly by 3.09% to $85.98. Gold prices edged up by 0.20% to $4487.20, and Silver gained 0.98% to $67.65. The US 10-year Treasury yield saw a notable increase of 1.03% to 4.72%.

**Interpretation:** A stronger Rupee (USD/INR down) is generally positive for importers and helps curb imported inflation, but can be a slight headwind for IT and pharma exporters. The rise in WTI crude, despite Brent's dip, suggests underlying demand concerns or supply disruptions, which if sustained, could put pressure on Indian oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors. The significant jump in US 10-year yields is a key concern, as higher global yields can make emerging markets like India less attractive for FII flows and increase borrowing costs. Gold and Silver showing gains could indicate some safe-haven buying amidst global uncertainties.

## FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape
On Friday, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the Indian cash market, offloading equities worth ₹-5,040 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide strong support, buying equities worth ₹5,184 crore. Over the last five sessions, FIIs have been net sellers to the tune of ₹-2,059 crore, while DIIs have been significant net buyers, pumping in ₹19,309 crore into the market.

    This week is packed with significant global events, including the G20 meeting, crucial US jobs data, and various central bank rate decisions.

    Jefferies has retained its 'Buy' rating on HDFC Bank but lowered its target price from ₹1,050 to ₹880 following CEO Sashidhar Jagdishan's decision not to seek reappointment. The brokerage cited near-term concerns.
    **Why it matters:** HDFC Bank is a major heavyweight in Indian indices. A target price cut, even with a 'Buy' rating, could weigh on the stock and potentially impact the broader banking sector and Nifty.
*   **Transformers and Rectifiers shares rally 8% after receiving order worth up to Rs 100-500 crore from MEIL**
    Shares of Transformers and Rectifiers surged after securing a large order from MEIL for manufacturing transformers and related work, valued between ₹100 crore to ₹500 crore.
    **Why it matters:** This is positive news for Transformers and Rectifiers, indicating strong order book visibility and potential for revenue growth, which could see the stock in focus today. It also highlights activity in the power infrastructure segment.
*   **Global Market: Chinese property stocks slide as new rules raise consolidation fears**
    Chinese property stocks faced pressure after Beijing introduced new rules aimed at reducing developers’ reliance on homebuyer funds and curbing risks from unfinished projects.
    **Why it matters:** Concerns in China's property sector can have ripple effects across global markets, including commodity prices and overall investor sentiment towards emerging markets. It could add to global risk aversion.
*   **India bonds slump on Fed rate hike bets, benchmark trades at discount**
    Indian government bonds saw a downturn as yields spiked significantly, driven by hawkish statements from the head of the U.S. Federal Reserve, fueling concerns about imminent rate hikes.
    **Why it matters:** Rising bond yields in India indicate higher borrowing costs for the government and corporations. This can impact interest-rate sensitive sectors and make equities less attractive compared to fixed-income investments, potentially affecting FII flows.
*   **Zee Entertainment shares crash 14% amid Subhash Chandra’s 99.97% loan haircut row**
    Zee Entertainment shares crashed after the NCLT approved a repayment plan involving founder Subhash Chandra, which entails a recovery of around ₹6.5 crore against admitted creditor claims.
    **Why it matters:** This news is a significant negative for Zee Entertainment and could lead to continued selling pressure on the stock. It also raises questions about corporate governance and could impact investor confidence in the media sector.
*   **IGL shares jump 3% after CNG price hike in Delhi NCR**
    Indraprastha Gas Limited (IGL) shares rose after the company hiked CNG prices by ₹3.89 per kg in Delhi-NCR, citing higher imported LNG costs.
    **Why it matters:** A price hike for CNG is positive for IGL's margins and profitability, which could see the stock perform well today. It also reflects the impact of global energy prices on domestic consumers and companies.
*   **Ather Energy shares rally 4% after launch of Konarc electric scooter at Rs 99,999**
    Ather Energy shares rose after the company launched its new Konarc electric scooter at a starting price of ₹99,999, aiming to attract a wider audience.
    **Why it matters:** The launch of a mass-market electric scooter could significantly boost Ather Energy's sales volumes and market share, providing a positive catalyst for the stock and potentially the broader EV segment.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
*   **G20 Meeting:** Major global summits like the G20 often lead to policy announcements or discussions on international trade, finance, and geopolitical issues. Markets typically react to any significant outcomes that could impact global economic stability or trade relations, often leading to volatility.
*   **US Jobs Data:** Key US economic data, particularly employment figures like Non-Farm Payrolls, are closely watched by global markets. Strong jobs data can fuel expectations of tighter monetary policy from the US Federal Reserve, potentially leading to higher US bond yields and a stronger dollar, which can impact FII flows into emerging markets. Weak data might suggest a slowdown, potentially leading to risk-off sentiment.
*   **Global Central Bank Rate Decisions:** This week includes several central bank rate decisions. Such announcements are critical for market direction. Hawkish stances (rate hikes) can lead to bond market volatility and a shift away from risk assets, while dovish stances (rate cuts or pauses) can support equity markets.

Investors should remain vigilant about global macroeconomic data and central bank actions throughout the week.
