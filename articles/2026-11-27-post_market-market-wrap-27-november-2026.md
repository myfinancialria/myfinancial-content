# Market Wrap — 27 November 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed lower today, breaking a multi-day winning streak.
- Weakness in IT and Auto sectors primarily dragged the headline indices down.
- Midcap and Smallcap indices, however, showed resilience and ended in positive territory.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) both remained net buyers in the cash market.
- BEML Ltd. was the top gainer in the Nifty 500, surging nearly 15%.
- Jupiter Wagons Ltd. was the biggest loser among Nifty 500 stocks, declining almost 6%.
- F&O positioning data is unavailable for today and will be updated from the next session.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equities witnessed a day of consolidation and profit-booking, with the Nifty 50 closing down by 1.07% at 25,607.20 and the Bank Nifty shedding 0.78% to end at 63,040.90. The session saw a gap-down open for the Nifty, which then trended downwards for most of the day before a slight recovery from its lows. In contrast, the broader market showed strength, with the Nifty Midcap 100 gaining 0.10% and the Nifty Smallcap 100 rising 0.56%.

India VIX, the volatility gauge, eased by 1.72% to 16.55, indicating a slight reduction in market nervousness despite the benchmark indices' fall.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market today saw a divergence in performance, with large-cap indices like Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty experiencing declines, while mid and small-cap segments showed resilience. The primary drag on the Nifty 50 came from heavyweights in the IT and Auto sectors, which were the biggest sectoral losers. Global cues were mixed; while US stocks logged weekly gains overnight, concerns over potential Fed rate hikes continued to weigh on gold prices and contributed to rising US Treasury yields. This, in turn, impacted Indian government bonds, which slumped as rising oil prices added to RBI hike bets earlier in the week. However, today's news of OPEC+ likely maintaining production targets and a significant drop in Brent crude prices by the end of the day could offer some relief. The sustained buying interest from both Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided a floor to the market, preventing a sharper fall and supporting the broader market. China's cabinet announcing support for its stock market also positively impacted global equities and copper prices.

## News that moved stocks
- **OPEC+ set to keep oil production targets steady: Report**
  OPEC+ is expected to maintain its production targets for Q1 2027, despite complexities from conflicting member estimates and the Iran conflict. This news could influence the Energy sector and broader market sentiment regarding inflation.
- **Copper sweeps higher as China offers stock market support**
  Copper prices rose significantly after China's cabinet announced support for its stock market, positively impacting global equities. This news could have contributed to the strength seen in the Metal sector today.
- **Gold pares gains as Fed-hike risks linger, eyes first weekly rise in three weeks**
  Gold prices saw a modest uptick but pared gains as the robust US dollar and surging Treasury yields made investors wary of potential Fed rate hikes. This indicates continued caution in precious metals.
- **Blackstone sells 1.55% stake in IGI for ₹280 crore to meet public shareholding norms**
  Blackstone completed the sale of a 1.5% stake in International Gemological Institute through block deals, selling over 67.16 lakh shares at an average of ₹417 each. This is a specific corporate action.
- **Capital market industry hopes for relief in capital gains taxation in Budget 2027-28**
  The capital market industry is anticipating relief in capital gains taxation, including a possible reduction in short-term capital gains tax, in the upcoming Union Budget 2027-28. This sentiment could positively influence investor confidence in the long run.
- **India bonds slump as oil spike adds to RBI hike bets**
  Indian government bonds declined this week, influenced by rising oil prices and US Treasury yields, with the benchmark bond yield reaching its highest point in a week. This reflects market concerns about inflation and potential monetary policy tightening.
- **Why is RBI moving gold back to India? What Russia's frozen reserves and 1991 crisis reveal**
  The RBI's domestic gold holdings increased to 740.13 metric tonnes by September 2026, up from 680.05 metric tonnes in March 2026. This strategic move by the central bank could be a measure to strengthen reserves and manage geopolitical risks.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was primarily driven by a combination of global cues and sector-specific profit-booking rather than a single defining event. The weakness in the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty can be attributed to selling pressure in the IT and Auto sectors, which were the top losers. Despite this, the resilience in Midcap and Smallcap indices, coupled with strong buying from both FIIs and DIIs, prevented a deeper cut. Global factors like the US stock market's weekly gains provided some positive backdrop, but lingering concerns about Fed rate hikes and rising US Treasury yields kept a lid on enthusiasm. News regarding OPEC+ maintaining production targets and China's support for its stock market offered some commodity-specific positive cues, benefiting sectors like Metal.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead, global cues will continue to play a crucial role. The US Dollar Index closed higher at 104.05, and the USD/INR pair also strengthened to 92.98, which could put pressure on the rupee. US 10-year Treasury yields rose to 4.94%, indicating continued global interest rate concerns. However, a significant drop in Brent Crude prices to $90.64 could be a positive for India, an oil-importing nation. Gold and Silver saw substantial gains, suggesting a flight to safety or increased commodity demand. Investors will be watching for any further developments on global interest rates and commodity prices. Sectors like IT and Auto will be on watch to see if today's selling pressure continues, while Metal and Energy sectors might see continued interest. Key levels for Nifty 50 to watch would be around 25,500 on the downside and 25,750 on the upside.
