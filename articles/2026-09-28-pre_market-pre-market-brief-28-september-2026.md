# Pre-Market Brief — 28 September 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets show a mixed picture, with US indices closing higher but Asian markets opening mixed, indicating caution.
- GIFT Nifty is signalling a significant gap-down open for the Indian benchmark Nifty 50.
- FIIs continued their selling streak for the fourth out of five sessions, while DIIs remained net buyers, providing support.
- Elevated US 10-year yields and India's own rising bond yields are a key concern, potentially impacting FII flows and borrowing costs.
- Nifty 50's immediate support is around 22693-22800, with resistance at 22988-23000.
- The day's main risk is the sustained FII selling pressure combined with rising bond yields globally and domestically.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Overnight, US markets closed with gains, with the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all posting positive returns. European markets also ended in the green. However, this morning, Asian markets are showing a mixed trend, with Japan's Nikkei 225 down and China's Shanghai Composite experiencing a notable decline, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng saw modest gains.

**Interpretation:** The positive close in US and European markets might offer some initial relief, but the mixed to negative cues from key Asian markets this morning, particularly the significant drop in Shanghai and Nikkei's decline, suggest a cautious start for Indian equities. The global tone is not uniformly supportive.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) strengthened slightly against the Dollar, closing at ₹95.96, a 0.21% gain. The Dollar Index, a measure of the US dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies, saw a marginal increase of 0.10% to 101.07. In the commodities space, Brent Crude oil experienced a significant decline of 4.04% to $100.11, while WTI Crude saw a rise of 2.82% to $95.02. Gold prices fell sharply by 3.21% to $4182.60, and Silver also declined by 4.16% to $61.58. The US 10-year Treasury yield rose by 0.43% to 5.18%.

**Interpretation:** A stronger Rupee (USD/INR down) is generally positive for importers and helps to curb imported inflation, while a slightly stronger Dollar Index indicates broader dollar strength. The sharp drop in Brent Crude is a significant positive for India, potentially easing pressure on oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors by reducing input costs. However, the rise in US 10-year yields to 5.18% is a concern, as higher global yields can make emerging markets like India less attractive for FII investments, potentially leading to outflows. The sharp fall in gold and silver could indicate a shift away from safe-haven assets, or profit booking.

The sustained FII selling remains a key headwind.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

- **Global Bond Yields:** The rise in US 10-year yields to 5.18% and India's 10-year yield hitting a two-year high are critical. Markets typically react to rising yields with caution, as they increase the cost of capital for businesses and can divert funds from equities to fixed-income assets. Sustained high yields can lead to FII outflows.
- **Crude Oil Prices:** While Brent crude saw a sharp decline yesterday, the news mentions earlier "surging oil prices" due to geopolitical tensions. Volatility in crude oil prices directly impacts India's import bill and inflation, often leading to market reactions in sectors like OMCs, aviation, and chemicals.
- **FII Flows:** The consistent FII selling trend is a major factor. Markets tend to be sensitive to FII activity, with sustained outflows often leading to downward pressure on indices, especially in large-cap stocks.
- **IPO Pipeline:** The significant IPO pipeline for the second half of 2026 indicates strong primary market activity. While not an immediate market mover, a healthy IPO market can reflect broader economic confidence and attract investor capital.
