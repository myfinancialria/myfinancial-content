# Selling Property in India by an NRI: Tax & TDS Demystified

**TL;DR**
- Capital gains tax implications for an NRI selling property in India remain the same, irrespective of whether the buyer is a resident Indian or another NRI.
- Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) rates for an NRI seller are 20% for long-term capital gains and 30% for short-term capital gains, plus applicable surcharge and cess.
- If a resident Indian buys property from an NRI, the resident buyer is responsible for deducting and depositing the TDS.
- If an NRI buys property from another NRI, the NRI buyer is also obligated to deduct and deposit TDS, which requires obtaining a Tax Deduction Account Number (TAN).
- NRIs can apply for a lower TDS certificate (Form 13) if their actual tax liability is expected to be less than the prescribed TDS rate.

## What this means in plain terms

When a Non-Resident Indian (NRI) decides to sell a property in India, there are specific tax rules that come into play, primarily concerning capital gains tax and Tax Deducted at Source (TDS). Many NRIs wonder if selling to another NRI is different from selling to a resident Indian, especially concerning the tax deducted at the source.

The core principle is that the capital gains tax you, as an NRI seller, are liable to pay on the sale of your property remains consistent, regardless of whether your buyer is an Indian resident or another NRI. The tax liability is determined by how long you held the property (short-term vs. long-term) and the profit you make. However, the process of deducting and depositing the TDS can differ slightly based on the buyer's residency status, and this is a crucial point for both the seller and the buyer to understand to ensure compliance.

## Understanding Capital Gains Tax for NRIs

When an NRI sells immovable property in India, the profit made from the sale is subject to capital gains tax. This tax is categorised into two types based on the holding period of the property:

### Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG)
If you sell the property within 24 months of its acquisition, the profit is considered a Short-Term Capital Gain. These gains are added to your total taxable income in India and taxed at your applicable slab rates. For NRIs, the maximum marginal rate can be up to 30%, plus surcharge and cess.

### Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG)
If you sell the property after holding it for more than 24 months, the profit is treated as a Long-Term Capital Gain. LTCG is taxed at a flat rate of 20%, plus applicable surcharge and cess. For LTCG, you can also benefit from indexation, which adjusts the purchase cost for inflation, thereby reducing your taxable gains.

### Exemptions and Deductions
NRIs can avail certain exemptions to reduce their LTCG tax liability, similar to resident Indians. These include:
*   **Section 54:** Investing the capital gains into purchasing or constructing a new residential house in India within specified timelines.
*   **Section 54EC:** Investing the capital gains into specified bonds (e.g., NHAI, REC bonds) within six months of the sale, up to a maximum of ₹50 lakhs. These bonds have a lock-in period of five years.

## TDS on Property Sale by an NRI: The Buyer's Role

Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is a mechanism where the buyer of the property is mandated to deduct a portion of the sale consideration and deposit it with the Indian government on behalf of the NRI seller. This ensures that the tax liability of the NRI seller is secured at the point of transaction.

### TDS Rates for NRI Sellers
The TDS rates applicable when an NRI sells property are generally:
*   **20%** on Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) on the sale consideration.
*   **30%** on Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) on the sale consideration.
*   Additionally, applicable surcharge and health and education cess are levied on the TDS amount, making the effective rate slightly higher.

It is crucial to note that the TDS is deducted on the *entire sale consideration* if the capital gains cannot be accurately determined at the time of sale, or on the *capital gains* if a lower TDS certificate is obtained.

### Resident Indian Buyer vs. NRI Buyer: The Key Distinction
While the capital gains tax liability for the NRI seller remains the same, the compliance procedure for TDS differs based on the buyer's residency:

*   **When a Resident Indian Buys from an NRI:** Under Section 195 of the Income Tax Act, a resident Indian buyer is obligated to deduct TDS at the prescribed rates and deposit it with the government. The resident buyer must have a Tax Deduction Account Number (TAN) to deduct and deposit TDS. They then provide Form 16A to the NRI seller as proof of TDS deduction.

*   **When an NRI Buys from an NRI:** This is a common point of confusion. Even if the buyer is an NRI, they are still obligated under Section 195 to deduct TDS at the prescribed rates when purchasing property from another NRI. The NRI buyer must *also* obtain a TAN to deduct and deposit the TDS. Failing to do so can result in penalties for the NRI buyer. This ensures that the tax on the NRI seller's gains is captured, irrespective of the buyer's residency.

## Applying for a Lower TDS Certificate (Form 13)

The prescribed TDS rates (20% or 30%) are applied to the *gross sale consideration* or *capital gains*, which might be higher than the actual tax liability, especially after considering indexation benefits and eligible exemptions under Section 54 or 54EC. To avoid excessive TDS deduction, an NRI seller can apply to the Assessing Officer for a "Lower Deduction Certificate" under Section 197 of the Income Tax Act, using Form 13.

### Process for Form 13
1.  **Application:** The NRI seller files Form 13 with the Income Tax Department, providing details of the property sale, cost of acquisition, proposed capital gains, and any eligible exemptions.
2.  **Assessment:** The Assessing Officer reviews the application and, if satisfied, issues a certificate specifying a lower or nil TDS rate.
3.  **Submission to Buyer:** The NRI seller provides this certificate to the buyer, who then deducts TDS at the rate mentioned in the certificate, or no TDS if specified.

This process can significantly help NRIs manage their cash flow by ensuring only the actual tax liability (or a reasonable estimate) is withheld at the source.

## Repatriation of Funds and Other Compliance

After selling property in India, an NRI may wish to repatriate the sale proceeds to their country of residence. This process is governed by the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) regulations, overseen by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).

### Repatriation Rules
*   **Residential Property:** An NRI can repatriate up to USD 1 million per financial year from the sale proceeds of residential property held for at least 10 years, provided the property was acquired from foreign exchange remittances. If the property was acquired from NRO funds or held for less than 10 years, the sale proceeds must first be credited to an NRO account. Subsequent repatriation from the NRO account is permitted up to USD 1 million per financial year, subject to applicable taxes and documentation.
*   **Other Properties:** For commercial property or land, the entire sale proceeds must first be credited to an NRO account. Repatriation from the NRO account is allowed up to USD 1 million per financial year, after tax deductions and submission of Form 15CA/15CB.

### Essential Documents and Filings
*   **PAN:** The NRI seller must have a valid Permanent Account Number (PAN) in India. Without a PAN, TDS will be deducted at a higher rate (20% to 30% even for STCG, or 20% for LTCG, but without the benefit of a lower TDS certificate).
*   **Income Tax Return (ITR):** Even if TDS has been deducted, the NRI seller is required to file an Income Tax Return in India to declare the capital gains and claim any refunds if the TDS deducted was higher than the actual tax liability.
*   **Form 15CA/15CB:** For repatriation of funds exceeding certain limits, an authorised dealer bank will require Form 15CA (undertaking by remitter) and Form 15CB (certificate from a Chartered Accountant) to confirm tax compliance.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 45, an NRI based in Dubai, selling her flat in Gurugram.**

Priya purchased a flat in Gurugram for ₹60 lakhs in April 2018. She sells it in November 2026 for ₹1.5 crores.
*   **Holding Period:** April 2018 to November 2026 = 8 years 7 months (Long-Term Capital Gain).
*   **Original Cost:** ₹60,00,000
*   **Sale Consideration:** ₹1,50,00,000

To calculate indexed cost of acquisition:
*   CII for FY 2018-19 (purchase year) = 280
*   CII for FY 2026-27 (sale year) = Let's assume 360 (for illustrative purposes, actual CII will be released by CBDT)
*   **Indexed Cost of Acquisition** = Original Cost * (CII of Sale Year / CII of Purchase Year)
    = ₹60,00,000 * (360 / 280)
    = ₹60,00,000 * 1.2857
    = ₹77,14,286 (approximately)

*   **Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG)** = Sale Consideration - Indexed Cost of Acquisition
    = ₹1,50,00,000 - ₹77,14,286
    = ₹72,85,714

*   **Tax on LTCG** = 20% of ₹72,85,714 = ₹14,57,143 (plus surcharge and cess)

**Scenario 1: Buyer is a Resident Indian (Mr. Sharma)**
Mr. Sharma, the resident buyer, is obligated to deduct TDS.
*   **TDS Rate:** 20% on the sale consideration for LTCG (assuming no Form 13).
*   **TDS Amount:** 20% of ₹1,50,00,000 = ₹30,00,000.
*   Mr. Sharma will pay ₹1,20,00,000 to Priya and deposit ₹30,00,000 as TDS. He will issue Form 16A to Priya.
*   Priya's actual tax liability is around ₹14.57 lakhs (plus surcharge/cess). The TDS of ₹30 lakhs is significantly higher. Priya should apply for a Form 13 certificate to reduce the TDS to her actual tax liability.

**Scenario 2: Buyer is another NRI (Mr. Khan)**
Mr. Khan, an NRI buyer, is *also* obligated to deduct TDS.
*   **TDS Rate:** 20% on the sale consideration for LTCG (assuming no Form 13).
*   **TDS Amount:** 20% of ₹1,50,00,000 = ₹30,00,000.
*   Mr. Khan must obtain a TAN, deduct ₹30,00,000, and deposit it with the Income Tax Department. He will pay ₹1,20,00,000 to Priya and issue Form 16A.
*   Again, Priya's actual tax liability is lower, so she should apply for a Form 13 certificate. The key takeaway is that the NRI buyer has the same TDS deduction responsibility as a resident buyer.

## What to do this week

1.  **Assess Your Property's Holding Period:** Determine if your potential sale will result in short-term or long-term capital gains, as this impacts the tax rate and available exemptions.
2.  **Obtain Your PAN (if you don't have one):** Ensure you have a valid Permanent Account Number (PAN) linked to your bank accounts in India, as it's mandatory for property transactions and TDS credit.
3.  **Consult a Tax Advisor:** Engage with a qualified Chartered Accountant (CA) in India to understand your specific capital gains tax liability and explore options for applying for a lower TDS certificate (Form 13).

5.  **Review Repatriation Rules:** Familiarise yourself with RBI's FEMA guidelines on repatriating sale proceeds, especially the USD 1 million limit per financial year and the need for NRO/NRE account management.

## FAQ

### What is the primary difference in tax implications for an NRI selling property to a resident vs. another NRI?
The primary difference lies in the TDS deduction and compliance process for the buyer, not the NRI seller's capital gains tax liability. The NRI seller's tax on capital gains remains the same regardless of the buyer's residency.

### Does an NRI buyer need a TAN to deduct TDS when buying from another NRI?
Yes, an NRI buyer is legally obligated under Section 195 of the Income Tax Act to deduct TDS when purchasing property from another NRI and must obtain a Tax Deduction Account Number (TAN) to do so.

### Can an NRI seller avoid TDS deduction completely?
No, TDS deduction is mandatory on property sales by NRIs. However, an NRI seller can apply for a Lower Deduction Certificate (Form 13) under Section 197 to reduce the TDS rate to their actual tax liability or even to nil if eligible.

### How does indexation benefit an NRI seller?
Indexation allows the NRI seller to adjust the original cost of acquisition for inflation, significantly reducing the taxable Long-Term Capital Gains and thus the overall tax liability.

### What happens if the buyer fails to deduct TDS?
If the buyer (resident or NRI) fails to deduct or deposit TDS, they can be held liable for the unpaid tax, along with interest and penalties, by the Income Tax Department.

### Can I repatriate all the sale proceeds immediately?
Repatriation of sale proceeds is subject to FEMA guidelines. Generally, an NRI can repatriate up to USD 1 million per financial year from their NRO account, after all taxes are paid and necessary documentation (like Form 15CA/15CB) is submitted.
