# Market Wrap — 01 September 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian equities witnessed a negative close, with Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty ending in the red, largely influenced by global inflation concerns.
- Surging crude oil prices and rising US bond yields fueled global inflation anxiety, leading to a sell-off in US and European markets, which impacted Indian sentiment.
- Caplin Point Laboratories Ltd. emerged as the top Nifty 500 gainer, while KEI Industries Ltd. was the biggest loser.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were significant net sellers for the day, offloading ₹7,986 crore, continuing a recent trend of outflows.
- Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support, with net buying of ₹4,589 crore.

## How the market behaved today
Indian markets concluded the day in negative territory, with the benchmark Nifty 50 closing marginally lower after a day marked by volatility. The index traded within a range, failing to hold onto higher levels amidst broad-based selling pressure. The Bank Nifty, Midcap 100, and Smallcap 100 indices also ended the session with notable losses.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market's subdued performance today was primarily driven by escalating global inflation fears, which triggered a broad sell-off across international markets. Renewed US-Iran strikes pushed crude oil prices up by over 2%, with Brent Crude topping $92.51 and WTI Crude at $88.03, intensifying concerns about inflationary pressures worldwide. This led to a surge in US bond yields, with the 10-year yield hitting 4.77%, further dampening investor sentiment and causing Wall Street stocks, particularly the Nasdaq, to crash over 1%.

The ripple effect was seen in India, with domestic bonds also inching lower, joining the global debt selloff. Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were significant net sellers, offloading ₹7,986 crore, indicating a cautious stance amidst the global uncertainty and higher bond yields making emerging markets less attractive. This sustained FII outflow, coupled with the broader global cues, put pressure on Indian equities.

Domestically, while the Indian rupee strengthened to a two-month peak against the US dollar due to RBI intervention and dollar inflows, this positive factor was overshadowed by the global headwinds. Sector-wise, defensive sectors like IT and FMCG showed resilience, while interest-rate sensitive and consumption-driven sectors such as Pharma, Realty, and Auto faced selling pressure. News regarding the government halving the holding limit for sugar dealers also negatively impacted sugar stocks.

## News that moved stocks
- **Global Inflation Fears Intensify**: Surging crude oil prices, up more than 2% due to renewed US-Iran strikes, coupled with rising US bond yields, fueled inflation anxiety across global markets. This led to a significant sell-off in US stocks, particularly the Nasdaq, which crashed over 1%, and kept European shares muted. This broad global sentiment directly impacted the overall Indian market, contributing to the negative close and FII outflows.
- **Indian Rupee Strengthens on RBI Intervention**: The Indian rupee surged to a two-month peak against the U.S. dollar, buoyed by decisive central bank interventions and dollar inflows from foreign banks. This positive development for the currency market, however, was not enough to offset the broader negative sentiment in equities.
- **Sugar Stocks Crash on Dealer Limit Halving**: News of the government halving the holding limit for sugar dealers from 4,000 quintals to 2,000 quintals negatively impacted the prices of sugar stocks, sending the sector crashing into the red today. This specific policy move aimed at reducing retail prices directly affected the sugar sector.
- **Japan's 30-Year High Bond Yields Add to Global Liquidity Strain**: Japan’s 30-year high bond yields, along with hawkish US Federal Reserve policies and rising crude oil prices, are straining global liquidity. These factors exert pressure on Indian equities, challenging investor sentiment and contributing to FII outflows.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was predominantly driven by a confluence of global factors, primarily the resurgence of inflation fears. The renewed US-Iran strikes led to a significant spike in crude oil prices, which immediately translated into higher global inflation expectations. This anxiety pushed US bond yields higher, making equities less attractive and triggering a sell-off in major global markets like the US and Europe.

The Indian market reacted to these global cues with FIIs turning significant net sellers, withdrawing ₹7,986 crore. This outflow impacted broad market sentiment, leading to declines across benchmark indices like the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, and particularly affecting interest-rate sensitive sectors like Realty and Auto. While the Indian rupee strengthened due to RBI intervention, this positive domestic development was overshadowed by the global headwinds. Additionally, specific news regarding the government halving the holding limit for sugar dealers caused a notable downturn in sugar stocks, highlighting the impact of policy decisions on specific sectors.
