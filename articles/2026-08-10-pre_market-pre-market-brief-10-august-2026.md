# Pre-Market Brief — 10 August 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight, with US indices closing higher while Asian markets started the day with varied trends.
*   GIFT Nifty is signalling a cautious opening for Indian equities, pointing to a potential gap-down.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net buyers in the cash segment yesterday, providing some support.
*   Adani Group's significant capital expenditure plans are creating new opportunities and order book growth for various listed companies.
*   Nifty 50's 24600 level appears to be a crucial resistance point to watch today, as per Option Chain data.
*   Rising crude oil prices and the negative bias from GIFT Nifty are key risks for the Indian market today.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Overnight, US markets closed on a positive note, with technology stocks leading the charge. The Nasdaq surged over 1%, while the Dow Jones and S&P 500 also posted decent gains. Across Asia this morning, the sentiment was mixed. Japan's Nikkei 225 saw a strong rally, primarily driven by AI and semiconductor stocks, while other Asian indices like the Hang Seng and Shanghai Composite recorded modest gains.

**Interpretation:** The positive close in US markets and a strong showing in Japan could offer some tailwinds. However, the overall mixed Asian cues suggest that Indian markets might open cautiously, especially given the negative indication from GIFT Nifty. The strength in tech and AI globally might provide some momentum for Indian IT and related sectors.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) showed a slight appreciation against the US Dollar, closing at ₹95.23 with a marginal decline of 0.09%. The Dollar Index, which measures the greenback against a basket of major currencies, edged up by 0.12% to 99.72.

Crude oil prices saw an uptick, with Brent Crude rising by 0.96% to $84.35 per barrel and WTI Crude gaining 0.79% to $78.80 per barrel. Gold prices also moved higher, increasing by 1.03% to $4385.30. Silver followed suit, up 0.68% to $63.76.

The US 10-Year Treasury Yield saw a minor dip of 0.21%, settling at 4.66%.

**Interpretation:** A slightly stronger Rupee is generally positive for importers and helps contain imported inflation. However, the rising crude oil prices are a concern for India, a major oil importer. This could put pressure on sectors like Oil Marketing Companies (OMCs), paint manufacturers, and aviation. A higher Dollar Index typically indicates global risk aversion, which can sometimes lead to FII outflows from emerging markets. The slight dip in US 10-year yields could be marginally supportive for global equity flows. Rising gold prices often suggest increased safe-haven demand amidst global uncertainties.
