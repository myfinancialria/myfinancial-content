# Pre-Market Brief — 21 May 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets ended mixed, with US indices closing lower and most Asian markets showing weakness, while European markets saw minor gains.
*   Crude oil prices surged significantly on renewed Middle East concerns, potentially impacting India's import bill and inflation outlook.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, offloading ₹1,597 crore in Indian equities, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support with net purchases of ₹1,968 crore.
*   LIC announced a 1:1 bonus issue, a significant corporate action for the insurance giant, with a record date of May 29.
*   SEBI has proposed changes to the pre-open listing session for IPOs and relisted stocks, aiming for better price discovery.

## Where global markets closed
Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight. US indices, including the Dow, Nasdaq, and S&P 500, closed in negative territory, largely influenced by rising oil prices and renewed geopolitical concerns in the Middle East. European markets, however, managed to eke out minor gains. In Asia, the Hang Seng and Shanghai indices saw notable declines, while data for Japan's Nikkei 225 was unavailable. The overall global tone appears cautious, with investors weighing inflation risks from higher crude and geopolitical tensions.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, with USD/INR closing at ₹96.19, a decline of 0.39%. This appreciation of the rupee could offer some relief on import costs. Conversely, the Dollar Index, which measures the US dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies, rose by 0.30% to 99.41, indicating broader dollar strength globally.

In commodities, crude oil prices saw a significant jump. Brent Crude surged by 2.57% to $107.72 per barrel, and WTI Crude rose by 3.22% to $101.42 per barrel. This sharp increase is attributed to renewed concerns in the Middle East and could be a key factor for Indian markets, potentially pushing up domestic fuel prices and impacting inflation. Gold prices saw a marginal dip of 0.36% to $4514.90, while Silver also declined slightly by 0.01% to $75.85.

The US 10-Year Treasury Yield increased by 1.03% to 4.62%. Rising US bond yields can make dollar-denominated assets more attractive, potentially leading to capital outflows from emerging markets like India.

## Corporate actions today
Several companies have corporate actions scheduled with an ex-date today:

*   **GMBREW**: Goes ex-dividend for a payout of ₹9 per share. Shareholders who buy the stock today or later will not be eligible for this dividend.
*   **ITCHOTELS**: Goes ex-dividend for a payout of ₹1 per share. Investors need to have held the stock yesterday to be eligible for this dividend.
*   **AVG**: The stock goes ex-rights for its Rights Issue in the ratio of 8:33 at a premium of ₹135 per share. This means for every 33 shares held, eligible shareholders can apply for 8 new shares at the specified price.
*   **PGIL**: Goes ex-interim dividend for a payout of ₹8.50 per share. To receive this dividend, shares must have been bought before today.

## Recent FII/DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued to be net sellers in the Indian equity market yesterday, offloading shares worth ₹1,597 crore. ## Overnight news that matters

**US stocks slips as oil jumps on renewed Mideast concerns**
Wall Street indices declined as crude oil prices surged following reports of heightened tensions in the Middle East and Iran signaling a tougher nuclear stance.
*Why it matters:* This directly impacts global market sentiment, and the rise in crude oil prices is a significant concern for India, a major oil importer, potentially leading to higher inflation and a larger import bill.

**LIC announces 1:1 bonus issue, sets May 29 as record date**
Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) has announced a 1:1 bonus issue, meaning shareholders will receive one additional share for every share they hold, with May 29 set as the record date.
*Why it matters:* This is a major corporate action for one of India's largest public sector entities. A bonus issue typically increases liquidity and can be seen as a positive signal to shareholders, potentially impacting LIC's stock performance.

**Sebi proposes changes to pre-open listing session of IPOs for better price discovery**
SEBI is reviewing the pre-open call auction session for IPOs and relisted stocks, aiming to address issues like dummy price bands and base price calculations to facilitate more efficient price discovery.
*Why it matters:* This regulatory proposal could improve transparency and fairness in the primary market, benefiting both companies going public and investors participating in IPOs.

**GAIL Q4FY26 Results: Net profit drops 41% YoY to ₹1,481 crore, revenue slips 2.3%; final dividend announced**
GAIL (India) reported a significant 41% year-on-year drop in net profit to ₹1,481 crore for Q4FY26, with revenue also declining by 2.3% to ₹35,705 crore, though a final dividend was declared.
*Why it matters:* As a major public sector undertaking in the gas sector, GAIL's weak earnings could impact investor sentiment towards the energy and infrastructure sectors, especially given the current volatility in crude prices.

**Max Healthcare Q4 results: Cons PAT grows 7% YoY to Rs 342 crore, revenue rises 12%**
Max Healthcare reported a 7% year-on-year growth in consolidated net profit to ₹342 crore and a 12% rise in revenue for Q4FY26, with strong performance from its diagnostic and home healthcare segments.
*Why it matters:* These results indicate continued growth in the healthcare sector, which could provide positive cues for other hospital and healthcare service providers in India.

## What to watch in today's session
1.  **Crude Oil Movement:** Monitor Brent and WTI crude oil prices closely. Any further escalation in Middle East tensions or supply concerns could lead to continued price hikes, impacting India's economic outlook and potentially leading to inflationary pressures.
2.  **FII vs DII Flows:** Observe if the trend of FII selling persists and if DIIs continue to absorb this selling pressure. Sustained FII outflows could weigh on market sentiment, while strong DII buying provides a crucial cushion.
3.  **Corporate Actions Impact:** Keep an eye on stocks like LIC, GMBREW, ITCHOTELS, AVG, and PGIL as they trade ex-dividend or ex-rights today, which could influence their trading patterns.
4.  **Sectoral Performance:** Watch the performance of sectors related to recent earnings, such as healthcare (Max Healthcare) and gas (GAIL), as well as consumer-facing businesses (Honasa Consumer, LG Electronics).
5.  **Global Cues:** Pay attention to how other Asian markets open and trade, as well as any fresh developments from global geopolitical events, which could set the tone for Indian markets.

## Bottom line
The Indian market is likely to open with a cautious bias today, influenced by mixed global cues, particularly the weakness in US and some Asian markets, combined with surging crude oil prices. While domestic institutional buying has provided support, sustained FII selling and geopolitical uncertainties warrant prudence. Investors should remain vigilant about crude oil price movements and their potential impact on inflation and corporate earnings.
