# Market Wrap — 11 August 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed in the red today, extending losses into the afternoon session.
- Global geopolitical tensions, particularly in the Middle East, pushed oil prices higher, weighing on market sentiment and the Indian Rupee.
- Chennai Petroleum Corporation Ltd. (CHENNPETRO) was a standout gainer, surging 15.00% amid rising oil prices.
- Triveni Turbine Ltd. (TRITURBINE) was among the top losers, declining 5.72%.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers in the cash market, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers.
- F&O positioning data is unavailable for this session and will be included in subsequent wraps.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equities experienced a subdued session today, with benchmark indices Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing lower. After opening flat to marginally negative, the market saw selling pressure intensify through the day, leading to a negative close. The Nifty 50 ended at 24471.70, down 0.46%, while the Bank Nifty closed at 57446.25, declining 0.42%. Midcap and Smallcap indices showed a mixed trend, with Midcap 100 marginally down and Smallcap 100 posting slight gains.

| Index | Close | Change | High | Low |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nifty 50 | 24471.70 | -0.46% | 24576.85 | 24429.25 |
| Bank Nifty | 57446.25 | -0.42% | 57607.25 | 57158.10 |
| Nifty Midcap 100 | 63843.85 | -0.02% | 63953.95 | 63586.75 |
| Nifty Smallcap 100 | 19857.15 | +0.22% | 19909.60 | 19834.40 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, eased by 3.18% to 11.86, indicating a slight reduction in expected near-term market fluctuations despite the negative close.

## What moved the market — the factors
The primary driver for today's market behaviour appeared to be global geopolitical tensions, specifically the ongoing uncertainty in the Middle East, which led to a surge in international crude oil prices. This pushed the Indian Rupee to a near two-week low against the US Dollar, closing at 95.43. Higher oil prices typically pose a concern for India, a net oil importer, impacting inflation and current account deficit outlooks.

European markets, in contrast, saw energy stocks gain on elevated oil prices, reflecting a divergence in regional impacts. Domestically, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers, infusing ₹1,975 crore into the market, which provided some support. However, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers, pulling out ₹1,290 crore.

Sectorally, the impact of rising oil was visible, with Oil Gas & Consumable Fuels stocks like Chennai Petroleum Corporation and Mangalore Refinery & Petrochemicals featuring prominently among the top gainers. Conversely, sectors like FMCG, Realty, and Metal faced selling pressure. News of retail traders losing a significant ₹916.85 billion in equity derivatives also highlighted a cautious sentiment among a section of investors.

## Sectors — winners and losers
Today saw a mixed sectoral performance, with Pharma and IT leading the gains, while FMCG and Realty sectors faced significant headwinds.

**Top 3 Gainer Sectors:**
- **PHARMA:** +1.02% - The sector showed resilience, possibly driven by defensive buying or specific stock-related news.
- **IT:** +0.61% - The IT sector saw positive momentum, perhaps buoyed by global tech sentiment, as seen with news around Microsoft and AI.
- **PSU BANK:** +0.01% - A marginal gain, indicating a largely neutral day for public sector banks.

**Top 3 Loser Sectors:**
- **FMCG:** -1.17% - This sector experienced the sharpest decline, possibly due to broader market weakness or specific stock underperformance like Balrampur Chini Mills.
- **REALTY:** -0.99% - The real estate sector also saw significant selling pressure.
- **METAL:** -0.95% - Metal stocks were among the top losers, likely impacted by global commodity price movements beyond oil.

## News that moved stocks
- **Retail traders lost $9.6 billion in equity derivatives.** Indian equity market saw investors experiencing a striking loss of ₹916.85 billion in futures and options trading, indicating a significant reduction in retail trader involvement in equity derivatives. This news highlights the risks associated with F&O trading and could lead to increased caution among retail investors.
- **Rupee retreats to near two-week low, closes at 95.43 vs USD as US-Iran standoff pushes oil higher.** The Indian Rupee tumbled due to surging oil prices and waning optimism regarding a U.S.-Iran deal, with the RBI intervening to mitigate the pressure. This directly impacted the broader market sentiment and could influence import-heavy sectors.
- **Oil rises toward $90 as fresh Trump demands cloud Hormuz deal.** Oil extended its rebound after President Donald Trump made new demands on Iran, further clouding the outlook for a deal to reopen the crucial Strait of Hormuz. This news directly contributed to the gains in Oil & Gas stocks like Chennai Petroleum and MRPL.
- **European shares edge higher as energy stocks gain on Middle East uncertainty.** European markets saw energy stocks rise due to elevated oil prices amid Middle East tensions. This shows how global geopolitical events are influencing sector-specific performance across different markets.
- **MFs pull back: Stakes cut in 12 smallcap stocks after two quarters of buying; stocks fall up to 40%.** Mutual fund holdings declined across 83 BSE smallcaps in June 2026, with KEC International among the stocks experiencing a fall. This news explains the decline in KEC International and signals a shift in institutional interest in some smallcap names.
- **KPI Green Energy Q1 Results: Net profit dips 14% at Rs 95 crore.** KPI Green Energy reported a 14% dip in net profit for the June quarter, citing geopolitical tensions and rising costs. This specific earnings news impacted the stock.
- **Zydus Lifesciences Q1 Results: Net profit down 36% at Rs 939.8 crore.** Zydus Lifesciences reported a significant year-on-year decline in net profit for the first quarter despite robust operational revenue growth. This earnings report likely influenced the stock's performance.
- **Siemens Q1 Results: Net profit declines 18% to Rs 343 crore.** Siemens Ltd reported an 18% decline in net profit in the June quarter, primarily due to fluctuating commodity prices and rising material costs. This news could have weighed on the capital goods sector.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's market session was largely driven by global cues, particularly the escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. News of fresh demands from President Trump on Iran further clouded the outlook for a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, directly leading to a rise in international crude oil prices. This had a dual impact on the Indian market:
1.  **Oil & Gas Sector:** Stocks in the Oil Gas & Consumable Fuels sector, such as Chennai Petroleum Corporation and Mangalore Refinery & Petrochemicals, saw significant gains as higher crude prices typically benefit upstream and refining companies.
2.  **Rupee Depreciation:** The rise in oil prices put pressure on the Indian Rupee, which retreated to a near two-week low against the US Dollar. A weaker rupee can make imports more expensive and contribute to inflationary pressures, impacting overall market sentiment.

Beyond this, a series of Q1 earnings reports from companies like KPI Green Energy, Zydus Lifesciences, and Siemens, which largely showed a decline in net profits, also contributed to stock-specific movements and overall cautiousness.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will continue to play a significant role. The ongoing geopolitical situation in the Middle East and its impact on crude oil prices will be closely watched. The US Dollar Index remained stable at 99.82, while the USD/INR closed at 95.43, indicating continued pressure on the rupee. US 10-year yields edged higher to 4.70. Investors will also monitor any overnight developments in US and Asian markets. Sectors sensitive to crude oil prices, such as Oil & Gas, Airlines, and Chemicals, could see continued volatility. Key levels for Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty will be crucial to watch for directional cues.
