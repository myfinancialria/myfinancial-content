# Market Wrap — 02 September 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian equities concluded the day in negative territory, with both Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty registering declines.
- Global cues, including mixed US jobs data and escalating US-Iran tensions, contributed to the cautious sentiment.
- IFCI Ltd. emerged as the top Nifty 500 gainer, while R R Kabel Ltd. was the biggest loser.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned net buyers today, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their buying streak.
- The Auto, Media, and IT sectors faced significant selling pressure.
- F&O positioning data is unavailable for today, but will be included in future wraps.

## How the market behaved today
Indian benchmark indices witnessed a subdued trading session, closing lower after a day marked by volatility. The Nifty 50 opened flat and saw some recovery from its lows but ultimately closed in the red, while the Bank Nifty also ended with losses. Midcap and Smallcap indices mirrored the broader market sentiment, also finishing lower.

| Index | Close | Change | High | Low |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nifty 50 | 23914.45 | -0.59% | 23914.45 | 23786.80 |
| Bank Nifty | 57172.00 | -0.41% | 57221.10 | 56823.20 |
| Nifty Midcap 100 | 63001.60 | -0.53% | 63007.25 | 62445.15 |
| Nifty Smallcap 100 | 19812.25 | -0.37% | 19833.20 | 19662.15 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, edged up by 0.87% to 11.59, indicating a slight increase in market uncertainty.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market's negative close today was largely influenced by a combination of global cues and a broad-based sell-off in specific sectors. US stocks traded mixed as investors digested fresh US jobs data and monitored escalating attacks between the US and Iran, which kept global sentiment cautious. This global uncertainty, coupled with a surge in oil prices, reignited inflation concerns and contributed to a fifth consecutive session of declines for Indian government bonds, as US Treasury yields approached three-year peaks.

On the domestic front, while Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned net buyers today after a period of selling, and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their buying, this inflow wasn't enough to counter the selling pressure in key sectors. The Auto, Media, and IT sectors bore the brunt of the selling, with Hero MotoCorp Ltd. from the Automobile and Auto Components sector being a notable loser. Conversely, the Energy, Realty, and PSU Bank sectors showed some resilience, providing limited support to the indices.

## Sectors — winners and losers
The market saw a clear divergence in sectoral performance today, with some sectors showing strength while others faced significant headwinds.

**Top 3 gainer sectors:**

- **REALTY:** +0.21% - The real estate sector managed to eke out gains, indicating some underlying demand.
- **PSU BANK:** +0.07% - Public sector banks closed marginally in the green, suggesting selective buying interest.

**Top 3 loser sectors:**
- **AUTO:** -1.79% - The automobile sector was the biggest laggard, facing broad selling pressure.
- **MEDIA:** -1.75% - Media stocks also saw significant declines today.
- **IT:** -1.25% - The information technology sector continued to witness weakness.

## News that moved stocks
- **MSCI to delete Swiggy from standard indices on foreign ownership limit breach risk**
  MSCI announced it will remove Swiggy from its Global Standard Indexes on September 7, 2026, due to potential foreign ownership limit breaches, which could force passive funds to reduce their exposure to the stock.
- **NCDEX, CAI join hands to boost India’s cotton derivatives market**
  The National Commodity and Derivatives Exchange (NCDEX) and the Cotton Association of India (CAI) have entered a five-year partnership aimed at expanding participation in cotton derivatives, improving price discovery, and strengthening risk management through increased futures trading.
- **US stocks drift amid jobs data, Iran war escalation**
  US stock markets traded mixed as investors assessed new US jobs data and escalating military actions between the US and Iran, contributing to a cautious global market sentiment.
- **Indian bonds join global debt selloff, bruised by oil and US Treasury yields**
  Indian government bonds experienced declines for the fifth consecutive session, mirroring a global debt sell-off. This was primarily driven by rising US Treasury yields approaching three-year highs and surging oil prices, which reignited inflation concerns.
- **India gets record $127 billion under FCNR window**
  The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) reported that its dollar swap facility attracted a total of $136.4 billion by August 31, with FCNR-B deposits accounting for the majority of this record inflow.

## Market breadth
The market breadth for the day was negative, with declines significantly outnumbering advances. Out of 498 stocks, 163 advanced while 335 declined, indicating a broad-based selling pressure across the market.

## FII / DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned net buyers today after several sessions of selling, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide strong support to the market.

| Investor Type | Net Flow (₹ crore) |
|---|---|
| FII | +1,143 |

Looking at the 5-day history, FIIs have been net sellers for three out of the last five sessions, with today marking a positive inflow. DIIs, on the other hand, have consistently been net buyers over the past five trading days, providing crucial liquidity and absorbing selling pressure from foreign investors. This consistent DII buying suggests underlying domestic confidence in the Indian market.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was largely driven by a combination of global factors and specific domestic news. The broader market sentiment was influenced by mixed signals from US jobs data and ongoing geopolitical tensions between the US and Iran, which led to cautious trading globally.

A significant event impacting the domestic market was the continued sell-off in Indian government bonds. This was primarily triggered by the surge in US Treasury yields nearing three-year peaks and rising oil prices, which fueled inflation concerns. This global debt sell-off contributed to the overall negative sentiment in equities.

On a more positive note, the Reserve Bank of India announced a record $127 billion inflow under its FCNR window, part of a total $136.4 billion received through its dollar swap facility. This substantial inflow indicates strong confidence from Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and overseas investors in the Indian economy and its currency.

Sectorally, the Auto, Media, and IT sectors faced considerable selling pressure, while Energy, Realty, and PSU Banks managed to hold their ground or post marginal gains. The news of MSCI deleting Swiggy from its standard indexes due to foreign ownership limit risks also highlighted the impact of regulatory and index-related developments on specific stocks.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will continue to play a significant role. US stocks closed mixed today, with the Dow and S&P 500 up slightly, while the Nasdaq slipped. Brent crude prices are still elevated at $94.80, and the US 10-year yield has seen a slight dip to 4.79. Investors will be watching for further developments in the US jobs market and the geopolitical situation. Domestically, the resilience of DII flows and the impact of the record FCNR inflows could provide some support. Key levels for Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty will be crucial to watch for directional cues.
