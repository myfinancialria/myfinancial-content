# Pre-Market Brief — 26 November 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets presented a mixed picture, with US tech indices down, while European and most Asian markets closed higher.
*   GIFT Nifty signals a flat to slightly negative start for the Indian benchmark Nifty 50.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) have been consistent net buyers, driving market momentum, supported by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) yesterday.
*   Rising crude oil prices and the resulting pressure on the Indian rupee are significant headwinds.
*   Nifty's 26000 level is a crucial resistance point to watch today.
*   The US market holiday for Thanksgiving means lower global liquidity, potentially leading to increased volatility.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Global markets saw a mixed performance overnight. US tech-heavy Nasdaq and S&P 500 closed lower, reflecting some weakness in the technology sector, while the Dow Jones managed a modest gain. European markets, led by the FTSE 100, posted strong gains. Asian markets, including Nikkei 225, Hang Seng, and Shanghai, also ended in positive territory this morning.

**Interpretation:** The mixed global cues, with US tech under pressure but other major indices showing strength, suggest a cautious but not overtly negative start for Indian markets. The positive close in most Asian markets could provide some support, but the US market being closed today for Thanksgiving will mean lower global liquidity, which can sometimes amplify local market moves.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) saw a slight appreciation yesterday, closing at 92.54, down 0.29%. However, recent news indicates the rupee has fallen today against the US dollar due to higher crude oil prices. The Dollar Index edged up by 0.08% to 103.75. Brent Crude fell by 3.79% to $91.02, while WTI Crude rose by 0.90% to $84.43, presenting a mixed picture for oil, though news highlights Brent topping $95. Gold gained 1.48% to $3588.30, and Silver surged 4.01% to $41.66. The US 10-Year Yield declined by 1.14% to 4.85%.

**Interpretation:** The news of the rupee falling today, despite yesterday's slight appreciation, is a key concern, primarily driven by rising crude oil prices. A depreciating rupee makes imports costlier and can fuel inflation, putting pressure on sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paints, and aviation. Rising crude prices are also a negative for India as a major oil importer. The uptick in gold and silver could signal increasing safe-haven demand amidst global uncertainties. The fall in US 10-year yields might ease some pressure on global equity flows, but domestic bond yields are reportedly rising due to inflation concerns.

## Stock & sector news that matters

*   **Spike in oil prices partly reverses rupee rally, forward premiums surge**
    The Indian rupee has faced depreciation as a result of escalating oil prices combined with pressures from surrounding currencies. With Brent crude exceeding $96 per barrel, worries about India's import bill are rising.
    **Why it matters:** This is a significant macroeconomic headwind for India. A weaker rupee and higher crude prices can impact inflation, corporate margins (especially for OMCs, paint, and aviation companies), and potentially lead to FII outflows.
*   **India bonds tumble as Brent tops $95 per barrel, RBI flags food inflation risks**
    Indian government bonds fell as oil prices surged, raising concerns about inflation and economic stability. The Reserve Bank of India highlighted rising food inflation pressures, indicating widespread inflationary risks.
    **Why it matters:** Rising bond yields increase borrowing costs for the government and corporations. RBI's concern about food inflation suggests a cautious stance on monetary policy, potentially limiting scope for rate cuts and impacting interest-rate sensitive sectors.
*   **Adani firms plan public bond sale to raise at least Rs 2,000 crore**
    Adani Enterprises and Adani Power are planning public bond offerings to raise at least ₹2,000 crore ($220 million), according to sources.
    **Why it matters:** This fundraising could help revive India's subdued public debt market and indicates the Adani group's continued expansion and funding activities. It could bring Adani group stocks into focus.
*   **Eternal, Adani Enterprises among 10 stocks with highest FII buying in Q2.**
    In the September 2026 quarter, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) boosted their stakes in various firms, notably in Eternal and Adani Enterprises.
    **Why it matters:** This highlights FII confidence in specific stocks, including Adani Enterprises, which could see continued interest from investors.
*   **Why is Alphabet stock sliding? Key factors behind the sell-off**
    Alphabet shares plunged over 5% amid AI investment concerns, regulatory challenges, legal disputes, weakening chip demand expectations, Google's spending uncertainty and broader technology stock weakness.
    **Why it matters:** While specific to a US tech giant, this news reflects broader concerns about AI investment costs and regulatory scrutiny in the tech sector. This could weigh on sentiment for Indian IT and tech-related stocks.
*   **Redington shares jump as Apple expands India retail network**
    Redington shares rose nearly 2% after Apple announced a new Hyderabad store, expanding its Indian retail network. Apple accounts for 30% of Redington’s revenue.
    **Why it matters:** This is positive news for Redington, indicating potential for increased revenue from its significant partnership with Apple. It could lead to positive sentiment for the stock today.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

*   **US Thanksgiving Holiday:** The US stock market will remain shut today, Thursday, 26 November, in observance of Thanksgiving.
    **How markets usually react:** A holiday in major global markets like the US often leads to lower trading volumes and liquidity in other markets, including India. This reduced liquidity can sometimes lead to exaggerated price movements or increased volatility on local news or events.
*   **RBI flags food inflation risks:** The Reserve Bank of India has highlighted rising food inflation pressures.
    **How markets usually react:** Central bank commentary on inflation is closely watched. Persistent inflation concerns can lead to expectations of tighter monetary policy (e.g., higher interest rates), which can negatively impact equity valuations and bond markets.

## What to watch in today's session
1.  **Nifty's 26000 level:** Both technical and option chain data point to this as a crucial resistance. A sustained move above or below this could dictate the day's trend.
2.  **Crude Oil Prices & Rupee Movement:** The trajectory of Brent crude and the USD/INR pair will be critical, given the recent news of rupee depreciation due to oil.
3.  **FII Flows:** While FIIs have been strong buyers, any shift in their stance could impact market momentum.
4.  **Adani Group Stocks:** News of public bond sales and FII buying in Adani Enterprises could keep these stocks in focus.
5.  **Global Tech Sentiment:** Despite the US holiday, the weakness seen in US tech giants like Alphabet and SanDisk could spill over into Indian IT stocks.
6.  **Liquidity Impact of US Holiday:** Be mindful of potentially lower trading volumes and increased volatility due to the US market closure.

## Bottom line
Indian markets are likely to open flat to slightly negative, as indicated by GIFT Nifty, with global cues being mixed. The 26000 level for Nifty will be a key resistance to watch, while strong FII buying over the past few sessions provides underlying support. However, rising crude oil prices and the resulting pressure on the rupee, coupled with the US market holiday impacting global liquidity, remain significant factors that could introduce volatility throughout the day.
