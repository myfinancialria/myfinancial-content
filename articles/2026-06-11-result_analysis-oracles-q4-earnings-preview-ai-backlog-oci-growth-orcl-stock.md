# Oracle's Q4 Earnings Preview: AI Backlog, OCI Growth & ORCL Stock

**TL;DR**
- Oracle's Q4 FY2026 earnings are expected to highlight significant growth driven by AI demand and its cloud infrastructure.
- Analysts anticipate a 20% year-over-year revenue increase to approximately $19.1 billion, with adjusted earnings per share around $1.96.
- A key focus for investors will be how Oracle converts its record $553 billion (as of Q3 FY2026) remaining performance obligations (RPO) into actual revenue.
- Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) is projected to continue its strong momentum, fueled by demand for AI workloads and multi-cloud partnerships.
- The company's substantial capital expenditure for AI data centre expansion and its impact on free cash flow will be closely scrutinised.

## What this means in plain terms

When a company like Oracle announces an "earnings preview," it means analysts and the market are looking ahead to what the company is expected to report for its financial performance in the upcoming quarter. For Oracle's fourth quarter of fiscal year 2026, the buzz is primarily around its significant investments and growth in Artificial Intelligence (AI) and its cloud computing platform, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI).

Investors are keen to see if Oracle's massive AI-related contracts, which have led to a record backlog of future revenue, are starting to translate into actual earnings. This preview isn't just about past performance; it's also about the company's future outlook, especially how it plans to fund its ambitious expansion plans and manage the costs associated with building out its AI infrastructure. Essentially, it's a peek into whether Oracle's big bets on AI and cloud are paying off and what that means for its financial health going forward.

## Expected AI Backlog & Cloud Demand

Oracle's strategic pivot towards AI and cloud infrastructure has been a central theme in its recent performance, and Q4 FY2026 is expected to reinforce this trend. The company entered the quarter with a substantial remaining performance obligations (RPO) backlog, which stood at a record $553 billion at the end of Q3 FY2026. This figure, primarily driven by large-scale AI cloud contracts, signifies strong future demand for Oracle's services. These contracts include commitments from major AI players like OpenAI, Meta, and xAI.

However, investors are keenly focused on the conversion of this backlog into recognised revenue. While a large RPO indicates robust demand, the challenge lies in scaling the infrastructure quickly enough to deliver on these commitments. Oracle's AI strategy involves embedding AI across its entire technology stack, from infrastructure and databases to enterprise applications, positioning AI as a core platform capability rather than a standalone product. This integrated approach aims to make its cloud ecosystem more powerful and appealing to enterprises.

### The Role of AI Infrastructure
Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) is at the forefront of this AI-driven growth. OCI's rapid expansion is attributed to deliberate investments in AI-ready infrastructure, cost-efficient services, deep application integration, robust security, and multi-cloud interoperability. The company has been actively building data centres designed to use clean energy, further aligning with global sustainability goals while positioning itself as a leader in the cloud data centre market for AI applications. The Oracle Multicloud AI Database, for instance, saw a remarkable growth rate of 404% in Q4, making it Oracle's fastest-growing segment. This highlights the increasing demand for high-performance, secure, and compliant cloud solutions, particularly in sectors like healthcare, financial services, and retail.

## Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) Momentum

OCI's performance is a critical driver for Oracle's overall growth. Analysts anticipate continued strength in Oracle's cloud business, with some expecting accelerating Infrastructure-as-a-Service (IaaS) growth. Bloomberg analysts, for example, projected OCI revenue to climb 92% in Q4 FY2026, a significant acceleration from 52% a year earlier. This robust growth is expected to fuel faster overall revenue expansion for the company.

Oracle's multi-cloud strategy, which includes partnerships with hyperscalers like AWS, Microsoft, and Google, allows customers to deploy applications across various clouds seamlessly. This flexibility and interoperability are key differentiators for OCI, with revenue from these hyperscaler partnerships showing substantial growth in previous quarters. OCI also offers industry-specific solutions, appealing to sectors that require high performance, security, and compliance for mission-critical applications.

### Capital Expenditure and Funding
The rapid expansion of OCI and its AI infrastructure comes with significant capital expenditure (CapEx). Oracle's CapEx surged to $50 billion in the nine-month period leading up to Q4 FY2026, compared to $12.1 billion in the prior year. To fund this expansion, the company indicated in February that it might raise up to $50 billion via bonds and convertible preferred stock. This aggressive spending has led to negative free cash flow (FCF), which analysts expect to remain negative, around $3.5 billion, in Q4. Investors will be closely watching management's commentary on its funding plans and how it intends to balance growth with capital intensity to achieve positive free cash flow by 2029.

## ORCL Stock Performance & Investor Outlook

Oracle's stock (ORCL) has experienced volatility, reflecting the market's weighing of immense cloud growth against massive capital expenditures. While ORCL shares were up about 9% year-to-date in 2026, recovering from earlier lows, they remained below their September peaks due to concerns over heavy exposure to OpenAI and the debt taken on for data centre expansion.

Wall Street analysts generally hold a "Strong Buy" consensus rating on ORCL stock, with an average price target that implies significant upside potential. However, there's a division among analysts regarding the stock's future trajectory, with some maintaining bullish targets while others are more cautious due to concerns about execution risks and negative free cash flow. The options market has priced in a potential swing of approximately 12% for Oracle shares following the earnings report, indicating expectations of a sharp move.

### Key Investor Watchpoints
Beyond the headline numbers, investors will be scrutinising several key areas:
*   **Backlog Conversion:** How effectively Oracle is converting its substantial RPO into actual revenue.
*   **OCI Growth Acceleration:** Continued strong growth in OCI, particularly IaaS, is crucial.
*   **Capital Allocation & Free Cash Flow:** Clarity on funding strategies for AI infrastructure and a path towards positive free cash flow.
*   **Guidance for FY2027:** Any updates to revenue and earnings guidance for the next fiscal year will significantly influence investor sentiment. Oracle has already raised its FY2027 revenue guidance to $90 billion, signalling expectations for a much larger revenue base.

## FAQ

### What is "Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO)"?
RPO represents the total value of future contracted revenue that a company expects to recognise. For Oracle, a high RPO signifies strong customer demand and future revenue visibility, especially from large AI cloud contracts.

### Why are capital expenditures a concern for Oracle?
Oracle is investing heavily in building AI data centres to meet the surging demand for its cloud services. This requires substantial capital expenditure (CapEx), which can impact the company's free cash flow in the short term, leading to investor scrutiny on funding and profitability.

### How does OCI compete with other cloud providers?
Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) differentiates itself through investments in AI-ready infrastructure, cost-efficient services, robust security, and multi-cloud interoperability, including partnerships with other major cloud providers.

### What is the significance of AI backlog for Oracle?
The AI backlog indicates strong, pre-committed demand for Oracle's AI cloud services. It suggests that companies are increasingly relying on Oracle's infrastructure for their AI workloads, positioning Oracle as a key player in the generative AI market.

### How might Oracle's Q4 results impact its stock price?
The stock price reaction will depend on whether the reported figures for revenue, earnings, OCI growth, and management's guidance for future CapEx and free cash flow meet or exceed analyst expectations. Positive surprises typically lead to stock appreciation, while unmet expectations can cause declines.

### What does "multi-cloud interoperability" mean?
Multi-cloud interoperability refers to Oracle's ability to allow customers to deploy and manage applications and data across different cloud environments (e.g., OCI, AWS, Azure) in a seamless and efficient manner, often through direct partnerships.

### Is Oracle's shift to cloud and AI affecting its traditional software business?
Yes, Oracle's software revenues are seeing a decline as customers migrate from on-premise software to cloud-based solutions. This reflects the company's ongoing transformation from a legacy software provider to a cloud and AI infrastructure leader.
