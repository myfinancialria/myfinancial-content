# Pre-Market Brief — 23 July 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight, with US tech indices closing lower while European and most Asian markets posted gains.
*   GIFT Nifty is signalling a positive start for Indian equities, pointing to a potential gap-up open.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were both net sellers in the cash market yesterday, indicating cautious sentiment.
*   Escalating Middle East tensions have pushed crude oil prices higher, nearing $100 a barrel, raising concerns about inflation and bond yields.
*   For Nifty, the 24000 level appears to be a crucial resistance point, with support around 23900-23910.
*   The primary risk for today's session remains the continued rise in crude oil prices and the upward trend in US Treasury yields.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Global markets displayed a mixed performance overnight. In the US, major indices like the Nasdaq and S&P 500 closed lower, primarily dragged down by technology stocks following disappointing earnings from Tesla and concerns over Alphabet's capital expenditure plans for AI. The Dow Jones saw a marginal dip. In contrast, European markets, led by the FTSE 100, closed with decent gains. Asian markets this morning are largely positive, with the Nikkei 225 and Hang Seng trading higher, while Chinese stocks saw a slight correction in AI and chip shares.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) depreciated slightly by 0.19% to 96.53 against the US Dollar. The Dollar Index, measuring the dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies, saw a minor dip of 0.15% to 100.99. Crude oil prices surged significantly, with Brent Crude up 1.96% to $95.91 and WTI Crude rising 1.38% to $88.03, driven by escalating Middle East tensions. Gold prices saw a slight decline of 0.48% to $4126.90, while Silver also dipped marginally by 0.05% to $59.99. The US 10-Year Treasury Yield climbed 0.63% to 4.66%.

**Interpretation:** The rise in crude oil prices is a significant headwind for the Indian economy, potentially increasing import bills, stoking inflation fears, and putting pressure on sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paints, and aviation. Higher crude prices are also impacting Indian government bonds, pushing yields up. A depreciating rupee (USD/INR up) generally benefits Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters but increases costs for importers. The rising US 10-year yield could make emerging markets like India less attractive for foreign capital, posing a risk to FII flows.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
*   **Crude Oil Price Movement:** Continued escalation of Middle East tensions could further push crude prices higher. Markets typically react negatively to rising crude, fearing inflation, higher input costs for companies, and potential interest rate hikes.
*   **US 10-Year Treasury Yields:** The upward trend in US yields is a key global factor. Higher US yields can divert foreign capital from emerging markets, leading to FII outflows and putting pressure on domestic bond yields and equity markets.
*   **Ongoing Q1 Earnings Season:** While no Nifty 500/large-cap results are scheduled today, the broader earnings season is underway. Individual company results, especially from bellwether sectors, can cause significant stock-specific movements and set the tone for their respective sectors.
*   **FII/DII Flow Trends:** Daily FII and DII cash market activity will be closely monitored. Sustained selling by FIIs can be a drag on the market, while DII buying often provides a crucial cushion.

1.  **Crude Oil Prices:** Keep a close eye on international crude oil benchmarks as geopolitical tensions continue to influence prices.

## Bottom line
The Indian market is poised for a cautiously positive open, primarily driven by a positive signal from GIFT Nifty and supportive Asian cues. However, the surge in crude oil prices due to escalating Middle East tensions and rising US Treasury yields present significant headwinds, raising concerns about inflation and FII outflows. The 24000 level for Nifty will be critical to watch, as a decisive break on either side could set the tone for the day. Investors should remain vigilant about global developments and their potential impact on key commodities and capital flows.
