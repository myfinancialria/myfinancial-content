# ITR-4 (Sugam) for AY 2026-27: Eligibility, Presumptive Taxation & Filing Guide

**TL;DR**
- ITR-4 (Sugam) is a simplified income tax return form for resident individuals, HUFs, and partnership firms (excluding LLPs) opting for presumptive taxation.
- It is applicable for those with a total income up to ₹50 lakhs and business/professional income under Sections 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE.
- Presumptive taxation allows taxpayers to declare income at a prescribed rate without maintaining detailed books of accounts or undergoing audits.
- The turnover limit for Section 44AD is ₹3 crore (if 95% digital receipts) or ₹2 crore otherwise, while for Section 44ADA, it's ₹75 lakhs (if 95% digital receipts) or ₹50 lakhs otherwise.
- For AY 2026-27, the due date for filing ITR-4 for non-audit cases has been permanently shifted to August 31st.

## What this means in plain terms

For many small business owners, freelancers, and professionals in India, filing income tax returns can seem like a daunting task, often involving complex accounting and audits. This is where ITR-4, also known as Sugam (meaning 'easy' in Hindi), comes in handy. It's a simplified income tax return form designed to reduce the compliance burden for eligible taxpayers.

Essentially, ITR-4 allows you to declare your business or professional income on a "presumptive" basis. Instead of meticulously tracking every expense and calculating your exact profit, you can declare a fixed percentage of your turnover or gross receipts as your income. This scheme is particularly beneficial because it exempts you from the tedious requirement of maintaining detailed books of accounts and getting them audited, provided you meet certain conditions.

## Who can and cannot file ITR-4 (Sugam)

ITR-4 (Sugam) is specifically designed for resident individuals, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), and partnership firms (excluding Limited Liability Partnerships or LLPs) who meet certain criteria.

### Eligibility Criteria for ITR-4

You are generally eligible to file ITR-4 if:
*   You are a Resident Individual, HUF, or a Firm (other than LLP).
*   Your total income for the financial year does not exceed ₹50 lakhs.
*   You have income from business and profession computed on a presumptive basis under Section 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE.
*   You also have income from salary/pension, income from up to two house properties, agricultural income up to ₹5,000, and other sources (excluding winning from lottery, racehorses, etc.).
*   You may also have long-term capital gains under Section 112A up to ₹1.25 lakhs, provided there are no brought-forward or carried-forward capital losses.

### Who cannot file ITR-4

ITR-4 has strict exclusion criteria. You cannot file ITR-4 if:
*   Your total income exceeds ₹50 lakhs.
*   You are a Non-Resident Indian (NRI) or a Resident but Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR).
*   You are a director in a company or have invested in unlisted equity shares.
*   You have income from more than two house properties.
*   You have income from sources like lottery, racehorses, legal gambling, or income taxable at special rates.
*   You have agricultural income exceeding ₹5,000.
*   You have any capital gains (except specific LTCG under 112A up to ₹1.25 lakhs without carry-forward/brought-forward loss).
*   You have foreign assets or foreign income, or signing authority in any foreign account.
*   You are required to maintain regular books of accounts or get your accounts audited as per the Income Tax Act, 1961 (unless your income falls below the presumptive rate and exceeds the basic exemption limit).

## Understanding Presumptive Taxation Schemes (Sections 44AD, 44ADA, 44AE)

The core of ITR-4 lies in the presumptive taxation scheme, which simplifies tax compliance for small taxpayers. This scheme is outlined in three main sections of the Income Tax Act, 1961.

### Section 44AD: For Small Businesses

This section is applicable to resident individuals, HUFs, and partnership firms (other than LLPs) engaged in any business, provided their total turnover or gross receipts do not exceed a certain limit.
*   **Turnover Limit:** For AY 2026-27, the turnover limit for Section 44AD is ₹3 crore, provided that the aggregate of amounts received in cash during the previous year does not exceed 5% of the total gross receipts. If the cash receipts exceed 5%, the limit remains ₹2 crore.
*   **Deemed Income:** Under this scheme, income is presumed to be:
    *   8% of the total turnover or gross receipts.
    *   6% of the total turnover or gross receipts if the amount is received through digital modes (account payee cheque, bank draft, ECS, or other electronic means) before the due date of filing the return.
*   **Exclusions:** This section does not apply to businesses of plying, hiring, or leasing goods carriages (covered under Section 44AE), agency businesses, or businesses earning income in the form of commission or brokerage.

### Section 44ADA: For Specified Professionals

This scheme is for resident individuals and partnership firms (other than LLPs) engaged in specified professions. These include legal, medical, engineering, architectural, accountancy, technical consultancy, and interior decoration professions, along with any other notified by the CBDT.
*   **Gross Receipts Limit:** The gross receipts from the profession should not exceed ₹75 lakhs. This limit is ₹50 lakhs if the amount or aggregate of amounts received in cash during the previous year exceeds 5% of the total gross receipts.
*   **Deemed Income:** Under Section 44ADA, 50% of the total gross receipts are deemed to be the professional income. This 50% is considered the net profit, and no further expenses are allowed for deduction.

### Section 44AE: For Plying, Hiring, or Leasing Goods Carriages

This section applies to individuals, HUFs, and firms (other than LLPs) who own not more than ten goods carriages at any time during the previous year and are engaged in the business of plying, hiring, or leasing such goods carriages.
*   **Deemed Income:** Income is presumed at ₹7,500 per goods carriage for every month or part of a month during which the goods carriage is owned by the taxpayer. For heavy goods vehicles, the presumptive income is ₹1,000 per ton of gross vehicle weight per month or part of a month.

## Key Aspects of Filing ITR-4 for AY 2026-27

Filing ITR-4 involves several steps, and understanding the nuances for AY 2026-27 is crucial for a smooth process.

### Important Changes and Considerations for AY 2026-27
*   **Due Date Shift:** For non-audit cases, the due date for filing ITR-4 has been permanently shifted from July 31st to August 31st, as per the Finance Act 2026.
*   **Two House Properties:** Taxpayers can now report income from up to two house properties in ITR-4, an increase from the previous limit of one.
*   **Unrealised Rent Field:** A new, specific field for "rent which cannot be realised" has been added, which is helpful for taxpayers with rented properties.
*   **Investment Disclosure:** Taxpayers opting for presumptive taxation must report their investments held up to March 31, 2026.
*   **Advance Tax:** Taxpayers opting for presumptive taxation under Sections 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE are required to pay their entire advance tax liability in a single instalment on or before March 15th of the financial year. Failure to do so may attract interest under Section 234C.
*   **Continuity of Scheme:** If you opt for the presumptive taxation scheme under Section 44AD, you are generally required to continue it for the next five years. If you opt out before this period, you will not be eligible to re-opt for the scheme for the subsequent five assessment years.

### Documents Required
To file ITR-4 accurately, keep the following documents ready:
*   PAN and Aadhaar card.
*   Bank statements.
*   Form 26AS, Annual Information Statement (AIS), and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS) to verify TDS/TCS and other financial transactions.
*   Form 16 (if you have salary income).
*   Details of gross receipts/turnover for your business/profession, clearly distinguishing between cash and digital receipts.
*   Details of other income sources like interest from savings accounts, fixed deposits, etc.
*   Proofs for any deductions you wish to claim under Chapter VI-A (e.g., 80C, 80D).

### Filing Process
The Income Tax Department has enabled online filing and utility for ITR-4 for AY 2026-27.
1.  **Login to e-filing portal:** Go to the Income Tax e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in) and log in using your PAN and password.
2.  **Select 'File Income Tax Return':** Navigate to 'e-File' -> 'Income Tax Returns' -> 'File Income Tax Return'.
3.  **Choose Assessment Year and Form:** Select Assessment Year 2026-27 and ITR-4 (Sugam).
4.  **Select Filing Mode:** Choose 'Online' filing mode.
5.  **Pre-filled Data:** Verify pre-filled personal details, salary income, TDS credits, and AIS data against your documents. Make corrections if necessary.
6.  **Enter Income Details:**
    *   Input your gross turnover/gross receipts and the split between digital and cash receipts.
    *   Enter the deemed profit under the relevant presumptive taxation section (44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE). The form does not auto-calculate this.
    *   Declare income from salary/pension, house property, and other sources.
7.  **Claim Deductions:** Fill in eligible deductions under Chapter VI-A (e.g., 80C, 80D).
8.  **Tax Regime Choice:** Explicitly choose your tax regime (old or new). Individuals with business income have restrictions on switching regimes once chosen.
9.  **Review and Validate:** Review all entered information carefully. Validate the form to check for errors.
10. **Pay Balance Tax (if any):** If there's any balance tax payable after accounting for advance tax and TDS, pay it.
11. **Submit and E-Verify:** Submit the return and e-verify it within 30 days of filing. E-verification is crucial for the return to be considered valid.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 35, a freelance graphic designer, based in Mumbai**, with an annual gross professional receipt of ₹40 lakhs for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27). She primarily receives payments digitally, with less than 5% in cash. She also has a salary income of ₹8 lakhs from a part-time consulting role and pays ₹1.5 lakhs towards PPF (eligible for 80C deduction). Her total income is below ₹50 lakhs.

Priya is eligible to file ITR-4 under Section 44ADA as her gross professional receipts are within the ₹75 lakh limit (due to digital receipts exceeding 95%).

Here’s how her income calculation for ITR-4 would look:

1.  **Income from Profession (Section 44ADA):**
    *   Gross Professional Receipts: ₹40,00,000
    *   Deemed Income (50% of gross receipts): ₹40,00,000 * 50% = ₹20,00,000

2.  **Income from Salary:** ₹8,00,000

3.  **Gross Total Income:** ₹20,00,000 (Professional) + ₹8,00,000 (Salary) = ₹28,00,000

4.  **Deductions:**
    *   Standard Deduction (for salary income): ₹50,000
    *   80C (PPF contribution): ₹1,50,000

5.  **Total Deductions:** ₹50,000 + ₹1,50,000 = ₹2,00,000

6.  **Taxable Income:** ₹28,00,000 - ₹2,00,000 = ₹26,00,000

Priya would then calculate her tax liability based on the applicable tax regime (old or new) on her taxable income of ₹26,00,000. She would also need to ensure she paid her advance tax for her professional income by March 15, 2026.

## FAQ

### Who is ITR-4 (Sugam) meant for?
ITR-4 (Sugam) is for resident individuals, HUFs, and partnership firms (excluding LLPs) with a total income up to ₹50 lakhs, who opt for presumptive taxation under Sections 44AD, 44ADA, or 44AE.

### What is presumptive taxation?
Presumptive taxation is a simplified scheme that allows eligible small taxpayers to declare their income at a prescribed rate (e.g., 6%, 8%, or 50% of turnover/receipts) instead of maintaining detailed books of accounts and undergoing audits.

### What are the turnover limits for presumptive taxation under Section 44AD and 44ADA for AY 2026-27?
For Section 44AD, the limit is ₹3 crore if cash receipts are ≤5% of total receipts, otherwise ₹2 crore. For Section 44ADA, the limit is ₹75 lakhs if cash receipts are ≤5% of total receipts, otherwise ₹50 lakhs.

### Can I claim expenses if I opt for presumptive taxation?
Under presumptive taxation, a fixed percentage of turnover/receipts is deemed as profit, and no further deduction for business expenses is generally allowed. The deemed income is considered after all expenses.

### What if my actual income is lower than the presumptive income?
If you declare income lower than the presumptive rates and your total income exceeds the basic exemption limit, you will be required to maintain books of accounts and get them audited.

### Is it mandatory to continue presumptive taxation once opted for?
Under Section 44AD, once you opt for presumptive taxation, you must continue it for the next five years. If you opt out before this period, you become ineligible for the scheme for the subsequent five assessment years.

### What is the due date for filing ITR-4 for AY 2026-27?
For non-audit cases, the due date for filing ITR-4 (Sugam) for AY 2026-27 is August 31, 2026.
