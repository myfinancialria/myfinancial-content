# HRA for Non-Salaried: Understanding Section 80GG

**TL;DR**
- Non-salaried individuals, including self-employed, cannot claim House Rent Allowance (HRA) but can claim a similar deduction under Section 80GG of the Income Tax Act, 1961.
- This deduction is available to those who do not receive HRA from an employer and pay rent for their accommodation.
- To claim the Section 80GG deduction, you must not own any residential property in your name or your spouse's/minor child's name in the city where you reside.
- The maximum deduction allowed under Section 80GG is capped at ₹60,000 per financial year.
- Filing Form 10BA is mandatory to claim this deduction, declaring that you meet all specified conditions.

## What this means in plain terms

Many salaried individuals are familiar with House Rent Allowance (HRA), a component of their salary package that allows them to claim a tax deduction for the rent they pay. However, what about those who are self-employed, freelancers, or work for companies that don't offer HRA? Do they miss out on tax benefits for their rent payments? Not entirely.

While non-salaried individuals cannot claim HRA, the Income Tax Act, 1961, provides a specific provision under Section 80GG for them to claim a deduction for the rent they pay. This section is designed to offer a similar relief to individuals who incur rental expenses but do not receive HRA as part of their income. It ensures that even if you're not on a traditional payroll with HRA benefits, you can still reduce your taxable income by a portion of your rent paid, provided you meet certain conditions.

## Understanding Section 80GG: Eligibility and Conditions

Section 80GG of the Income Tax Act, 1961, allows individuals and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) to claim a deduction for rent paid, provided they do not receive HRA from any employer. This provision is particularly beneficial for self-employed professionals, business owners, or employees whose salary structure does not include HRA.

To be eligible for claiming a deduction under Section 80GG, certain crucial conditions must be met:
*   **No HRA Received:** The assessee (taxpayer) must not have received HRA from their employer at any point during the financial year for which the deduction is being claimed.
*   **Rent Paid for Accommodation:** The assessee must be paying rent for furnished or unfurnished accommodation for their own residence. This means they are living in rented premises.
*   **No Self-Owned Residential Property:** The assessee, their spouse, or their minor child should not own any residential accommodation in the city where they reside and are employed or carry on business. If the assessee owns a house in another city, they can still claim the deduction under Section 80GG, provided they do not derive any benefit from that property (e.g., rent it out and claim HRA for it). If they own a self-occupied property in another city, they must not claim it as self-occupied under Section 23(2)(a) or 23(2)(b) of the Income Tax Act.
*   **Declaration in Form 10BA:** The assessee must file a declaration in Form 10BA, stating that all the conditions mentioned above are satisfied. This form is mandatory to claim the deduction.

It's important to note that the deduction is available only for rent paid for residential accommodation. Rent paid for commercial properties or for accommodation used by others is not eligible.

## Calculating Your 80GG Deduction

The amount of deduction allowed under Section 80GG is the least of the following three amounts:

1.  **Rent paid minus 10% of Adjusted Gross Total Income (AGTI):**
    *   *Adjusted Gross Total Income* for this purpose means your Gross Total Income (GTI) less all deductions under Chapter VI-A (like 80C, 80D, etc.) but *excluding* the deduction under Section 80GG itself. It also excludes any long-term capital gains, short-term capital gains taxable under Section 111A, and income referred to in Sections 115A or 115AB.
    *   For example, if your AGTI is ₹15 lakhs and you pay ₹1.8 lakhs (₹15,000 per month) in rent, this component would be ₹1,80,000 - (10% of ₹15,00,000) = ₹1,80,000 - ₹1,50,000 = ₹30,000.

2.  **25% of Adjusted Gross Total Income (AGTI):**
    *   Using the above example, this would be 25% of ₹15,00,000 = ₹3,75,000.

3.  **₹5,000 per month or ₹60,000 per annum:**
    *   This is the statutory limit, fixed at ₹60,000 for a financial year.

The actual deduction you can claim will be the lowest of these three calculated figures. This calculation ensures that the benefit is reasonable and proportionate to both your income and the rent paid, while also setting an upper limit.

## The Role of Form 10BA

To successfully claim the deduction under Section 80GG, filing Form 10BA is not optional; it is a mandatory requirement. This form serves as a declaration by the taxpayer that they meet all the eligibility criteria specified under Section 80GG of the Income Tax Act, 1961.

Form 10BA requires you to provide details such as:
*   Your name and PAN.
*   The total amount of rent paid during the financial year.
*   The name and address of the landlord.
*   The period for which the rent was paid.
*   A declaration that you, your spouse, or minor child do not own any residential accommodation in the city where you reside or carry on business/employment.
*   A confirmation that you have not received HRA from any employer during the relevant period.

This form needs to be submitted electronically along with your Income Tax Return (ITR). Without filing Form 10BA, your claim for deduction under Section 80GG will not be processed by the Income Tax Department. It acts as a crucial piece of evidence supporting your claim and ensures compliance with the tax regulations.

## Distinguishing 80GG from HRA

While both Section 80GG and HRA deductions aim to provide tax relief for rent payments, they cater to different sets of taxpayers and operate under distinct rules. Understanding these differences is key for proper tax planning.

*   **Eligibility:**
    *   **HRA:** Exclusively for salaried individuals who receive HRA as a component of their salary from their employer. The amount is typically declared by the employer and reflected in Form 16.
    *   **Section 80GG:** Primarily for non-salaried individuals (self-employed, freelancers, business owners) or salaried individuals who do not receive HRA from their employer.

*   **Mechanism of Claim:**
    *   **HRA:** Can often be adjusted by the employer during the year, leading to lower TDS. If not fully adjusted, the balance can be claimed while filing the ITR. Requires rent receipts and landlord's PAN if annual rent exceeds ₹1 lakh.
    *   **Section 80GG:** Can only be claimed at the time of filing the Income Tax Return (ITR). Requires mandatory filing of Form 10BA. Rent receipts are essential documentation to support the claim, though not directly submitted with the ITR, they must be kept for record.

*   **Ownership of Property:**
    *   **HRA:** A salaried employee can own a house in a different city and still claim HRA for rent paid in their city of employment, provided they are genuinely paying rent and residing there.
    *   **Section 80GG:** Strict condition that the taxpayer, their spouse, or minor child must *not* own any residential accommodation in the city where they reside and claim the deduction. If they own a house elsewhere, it must not be claimed as self-occupied.

*   **Maximum Limit:**
    *   **HRA:** The deduction is the least of actual HRA received, actual rent paid minus 10% of basic salary plus DA, or 50% (for metro cities) / 40% (for non-metro cities) of basic salary plus DA. There is no absolute monetary cap.
    *   **Section 80GG:** Capped at ₹60,000 per financial year, regardless of the rent paid or income.

These distinctions highlight that while the spirit of both provisions is similar – to provide relief for rent expenses – their application and conditions are tailored to different taxpayer categories.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 35, a self-employed graphic designer in Mumbai, earning ₹18 lakhs per year.** She lives in a rented apartment, paying ₹20,000 per month in rent. Priya does not receive HRA as she is self-employed.

Here's how she would calculate her deduction under Section 80GG for the financial year:

1.  **Gross Total Income (GTI):** ₹18,00,000
2.  **Other deductions (e.g., 80C, 80D):** Let's assume Priya claims ₹1,50,000 under Section 80C and ₹25,000 under Section 80D. Total = ₹1,75,000.
3.  **Adjusted Gross Total Income (AGTI) for 80GG:**
    GTI - (Other Chapter VI-A deductions excluding 80GG)
    ₹18,00,000 - ₹1,75,000 = ₹16,25,000

Now, let's calculate the three limits for Section 80GG:

*   **Condition 1: Rent paid minus 10% of AGTI**
    *   Total annual rent paid: ₹20,000 x 12 = ₹2,40,000
    *   10% of AGTI: 10% of ₹16,25,000 = ₹1,62,500
    *   Amount: ₹2,40,000 - ₹1,62,500 = ₹77,500

*   **Condition 2: 25% of AGTI**
    *   Amount: 25% of ₹16,25,000 = ₹4,06,250

*   **Condition 3: Statutory limit**
    *   Amount: ₹60,000 per annum

Comparing the three amounts:
1.  ₹77,500
2.  ₹4,06,250
3.  ₹60,000

The least of these three amounts is ₹60,000.

Therefore, Priya can claim a deduction of **₹60,000** under Section 80GG for the financial year. She must also file Form 10BA along with her Income Tax Return to claim this deduction.

## FAQ

### Who is eligible to claim a deduction under Section 80GG?
Individuals and HUFs who do not receive HRA from an employer and pay rent for their accommodation are eligible. They must not own a residential property in the city of their residence and employment.

### Is Form 10BA mandatory for claiming 80GG deduction?
Yes, filing Form 10BA is mandatory to claim the deduction under Section 80GG. It is a declaration that you meet all the prescribed conditions.

### What is "Adjusted Gross Total Income" for 80GG calculation?
It's your Gross Total Income minus all Chapter VI-A deductions (like 80C, 80D, etc.) except 80GG itself, and excluding certain capital gains and other specified incomes.

### Can I claim 80GG if I own a house in another city?
Yes, you can claim 80GG if you own a house in another city, provided you do not reside in that house and do not claim it as self-occupied under Section 23(2)(a) or 23(2)(b) of the Income Tax Act.

### What is the maximum deduction allowed under Section 80GG?
The maximum deduction allowed under Section 80GG is capped at ₹60,000 per financial year (₹5,000 per month).

### Do I need to provide rent receipts for 80GG?
While rent receipts are not directly uploaded with Form 10BA, it is crucial to keep them as proof. The Income Tax Department may ask for them during assessment.
