# Understanding Loan Risks: Impact on Borrowers & Guarantors in India

**TL;DR**
-   Acting as a loan guarantor makes you equally liable for the debt if the primary borrower defaults.
-   A loan default by either the borrower or guarantor can severely damage their credit score, affecting future borrowing capacity.
-   Lenders have the legal right to pursue the guarantor's assets and income to recover the outstanding loan amount.
-   Guarantor responsibilities can lead to significant financial strain and strained personal relationships.
-   Always seek professional financial advice before agreeing to guarantee a loan to fully understand the implications.

## What this means in plain terms

When you take a loan, you're responsible for repaying it. This is straightforward for a borrower. However, many people overlook the significant responsibility that comes with being a loan guarantor. In simple terms, a guarantor is someone who promises to repay the loan if the primary borrower fails to do so. This isn't just a formality; it's a legally binding commitment.

This means that if your friend, family member, or colleague defaults on a loan you've guaranteed, the bank or financial institution can come directly to you for the full outstanding amount. This can have serious repercussions, not just for your finances but also for your credit score and even your personal relationships. Understanding these risks before you sign on the dotted line is crucial for both borrowers and potential guarantors.

## Credit Score Impact and Future Borrowing Capacity

One of the most immediate and far-reaching consequences of a loan default, whether you are the primary borrower or the guarantor, is the severe impact on your credit score. In India, credit bureaus like CIBIL, Experian, and Equifax maintain detailed records of your borrowing and repayment history.

### How Defaults Affect Your Credit Score

When a loan payment is missed, it's reported to these credit bureaus. A default by the primary borrower on a loan you've guaranteed will show up on your credit report just as it would on the borrower's. This instantly lowers your credit score. A low credit score can make it incredibly difficult to secure new loans, credit cards, or even mortgages in the future. Lenders view individuals with poor credit scores as high-risk, leading to either outright rejection of applications or offers with significantly higher interest rates.

### Long-Term Repercussions

The negative mark on your credit report can linger for several years, typically up to 7-10 years, making it challenging to access credit for major life events like buying a home, funding higher education, or starting a business. Even if you eventually clear the defaulted amount as a guarantor, the historical record of the default will remain, impacting your financial standing for a considerable period. This effectively puts a brake on your financial aspirations and can lead to missed opportunities.

## Financial and Legal Liabilities for Guarantors

Many individuals agree to be a guarantor without fully grasping the extent of their financial and legal obligations. It's not merely a moral commitment; it's a legal one that places you squarely in the lender's crosshairs if the borrower falters.

### Joint and Several Liability

In India, a guarantor's liability is often "joint and several" with the principal borrower. This means the lender can pursue either the borrower or the guarantor, or both simultaneously, to recover the debt. The bank is not obligated to exhaust all recovery options against the primary borrower before approaching you. If the borrower misses payments, the bank can immediately demand the outstanding amount from you, the guarantor. This includes the principal amount, accrued interest, penalties, and any legal costs incurred by the lender.

### Asset Seizure and Income Attachment

If you, as a guarantor, are unable to repay the defaulted loan, the lender has legal recourse to recover the money. This can include attaching your assets, such as your bank accounts, property (both movable and immovable), and even a portion of your salary or other income. The process can involve legal notices, court proceedings, and ultimately, the auctioning of your assets to settle the debt. This can lead to significant financial distress, loss of hard-earned savings, and even your home.

## Strained Relationships and Personal Costs

Beyond the financial and legal ramifications, guaranteeing a loan can have a profound impact on personal relationships, often leading to irreparable damage.

### Erosion of Trust

When a borrower defaults on a loan, and the guarantor is forced to step in, it often leads to a severe erosion of trust. The guarantor might feel betrayed, while the borrower might feel embarrassed or defensive. This can turn what was once a strong bond into one filled with resentment and anger. The financial burden placed on the guarantor can overshadow years of friendship or familial ties.

### Social and Emotional Stress

The stress of dealing with a defaulted loan, constant calls from recovery agents, potential legal battles, and the fear of losing assets can take a heavy toll on the guarantor's mental and emotional well-being. This stress can impact their work, family life, and overall health. The social stigma associated with financial difficulties, even when you are not the primary defaulter, can also be significant. It's a heavy price to pay for a favour that seemed simple at the outset.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 38, earning ₹22 lakhs CTC, based in Gurugram**. Her younger brother, Rohan, 32, wanted a personal loan of ₹8 lakhs for a new business venture. The bank asked for a guarantor due to Rohan's limited credit history. Priya, wanting to support her brother, agreed to be the guarantor without fully understanding the implications.

The loan was disbursed with an EMI of ₹16,500 over 60 months. For the first 18 months, Rohan paid his EMIs regularly. However, his business faced unexpected challenges, and he started missing payments. After three consecutive missed EMIs, the bank sent a notice to Rohan and, subsequently, to Priya, demanding the outstanding amount.

At this point, Rohan had an outstanding principal of approximately ₹6.5 lakhs, plus accrued interest and penalties, bringing the total to about ₹7.1 lakhs. Priya was shocked. The bank informed her that if she didn't pay, her credit score would be impacted, and they would initiate recovery proceedings against her.

Priya tried to reason with Rohan, but he was unable to arrange the funds. To protect her own credit score and assets, Priya reluctantly paid the outstanding ₹7.1 lakhs. This significantly depleted her emergency fund and strained her relationship with Rohan to a breaking point. She also had to defer her plans for a down payment on a new flat, as her savings were now gone. This single act of guarantorship cost her a substantial amount and caused immense personal stress.

## What to do this week

1.  **Review existing guarantees:** If you've guaranteed any loans, pull out the documents and re-read the terms and conditions, specifically focusing on your liabilities.
2.  **Check your credit report:** Access your latest credit report from CIBIL, Experian, or Equifax to ensure no unknown guarantees or defaults are impacting your score. You are entitled to one free credit report annually from each bureau.
3.  **Discuss with potential borrowers:** If someone asks you to be a guarantor, have an open and frank discussion about their financial stability, repayment plan, and what would happen in case of default.
4.  **Seek professional advice:** Before agreeing to guarantee a loan, consult a Certified Financial Planner (CFP) to understand the full scope of risks and how it might impact your financial plan.
5.  **Consider alternatives:** Explore other ways to help someone without taking on direct financial liability, such as providing a smaller, manageable personal loan from your own funds if you can afford to lose it, or offering business advice.

## FAQ

### What is the primary responsibility of a loan guarantor?
A loan guarantor's primary responsibility is to repay the entire outstanding loan amount, including principal, interest, and penalties, if the primary borrower fails to do so. This liability is legally binding.

### How does a guarantor's credit score get affected by a borrower's default?
When a borrower defaults, the default is reported to credit bureaus and appears on both the borrower's and the guarantor's credit reports. This negatively impacts the guarantor's credit score, making it harder for them to obtain future credit.

### Can a bank directly approach the guarantor without pursuing the borrower first?
Yes, in many cases, especially with "joint and several" liability clauses, banks can directly approach the guarantor for repayment without necessarily exhausting all recovery options against the primary borrower first.

### What assets can a bank seize from a guarantor in case of default?
If a guarantor fails to repay a defaulted loan, the bank can legally pursue and seize various assets, including bank accounts, fixed deposits, property (movable and immovable), and even a portion of their salary or income, through legal proceedings.

### Can I remove myself as a guarantor from a loan?
Generally, removing yourself as a guarantor requires the consent of the lender and the primary borrower. The lender might require a new guarantor or additional collateral before releasing you from your obligation, which is often difficult to arrange.

### What should I do if I'm asked to be a loan guarantor?
Carefully assess the borrower's financial stability, understand the loan terms, and consider the potential impact on your own finances and credit score. It is highly advisable to seek independent financial and legal advice before making such a commitment.

### Is there any tax implication for a guarantor who repays a defaulted loan?
Repaying a defaulted loan as a guarantor is generally considered a capital loss if it cannot be recovered from the original borrower, but specific tax implications can be complex. It's best to consult a tax advisor for personalised guidance.
