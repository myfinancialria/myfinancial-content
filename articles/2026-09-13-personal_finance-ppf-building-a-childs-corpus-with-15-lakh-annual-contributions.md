# PPF: Building a Child's Corpus with ₹1.5 Lakh Annual Contributions

**TL;DR**
- The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a popular, tax-efficient savings scheme for long-term goals in India.
- Contributing the maximum ₹1.5 lakh annually to a PPF account for your child can build a substantial corpus.
- Over 18 years, with the current interest rate, this can accumulate close to ₹50 lakhs for their future needs.
- PPF offers EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) tax benefits, meaning contributions, interest, and maturity amounts are all tax-free.
- It's a low-risk, government-backed option ideal for conservative investors planning for a child's education or marriage.

## What this means in plain terms

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a government-backed savings scheme that helps individuals build a significant corpus over the long term. It's particularly popular in India due to its attractive tax benefits and guaranteed returns, making it a reliable choice for crucial life goals like a child's higher education or marriage.

When you invest in a PPF account, your money earns a fixed interest rate, which is declared quarterly by the Ministry of Finance. What makes PPF stand out is its "Exempt-Exempt-Exempt" (EEE) status: the money you put in (up to ₹1.5 lakh per financial year) is eligible for tax deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, the interest earned is tax-free, and the entire maturity amount is also exempt from tax. This triple tax benefit, combined with the power of compounding over 15 years (extendable), allows your savings to grow significantly without tax erosion.

## Understanding the PPF Scheme

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a long-term investment option introduced by the National Savings Institute in 1968, aiming to mobilise small savings and provide a retirement security net. It's widely regarded as one of the safest investment avenues in India due to its government backing.

### Key Features of PPF

*   **Tenure:** A PPF account has a minimum maturity period of 15 years. After this, you can choose to withdraw the entire amount or extend the account in blocks of five years, with or without further contributions.
*   **Interest Rate:** The interest rate for PPF is declared quarterly by the Ministry of Finance. It is typically revised in line with government bond yields. The interest is compounded annually, but calculated monthly on the lowest balance between the 5th and the last day of the month. To maximise interest, it's advisable to deposit funds before the 5th of each month.
*   **Contribution Limits:** You can contribute a minimum of ₹500 and a maximum of ₹1.5 lakh in a financial year. Contributions can be made in a lump sum or in instalments.
*   **Eligibility:** Any Indian resident individual can open a PPF account. One can open an account for a minor child, but only one account per individual (self or minor) is allowed. Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are generally not allowed to open new PPF accounts, but existing accounts held by residents who later become NRIs can continue till maturity.

## Tax Benefits and EEE Status

One of the most compelling reasons to invest in PPF is its favourable tax treatment, often referred to as EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) status.

### Section 80C Deduction

Contributions made to a PPF account are eligible for a tax deduction under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act, 1961. This means that up to ₹1.5 lakh contributed in a financial year can be deducted from your taxable income, thereby reducing your overall tax liability. This benefit is available to individuals and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs).

### Tax-Free Interest and Maturity

The interest earned on your PPF deposits is entirely exempt from income tax. This is a significant advantage, as interest from many other savings instruments is taxable. Furthermore, the entire maturity amount, including both the principal contributed and the accumulated interest, is also tax-free upon withdrawal after 15 years or extended periods. This EEE status makes PPF an excellent tool for tax-efficient wealth creation over the long term.

## PPF for a Minor Child

Opening a PPF account in the name of a minor child is a strategic move for long-term financial planning, especially for their higher education or marriage.

### How to Open an Account for a Minor

A parent or a legal guardian can open a PPF account on behalf of a minor child. Only one PPF account is allowed per minor. If both parents are alive, either parent can open the account. The parent operating the minor's account cannot open another PPF account for a second minor child if they already have an account in their own name and one for another minor. The combined contribution limit for the parent's account and the minor's account(s) is ₹1.5 lakh per financial year. For example, if a parent contributes ₹1 lakh to their own PPF account, they can only contribute up to ₹50,000 to their minor child's PPF account in that financial year. The contributions made to the minor's account are also eligible for deduction under Section 80C, subject to the overall limit of ₹1.5 lakh for the parent.

### Benefits for Child's Future

Starting a PPF account early for a child allows the investment to benefit from the power of compounding for a longer duration. Even though the contributions stop when the child turns 18 and the account becomes a major's account, the accumulated corpus continues to earn interest until maturity or further extension. This long investment horizon ensures a substantial fund is available when the child needs it for significant life events.

## A real example

Let's consider Priya, 35, a salaried professional in Mumbai with a ₹20 lakh CTC. She decides to open a PPF account for her daughter, Anya, who is currently 1 year old, with the goal of funding Anya's higher education when she turns 18. Priya plans to contribute the maximum allowed amount of ₹1.5 lakh every financial year to Anya's PPF account.

Here's how the corpus would accumulate over 17 years (until Anya turns 18), assuming a consistent PPF interest rate of 7.1% per annum, compounded annually:

*   **Annual Contribution:** ₹1,50,000
*   **Investment Period:** 17 years (from Anya's age 1 to 18)
*   **Assumed Interest Rate:** 7.1% per annum (current rate, subject to change by Ministry of Finance)

Using the Future Value of Annuity formula, or a standard PPF calculator:

1.  **Total Investment:** ₹1,50,000 per year for 17 years = ₹25,50,000
2.  **Total Interest Earned:** Approximately ₹20,33,404
3.  **Maturity Amount (Corpus at age 18):** Approximately ₹45,83,404

So, by consistently investing ₹1.5 lakh annually, Priya would accumulate a corpus of approximately ₹45.83 lakhs for Anya by the time she turns 18. This entire amount would be tax-free upon withdrawal. If Priya were to continue the account for longer by extending it in 5-year blocks, the corpus would grow significantly further due to the power of compounding.

## FAQ

### Can I open more than one PPF account for my minor children?
No, a parent or guardian can open only one PPF account for a minor child. The parent themselves can also only hold one PPF account.

### What is the maximum I can contribute to a minor's PPF account?
The maximum combined contribution to your own PPF account and any minor's PPF account(s) linked to your name is ₹1.5 lakh in a financial year.

### Is the interest rate on PPF fixed for the entire 15 years?
No, the interest rate on PPF is not fixed for 15 years. It is reviewed and declared quarterly by the Ministry of Finance and can change.

### Can I withdraw money from a minor's PPF account before maturity?
Partial withdrawals are allowed from the 7th financial year onwards, subject to certain conditions and limits. Full withdrawal is only at maturity after 15 years.

### What happens to the minor's PPF account when they turn 18?
When the minor turns 18, the account status changes from 'minor' to 'major'. The erstwhile minor (now major) will need to submit an application along with KYC documents to the bank or post office to regularise the account.

### Are NRIs allowed to open a PPF account?
No, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are generally not allowed to open new PPF accounts. However, if a resident Indian becomes an NRI after opening a PPF account, they can continue the account until its maturity.

### Is the PPF interest compounded monthly or annually?
PPF interest is compounded annually, but it is calculated monthly on the lowest balance between the 5th and the last day of the month.
