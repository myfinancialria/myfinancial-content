# Market Wrap — 05 August 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian benchmark indices ended a volatile session with marginal gains for Nifty 50 and Mid/Smallcaps, while Bank Nifty closed lower.
- The Reserve Bank of India's decision to keep the repo rate unchanged, alongside lower oil prices, was a key driver.
- Hindustan Copper Ltd. emerged as the top gainer among Nifty 500 stocks, surging over 8%.
- C.E. Info Systems Ltd. (MAPMYINDIA) was the biggest loser in the Nifty 500, dropping over 8%.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers in the cash market, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers.
- F&O build-up data is unavailable for this session and will be populated from the next wrap.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equity benchmarks witnessed a day of trimmed gains, despite early upswings, following the RBI's monetary policy announcement. The Nifty 50 closed marginally higher at 24624.65, up by 0.04%. In contrast, the Bank Nifty ended in the red, settling at 57739.95 with a 0.29% decline. Broader markets showed more strength, with the Nifty Midcap 100 gaining 0.18% and the Nifty Smallcap 100 rising a notable 0.76%.

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, eased by 1.07% to 12.06, indicating a slight reduction in market uncertainty.

## What moved the market — the factors
The primary driver for today's market action was the Reserve Bank of India's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) decision to keep the repo rate unchanged, maintaining a neutral stance. This "dovish" hold, coupled with lower crude oil prices, initially bolstered market sentiment and led to early gains. However, these gains were trimmed as the session progressed, particularly impacting the banking sector. Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their buying spree, injecting ₹2,446 crore into the market, which provided some support. Domestically, DIIs were net sellers, pulling out ₹936 crore. Sector-specific movements also played a role, with Metal and Auto sectors showing strong upward momentum, while Media and Banking sectors faced selling pressure. The Indian rupee also strengthened, hitting a one-month closing high, primarily due to the dip in crude oil prices.

## Sectors — winners and losers
The market saw a clear divergence in sector performance today, with Metals and Automobiles leading the charge.

**Top 3 Gainer Sectors:**
- **METAL:** +1.72% - The sector witnessed strong buying interest, contributing significantly to the Nifty's marginal gains.
- **AUTO:** +1.27% - Automobile stocks performed well, with Ola Electric Mobility Ltd. being a standout gainer within the Nifty 500.
- **REALTY:** +0.84% - The realty sector also posted healthy gains, indicating positive sentiment.

**Top 3 Loser Sectors:**
- **MEDIA:** -1.58% - The media sector experienced notable selling pressure, with Zee Entertainment Enterprises Ltd. among the top losers.
- **FMCG:** -0.29% - Fast-moving consumer goods stocks saw a slight decline.
- **BANK:** -0.29% - The banking sector, despite the RBI's dovish stance, closed in the red, contributing to the Bank Nifty's underperformance.

- **LIC OFS gets strong response from retail investors, subscribed 1.82 times the base size**: The government's offer for sale (OFS) of a 6.5% stake in LIC garnered robust interest from retail investors, with the base retail issue subscribed 1.82 times. ## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was predominantly shaped by the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) meeting outcome. The RBI decided to keep the repo rate unchanged and maintained its "withdrawal of accommodation" stance, which was largely in line with market expectations. This "dovish" hold, coupled with a decline in global crude oil prices, spurred a rally in Indian government bonds, pushing yields lower. The Indian rupee also reacted positively, hitting a one-month closing high against the dollar, primarily due to the lower oil prices.

While the initial market reaction was positive, leading to early gains for the Nifty, these gains were trimmed as the day progressed. The Bank Nifty, in particular, saw some selling pressure and closed in the red. The RBI also raised its FY27 GDP growth outlook, which is a positive indicator for the economy. Overall, the session reflected a market digesting the policy continuity signal from the RBI, with specific sectors reacting to broader flows and global cues like falling oil prices.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead to tomorrow's session, global cues will continue to play a significant role. The US Treasury Secretary's comments have raised expectations for a Bank of Japan rate hike in September, which could influence Asian markets. European shares climbed today on positive earnings and US-Iran diplomatic talks, suggesting a potentially positive handover from Western markets.

Key levels and sectors to watch will include the banking sector, which saw declines today, and the Metal and Auto sectors, which showed strength. The movement of crude oil prices and the USD/INR pair will also be crucial. Overnight, the USD/INR closed lower at 95.12, while Brent Crude saw a slight increase to $80.48. Gold and Silver also saw significant gains. Investors will also be watching for the listing of MV Electrosystems and Juniper Green Energy shares tomorrow.
