# Your ITR Filing Checklist: A Month-Wise Guide for Salaried Taxpayers

**TL;DR**
- Proactive tax planning throughout the year can significantly simplify your Income Tax Return (ITR) filing process.
- Begin by understanding your current tax situation, including Form 16, AIS, and Form 26AS, well before the July 2027 deadline.
- Regularly review your investments and expenses to maximise eligible deductions under both old and new tax regimes.
- Maintain meticulous records of all income, investments, and expenses to avoid last-minute stress and errors.
- Regularly compare the old and new tax regimes to choose the one that offers you the most tax-efficient outcome.

## What this means in plain terms

For many salaried individuals, filing Income Tax Returns (ITR) can feel like a daunting annual chore, often rushed in the weeks leading up to the July 31st deadline. However, with a little foresight and a structured approach, this process can become seamless and even empowering. Instead of scrambling to gather documents and reconcile figures at the last minute, a month-wise checklist allows you to spread the effort throughout the year, ensuring accuracy and potentially optimising your tax outgo. This proactive strategy is particularly beneficial for the upcoming ITR filing for the financial year 2026-27, due in July 2027, giving you ample time to prepare and make informed financial decisions.

Starting early means you have sufficient time to identify any discrepancies in your tax statements, gather necessary proofs for deductions, and strategically plan your investments to maximise tax benefits. It transforms tax filing from a reactive sprint into a well-managed marathon, leading to a calmer experience and greater confidence in your financial compliance.

## Understanding Your Tax Landscape: Form 16, AIS, and Form 26AS

A crucial first step in year-round tax preparation is to understand the key documents that reflect your income and tax deductions. These documents serve as the foundation for your ITR.

### Form 16: Your Employer's Tax Statement
Form 16 is issued by your employer and is a certificate stating the income paid to you and the Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on that income. It has two parts: Part A details the TDS by the employer, your PAN, and the employer's TAN, while Part B provides a detailed breakdown of your salary, perquisites, and any deductions claimed under Chapter VI A. You typically receive this by June 15th after the financial year ends. However, understanding its components throughout the year helps you track your income and TDS.

### Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS)
The AIS is a comprehensive statement containing details of all financial transactions reported by various entities to the Income Tax Department. This includes salary, interest income from savings accounts, fixed deposits, mutual fund transactions, dividend income, securities transactions, and more. The Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS) is a simplified view of the information in AIS, categorised and processed for easier understanding. These statements are dynamic and can be accessed via the income tax e-filing portal. Regularly checking your AIS helps ensure that all reported transactions match your records and allows you to identify any discrepancies early.

### Form 26AS: Your Tax Credit Statement
Form 26AS is an annual consolidated tax statement that shows the tax deducted at source (TDS), tax collected at source (TCS), advance tax paid, and self-assessment tax paid by you. It also reflects details of high-value transactions. Like AIS, it's accessible through the income tax e-filing portal. It's vital to cross-verify the TDS figures in your Form 16 with those in Form 26AS. Any mismatch should be addressed with your employer or the deductor well in advance of filing.

## Optimising Deductions and Exemptions Throughout the Year

Effective tax planning isn't just about filing; it's about strategically managing your finances to legally reduce your tax liability. This involves making the most of available deductions and exemptions.

### Chapter VI A Deductions (e.g., 80C, 80D)
Section 80C allows deductions up to ₹1.5 lakhs for investments like Public Provident Fund (PPF), Employees' Provident Fund (EPF), Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS), life insurance premiums, home loan principal repayment, and children's tuition fees. Section 80D offers deductions for health insurance premiums, while Section 80E covers interest on education loans. Sections 80G and 80GGA provide deductions for donations. By planning these investments and expenses throughout the year, you can ensure you utilise the full potential of these sections. Keep digital and physical records of all premium receipts, investment proofs, and payment acknowledgements.

### House Rent Allowance (HRA) Exemption
If you live in rented accommodation and receive HRA as part of your salary, you can claim an exemption. The exemption amount depends on your salary, the HRA received, and the actual rent paid, subject to certain conditions. Maintaining rent receipts, especially for payments exceeding ₹1 lakh annually (which require the landlord's PAN), is critical.

### Other Key Exemptions and Allowances
Beyond the common deductions, be aware of other exemptions like leave travel allowance (LTA), standard deduction for salaried employees (₹50,000), and interest on home loans (Section 24(b)). For those with home loans, ensure you have the interest certificate from your lender. For LTA, keep travel tickets and boarding passes as proof.

## Choosing Your Tax Regime: Old vs. New

Since Financial Year 2020-21, taxpayers have had the option to choose between the old tax regime with various deductions and exemptions, and a new, simplified tax regime with lower tax rates but fewer exemptions.

### The Old Tax Regime
This regime allows you to claim numerous deductions and exemptions, such as those under Section 80C, 80D, HRA, LTA, and the standard deduction. It's often beneficial for individuals who make significant investments in tax-saving instruments, pay substantial health insurance premiums, or have home loans. The tax slabs are generally higher compared to the new regime.

### The New Tax Regime
Introduced to simplify the tax structure, this regime offers lower tax rates across various income slabs. However, it requires taxpayers to forgo most of the common deductions and exemptions available under the old regime, including 80C, 80D, HRA, LTA, and the standard deduction. It can be advantageous for individuals who prefer not to invest in tax-saving instruments or do not have many eligible deductions.

### Making an Informed Choice
The decision between the old and new regimes should be made after careful calculation, comparing your potential tax liability under both scenarios. Your employer will typically ask for your preferred regime at the start of the financial year for TDS purposes. However, salaried individuals can choose to switch their regime at the time of ITR filing. This flexibility means you can plan your investments and expenses throughout the year, then make the final decision based on which regime results in lower tax payable. Use online tax calculators and consult with a financial planner to assess your specific situation.

## A real example

Let's consider **Aisha, 35, earning ₹22 lakhs CTC, based in Bengaluru.** She is planning for her ITR filing for FY 2026-27.

**September 2026:**
Aisha reviews her salary slip and last year's Form 16. She checks her AIS and Form 26AS on the income tax portal to ensure all reported transactions match her records. She notes her current investments and deductions.
*   **Action:** Identified that her health insurance premium for her parents (₹30,000) is eligible for 80D, and her PPF contribution (₹50,000) and ELSS SIPs (₹60,000 annually) contribute to her 80C limit.

**December 2026:**
Aisha realises she still has room in her 80C limit. She decides to increase her ELSS SIPs by ₹2,500 per month (₹30,000 annually) and makes a one-time payment of ₹10,000 towards a life insurance premium.
*   **Action:** Maxed out her 80C to ₹1.5 lakhs (₹50,000 PPF + ₹90,000 ELSS + ₹10,000 life insurance).

**March 2027:**
As the financial year-end approaches, Aisha ensures all her rent receipts are collected and organised. She also confirms her home loan interest certificate will be available from her bank. She cross-checks her projected TDS with her actual investments and deductions.
*   **Action:** Confirmed rent receipts for ₹3.6 lakhs (₹30,000/month) and estimated home loan interest of ₹2 lakhs (eligible for Section 24(b)).

**June 2027:**
Aisha receives her Form 16 from her employer. She compares it with her AIS and Form 26AS. All figures match. She then uses an online calculator to compare her tax liability under the old and new regimes.

**Calculations (Simplified):**

**Old Regime:**
*   Gross Salary: ₹22,00,000
*   Standard Deduction: -₹50,000
*   80C Deduction: -₹1,50,000
*   80D Deduction (Parents' health insurance): -₹30,000
*   Home Loan Interest (Section 24(b)): -₹2,00,000
*   HRA Exemption (approx): -₹1,50,000 (assuming she lives in a metro and meets conditions)
*   **Total Deductions & Exemptions:** ₹5,80,000
*   **Taxable Income:** ₹22,00,000 - ₹5,80,000 = ₹16,20,000
*   **Approx. Tax (Old Regime):** ₹2,90,000 (excluding cess)

**New Regime:**
*   Gross Salary: ₹22,00,000
*   Standard Deduction: -₹50,000 (available in new regime from FY 2023-24 onwards for salaried individuals)
*   **Taxable Income:** ₹22,00,000 - ₹50,000 = ₹21,50,000
*   **Approx. Tax (New Regime):** ₹2,85,000 (excluding cess)

In Aisha's case, the new regime might offer a slightly lower tax outgo due to her higher income slab, even with significant deductions in the old regime. However, this comparison requires precise calculations based on current tax slabs and specific exemptions. By doing this comparison early, Aisha can confirm her choice and proceed confidently with filing in July.

## What to do this week

1.  **Locate Last Year's ITR & Form 16:** Dig out your ITR acknowledgement for FY 2025-26 and your Form 16. This will give you a baseline of your income and deductions. Keep them accessible for reference.
2.  **Access Your Income Tax Portal Account:** Log in to the Income Tax e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in). Ensure you remember your password and update your profile if needed. Familiarise yourself with where to find AIS and Form 26AS.
3.  **Review Your AIS and Form 26AS:** Download your current Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Form 26AS for the current financial year (2026-27) if available, or for the previous year to understand what information is being reported against your PAN. Cross-check for any discrepancies.
4.  **Create a Dedicated Tax Folder (Digital/Physical):** Start a folder (digital on your computer/cloud or a physical file) specifically for tax documents for FY 2026-27. Begin by saving salary slips, investment proofs, and any major expense receipts.
5.  **List Your Current Investments & Deductions:** Make a quick list of all your existing tax-saving investments (PPF, ELSS, insurance) and potential deductions (health insurance, home loan interest, HRA). This initial assessment will highlight areas for future planning.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between AIS and Form 26AS?
AIS (Annual Information Statement) is a comprehensive view of all financial transactions reported against your PAN, including high-value transactions. Form 26AS primarily focuses on tax credits like TDS, TCS, and advance tax paid. AIS is more detailed and covers a broader range of information.

### Can I change my tax regime choice after informing my employer?
Yes, for salaried individuals, the choice of tax regime (old or new) declared to the employer for TDS purposes is provisional. You can make the final choice at the time of filing your Income Tax Return.

### What if there's a mismatch between my Form 16 and Form 26AS/AIS?
If you find a discrepancy, first contact your employer or the respective deductor (e.g., bank for interest) to get it rectified. If it's a significant issue, you might need to approach the Income Tax Department for clarification or correction.

### How long should I keep my tax-related documents?
It is generally recommended to retain tax-related documents for at least 7-8 assessment years from the end of the relevant assessment year. This is because the Income Tax Department can reopen assessments for up to 7 years in certain cases.

### Is it mandatory to file ITR even if my tax is fully deducted at source?
Yes, if your gross total income exceeds the basic exemption limit, filing an ITR is mandatory, even if all your tax has been deducted at source. Filing ITR also helps claim refunds, carry forward losses, and is often required for loan applications or visa processing.

### What is the basic exemption limit for salaried individuals?
For individuals below 60 years of age, the basic exemption limit is ₹2.5 lakhs under the old tax regime. Under the new tax regime (default from FY 2023-24), the basic exemption limit is ₹3 lakhs.

### Do I need to report exempt income in my ITR?
Yes, even exempt income (like agricultural income or certain allowances) should be reported in your ITR, although it won't be subject to tax. This provides a complete picture of your financial inflows to the tax authorities.
