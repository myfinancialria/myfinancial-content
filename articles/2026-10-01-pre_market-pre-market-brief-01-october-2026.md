# Pre-Market Brief — 01 October 2026

**TL;DR**
- **Global Tone:** Mixed to cautious, with US markets split, Europe down, and Asian markets showing a mixed trend.
- **GIFT Nifty Bias:** Signalling a negative open for Indian markets, pointing to a potential gap-down.
- **FII/DII Stance:** Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continue their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remain strong net buyers, providing crucial support.
- **Biggest News:** US 10-year Treasury yield has hit a 24-year high, contributing to the Nifty's longest losing streak in 25 years.
- **Key Nifty Level:** Watch 22378 as immediate support and 22413 as immediate resistance for initial direction.
- **Day's Main Risk:** Sustained FII outflows, elevated US bond yields, and the upcoming market holiday impacting liquidity.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight. In the US, the Dow Jones and S&P 500 closed lower, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq managed a modest gain. European markets, represented by the FTSE 100, saw significant declines. Asian markets this morning are largely positive, with the Nikkei 225 showing strong gains and the Hang Seng and Shanghai Composite also in the green. This mixed global sentiment, coupled with a negative signal from GIFT Nifty, suggests a cautious start for Indian equities today.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) weakened slightly against the US Dollar, closing at 96.22, a 0.18% increase. The Dollar Index strengthened by 0.33% to 101.79, indicating broader dollar strength. In commodities, Brent Crude fell by 2.77% to $100.66, while WTI Crude gained 2.55% to $92.73. Gold saw a marginal gain of 0.04% to $4188.20, and Silver rose by 1.13% to $60.78. A significant development was the US 10-year Treasury yield, which climbed by 0.72% to 5.29%, reaching its highest level since 2002.

**Interpretation:** A rising USD/INR could be a slight tailwind for IT and pharma exporters but puts pressure on importers. A stronger Dollar Index generally signals global risk aversion. The mixed crude oil movement needs close watching, with Brent's fall potentially easing some inflationary concerns, but WTI's rise could still be a factor. The surge in US 10-year Treasury yields is a major concern, as higher global yields often lead to FII outflows from emerging markets like India, impacting bond and equity markets negatively. Gold's slight uptick might reflect some safe-haven demand amidst market uncertainty.

## GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling
The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 22412.50, down 1.07%. This indicates a negative bias for the Indian market open, suggesting a potential gap-down for the Nifty 50 today.

**Interpretation:** The significant drop in GIFT Nifty points to a weak start for the Nifty 50, likely opening below yesterday's close. Traders should prepare for a gap-down opening.

## FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree yesterday, offloading Indian equities worth ₹-10,148 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net buyers, pumping in ₹11,272 crore into the market. This trend of FII selling and DII buying has been consistent over the last five sessions, with FIIs being net sellers in every session, while DIIs have consistently provided buying support.

DIIs have been the primary support system, absorbing the FII selling pressure and preventing a sharper decline. The market direction today will largely depend on whether DII buying can continue to offset FII selling.

## Stock & sector news that matters
- **India bonds slide as US Treasury rout, supply weigh; US 10-year US Treasury yield hits highest level since 2002**
  Indian government bonds declined as US Treasury yields surged to a 24-year high, and supply increased ahead of the RBI meeting.
  **Why it matters:** Rising US yields make emerging market bonds less attractive, potentially leading to FII outflows from Indian debt and equity markets. This increases borrowing costs for the Indian government and corporates, impacting overall economic sentiment.
- **Nifty’s longest losing streak in 25 years raises bear market fears**
  The Nifty has experienced its longest losing streak in 25 years, driven by foreign investor outflows, high US bond yields, and geopolitical tensions.
  **Why it matters:** This headline captures the prevailing negative sentiment. A prolonged losing streak can erode investor confidence and lead to further selling pressure, especially from retail investors.
- **Tata Motors PV shares fall over 3% despite 40% Q2 sales growth; Nifty Auto index down over 4%**
  Despite a strong 40% year-on-year rise in Q2 FY27 passenger vehicle sales, Tata Motors shares declined, and the Nifty Auto index fell over 4%.
  **Why it matters:** This indicates that even positive company-specific news might not be enough to counter broader market weakness or sector-specific concerns. The Nifty Auto index's decline suggests sector-wide pressure, which could impact other auto stocks today.
- **MTNL shares rally 17% as board approves Rs 892 crore Powai property sale amid debt worries**
  MTNL's shares surged after its board approved the sale of its Powai property for ₹891.53 crore to the Income Tax Department, aimed at addressing its debt.
  **Why it matters:** This is a positive development for the debt-laden PSU, showing progress in asset monetisation. It could lead to continued interest in MTNL and other PSUs undertaking similar debt-reduction strategies.
- **RIL Q2 Results: Nuvama sees 17% EBITDA growth, led by O2C biz. Here’s what to expect**
  Reliance Industries is projected to report a 17% year-on-year growth in Q2 FY27 EBITDA, primarily driven by stronger refining margins and higher throughput in its Oil-to-Chemicals (O2C) business.
  **Why it matters:** As a market heavyweight, RIL's performance significantly influences benchmark indices. Strong earnings expectations could provide some support to the market, especially if the O2C business delivers as anticipated.
- **Stock Market Holiday: Are BSE, NSE open or closed for Gandhi Jayanti tomorrow? Check upcoming market holidays**
  Indian stock markets (BSE, NSE, MCX, NCDEX) will be closed tomorrow, October 2, for Mahatma Gandhi Jayanti.
  **Why it matters:** A market holiday means a three-day break for investors. This can lead to reduced liquidity and increased volatility in the session before the holiday as positions are adjusted.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
- **Indian Stock Market Holiday (October 2nd):** The market will be closed tomorrow for Gandhi Jayanti. Markets typically see reduced trading volumes and potential position squaring in the session preceding a long weekend, which can sometimes lead to increased volatility.
- **RBI Meeting:** Indian government bonds slid ahead of the Reserve Bank of India meeting. Central bank meetings are always key events, with markets reacting sharply to policy decisions, interest rate changes, and forward guidance on inflation and growth. Volatility is common around such announcements.
- **US Treasury Yields:** The continued surge in US 10-year Treasury yields to multi-year highs will be closely watched. Historically, rising US yields can trigger capital outflows from emerging markets, putting pressure on Indian equities and bonds.

## Bottom line
Indian markets are poised for a cautious, likely gap-down open today, primarily influenced by negative global cues, the surge in US bond yields, and persistent FII selling. The Nifty's immediate direction will be dictated by its ability to hold the 22378 CPR bottom, with 22000 acting as a crucial psychological and option-chain support. Investors should remain vigilant about global yield movements and the continued FII selling pressure, especially ahead of tomorrow's market holiday.
