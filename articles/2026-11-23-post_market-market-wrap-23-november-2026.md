# Market Wrap — 23 November 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian equities witnessed a sharp decline today, with both Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing significantly lower, breaking a recent positive trend.
- Despite a strong rebound in US markets overnight driven by AI stock rallies, domestic selling pressure, particularly from DIIs, and weakness in key sectors like Metal and Banking, weighed heavily on the indices.
- ITI Ltd. emerged as the top gainer in the Nifty 500 pack, surging over 12%.
- APL Apollo Tubes Ltd. was the biggest laggard, plummeting more than 21% today.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their buying spree, pouring over ₹10,300 crore into Indian equities, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers.
- F&O build-up data is unavailable for today's session.

## How the market behaved today
Indian benchmark indices suffered significant losses today, with the Nifty 50 closing down 1.75% at 25349.65 and the Bank Nifty plummeting 3.17% to settle at 62585.35. The market opened lower and witnessed sustained selling pressure throughout the session, with both Nifty and Bank Nifty closing near their day's lows. Broader markets also ended in the red, though with relatively smaller declines.

| Index | Close | Change | High | Low |
| :------------------ | :---------- | :------- | :---------- | :---------- |
| Nifty 50 | 25349.65 | -1.75% | 25992.60 | 25343.85 |
| Bank Nifty | 62585.35 | -3.17% | 65193.40 | 62546.20 |
| Nifty Midcap 100 | 61403.20 | -1.01% | 62372.05 | 61397.05 |
| Nifty Smallcap 100 | 19314.40 | -0.66% | 19522.40 | 19273.90 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, rose by 2.19% to 17.70, indicating an increase in market uncertainty and investor apprehension.

## What moved the market — the factors
Today's market downturn was primarily driven by a combination of domestic selling pressure and weakness in heavyweight sectors, despite positive global cues. While US markets saw a significant rebound overnight, fueled by a rally in AI-linked stocks following Nvidia news, Indian indices failed to draw inspiration.

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained strong net buyers, infusing a substantial ₹10,308 crore into the market. However, this was largely offset by aggressive selling from Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), who pulled out ₹4,860 crore. This tug-of-war, with DII selling dominating, contributed significantly to the market's decline.

Sector-specific weakness was pronounced, with the Metal and Banking sectors being the biggest drags. The Nifty Metal index fell by 3.68%, impacted by stocks like Tata Steel, while the Nifty Bank and PSU Bank indices dropped over 3%, with Union Bank of India among the top losers. This broad-based selling in key cyclical sectors pushed the benchmark indices lower.

Adding to the domestic concerns, the Indian rupee retreated against the dollar after a five-day rally, driven by increased dollar demand from corporations and profit-taking activities. This, coupled with a fall in Indian government bonds due to concerns over debt supply, further dampened market sentiment. The rising India VIX also reflected increased investor nervousness.

## News that moved stocks

**Global Tech & AI Rebound:**
- **Nasdaq surges 6%, Dow soars 1,400 pts as markets rebound on Huang’s return.** US stock markets witnessed a strong recovery as investor interest returned to AI-linked stocks following the reinstatement of Jensen Huang as Nvidia's CEO, leading to a significant rally in chip stocks. This global sentiment, however, did not translate into gains for the broader Indian market today.
- **Nasdaq futures surge 3% as battered AI stocks gain ground.** US stock futures showed a significant rebound, indicating a return of investor interest in AI-linked stocks after recent declines.

**Domestic Corporate & Market Specifics:**
- **NSEL releases Rs 1,950 crore in one-time settlement, ends 14-year payment default.** The National Spot Exchange Ltd (NSEL) announced a substantial settlement payout of ₹1,950 crore to claimants, concluding a long-standing legal battle. This news is positive for the overall market sentiment regarding dispute resolution but did not significantly impact today's broader market movement.
- **Adani Airport raises $300 mn from 360 ONE Asset, taking total equity infusion to $1.35 bn.** Adani Airport Holdings Ltd secured USD 300 million to expand its presence in India's aviation sector, aiming to increase passenger capacity. This is positive news for the Adani Group and the aviation infrastructure sector.
- **Rupee retreats with stocks as rally takes a breather, positive bias lingers.** After a five-day upward trend, the Indian rupee weakened against the dollar today, driven by increased corporate dollar demand and profit-taking activities. A weaker rupee can sometimes be a concern for import-dependent sectors and overall market sentiment.
- **Rupee reversal, debt supply drag India bonds in holiday-shortened week.** Indian government bonds declined as the rupee lost momentum and concerns about bond supply arose after states auctioned bonds at higher yields. This indicates some pressure in the domestic fixed income market.

**Commodities:**
- **Oil falls around 3% on increased Middle East flows, Libyan pipeline restart.** Global crude oil prices declined today due to increased supply from the Middle East and the restart of a Libyan pipeline. Lower crude oil prices are generally beneficial for India, a major oil importer.
- **Gold rises as bargain hunters step in after steep weekly drop.** Gold prices rebounded as investors sought value after a significant weekly decline.

The market breadth was overwhelmingly negative today, with 360 declines significantly outnumbering 133 advances out of a total of 493 traded stocks. This indicates broad-based selling pressure across the market.

## FII / DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained strong net buyers today, injecting ₹10,308 crore into Indian equities. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers, pulling out ₹4,860 crore.

| Investor Type | Net Flow (₹ crore) |
| :------------ | :----------------- |
| FII | 10,308 |
| DII | -4,860 |

Over the past five trading sessions, FIIs have been consistent net buyers, showing strong conviction in Indian markets. However, DIIs have largely been net sellers during this period, indicating a divergence in investment strategies and contributing to the volatility and recent market corrections.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was primarily driven by a combination of strong domestic selling pressure, particularly from DIIs, and significant weakness in key sectors like Metal and Banking. Despite a robust rebound in US markets overnight, fueled by positive news surrounding AI stocks and Nvidia, Indian indices failed to capitalize on this global optimism. Instead, concerns over the retreating rupee and bond market weakness, alongside broad-based profit-taking in heavy-weight sectors, led to a sharp decline across the board. There was no single defining event for the Indian market today; rather, it was a confluence of these factors that shaped the negative sentiment.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will remain crucial. While US markets showed a strong rebound today, it remains to be seen if this positive momentum will sustain and translate into a more favorable opening for Indian markets. Crude oil prices have fallen significantly, which is a positive for India. Investors will closely watch the movement of the rupee against the dollar and continue to monitor FII and DII flow patterns. Key sectors like Banking and Metals, which saw heavy selling today, will be in focus, alongside the resilient IT sector.
