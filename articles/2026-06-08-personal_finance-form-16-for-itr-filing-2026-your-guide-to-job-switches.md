# Form 16 for ITR Filing 2026: Your Guide to Job Switches

**TL;DR**
- Form 16 is a crucial document for salaried individuals to file their Income Tax Return (ITR) for FY 2025-26.
- Your employer is legally mandated to issue Form 16 by June 15, 2026, for the financial year ending March 31, 2026.
- If you switched jobs during FY 2025-26, each employer you worked for must issue a separate Form 16 for their respective employment period.
- These multiple Form 16s must all be used to consolidate your total income and tax deductions when filing your ITR by July 31, 2026.
- It's essential to cross-verify the details in your Form 16 with other documents like your payslips, bank statements, and the Annual Information Statement (AIS).

## What this means in plain terms

For most salaried individuals in India, Form 16 is the cornerstone of filing their Income Tax Return (ITR). Think of it as a comprehensive summary provided by your employer, detailing the salary you earned and the tax (TDS – Tax Deducted at Source) that was cut from your pay and deposited with the government during the financial year. It simplifies the process of reporting your income and claiming deductions, ensuring you pay the correct amount of tax.

This document is particularly important because it's proof that your employer has accounted for your earnings and tax deductions as per the Income Tax Act. Without it, accurately reporting your income and the TDS already paid on your behalf becomes significantly more challenging. As the ITR filing season for the financial year 2025-26 (assessment year 2026-27) approaches, ensuring you have your Form 16 in hand is the first step towards a smooth filing experience.

## Understanding Form 16: Parts A & B

Form 16 is divided into two main parts, each serving a distinct purpose in your tax filing journey.

### Part A: TDS Details

Part A of Form 16 is generated by the employer and downloaded from the TRACES portal of the Income Tax Department. It primarily contains details about the tax deducted at source (TDS) from your salary. Key information includes:
*   Name and address of the employer.
*   PAN (Permanent Account Number) of the employer.
*   TAN (Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number) of the employer.
*   PAN of the employee.
*   Summary of tax deducted and deposited quarterly with the government. This includes the assessment year, period of employment, and the TDS amount.
*   Acknowledgement numbers of the TDS challans.

This section is crucial as it verifies that the tax deducted from your salary has indeed been remitted to the government.

### Part B: Detailed Income and Deductions

Part B is an annexure to Part A and is prepared by the employer. It provides a detailed breakup of your salary and any deductions or exemptions claimed. This includes:
*   Detailed breakup of salary, including allowances, perquisites, and profit in lieu of salary.
*   Exempt allowances under Section 10 (e.g., House Rent Allowance, Leave Travel Allowance).
*   Deductions allowed under Chapter VI-A of the Income Tax Act (e.g., Section 80C for investments like PPF, ELSS; Section 80D for health insurance; Section 80E for education loan interest).
*   Taxable income.
*   Tax on total income.
*   Surcharge and education cess.
*   Relief under Section 89 (if applicable).

Part B essentially helps you calculate your net taxable income and the final tax liability for the financial year.

## When to Expect Form 16 and the Deadline

Employers are legally obligated to issue Form 16 to their employees by a specific deadline each year. For the financial year 2025-26, which concluded on March 31, 2026, employers must provide Form 16 by **June 15, 2026**. This deadline is set by the Income Tax Department to give employees ample time to prepare and file their ITR.

Once you receive your Form 16, it's advisable to review it promptly. Check for any discrepancies in your personal details, income figures, or tax deductions. If you notice any errors, you should immediately contact your employer for correction. The last date for filing your Income Tax Return for FY 2025-26 (Assessment Year 2026-27) is typically **July 31, 2026**, for most individual taxpayers. Missing this deadline can lead to penalties and interest on unpaid taxes.

## Navigating Form 16 When You've Switched Jobs

A common scenario for many salaried professionals is switching jobs during a financial year. If you have worked with more than one employer between April 1, 2025, and March 31, 2026, the process of obtaining Form 16 requires a slightly different approach.

### Each Employer Issues a Form 16

The crucial point to remember is that **each employer you worked for during the financial year is responsible for issuing a separate Form 16** for the period you were employed with them. For instance, if you worked with Company A from April 2025 to September 2025 and then joined Company B from October 2025 to March 2026, you will receive two Form 16s: one from Company A for the first six months and another from Company B for the latter six months.

### Consolidating Multiple Form 16s for ITR Filing

When filing your ITR, you must consolidate the information from all your Form 16s. This means adding up the income from all employers and combining the deductions and taxes paid. It's important to declare your total income from all sources to the Income Tax Department. While your new employer might consider your previous income for TDS calculations if you declared it, they will only issue a Form 16 for the income paid by them. Therefore, you need to manually combine the figures from all Form 16s to arrive at your total taxable income.

### What if a Previous Employer Doesn't Issue Form 16?

In some cases, a previous employer might delay or fail to issue Form 16. If this happens, you can still file your ITR using other documents. You can rely on your payslips, bank statements showing salary credits, and Form 26AS or Annual Information Statement (AIS) to ascertain your income and TDS details. Form 26AS provides a consolidated statement of tax deducted at source, while AIS offers a more comprehensive view of all financial transactions reported to the Income Tax Department. However, always try to obtain Form 16 first, as it simplifies the process considerably.

## A real example

Meet **Suresh, 38, ₹22L CTC, Chennai**. Suresh worked for Company X from April 1, 2025, to September 30, 2025, with an annual CTC of ₹18 lakhs. On October 1, 2025, he switched to Company Y, where his CTC increased to ₹26 lakhs annually for the remainder of the financial year.

Here's how Suresh will manage his Form 16s for ITR filing:

1.  **Form 16 from Company X:** By June 15, 2026, Company X will issue a Form 16 to Suresh, covering his income and TDS for the period April 1, 2025, to September 30, 2025. This Form 16 will reflect approximately six months of his ₹18 lakh CTC. Let's assume his gross salary for this period was ₹9,00,000 and TDS deducted was ₹45,000.
2.  **Form 16 from Company Y:** Company Y will issue a separate Form 16 to Suresh, covering his income and TDS for the period October 1, 2025, to March 31, 2026. This Form 16 will reflect approximately six months of his ₹26 lakh CTC. Let's assume his gross salary for this period was ₹13,00,000 and TDS deducted was ₹1,30,000 (potentially higher as Company Y would have considered his previous income if he declared it).
3.  **Consolidating for ITR:** When Suresh files his ITR, he will add up his gross salary from both Form 16s: ₹9,00,000 (Company X) + ₹13,00,000 (Company Y) = ₹22,00,000. He will also add up his total TDS: ₹45,000 (Company X) + ₹1,30,000 (Company Y) = ₹1,75,000.
4.  **Deductions and Final Tax:** Suresh will then apply his eligible deductions (e.g., 80C, 80D) to his total gross income of ₹22,00,000. Let's say he has ₹1,50,000 under 80C and ₹25,000 under 80D. His net taxable income would be ₹22,00,000 - ₹1,75,000 = ₹20,25,000. He will calculate his final tax liability on this amount and then subtract the total TDS of ₹1,75,000 already paid. If there's any balance tax payable, he will pay it; if there's an excess, he will claim a refund.

This consolidated approach ensures all income is reported and all TDS is accounted for, preventing any discrepancies with the Income Tax Department.

## What to do this week

1.  **Verify Employer Details:** Check with your current and previous employers (if you switched jobs in FY 2025-26) regarding the issuance of Form 16. Confirm their internal process and expected delivery method (email, online portal, physical copy).
2.  **Gather Payslips:** Collect all your payslips for the entire financial year (April 2025 - March 2026) from all employers. These will serve as a backup to cross-verify the income and TDS details once you receive your Form 16.
3.  **Check AIS/TIS:** Log in to the Income Tax e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in) and check your Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS) for FY 2025-26. This will show all financial transactions reported against your PAN, including salary income and TDS, allowing for early reconciliation.
4.  **Organise Investment Proofs:** Start compiling proofs for all your tax-saving investments and expenses (e.g., Section 80C investments, health insurance premiums under 80D, home loan interest certificates) for FY 2025-26. These will be needed to claim deductions in your ITR.
5.  **Update Contact Information:** Ensure your current and previous employers have your correct email address and postal address to avoid delays in receiving your Form 16.

## FAQ

### What if my employer doesn't issue Form 16 by the deadline?
If your employer fails to issue Form 16 by June 15, 2026, you should first follow up with them. If it's still not issued, you can use your payslips, bank statements, and Form 26AS/AIS to file your ITR, as these documents also contain income and TDS details.

### Is Form 16 mandatory for filing ITR?
While Form 16 greatly simplifies ITR filing for salaried individuals, it is not strictly mandatory if you have other documents to substantiate your income and TDS. You can use payslips, bank statements, and Form 26AS/AIS as alternatives.

### What should I do if there are errors in my Form 16?
If you find any discrepancies in your Form 16, immediately inform your employer. They are required to issue a revised Form 16 after making the necessary corrections. Ensure the revised document is also downloaded from the TRACES portal.

### Can I file ITR without Form 16 if I have only one employer?
Yes, you technically can, but it is highly advisable to obtain Form 16. Without it, you would need to manually calculate your taxable income and deductions, which increases the chance of errors and potential scrutiny from the Income Tax Department.

### What if I had multiple employers and one of them is now closed?
If a previous employer has closed down and cannot issue Form 16, you must rely on your payslips, bank statements, and particularly your Form 26AS/AIS for that period. These government-maintained records will show the TDS deducted by the closed entity.

### Do I need to report income from other sources (e.g., freelancing, interest) if I have Form 16?
Yes, Form 16 only covers your salary income and TDS. You must report all other sources of income, such as interest from savings accounts, fixed deposits, capital gains, rental income, or any freelance earnings, in your ITR.

### How is Form 16 different from Form 16A?
Form 16 is for tax deducted on salary income. Form 16A is for TDS on non-salary income, such as interest income from fixed deposits, professional fees, or rent. Both are TDS certificates, but for different types of income.
