# Dalal Street's Diwali Sparkle: Can the Indian Market Rally Last?

**TL;DR**
- Indian equities witnessed a significant pre-Diwali rally, with benchmark indices surging nearly 9% in a week, marking the strongest such gain in years.
- This robust performance was primarily driven by declining crude oil prices, a resurgence in foreign institutional investor (FII) buying, and positive corporate earnings.
- Global factors like a potential slowdown in AI spending abroad and India's limited exposure to global tech sell-offs also contributed to its outperformance.
- While the short-term sentiment is optimistic, historical trends suggest that festive rallies can often be followed by profit-booking.
- Investors should focus on fundamental analysis, diversified portfolios, and aligning investments with long-term goals rather than chasing short-term market euphoria.

## What this means in plain terms

The Indian stock market, often referred to as Dalal Street, has recently experienced a strong upward movement, particularly in the lead-up to Diwali. This means that the value of shares in many companies has gone up significantly. This positive trend is good news for investors, as their portfolios have likely seen healthy gains.

Several key factors are contributing to this cheerful mood. Firstly, international crude oil prices have fallen, which is a big relief for India, as we import a large portion of our oil. Lower oil prices help reduce inflation and improve company profits. Secondly, foreign investors, who had been selling Indian shares for some time, have now started buying again, injecting fresh capital into the market. Lastly, Indian companies have shown strong financial results for the latest quarter, indicating healthy business performance. While this rally is exciting, it's crucial to understand what's driving it and whether these gains are likely to continue in the long run, or if it's a temporary festive boost.

## Market Performance & Key Drivers

The Indian equity markets have shown remarkable resilience and strength recently, particularly in the week preceding Diwali. The Nifty and Sensex both recorded gains of nearly 9% during this period, marking their strongest weekly performance since February 2021. This surge saw the Nifty cross the 25,000-mark and the Sensex surpass 81,000. This strong rally also ended a two-month losing streak for the benchmark indices.

### FII and DII Activity
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs), who had been net sellers in October with outflows of ₹45,161.71 crore, reversed their stance dramatically in early November. In the first few days of November alone, FIIs pumped a net ₹22,693 crore into Indian equities, indicating renewed confidence. This influx of foreign capital is a significant driver of the current rally. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), on the other hand, have consistently provided support, recording a net inflow of ₹62,220.11 crore in October, effectively absorbing the FII selling pressure during that month. This sustained domestic buying has been a crucial stabilising factor for the market.

### Global Context and Outperformance
The Indian market's performance stands in stark contrast to several global peers. While some Asian markets like Taiwan, South Korea, and Japan experienced significant declines due to corrections in AI, semiconductor, and data-centre stocks, India's relatively limited exposure to the global artificial intelligence (AI) trade helped it buck the sell-off. Analysts suggest that a potential slowdown in AI spending globally might encourage investors to redirect capital towards emerging markets like India, which offer strong fundamentals.

## Crude Oil & Geopolitical Landscape

A major catalyst for the recent market optimism has been the significant drop in crude oil prices. Brent crude, a global benchmark, fell below $100 per barrel, marking a 16% decline from its peak on October 30. This reduction is largely attributed to hopes of de-escalation in geopolitical tensions, particularly concerning the US-Iran conflict, following the US midterm election results.

### Impact on India's Economy
India is a major importer of crude oil, fulfilling nearly 89% of its requirements through imports. Therefore, a sustained fall in crude prices is highly beneficial for the Indian economy. It directly helps in:
*   **Reducing the oil import bill:** This lessens the outflow of foreign currency, positively impacting the current account deficit.
*   **Easing inflationary pressures:** Lower fuel costs translate to reduced transportation expenses, which can help control overall inflation.
*   **Supporting the Indian Rupee:** Reduced demand for US dollars to pay for oil imports can strengthen the rupee against the dollar.
*   **Improving corporate margins:** Companies, especially those reliant on energy inputs, benefit from lower operating costs.

However, the trajectory of crude oil prices remains difficult to predict, and geopolitical situations can change rapidly. While lower prices are always favourable for India, the volatility highlights the need for cautious optimism.

## Corporate Earnings & Sectoral Trends

The latest corporate earnings season, specifically for the second quarter of FY27, has provided another strong pillar for the market rally. Companies have reported robust performance, with year-on-year net profit growth averaging around 19%. This significantly better performance compared to the previous three quarters has strengthened the positive outlook for Indian equities.

### Sectoral Leadership
The recent rally has seen broad-based participation, but certain sectors have clearly led the charge. Consumer durables, realty, and financial services were among the top performers, each gaining around 10% or more in the week leading up to Diwali. This indicates investor preference for businesses tied to domestic demand and consumption. The automotive sector also saw gains of over 9%.

While the Nifty IT index had a strong October, jumping 17.7%, the overall market recovery in October was concentrated in larger stocks, with Nifty Smallcap 100 and Nifty Midcap 100 declining. However, in the last week of October, broader markets (Midcap and Smallcap indices) did show outperformance, gaining 2.69% and 4.08% respectively, with Pharma stocks also showing strong gains. Management commentary and guidance from various companies suggest expectations of sustained profit growth over the next two years, further boosting investor confidence.

## The Sustainability Question

While the current market enthusiasm is palpable, the crucial question for investors is whether this rally can be sustained. Several factors will determine the long-term trajectory of Dalal Street.

### Historical Precedent
Historically, pre-Diwali rallies and Muhurat trading sessions often exhibit a positive bias, but these gains are not always long-lasting. A Mint analysis indicated that while the Sensex rose in 76% of Muhurat sessions between 1997 and 2025, it fell in the very next trading session 69% of the time. This pattern is often attributed to positive sentiment and light trading during Muhurat, followed by profit-booking when normal trading resumes. The Nifty 50 has delivered positive returns in six out of ten weeks immediately preceding Diwali between 2015 and 2024, with an average return of 0.78%, but this is not a consistent upward trend every year.

### Key Factors for Future Performance
Experts point to several critical factors that will influence market direction in the coming Samvat year:
*   **Corporate Earnings Growth:** The ability of companies to deliver consistent earnings growth will be paramount.
*   **Crude Oil Prices:** Continued stability or further decline in crude oil prices will be crucial for India's economic health and corporate profitability.
*   **FII Flows:** Sustained foreign institutional investor inflows are essential for market momentum.
*   **Global Interest Rates and US Bond Yields:** Movements in global interest rates and US Treasury yields can impact FII flows and the attractiveness of emerging markets.
*   **Domestic Consumption & Government Spending:** Strong domestic demand and government expenditure on infrastructure and manufacturing will underpin economic growth.

### What it Means for Investors
For long-term investors, the current rally, while encouraging, should be viewed in the broader context of India's structural growth story. The underlying economic fundamentals, improving corporate earnings, and resilient domestic demand provide a strong foundation. However, short-term investors should be mindful of potential volatility and profit-booking, especially after such sharp gains. It is prudent to avoid chasing every market movement and instead focus on a well-diversified portfolio aligned with individual financial goals.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 35, a software engineer earning ₹35 lakhs CTC in Hyderabad**, who has been investing ₹50,000 per month in a diversified equity mutual fund via SIP. She also has a direct equity portfolio.

In October, her diversified mutual fund, largely invested in large-cap Indian equities, saw a modest gain of about 1.9% in line with the Nifty 50's performance for the month. So, her ₹50,000 SIP, combined with her existing ₹20 lakh portfolio, would have grown.

However, in the recent pre-Diwali rally (say, the last week of October and first week of November), her direct equity portfolio, which includes shares in a consumer durables company and a private sector bank, experienced a significant uplift. If she held ₹5 lakhs in these sectors, a 10% surge in these stocks, mirroring the sectoral leaders, would add ₹50,000 to her portfolio value.

This rapid appreciation in a short period might tempt Priya to book profits. However, as a long-term investor, she understands that while the immediate sentiment is positive due to factors like falling crude oil prices and renewed FII interest, the sustainability requires monitoring. She reviews her portfolio against her financial goals (e.g., down payment for a house in 5 years, retirement in 25 years) and decides to hold her core investments, perhaps rebalancing slightly to trim some of the significantly overperforming stocks if they now represent too large a portion of her portfolio. She also considers increasing her SIP amount slightly, leveraging the positive market sentiment for long-term compounding, but remains cautious about deploying a large lump sum without further analysis.

## What to do this week

1.  **Review your asset allocation:** Check if your equity exposure has significantly increased due to the recent rally. If it has, consider rebalancing your portfolio to bring it back to your desired asset allocation. This might involve booking some profits from overperforming equity segments and moving funds to debt or other asset classes.
2.  **Evaluate your holdings:** Take a quick look at the sectors that have performed exceptionally well. If you have concentrated exposure to these, assess if the underlying fundamentals still justify the valuations. This is not about panic selling but ensuring your portfolio isn't overly skewed.
3.  **Stay informed on crude oil prices:** Given India's reliance on oil imports, keep an eye on international crude oil price movements. Tools like financial news apps can provide real-time updates. Significant increases could signal future inflationary pressures.
4.  **Avoid impulsive decisions:** The festive cheer can sometimes lead to irrational exuberance. Resist the urge to make large, unresearched investments based solely on short-term market movements. Stick to your investment plan and long-term financial goals.
5.  **Check your emergency fund:** A strong market doesn't mean life's uncertainties disappear. Ensure your emergency fund is adequately stocked, covering 6-12 months of essential expenses, before considering additional equity investments.

## FAQ

### Is the current Indian market rally sustainable?
The rally is driven by strong factors like lower crude oil prices, renewed FII buying, and robust corporate earnings. While short-term volatility is always possible, India's underlying economic fundamentals and growth prospects provide a strong foundation for sustained performance.

### How do lower crude oil prices benefit Indian investors?
Lower crude oil prices reduce India's import bill, help control inflation, strengthen the rupee, and improve corporate profit margins. These factors collectively create a more favourable environment for economic growth and equity market performance.

### What role do FIIs play in the Indian market rally?
FIIs (Foreign Institutional Investors) bring significant capital into the Indian market. Their buying activity, particularly after a period of selling, injects liquidity and boosts investor confidence, acting as a key catalyst for market rallies.

### Should I invest more now, given the market rally?
While positive sentiment is high, it's crucial to align any new investments with your long-term financial goals and risk tolerance. Avoid chasing short-term gains. Consider staggered investments through SIPs rather than large lump sums to mitigate timing risks.

### What is Muhurat Trading and its significance?
Muhurat Trading is a special one-hour trading session held on Diwali, considered auspicious for making investments to usher in wealth. Historically, it often sees positive closes, but these gains do not always extend into subsequent trading days.

### Which sectors are leading the current rally?
Consumer durables, realty, and financial services have been prominent leaders in the recent rally, indicating strong investor interest in domestically-oriented businesses. Pharma, Midcap, and Smallcap segments also showed strength in late October.

### How does India's limited AI exposure affect its market performance?
India's relatively limited exposure to global AI-driven technology stocks helped it avoid the significant sell-offs seen in other international markets, making it an attractive destination for foreign capital looking for less volatile growth opportunities.
