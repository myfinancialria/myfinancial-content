# Market Wrap — 29 May 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, witnessed a significant sell-off today, closing down over 1%.
- Broader markets, including Midcap and Smallcap indices, also ended the session in the red.
- Metal, Auto, and Energy sectors bore the brunt of the selling pressure, emerging as the top losers.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided crucial buying support.
- The Indian Rupee strengthened considerably against the US Dollar, marking its best day in nearly two months.

## How the market closed

The Indian equity markets experienced a sharp downturn today, with the Nifty 50 shedding 1.50% to close at 23,547.75, while the Bank Nifty also saw a notable decline of 1.12%, settling at 54,239.20. The broader market sentiment remained weak, mirroring the benchmarks.

| Nifty Midcap 100 | 61723.80 | -1.33% | 62907.50 | 61581.80 |
| Nifty Smallcap 100 | 18138.80 | -0.85% | 18415.50 | 18093.80 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, surged by 8.08% to 16.19, indicating an increase in investor apprehension during today's trading session.

## Sectors — winners and losers

On a day dominated by selling, very few sectors managed to stay in the green. The Information Technology (IT) sector emerged as the top performer, posting a gain.

**Top 3 Gainer Sectors:**
- IT: +0.60% - The IT sector showed resilience, managing to close in positive territory amidst the broader market weakness.
- REALTY: -0.25% - Despite a slight dip, the Realty sector was among the best performers on a challenging day for the markets.
- MEDIA: -0.62% - The Media sector also showed relative strength, experiencing a smaller decline compared to most other sectors.

**Top 3 Loser Sectors:**
- METAL: -2.02% - The Metal sector faced significant selling pressure, ending the day as the biggest laggard.
- AUTO: -1.96% - Automobile stocks also witnessed a sharp decline, contributing heavily to the market's fall.
- ENERGY: -1.58% - The Energy sector closed notably lower, reflecting the overall negative sentiment.

## Market breadth

The market breadth was overwhelmingly negative today, with 366 stocks declining against only 134 advances out of the 500 stocks traded. This indicates that the selling pressure was broad-based and affected a large number of scrips across the market.

## FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers in the Indian equity market today, offloading shares worth ₹1,043 crore. However, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide strong support, injecting ₹3,821 crore into the market.

| Investor Type | Net Position (₹ crore) |
| :------------ | :--------------------- |
| FII | -1,043 |
| DII | 3,821 |

Looking at the past five trading sessions, FIIs have consistently been net sellers, with today marking their fourth day of outflows. In contrast, DIIs have been consistent net buyers, absorbing the FII selling pressure and providing crucial stability to the market.

## What moved markets today

Several factors contributed to the market's performance today, ranging from global cues to corporate earnings and domestic currency movements.

**Rupee's Strong Performance**
The Indian Rupee witnessed its best single-day gain in nearly two months, closing at 95 against the US Dollar. This surge was primarily driven by likely intervention from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and a notable drop in global oil prices. A stronger rupee is generally positive for the Indian economy, particularly for import-heavy sectors and in managing inflation.

**Weak Q4 Earnings Impact Key Stocks**
Several companies announced their fourth-quarter results, with some reporting weaker-than-expected performance, leading to declines in their stock prices. NATCO Pharma Ltd. saw its shares crash by 15% after reporting a 34% year-on-year tank in net profit. Budget carrier IndiGo also reported a net loss of ₹2,536 crore for Q4 FY26, compared to a profit a year ago. Similarly, Inox Wind's profit slumped by 45% to ₹106 crore in Q4. These weak earnings reports contributed to the overall negative sentiment in specific sectors and the broader market.

**Global Cues: US Markets Edge Higher**
US stock markets opened higher today, with the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq all showing gains. This positive sentiment in global markets was supported by optimism over updates regarding a possible US-Iran deal. While US futures and Asian markets pointed to a positive start, the Indian market largely moved independently today, driven by domestic factors and selling pressure.

**Persistent FII Selling**
The continued selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) for the fourth consecutive session played a significant role in the market's decline. Despite robust buying from Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), the consistent FII outflows weighed heavily on investor sentiment and contributed to the downward trend across major indices.

## Setup for tomorrow

As we head into tomorrow's session, market participants will be closely watching global cues and any further developments on the domestic front. The strong performance of the Rupee and the continued DII support could offer some stability, but persistent FII selling remains a concern.

Globally, US stocks are showing positive momentum, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq set to extend their record run, while Brent Crude and WTI Crude oil prices have seen a decline. Gold and Silver prices are up, and the US 10-year yield is slightly down. These mixed global signals, combined with the domestic market's recent volatility, suggest a cautious approach for tomorrow.
