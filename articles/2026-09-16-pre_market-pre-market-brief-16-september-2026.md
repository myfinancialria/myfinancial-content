# Pre-Market Brief — 16 September 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight, with US indices closing lower while most Asian markets traded in the green this morning.
*   GIFT Nifty is signalling a positive start for the Indian benchmark Nifty 50.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided consistent buying support.
*   The looming US Federal Reserve interest rate decision and persistent inflation concerns are key global drivers.
*   Nifty's immediate resistance to watch is around 23,326, with strong Put Open Interest at 23,200 acting as support.
*   The main risk for the day remains global macro uncertainty, particularly around central bank actions and continued FII outflows.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Overnight, US markets closed in negative territory, with the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all seeing declines. This cautious sentiment in the West was somewhat offset by a positive trend across most Asian markets this morning, with Japan's Nikkei, Hong Kong's Hang Seng, and China's Shanghai Composite all posting gains. This mixed global picture suggests a lack of strong directional cues from international markets for India's opening.

**Interpretation:** The mixed global cues, with US weakness and Asian strength, suggest that the Indian market might open with some indecision, though the positive Asian sentiment could provide a mild tailwind.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) saw a slight depreciation against the US Dollar, closing at ₹95.94, up 0.11%. The Dollar Index, which measures the dollar against a basket of major currencies, was marginally down by 0.08% at 99.57. Crude oil prices softened, with Brent Crude down 1.10% to $107.55 and WTI Crude falling 1.63% to $104.10. Gold prices rose by 1.06% to $4378.70, while Silver surged by 3.35% to $65.36, indicating a flight to safety or increased speculative interest in precious metals. The US 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 5.00%, an increase of 0.71%.

**Interpretation:** A depreciating Rupee (USD/INR up) could be a slight positive for IT and pharma exporters but poses pressure on importers. Falling crude oil prices are generally positive for India, potentially easing inflationary pressures and benefiting oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors. Rising gold and silver prices suggest global risk aversion. The increase in the US 10-year yield is a key concern, as higher US yields can make emerging markets like India less attractive, potentially leading to FII outflows.

The GIFT Nifty is trading at 23310.00, up 0.46%.

**Interpretation:** This indicates a positive bias for the Indian Nifty 50, suggesting a potential gap-up open for the domestic market today.

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers yesterday, offloading equities worth ₹2,978 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide strong support, with net purchases of ₹2,686 crore. This trend of FII selling being largely absorbed by DII buying has been consistent over the last five sessions.

## Stock & sector news that matters

**GE Vernova, CG Power, other power capex stocks tumble up to 16% in 2 days amid global AI caution.**
This report highlights a significant fall in power capex stocks like GE Vernova T&D India, CG Power, Hitachi Energy, and TD Power Systems.
**Why it matters:** This indicates a sector-specific correction, possibly driven by profit-booking after a sharp rally and broader global concerns around AI development impacting related infrastructure spending. Investors in these stocks or the broader power/infrastructure sector should monitor this trend.

**Tata Group stocks fall up to 7% after Tuesday’s IPO-buzz rally.**
Tata Group stocks saw a decline yesterday, likely due to profit booking following a rally driven by renewed expectations of a potential Tata Sons IPO.
**Why it matters:** This suggests that the market is reacting to speculative news (IPO buzz) and subsequent profit-taking. Investors holding Tata Group stocks should be aware of this volatility and the drivers behind it.

**Nithin Kamath says new UPI charges on investing, broking don’t make sense: 'I don't see how we can absorb this indefinitely'.**
Zerodha CEO Nithin Kamath has voiced concerns over the new UPI MDR framework, stating it may not be economically viable for investing and broking platforms.
**Why it matters:** This news is highly relevant for the broking and fintech sectors. New UPI charges could impact the business models of brokerage firms, potentially affecting their profitability and how they handle fund transfers for clients. This could lead to changes in transaction costs for retail investors.

**US Market: Fed’s Warsh faces tough rate call as inflation remains sticky.**
Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh is under pressure to balance persistent inflation with calls for lower or unchanged interest rates, with markets expecting a rate hike.
**Why it matters:** This is a critical global macro event. The US Federal Reserve's interest rate decision has significant implications for global liquidity, dollar strength, and FII flows into emerging markets like India. A rate hike could further strengthen the dollar and potentially lead to more FII outflows from India.

**Solar Industries’ defence share may fall to 22-25% by FY30 after Omnia deal: Jefferies.**
Jefferies predicts that Solar Industries' defence segment revenue contribution will decrease to 22-25% by FY30 following its Omnia acquisition, with agriculture and explosives gaining a larger share.
**Why it matters:** This is specific to Solar Industries India Ltd. and highlights a potential shift in its revenue mix. Investors in Solar Industries should consider this long-term strategic change and its implications for the company's growth profile.

**SEBI is said to ease arbitrage mutual fund rules to aid closing auction session.**
SEBI is reportedly allowing arbitrage mutual funds to temporarily hold unhedged positions of up to 1% of their total plan value.
**Why it matters:** This regulatory change could impact arbitrage mutual funds and potentially increase their flexibility. It might also influence trading dynamics during the closing auction session, which is relevant for active traders.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
*   **US Federal Reserve Interest Rate Decision:** This is the most significant global event to watch. Markets typically react with heightened volatility around Fed policy announcements, especially if the outcome deviates from expectations. A hawkish stance (rate hike or strong indication of future hikes) can lead to global equity market corrections and dollar strengthening, impacting FII flows.
*   **Implementation of new UPI MDR charges (from October 15):** While the full impact is from next month, the ongoing discussion and concerns raised by industry leaders like Nithin Kamath could keep the broking and fintech sectors in focus. Markets tend to react to regulatory changes that impact business models, with potential short-term adjustments in stock prices of affected companies.
*   **Hero Motors IPO Subscription Status:** The ongoing IPO will be watched for investor appetite. IPO subscription numbers and Grey Market Premium (GMP) often reflect broader market sentiment towards new listings and the primary market.
*   **Domestic Pharma Market Growth:** The sustained growth momentum but concerns over margins in the domestic pharma market will keep the sector in focus. Sector-specific news and performance trends can influence investor sentiment towards individual pharma stocks.

1.  **Global Cues:** Monitor how Asian markets continue to perform and any fresh developments from the US ahead of the Fed decision.
2.  **FII vs DII Flows:** Observe if the trend of FII selling and DII buying continues, and its impact on market direction.

4.  **Crude Oil Prices:** Continued softening of crude prices would be a positive for India, easing inflation concerns.
5.  **Sectoral Action:** Keep an eye on power capex stocks and Tata Group companies, which saw significant movements yesterday, as well as the broking/fintech sector due to UPI charge discussions.

## Bottom line
The Indian market is likely to open with a positive bias, as indicated by GIFT Nifty, despite mixed global cues and overnight weakness in US markets. The consistent buying support from DIIs has been crucial in offsetting FII outflows. The key for the day will be Nifty's ability to sustain above the 23200-23221 support zone, with 23500 acting as a significant resistance. Investors should remain cautious ahead of the impending US Federal Reserve decision and monitor global inflation signals, which could dictate FII sentiment.
