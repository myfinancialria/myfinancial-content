# Pre-Market Brief — 03 September 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets presented a mixed picture, with US indices closing higher while Asian markets traded with a slight negative bias this morning.
*   GIFT Nifty is signalling a cautious start for Indian equities, pointing to a potential gap-down open.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned significant net buyers yesterday after several sessions of selling, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their supportive stance.
*   RBI's foreign exchange inflows are boosting domestic bond markets and liquidity, a positive for overall sentiment.
*   Nifty's immediate resistance sits around 24000-24050, with strong support expected near 23850-23900.
*   The day's main risk could stem from global cues and potential profit booking after yesterday's FII buying, especially with Nifty and Sensex option data showing a cautious undertone.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Overnight, US markets closed positively, with the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all registering modest gains, driven by strong earnings from AI-related tech companies like Broadcom and Dell. This suggests a positive sentiment in the technology sector globally. However, Asian markets this morning are showing a mixed to slightly negative trend, with Japan's Nikkei 225 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng trading lower, while China's Shanghai Composite saw a marginal gain. This mixed global backdrop indicates that while there's underlying strength in certain sectors, overall sentiment remains somewhat cautious.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, with **USD/INR** trading at ₹94.46, down 0.53%. This indicates a stronger Rupee, which is generally favourable for importers as their costs decrease, but could be a slight headwind for exporters like IT and pharmaceutical companies in the short term. The **Dollar Index** also saw a decline of 0.39% to 99.17, suggesting broader weakness in the US Dollar globally.

In the commodities space, **Brent Crude** eased by 1.05% to $94.63 per barrel, and **WTI Crude** fell by 1.01% to $90.09 per barrel. This dip in crude oil prices is a positive development for India, a major oil importer, as it helps ease inflationary pressures and reduce input costs for sectors like aviation, paints, and oil marketing companies.

Precious metals saw a significant rally, with **Gold** jumping 2.40% to $4471.10 and **Silver** surging 2.70% to $66.47. This could indicate increased safe-haven demand amidst global uncertainties or renewed inflation concerns.

The **US 10-Year Yield** remained unchanged at 4.80%. While stable for now, the elevated yield continues to be a factor influencing global capital flows, including FII investments into emerging markets like India.

**Interpretation:** A stronger Rupee and lower crude oil prices are generally beneficial for the Indian economy and corporate earnings, potentially easing import bills and inflation. The rally in gold and silver might reflect global risk aversion or inflation hedging, which could be a nuanced signal for equity markets. The stable but high US 10Y yield means global interest rate dynamics remain a key factor for FII sentiment.

## GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling
The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 23989.00, down 0.45%. This suggests a negative bias for the Indian market open, pointing towards a potential gap-down for the Nifty 50 today.

**Interpretation:** The GIFT Nifty's negative signal indicates that Indian markets are likely to open lower, absorbing the mixed global cues and potentially some profit booking from recent gains. Traders should prepare for a cautious start.

    Indian government bonds saw gains, particularly shorter-term debt, as larger dollar inflows boosted rupee liquidity.
    **Why it matters:** This is positive for the overall financial system. Increased liquidity in the banking system can lead to lower short-term borrowing costs for companies and potentially support credit growth, which is good for banks and financial institutions.

    Motilal Oswal has initiated coverage on Adani Power with a 'Buy' rating, setting a target price of ₹250, implying a potential 20% upside. The brokerage highlights capacity expansion and a strong track record.
    **Why it matters:** This news could lead to significant buying interest and upward price movement in Adani Power shares today. Brokerage initiations with positive ratings often act as catalysts for stock performance.
*   **HUDCO shares jump 4% after signing ₹25,000 crore MoU with Bihar government for infrastructure development**
    HUDCO shares rallied after the company signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with the Government of Bihar to provide financial assistance of up to ₹25,000 crore over five years for industrial infrastructure and land development.
    **Why it matters:** This is a substantial deal for HUDCO, indicating strong business prospects and potential for future revenue growth. It could lead to continued positive momentum for the stock and potentially benefit other infrastructure-related companies.
*   **IFCI shares rally over 16% in two days as NSE IPO draws closer**
    IFCI shares have seen a significant rally amid expectations that the long-awaited NSE IPO might be nearing its launch. IFCI holds an indirect stake of over 4% in NSE.
    **Why it matters:** This news directly impacts IFCI's stock price due to its indirect holding in NSE. A potential NSE IPO could unlock value for IFCI and generate broader interest in the IPO market.
*   **Nominal GDP growth set to surge to 12%. Why the stock market may still struggle to rally**
    India’s nominal GDP growth is projected to accelerate to 11.5%-12% in FY27, which typically supports stronger corporate earnings. However, the report cautions that elevated valuations, rising equity supply, and potentially slower domestic flows could temper a market rally.
    **Why it matters:** This provides a crucial macro perspective. While strong GDP growth is fundamentally positive for corporate India, the accompanying concerns about valuations and equity supply suggest that market gains might be harder to come by, implying a need for selective stock picking.
*   **Broadcom’s AI chip boom powers record quarter, shares waver / Dell’s AI Boom: $95 billion backlog reshapes growth outlook**
    Broadcom reported strong fiscal third-quarter results driven by a surge in AI semiconductor revenue and demand for custom AI accelerators. Dell Technologies also reported record AI-server demand with a $95 billion backlog.
    **Why it matters:** These reports from global tech giants highlight the strong demand in the AI infrastructure space. This positive sentiment in the global tech sector could spill over to Indian IT services companies, especially those with exposure to AI and cloud infrastructure projects.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
*   **RBI Foreign Exchange Inflows & Liquidity:** The news of RBI's dollar inflows boosting rupee liquidity is a significant domestic factor. Typically, increased liquidity in the banking system tends to be positive for bond markets and can support equity markets by making funds cheaper for investment.
*   **US Jobs Data (Upcoming):** While not today, global investors are keenly awaiting upcoming US jobs data. Stronger-than-expected jobs data can signal a robust economy but might also fuel expectations of higher interest rates, potentially leading to volatility in global markets, including FII flows into India. Conversely, weaker data could signal economic slowdown concerns.
*   **F&O Expiry Week:** As we are in the early part of a new F&O series, the immediate impact of expiry-related churn is minimal. However, as the month progresses, expiry weeks typically see increased volatility and position adjustments.
