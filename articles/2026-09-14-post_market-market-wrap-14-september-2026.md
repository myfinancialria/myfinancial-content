# Market Wrap — 14 September 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Indian equities witnessed a mixed session, with Nifty 50 and broader markets closing in the red, while Bank Nifty showed resilience.
*   Global cues, particularly surging US bond yields, rising crude oil prices, and fears of an AI slowdown, were the primary drivers of market sentiment.
*   Pine Labs Ltd. emerged as the top gainer among Nifty 500 stocks, surging over 16%.
*   Cochin Shipyard Ltd. was the biggest laggard, dropping over 9%.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided crucial support.
*   F&O positioning data is unavailable for this session and will be included in the next wrap.

## How the market behaved today
The Indian market experienced a mixed trading day, with the benchmark Nifty 50 closing lower by 0.34% at 23,398.10, after trading in a range between 23,231.40 and 23,448.10. The broader market also saw weakness, with the Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100 declining. In contrast, the Bank Nifty showed strength, ending the day up 0.24% at 56,606.55, recovering from its lows.

| Index | Close | Change | High | Low |
| :---------------- | :---------- | :------- | :--------- | :--------- |
| Nifty 50 | 23398.10 | -0.34% | 23448.10 | 23231.40 |
| Bank Nifty | 56606.55 | +0.24% | 56645.90 | 55699.40 |
| Nifty Midcap 100 | 62197.20 | -0.26% | 62264.00 | 61374.40 |
| Nifty Smallcap 100 | 19906.30 | -0.58% | 19924.40 | 19728.10 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, rose by 4.15% to 12.29, indicating an increase in expected market fluctuations.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market's performance today was largely dictated by a confluence of global factors. A significant surge in the US 10-year Treasury yield, topping 5% for the first time since October 2023, sparked concerns about inflation and the likelihood of further interest rate hikes by the US Federal Reserve. This led to a sell-off in global bond markets and put pressure on equities worldwide.

Adding to the global unease, crude oil prices jumped by approximately 4%, with Brent Crude crossing $107 per barrel, further fueling inflation worries. This combination of rising bond yields and oil prices typically weighs on market sentiment, as it signals higher borrowing costs and input prices for businesses.

Furthermore, fears of a slowdown in Artificial Intelligence (AI) model development significantly impacted global tech stocks, with major chipmakers like Nvidia, Intel, and AMD experiencing sharp declines in US markets. While the direct impact on Indian tech heavyweights wasn't as pronounced in the top losers list, the sentiment contributed to the overall cautious mood.

Domestically, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued to be net sellers, withdrawing ₹931 crore from the Indian equity market. This outflow was, however, largely absorbed by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), who pumped in ₹1,968 crore, providing crucial support and preventing a steeper decline. Sectorally, the banking sector showed resilience, while realty, metal, and auto sectors bore the brunt of the selling pressure.

## Sectors — winners and losers
The banking sector stood out as a gainer today, while real estate, metals, and auto sectors faced significant selling pressure.

**Top 3 gainer sectors:**
*   **BANK:** +0.24% - Showed resilience amidst broader market weakness, with Bank Nifty closing in the green.
*   **MEDIA:** +0.16% - Recorded marginal gains, indicating some selective buying interest.
*   **IT:** +0.11% - Despite global tech slowdown fears, the Indian IT sector managed to close with slight gains.

**Top 3 loser sectors:**
*   **REALTY:** -2.70% - Was the biggest laggard, possibly impacted by rising interest rate concerns which can affect property demand and financing.
*   **METAL:** -2.30% - Faced selling pressure, likely due to global growth concerns and commodity price volatility.
*   **AUTO:** -0.86% - Declined, possibly influenced by broader market sentiment and concerns over consumer demand.

## Market breadth
The market breadth was negative today, with 148 advances and 347 declines out of a total of 495 traded stocks. This indicates that a larger number of stocks fell compared to those that gained, reflecting the overall cautious sentiment in the broader market.

## FII / DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued to be net sellers in the Indian equity market today, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net buyers, providing crucial support.

| Investor Type | Net Flow (₹ crore) |
| :------------ | :----------------- |
| FII | -931 |
| DII | +1,968 |

Looking at the 5-day history, FIIs have been net sellers for four out of the last five trading sessions, indicating a consistent outflow trend. In contrast, DIIs have been consistent net buyers throughout this period, absorbing the FII selling pressure and injecting capital into the market. This consistent DII buying has been a key factor in cushioning the market from sharper declines.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was primarily driven by broad global flows and macroeconomic concerns rather than a single defining domestic event. The significant event that weighed on market sentiment was the surge in the US 10-year Treasury yield above 5%, coupled with a sharp rise in crude oil prices. These developments intensified fears of global inflation and the likelihood of the US Federal Reserve continuing its rate hike cycle. This led to a risk-off sentiment globally, impacting Indian equities as well.

Additionally, concerns about a potential slowdown in AI development hit global tech stocks, adding to the cautious mood. While the Indian IT sector closed marginally positive, the broader market, especially sectors like Realty, Metal, and Auto, felt the pressure. FII selling further contributed to the negative bias, though DII buying provided a strong counter-balance.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will continue to be paramount. Investors will closely watch the US markets' reaction to the ongoing concerns about AI development and the implications of rising bond yields and crude oil prices on inflation and interest rate expectations. Overnight, the Dollar Index strengthened to 99.48 (+0.36%), while Brent Crude remained elevated at $107.29 (+2.56%). Gold and Silver prices retreated, reflecting the strength in the dollar and rate hike concerns. The US 10Y Yield closed at 4.95%. Key levels for Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, along with the performance of rate-sensitive sectors like Realty and Auto, will be important to monitor.
