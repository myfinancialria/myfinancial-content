# Pre-Market Brief — 22 October 2026

**TL;DR**
- **Global Tone:** US markets closed positive, but Asian markets are largely negative this morning, signalling a mixed start.
- **GIFT Nifty Bias:** Points to a flat to slightly positive opening for the Indian benchmark Nifty.
- **FII/DII Stance:** Both Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net buyers yesterday, offering support.
- **Biggest News:** Indian bond yields remain steady after RBI minutes hinted at potential further rate hikes.
- **Key Nifty Level:** Nifty's CPR pivot at 22661.02, with 23000 acting as a significant resistance level from Option Chain data.
- **Day's Main Risk:** Rising crude oil prices and the hawkish stance from the RBI regarding future rate adjustments.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Overnight, US markets closed on a positive note, driven by strong earnings from some tech companies. The Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all registered gains. However, Asian markets this morning are largely in the red, with Japan's Nikkei, Hong Kong's Hang Seng, and China's Shanghai Composite all seeing significant declines, partly due to fading China stimulus hopes and concerns over economic growth. This mixed global sentiment suggests a cautious open for Indian markets, with positive cues from the US potentially offset by weakness in key Asian peers.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The **USD/INR** pair saw a slight increase to ₹94.82 (+0.11%).
**Interpretation:** A rising USD/INR is generally positive for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters as their dollar earnings translate to more rupees. However, it puts pressure on importers and can contribute to imported inflation.

The **Dollar Index** declined to 101.54 (-0.23%).
**Interpretation:** A weaker Dollar Index typically signals increased risk appetite globally and can be supportive for emerging market currencies and equities, potentially attracting FII flows.

**Brent Crude** surged to $111.88 (+2.81%) and **WTI Crude** also rose to $99.63 (+2.55%).
**Interpretation:** A sharp rise in crude oil prices is a significant concern for India, a major oil importer. This can lead to increased inflation, higher current account deficit, and put pressure on sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint manufacturers, and aviation.

**Gold** fell to $4107.90 (-1.01%) and **Silver** declined to $56.08 (-2.34%).
**Interpretation:** The fall in precious metals could be attributed to a stronger dollar (despite the Dollar Index being down, the news mentions traders eyeing the US Fed meet next week, which often leads to dollar strength expectations) and potentially reduced safe-haven demand, or profit-booking ahead of key global central bank events.

The **US 10Y Yield** increased to 5.15% (+0.57%).
**Interpretation:** Rising US bond yields make dollar-denominated assets more attractive, potentially leading to capital outflows from emerging markets like India as FIIs seek higher risk-free returns elsewhere. This could exert downward pressure on Indian equities.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
- **RBI Monetary Policy Minutes:** The minutes released yesterday, indicating a hawkish stance and potential for further rate hikes, will continue to be a key driver for Indian bond yields and rate-sensitive sectors. Markets typically react with caution to signals of tightening monetary policy.
- **US Federal Reserve Meeting (next week):** Investors are closely watching the upcoming US Fed meeting. Expectations around the Fed's stance on interest rates can significantly influence global capital flows, the dollar index, and FII activity in emerging markets, often leading to increased volatility.
- **Bank of Japan (BOJ) Meeting:** Japanese bond yields fell ahead of the BOJ meeting. Central bank decisions in major economies can impact global liquidity and risk sentiment.
- **Weekly F&O Expiry (today):** Being a Thursday, today marks the weekly expiry for Nifty and Bank Nifty options. This typically leads to increased volatility and short covering or unwinding of positions, especially around key strike prices.
- **Crude Oil Price Movement:** The sharp rise in Brent and WTI crude oil prices will be a critical factor to monitor throughout the day. Sustained high crude prices are a negative for the Indian economy and can fuel inflation concerns.
