# Market Wrap — 26 November 2026

**TL;DR**
- The Nifty 50 closed marginally higher, while the Bank Nifty saw a slight dip. Broader markets, represented by Midcap and Smallcap indices, showed stronger gains.
- Robust buying from both Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) was a key driver, injecting over ₹8,400 crore combined.
- Action Construction Equipment Ltd. (ACE) was the standout gainer in the Nifty 500, surging by 8.79%.
- Bank of Maharashtra emerged as the top loser in the Nifty 500, declining by 4.57%.
- Both FIIs and DIIs were net buyers in the cash market today, indicating strong institutional support.
- Data unavailable for today's F&O build-up, so no clear positioning signal can be derived.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equities witnessed a mixed session today, with the benchmark Nifty 50 managing to close in positive territory, while the Bank Nifty ended slightly lower. The broader market, however, displayed strength, with Midcap and Smallcap indices outperforming. The Nifty 50 opened with a slight gap up and largely traded within a range, showing resilience despite some sector-specific pressures.

| Index | Close | Change | High | Low |

| Nifty 50 | 25884.50 | +0.25% | 25974.95 | 25733.50 |
| Bank Nifty | 63535.30 | -0.26% | 64104.75 | 63332.30 |
| Nifty Midcap 100 | 61781.85 | +0.32% | 62138.45 | 61630.10 |
| Nifty Smallcap 100 | 19372.15 | +0.74% | 19433.15 | 19241.55 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, cooled down by 4.70% to 16.84, suggesting a reduction in immediate market uncertainty.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market today was primarily driven by strong institutional buying, with both Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) being net purchasers. FIIs pumped in ₹4,479 crore, while DIIs added ₹4,019 crore, providing a significant boost to market sentiment. This robust inflow helped the Nifty 50 maintain its positive momentum, even as the Bank Nifty saw some profit booking.

Global cues presented a mixed picture. Concerns over rising Brent crude prices, which topped $95 per barrel, and the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) flagging food inflation risks, led to a tumble in Indian government bonds and partly reversed the rupee's rally. However, a positive long-term outlook from J.P. Morgan, which termed Indian stocks a 'safe haven' and projected the Nifty 50 to hit 29,000 by the end of 2027, likely bolstered investor confidence.

Sector-wise, the IT and Realty sectors led the gains, with companies like Prestige Estates Projects Ltd. hitting fresh 52-week highs. Conversely, FMCG, Auto, and PSU Bank sectors experienced selling pressure. The cooling down of the India VIX also indicated a more stable trading environment.

- **RBI Flags Food Inflation Risks Amidst Rising Oil Prices**: The Reserve Bank of India highlighted broad-based food inflation pressures, contributing to a decline in Indian government bonds as Brent crude topped $95 per barrel. This macroeconomic development impacted bond yields and the Indian rupee.
- **J.P. Morgan Sees Nifty 50 at 29,000, Calls Indian Stocks a 'Safe Haven'**: Global financial giant J.P. Morgan projected the Nifty 50 to reach 29,000 by the end of 2027, positioning Indian equities as a potential safe haven for investors diversifying beyond AI-focused trades. This positive outlook likely boosted overall market sentiment.
- **NSE Becomes World's Third-Largest Listed Exchange by Market Cap**: The National Stock Exchange (NSE) hit a fresh 52-week high and surpassed Nasdaq and LSE to become the world's third-largest listed exchange by market capitalization, reflecting strong performance and investor confidence in the exchange sector.
- **Realty Sector Buzzes with New Projects and 52-Week Highs**: Ashiana Housing Ltd. announced a joint venture to acquire 10 acres in Chennai for a senior living project, eyeing ₹1,100 crore in revenue. Concurrently, Prestige Estates Projects Ltd. was among six BSE 100 stocks that hit fresh 52-week highs, underscoring the positive momentum in the realty sector.
- **Supriya Lifescience to Consider Share Buyback**: Supriya Lifescience announced a board meeting on December 2, 2026, to consider a share buyback proposal. This news could generate investor interest in the stock.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was largely driven by a combination of strong institutional flows and mixed global cues. While there wasn't a single defining event, the market reacted to several key developments:

*   **Institutional Buying Spree:** The most significant factor was the substantial net buying by both FIIs (₹4,479 crore) and DIIs (₹4,019 crore). This strong liquidity injection provided a floor to the market and helped the Nifty 50 close in the green, despite some headwinds.
*   **Macroeconomic Headwinds:** Concerns over rising Brent crude prices, which breached $95 per barrel, and the RBI's warning about food inflation risks, led to a decline in Indian government bonds and put pressure on the rupee. This impacted interest-rate sensitive sectors and the forex market.
*   **Positive Market Outlook:** J.P. Morgan's optimistic forecast for the Nifty 50, calling Indian stocks a 'safe haven' and predicting 29,000 by 2027, provided a strong underlying positive sentiment, attracting investors.
*   **Sectoral Rotation:** The IT and Realty sectors saw significant buying, with several stocks hitting 52-week highs, indicating sector-specific strength. Conversely, FMCG, Auto, and PSU Banks experienced profit booking.

Overall, the session was characterized by a tug-of-war between positive institutional flows and long-term optimism versus short-term macroeconomic concerns.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead to tomorrow, the market will likely continue to monitor global crude oil prices and any further updates on inflation from the RBI. The strong institutional buying trend, particularly from FIIs, will be a key factor to watch. Globally, the Dollar Index closed slightly higher at 103.78, while Brent crude saw a significant drop to $91.73, which could be a positive for India's import bill. US 10-year yields also fell to 4.85%. Investors will keep an eye on these global cues, especially how Asian markets react overnight. Key sectors like IT and Realty, which saw strong momentum today, might continue to be in focus, along with any further news on corporate actions like buybacks.
