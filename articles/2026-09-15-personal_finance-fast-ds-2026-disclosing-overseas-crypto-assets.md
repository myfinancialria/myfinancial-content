# FAST-DS 2026: Disclosing Overseas Crypto Assets

**TL;DR**
- The Foreign Assets Disclosure Scheme (FAST-DS) 2026 offers a window for eligible taxpayers to voluntarily disclose undisclosed foreign assets and income.
- Whether overseas cryptocurrency holdings qualify for disclosure under FAST-DS 2026 depends on specific conditions.
- Key factors include where the crypto is held (e.g., foreign exchange, cold wallet), how it was acquired, and the taxpayer's residential status.
- Non-disclosure of foreign assets, including qualifying crypto, can lead to severe penalties under the Black Money Act, 2015.
- Taxpayers with unreported overseas crypto should carefully assess their situation and consider professional advice regarding this scheme.

## What this means in plain terms

The Indian government periodically introduces schemes to encourage taxpayers to come clean about assets and income that were not reported earlier. The Foreign Assets Disclosure Scheme (FAST-DS) 2026 is one such initiative, designed to provide a specific window for individuals to declare undisclosed foreign assets and the income generated from them. The primary goal is to bring these assets into the tax net without the harshest penalties that would otherwise apply.

In recent years, the landscape of investments has evolved significantly, with cryptocurrencies becoming a notable asset class. Many Indian residents and Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) hold cryptocurrencies on overseas exchanges or in foreign-hosted wallets. The crucial question for these individuals is whether such crypto holdings can be disclosed under FAST-DS 2026. The answer isn't straightforward and hinges on various factors, including the legal classification of crypto, the location of the assets, and the conditions laid out by the scheme itself.

## Understanding the Foreign Assets Disclosure Scheme (FAST-DS) 2026

The Foreign Assets Disclosure Scheme (FAST-DS) 2026 is a voluntary compliance window introduced by the government to allow taxpayers to declare undisclosed foreign assets and income. This scheme aims to encourage transparency and compliance with India's tax laws, particularly the Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) and Imposition of Tax Act, 2015. The core idea is to provide an opportunity for individuals to regularise their financial affairs concerning foreign holdings by paying a specified tax and penalty, thereby avoiding more stringent punitive actions later.

### Eligibility and Scope

Typically, such schemes are open to resident Indians who have undisclosed foreign assets. The scope of "foreign assets" can be broad, covering bank accounts, immovable properties, shares, debentures, and other financial instruments held outside India. The FAST-DS 2026 specifically targets assets that were acquired from undisclosed income or that were not reported as required by tax laws. It's crucial for taxpayers to understand the exact eligibility criteria, including their residential status during the period the assets were acquired and held, as defined by the Income Tax Act, 1961.

### Benefits of Disclosure

The primary benefit of disclosing under FAST-DS 2026 is the certainty it provides. By coming forward, taxpayers can settle their past non-compliance issues by paying the prescribed tax and penalty, typically at a lower rate than what would be levied if the assets were discovered through an investigation. This also helps avoid prosecution under the Black Money Act, which can include imprisonment for up to ten years. The scheme offers a chance for a fresh start, ensuring future compliance without the burden of past undisclosed assets.

## Overseas Crypto as a Foreign Asset

The classification of cryptocurrency under Indian tax law has been a evolving subject. While there isn't a standalone law specifically for crypto, the government has clarified that income from crypto assets is taxable. The crucial aspect for FAST-DS 2026 is whether overseas crypto holdings can be considered "foreign assets" for disclosure purposes.

### Where is the Crypto Held?

The physical location where the crypto is held plays a significant role. If the cryptocurrency is held on a foreign exchange (e.g., Binance, Coinbase, Kraken), it is generally considered an asset held outside India. Similarly, if it's stored in a hardware wallet (cold wallet) where the private keys are held by an individual residing in India, but the underlying assets are traded or managed through foreign platforms, it might still fall under the ambit of foreign assets. The key is the jurisdiction of the entity facilitating the holding or the location of the asset's underlying infrastructure.

### How was the Crypto Acquired?

The source of funds used to acquire the crypto is another critical factor. If the crypto was purchased using funds that were themselves undisclosed or generated from undisclosed income, then both the original funds and the subsequent crypto assets would be considered undisclosed. For example, if an individual earned income in a foreign country, did not report it in India, and then used that undisclosed foreign income to buy crypto on an overseas exchange, both the income and the crypto would be subject to disclosure. The scheme is designed to cover assets acquired from income that escaped Indian taxation.

## Conditions for Disclosure of Overseas Crypto under FAST-DS 2026

The applicability of FAST-DS 2026 to overseas crypto hinges on meeting the specific conditions outlined by the scheme. Taxpayers must carefully evaluate their situation against these requirements.

### Residential Status

The scheme typically targets individuals who were residents of India when the undisclosed foreign asset (in this case, crypto) was acquired or when the undisclosed income used to acquire it was earned. For NRIs, the situation can be more complex. If an individual was an NRI at the time of acquiring crypto with legitimately earned foreign income, it would generally not be considered an undisclosed foreign asset for Indian tax purposes. However, if they were a resident and failed to report foreign income used for crypto acquisition, or became a resident after acquiring the crypto and failed to report it, then the scheme might apply. The definition of "resident" under the Income Tax Act is paramount here.

### Nature of Crypto Holdings

While the scheme doesn't explicitly mention "crypto," its broad definition of "foreign assets" is expected to encompass digital assets that represent value and are held outside India. The intent is to cover any asset that has a monetary value and was not reported. The government's stance on taxing crypto income indicates its recognition as an asset for tax purposes. Therefore, if the crypto holdings represent a significant value and were acquired using undisclosed income or were themselves undisclosed, they would likely fall within the purview of the scheme.

### Voluntary Disclosure and Compliance

The essence of FAST-DS 2026 is voluntary disclosure. This means the taxpayer must proactively come forward before any notice or investigation is initiated by the tax authorities regarding these specific assets. Once a notice is received, the benefits of the voluntary disclosure scheme may no longer be available. The scheme requires full and truthful disclosure of all relevant details, including the acquisition cost, current value, and the source of funds.

## A real example

Meet Rohan, 38, a software engineer earning ₹28 lakhs CTC, based in Bengaluru. In 2021, Rohan received a bonus of $20,000 (approx. ₹15 lakhs at the time) from a foreign client for a freelance project, which he did not declare in his Indian income tax returns. Instead, he transferred this amount to an overseas crypto exchange and invested it in various cryptocurrencies. Over the past three years, his crypto portfolio has grown, and its current value is approximately $35,000 (around ₹29 lakhs).

Now, with the announcement of FAST-DS 2026, Rohan is concerned about the undisclosed foreign income and the subsequent crypto assets.

Here's how he might approach it:

1.  **Identify the undisclosed asset:** Rohan's undisclosed foreign asset is the crypto portfolio, which originated from an undisclosed foreign income of $20,000.
2.  **Calculate the undisclosed income:** The original undisclosed income was ₹15 lakhs.
3.  **Determine the current value:** The current value of his crypto assets is ₹29 lakhs.
4.  **Assess eligibility:** Rohan was a resident Indian when he earned the foreign income and invested it in crypto. The assets are held on an overseas exchange. He meets the general criteria for disclosing foreign assets.
5.  **Calculate potential tax and penalty:** Under FAST-DS 2026, a specific tax rate and penalty would apply to the undisclosed foreign asset. Let's assume, for illustrative purposes, the scheme specifies a combined tax and penalty rate of 60% on the value of the undisclosed asset.
    *   Tax and penalty = 60% of ₹29,00,000 = ₹17,40,000.
    *   This amount would need to be paid to regularise his holdings.
6.  **Future compliance:** After disclosure and payment, Rohan's crypto assets would be regularised. Any future income from these assets (e.g., capital gains from selling crypto) would then need to be declared annually in his Indian income tax returns, as per the prevailing tax laws.

By utilising FAST-DS 2026, Rohan can avoid potential prosecution and much higher penalties under the Black Money Act, which could have been significantly more severe if the assets were discovered by the tax authorities.

## FAQ

### What is the primary purpose of FAST-DS 2026?
The scheme aims to provide a window for taxpayers to voluntarily disclose previously unreported foreign assets and income, thereby regularising their tax affairs and avoiding severe penalties under the Black Money Act, 2015.

### Can all types of foreign crypto assets be disclosed under this scheme?
Generally, if the crypto assets represent value, are held outside India, and were acquired from undisclosed income or were themselves undisclosed, they are likely covered. The specific conditions, including the source of funds and residential status, must be met.

### What are the risks of not disclosing unreported overseas crypto?
Non-disclosure can lead to significant penalties, including tax at a high rate, additional fines, and even prosecution with imprisonment under the Black Money Act, 2015, if the assets are discovered by tax authorities.

### Is there a specific tax rate or penalty for disclosures under FAST-DS 2026?
Yes, disclosure schemes typically specify a combined tax and penalty rate that is applicable to the value of the undisclosed foreign asset. This rate is usually lower than what would be imposed if the assets were discovered through an investigation.

### Does this scheme apply to NRIs?
The applicability to NRIs depends on their residential status at the time the assets were acquired and the source of funds. If an NRI acquired crypto with legitimate foreign income while being a non-resident, it generally wouldn't be subject to this scheme. However, individual circumstances vary.

### What if my crypto was acquired through legitimate means but I forgot to report it?
Even if acquired legitimately, failure to report foreign assets (if required) can lead to compliance issues. FAST-DS 2026 might still offer a mechanism to regularise such oversights, depending on the scheme's specific terms and conditions.

### What is the deadline for disclosing under FAST-DS 2026?
The specific deadlines for FAST-DS 2026 would be announced through official notifications from the CBDT or Ministry of Finance. Taxpayers must closely monitor these announcements on official government websites.
