# Pre-Market Brief — 16 June 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets presented a mixed picture, with Asian and European indices showing varied movements, though European markets gained on US-Iran agreement optimism.
- Crude oil prices saw a significant decline, with Brent down over 2% and WTI over 4%, following news of a potential US-Iran deal easing supply concerns.
- The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, while the Dollar Index saw a slight dip.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned net buyers yesterday, infusing ₹200 crore, breaking a four-day selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their robust buying.
- Aluminium sector stocks like Hindalco, NALCO, and Vedanta Aluminium are likely to face pressure today as global aluminium prices crashed on the back of the US-Iran peace deal.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, with the USD/INR pair trading at ₹94.53, a decline of 0.61%. This indicates a stronger Rupee, which is generally positive for India as it makes imports cheaper and can help curb inflation. The Dollar Index also softened by 0.12% to 99.63, reflecting a broader weakness in the US Dollar.

In commodities, crude oil prices saw a notable decline. Brent Crude fell by 2.27% to $81.28 per barrel, and WTI Crude dropped significantly by 4.06% to $77.47 per barrel. This sharp fall in crude prices is a positive development for India, a major oil importer, as it could ease inflationary pressures and potentially improve corporate margins. Gold prices edged up by 0.73% to $4359.80, while Silver also gained 0.42% to $70.36. The US 10-year Treasury Yield increased by 0.54% to 4.49%, suggesting some upward pressure on global borrowing costs.

## Recent FII/DII flows
After a period of consistent selling, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned net buyers yesterday, injecting ₹200 crore into the Indian equity market. This marks a shift from the previous four sessions where FIIs were net sellers. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), on the other hand, continued their strong buying momentum, with a net inflow of ₹3,189 crore yesterday. Over the last five sessions, DIIs have consistently been net buyers, providing crucial support to the market amidst FII outflows.

## Overnight news that matters
**European shares inch up as investors assess US-Iran agreement**
European stock markets saw a positive start, continuing Monday's upward trend, as investors focused on the early stages of a possible agreement between the U.S. and Iran. This development is significant as it signals easing geopolitical tensions and could positively influence global energy markets and investor sentiment.

**Euro zone bond yields steady near two-week low after Middle East peace deal**
Euro zone bond yields stabilised after a recent dip, with a US-Iran deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz easing energy supply concerns. This reduction in inflation worries and slowing growth fears could lead to more stable interest rate environments globally, which is generally favourable for equity markets.

**Hindalco, NALCO, Vedanta Aluminium shares crash up to 6% as aluminium prices drop on US-Iran peace deal**
Indian aluminium stocks experienced a sharp decline as global aluminium prices crashed following news of a US-Iran peace deal framework, which improves prospects for increased supply. This directly impacts Indian metal producers, suggesting potential headwinds for the sector today.

**RIL shares jump 6% in 3 days, market cap soars by Rs 1 lakh cr ahead of AGM. Why Morgan Stanley still sees 38% upside**
Reliance Industries shares have gained over ₹1 lakh crore in market value in just three trading sessions leading up to its Annual General Meeting (AGM). Morgan Stanley maintains a 'Buy' rating with a significant upside, highlighting the market's anticipation and positive outlook for the conglomerate.

**NBFCs and private banks better positioned than PSU banks: Aman Chowhan**
Aman Chowhan of Abakkus Asset Manager suggests that NBFCs and private banks are better positioned than PSU banks, citing crude oil as a dominant macro risk that could impact corporate earnings. This expert view provides a sectoral preference for investors, favouring defensive and structural plays in the current environment.

## What to watch in today's session
1.  **Crude Oil Impact:** Monitor the sustained impact of falling crude oil prices on sectors sensitive to energy costs, such as airlines, logistics, and manufacturing, as well as its broader implications for inflation and corporate earnings.
2.  **Metal Sector Performance:** Keep a close watch on aluminium stocks like Hindalco, NALCO, and Vedanta Aluminium, which are likely to react to the significant drop in global aluminium prices.
3.  **Reliance Industries (RIL):** Observe the movement in RIL shares ahead of its closely watched AGM, given the recent surge in its market capitalisation and positive analyst outlook.
4.  **FII Flow Trend:** Assess if yesterday's positive FII inflow marks a sustained change in trend or if foreign investors revert to selling, which could influence overall market sentiment.
5.  **Nifty 50 Key Levels:** Traders should monitor Nifty 50's ability to sustain above key resistance levels, particularly around 24,000 and 24,100, given the significant call writing at these strikes.

## Bottom line
The Indian market is likely to open with mixed cues, but with a potential positive bias driven by the significant drop in crude oil prices and the return of FII buying yesterday. While global sentiment is cautiously optimistic due to the US-Iran agreement, investors should be mindful of the immediate pressure on the metal sector, particularly aluminium stocks. The Nifty 50's ability to breach and sustain above the 24,000 mark will be a key technical level to watch.
