# Save Tax on Property Capital Gains: A Practical Guide

**TL;DR**
-   Selling a residential property after two years yields Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) subject to 20% tax with indexation benefit.
-   You can claim exemption from this tax by reinvesting the capital gains into a new residential property.
-   The new property must be purchased one year before or two years after the sale, or constructed within three years.
-   If you haven't bought or constructed the new property by the tax filing deadline, deposit the gains into a Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS).
-   Failure to utilise the funds in CGAS within the stipulated time makes the unutilised amount taxable in the year of withdrawal.

## What this means in plain terms

When you sell a residential property, the profit you make is called a capital gain. Depending on how long you held the property, this gain is categorised as either short-term or long-term. If you've owned the property for more than 24 months (two years), the profit is considered a Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG). This LTCG is typically taxed at a rate of 20%, but the good news is that the government allows you to adjust the original purchase cost for inflation using an 'indexation' benefit, which reduces your taxable gain.

However, the Income Tax Act provides a significant opportunity to save this tax. If you reinvest the amount of your capital gain into buying or constructing another residential property within specific timeframes, you can claim an exemption from this tax. This provision encourages property owners to continue investing in the real estate sector, offering a substantial tax relief for those who plan their finances carefully after selling a property.

## Understanding Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) and Indexation

When you sell a residential property, the profit or loss is termed a capital gain or loss. For a property held for more than 24 months, the gain is classified as a Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG). This is distinct from Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG), which applies if the property is sold within 24 months of acquisition, and is taxed at your applicable income tax slab rates.

### Calculating LTCG with Indexation

The primary benefit for LTCG on property is indexation. This mechanism adjusts the cost of acquisition and improvement for inflation, significantly reducing your taxable gain. The Cost Inflation Index (CII) is notified by the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) each year.

The formula for indexed cost of acquisition is:
(Cost of Acquisition / CII for the year of acquisition) * CII for the year of sale

Similarly, for indexed cost of improvement:
(Cost of Improvement / CII for the year of improvement) * CII for the year of sale

Your Long-Term Capital Gain is then calculated as:
Sale Value - (Indexed Cost of Acquisition + Indexed Cost of Improvement + Expenses on Transfer)

LTCG on property is taxed at a flat rate of 20% (plus surcharge and cess, if applicable) after applying indexation.

## Exemption under Section 54: Reinvesting in a New Home

Section 54 of the Income Tax Act, 1961, offers a crucial exemption for individuals and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) who sell a residential property and reinvest the capital gains into another residential property. This exemption is available for LTCG only.

### Conditions for Exemption
To claim exemption under Section 54, you must meet specific conditions:
1.  **Assessee Type:** The exemption is available to individuals and HUFs.
2.  **Asset Sold:** The asset sold must be a long-term capital asset, being a residential house property.
3.  **Asset Purchased:** The capital gains must be reinvested in purchasing or constructing one new residential house property in India. For sales made on or after April 1, 2023, the exemption for reinvestment is capped at ₹10 crores. Prior to this, there was no such monetary limit.
4.  **Time Limits for Reinvestment:**
    *   Purchase a new residential house property within one year before the date of sale of the original property.
    *   Purchase a new residential house property within two years after the date of sale of the original property.
    *   Construct a new residential house property within three years after the date of sale of the original property.

### Quantum of Exemption
The exemption amount is the lower of:
*   The actual capital gains arising from the sale of the original property.
*   The amount invested in the purchase or construction of the new residential property.

If the amount of capital gains is fully reinvested, the entire LTCG becomes exempt from tax. If only a part of the capital gains is reinvested, the exemption is proportionate to the amount reinvested, and the remaining capital gains become taxable.

## The Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS)

Often, it's not practical to purchase or construct a new property immediately after selling the old one, especially within the financial year of the sale. To address this, the Income Tax Act provides for the Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS), 1988. This scheme allows you to deposit your unutilised capital gains into a designated account with public sector banks or other banks authorised by the Reserve Bank of India, thereby preserving your eligibility for Section 54 exemption.

### How CGAS Works
If you have not utilised the capital gains to buy or construct a new residential property before the due date for filing your Income Tax Return (ITR) for the financial year in which the sale occurred, you must deposit the unutilised amount into a CGAS account. This deposit must be made before the ITR filing deadline. Once deposited, the amount is deemed to have been utilised for the purpose of claiming exemption under Section 54.

### Types of Accounts and Usage
There are two types of CGAS accounts:
*   **Type A (Savings Account):** This account is suitable for parking funds for a short duration, offering interest similar to a regular savings account. Funds can be withdrawn easily.
*   **Type B (Term Deposit Account):** This is for funds that will be needed after some time, offering higher interest rates, similar to a fixed deposit. Withdrawals from this account require a written application and must be transferred to a Type A account first.

Funds deposited in CGAS can only be used for the specific purpose of purchasing or constructing a new residential house property. Any amount withdrawn for other purposes or not utilised within the stipulated time frames (two or three years from the date of sale, as applicable) will be treated as capital gains of the financial year in which the time limit expires and will be taxed accordingly.

## A real example

Meet Suresh, 52, a self-employed consultant in Chennai with an annual income of ₹35 lakhs. He sold a residential apartment in October 2025 for ₹1.20 crores. He had purchased this apartment in April 2008 for ₹30 lakhs. He also spent ₹5 lakhs on renovation in July 2015. He wants to save tax by buying a new, smaller apartment.

Let's calculate his capital gains and potential tax saving:

**1. Historical Cost Inflation Index (CII) values (Hypothetical for calculation):**
*   CII for FY 2008-09: 137
*   CII for FY 2015-16: 254
*   CII for FY 2025-26: 360 (Year of Sale)

**2. Calculate Indexed Cost of Acquisition:**
*   (Original Cost / CII of purchase year) * CII of sale year
*   (₹30,00,000 / 137) * 360 = ₹78,83,212

**3. Calculate Indexed Cost of Improvement:**
*   (Renovation Cost / CII of improvement year) * CII of sale year
*   (₹5,00,000 / 254) * 360 = ₹7,08,661

**4. Calculate Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG):**
*   Sale Value: ₹1,20,00,000
*   Less: Indexed Cost of Acquisition: ₹78,83,212
*   Less: Indexed Cost of Improvement: ₹7,08,661
*   **LTCG = ₹34,08,127**

**5. Tax on LTCG without exemption:**
*   20% of ₹34,08,127 = ₹6,81,625 (plus cess)

**6. Suresh's Reinvestment Plan:**
Suresh identifies a new apartment for ₹45 lakhs. He plans to purchase it by August 2026, well within the two-year window from the sale date (October 2025).

**7. Tax Exemption under Section 54:**
Since his capital gain is ₹34,08,127 and he is investing ₹45,00,000 (which is more than the capital gain) in a new residential property, the entire LTCG of ₹34,08,127 will be exempt from tax under Section 54.

**8. Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS) Requirement:**
Suresh sold the property in FY 2025-26. The due date for filing his ITR for this financial year is 31st July 2026. If he hasn't purchased the new apartment by this date, he must deposit ₹34,08,127 (the capital gain amount) into a CGAS account before 31st July 2026. He can then withdraw this amount to purchase the new apartment by October 2027 (within two years of sale). If he fails to do so, the exemption will be denied, and the ₹6,81,625 tax liability will arise.

By properly planning and utilising Section 54 and CGAS, Suresh can save over ₹6.81 lakhs in taxes.

## FAQ

### What is the difference between Short-Term and Long-Term Capital Gains on property?
Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) arise if you sell a property within 24 months of purchase, and are added to your income and taxed at your slab rates. Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) arise if you sell after 24 months, and are taxed at 20% with indexation benefits.

### Can I buy multiple properties to claim Section 54 exemption?
Until recently, you could reinvest in two residential properties if the capital gains did not exceed ₹2 crores. However, for sales made on or after April 1, 2023, the exemption under Section 54 is limited to investing in *one* residential house property, with a maximum reinvestment cap of ₹10 crores.

### What if I don't use the money in the Capital Gains Account Scheme (CGAS)?
Any amount deposited in CGAS that is not utilised for the purchase or construction of a new residential property within the specified time limits (2 or 3 years from the date of sale) will become taxable as LTCG in the financial year in which the time limit expires.

### Does Section 54 apply to Non-Resident Indians (NRIs)?
Yes, Section 54 exemption is available to NRIs as well, provided they meet all the conditions, including reinvesting the capital gains in a residential property located in India.

### Can I claim Section 54 exemption if I already own multiple properties?
Yes, there is no restriction on the number of properties you already own to claim Section 54 exemption. The condition is that you must reinvest the capital gains from the sale of one residential property into purchasing or constructing *one* new residential property in India within the specified timeframes.

### What documents are needed to open a CGAS account?
Typically, you will need a copy of your PAN card, address proof, the acknowledgement of your Income Tax Return, and a declaration stating the purpose of the deposit and the amount of capital gains.
