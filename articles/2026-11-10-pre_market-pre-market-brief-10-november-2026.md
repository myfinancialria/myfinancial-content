# Pre-Market Brief — 10 November 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets ended strongly positive overnight, with US tech leading the rally and Asian markets following suit this morning.
*   GIFT Nifty indicates a positive open for the Indian markets, pointing to a potential gap-up.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) have been aggressive net buyers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) have been consistent net sellers.
*   A key development is the RBI's query to banks regarding bond derivative valuations, which could impact the banking sector.
*   Nifty 50's 25000 level is crucial, acting as both a psychological and an option-chain support/resistance point.
*   The day's main risk factor will be the US inflation data release later today, which could influence global central bank actions.

This broad-based positive sentiment from major global economies is likely to provide a strong tailwind for the Indian markets at open.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The **USD/INR** pair is trading at 93.92, up marginally by 0.02%. The **Dollar Index** also edged up slightly to 101.71 (+0.05%).
**Brent Crude** futures saw a significant drop of 2.82% to $89.96 per barrel, while **WTI Crude** fell 2.60% to $84.59. This decline in oil prices was attributed to President Trump downplaying the prospect of escalation with Iran, easing supply concerns.
**Gold** is trading at $4091.70, down 0.55%, and **Silver** is at $52.52, down 0.08%, as investors await US inflation data.
The **US 10-Year Yield** rose slightly to 4.86% (+0.10%).

**Interpretation:**
*   **USD/INR & Dollar Index:** A slight uptick in both indicates minor pressure on Indian importers and a marginal tailwind for IT and pharma exporters. A stronger dollar generally means some caution for emerging market currencies.
*   **Brent/WTI Crude:** The notable fall in crude oil prices is a significant positive for India, a major oil importer. This could ease inflationary pressures, benefit oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint manufacturers, and aviation stocks due to lower input costs.
*   **Gold/Silver:** The dip in precious metals suggests investors are holding back ahead of crucial US inflation data, which could dictate the Federal Reserve's future policy stance.
*   **US 10Y Yield:** A slight increase in US yields could make US bonds more attractive, potentially diverting some FII flows away from emerging markets like India, though the impact is currently minor.

## Stock & sector news that matters

*   **RBI said to query bank valuations of some bond derivative trades**
    *   **Summary:** India's central bank has reportedly asked some lenders to adjust their accounting practices for bond forward trades, a change that could impact billions of dollars in assets across the banking sector.
    *   **Why it matters:** This news is crucial for the Indian banking sector. Any adjustments to bond valuation practices could lead to mark-to-market losses or require changes in balance sheet reporting, potentially affecting the profitability and valuations of banks. Investors will closely watch how banks react and what further clarity emerges from the RBI.

*   **Oil falls as Trump downplays prospect of escalation with Iran**
    *   **Summary:** Global oil prices declined significantly after President Trump's statement eased concerns about US military action against Iran, reducing supply worries.
    *   **Why it matters:** Lower crude oil prices are a direct positive for the Indian economy, as India is a net importer of oil. This can benefit sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), aviation, and paint manufacturers by reducing their input costs and potentially improving profit margins. It also helps in managing inflation.

*   **FPIs return to India stock markets with ₹53.7k cr inflows in Nov so far**
    *   **Summary:** After being net sellers for much of 2026, Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) have made a strong comeback in November, with inflows of ₹53,700 crore into Indian equities, driven by India's appeal amid global AI developments.
    *   **Why it matters:** Sustained FPI inflows are a major catalyst for market rallies and provide liquidity. This indicates renewed foreign investor confidence in the Indian growth story, which is a significant positive for the broader market sentiment and could support further upside.

*   **Tata Steel’s domestic business shines in Q2; Netherlands uncertainty poses risk**
    *   **Summary:** Tata Steel reported strong performance in its domestic business for Q2, though its overseas operations, particularly in the Netherlands, faced subdued volumes despite higher realizations.
    *   **Why it matters:** This news highlights the resilience of the domestic steel demand. While the strong domestic performance is positive for Tata Steel and potentially other steel players, the uncertainty surrounding overseas operations could be a factor to watch for the company's overall outlook.

*   **MRF dividend 2026: India's most expensive ₹1.25 lakh stock but ₹3 in Q2**
    *   **Summary:** MRF has declared an interim dividend of ₹3 per share for FY27, with November 17, 2026, as the record date. This comes despite a 28% fall in quarterly net profit.
    *   **Why it matters:** This is specific company news. While the dividend might be small relative to the share price, it indicates the company's commitment to shareholder returns. Investors in MRF will note the ex-date and payment schedule.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

*   **US Inflation Data (later today):** The release of US inflation data is a key global event today. Markets will be closely watching for clues on the Federal Reserve's future monetary policy decisions.
    *   **Typical Reaction:** Higher-than-expected inflation data typically leads to concerns about aggressive rate hikes, which can cause global equity markets to correct and bond yields to rise. Lower-than-expected inflation can be seen as positive, potentially easing rate hike pressures and boosting market sentiment. Volatility is common around such releases.

*   **Indian Stock Market Holiday (November 24, 2026):** Indian stock markets (NSE and BSE) will be closed on November 24, 2026, for Guru Nanak Jayanti.
    *   **Typical Reaction:** Market holidays generally lead to reduced trading activity and volume in the sessions immediately preceding and following the holiday. Traders might square off positions or reduce exposure ahead of a long weekend.

## What to watch in today's session
1.  **Global Cues:** The strong positive close in US markets and the rally in Asian equities will set a positive tone for the Indian open.
2.  **Crude Oil Prices:** The significant drop in Brent and WTI crude will be a key positive factor, potentially benefiting oil-sensitive sectors.
3.  **FII Flows:** The continued aggressive buying by FIIs is a crucial support for the market and will be closely monitored for sustained momentum.
4.  **RBI's Query on Bank Valuations:** Developments around the RBI's query to banks on bond derivative accounting practices could impact the banking sector's sentiment and performance.
5.  **Key Nifty Levels:** Watch the 25000 level closely, as it acts as a strong psychological and option-chain support/resistance point. A sustained move above or below this level could dictate intraday direction.
6.  **US Inflation Data:** While released after Indian market hours, anticipation of this data could lead to some caution or position adjustments during the latter half of the trading day.

## Bottom line
Indian markets are poised for a positive start today, largely driven by strong global cues and a significant drop in crude oil prices. The 25000 mark on Nifty will be a critical level to watch, with sustained FII buying providing a strong underlying support. However, investors should remain cautious about the RBI's query on bank bond valuations and the upcoming US inflation data, which could introduce volatility.
