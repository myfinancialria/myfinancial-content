# NPS & Alternatives: Boosting Returns with AIFs, REITs, InvITs

**TL;DR**
- The National Pension System (NPS) is exploring increased allocation to alternative investments like AIFs, REITs, and InvITs.
- These instruments offer a pathway to diversify retirement portfolios beyond traditional equity and debt options.
- Investing in AIFs, REITs, and InvITs can potentially lead to higher long-term returns and better inflation protection.
- However, these alternative assets carry inherent risks such as illiquidity and market-specific volatility.
- The Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA) currently allows a limited allocation to these assets, which may be reviewed for expansion.

## What this means in plain terms

The National Pension System (NPS) is a popular retirement savings scheme designed to help individuals build a corpus for their post-working years. Traditionally, NPS investments are primarily directed towards a mix of equities, corporate bonds, and government securities. However, to potentially enhance returns and offer better diversification, there's a growing discussion around allowing pension funds to increase their exposure to 'alternative investments'.

These alternative investments include Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs), Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs), and Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs). Simply put, AIFs are professionally managed funds that invest in a variety of non-traditional assets, including private equity, venture capital, and distressed assets. REITs allow you to invest in a portfolio of income-generating real estate properties, while InvITs enable investment in infrastructure projects like roads, pipelines, and power transmission lines. By incorporating these into NPS, subscribers could gain access to asset classes that behave differently from stocks and bonds, potentially leading to more stable and higher returns over the long term, albeit with different risk profiles.

## Understanding NPS and its Investment Landscape

The National Pension System (NPS) is a voluntary, defined contribution retirement scheme administered by the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA). It offers subscribers a choice of two account types: Tier I (a mandatory, long-term retirement savings account with tax benefits) and Tier II (a voluntary savings account with no tax benefits but greater withdrawal flexibility).

Subscribers can choose between an 'Active Choice' where they decide the asset allocation across different asset classes, or an 'Auto Choice' where the allocation is determined automatically based on their age. The primary asset classes available for investment under NPS are:
*   **Asset Class E (Equity):** Primarily invests in equity market instruments.
*   **Asset Class C (Corporate Bonds):** Invests in corporate debt instruments.
*   **Asset Class G (Government Securities):** Invests in central and state government bonds.
*   **Asset Class A (Alternative Assets):** This class is designed for alternative investments, currently capped at a maximum of 5% of the total corpus.

Pension Funds, regulated by PFRDA, manage these investments according to prescribed guidelines. The current framework allows for a limited exposure to alternative assets, and the discussion revolves around the potential to increase this allocation to tap into newer avenues for growth and diversification.

## The Promise of AIFs, REITs, and InvITs in NPS

Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs), Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs), and Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs) represent a class of investment vehicles that offer unique opportunities for diversification and potential returns.

### What are AIFs, REITs, and InvITs?
*   **Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs):** These are privately pooled investment vehicles which collect funds from sophisticated investors, whether Indian or foreign, for investing in accordance with a defined investment policy. AIFs typically invest in assets like private equity, venture capital, distressed assets, or hedge funds, which are not traditionally accessible to retail investors. They are regulated by SEBI.
*   **Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs):** A REIT is a company that owns, operates, or finances income-generating real estate. Similar to mutual funds, REITs allow individuals to invest in large-scale, income-producing real estate without actually buying, managing, or financing property themselves. They typically distribute a significant portion of their income to unitholders as dividends. REITs are listed on stock exchanges and regulated by SEBI.
*   **Infrastructure Investment Trusts (InvITs):** An InvIT is a collective investment scheme similar to a mutual fund, which enables direct investment of money from individual and institutional investors in infrastructure projects. These projects can include roads, power transmission lines, pipelines, and other income-generating infrastructure assets. Like REITs, InvITs are also listed on exchanges and are regulated by SEBI.

### Why are they attractive for NPS?
The inclusion, or increased allocation, of these instruments in NPS portfolios is attractive for several reasons:
*   **Diversification:** AIFs, REITs, and InvITs often have a low correlation with traditional asset classes like equity and debt. This means they can perform differently during various market cycles, potentially reducing overall portfolio volatility.
*   **Potential for Higher Returns:** Historically, some alternative assets have offered superior risk-adjusted returns compared to traditional investments, especially over longer horizons.
*   **Inflation Hedging:** Real estate and infrastructure assets, which form the core of REITs and InvITs, often provide a natural hedge against inflation as rental incomes and project tolls tend to rise with inflation.
*   **Income Generation:** REITs and InvITs are mandated to distribute a significant portion of their earnings as dividends or interest, providing a steady income stream, which can be beneficial for a retirement corpus.

## Potential Benefits and Associated Risks

While the prospect of including AIFs, REITs, and InvITs in NPS portfolios offers exciting possibilities, it's crucial to understand both the benefits and the inherent risks.

### Potential Benefits
*   **Enhanced Returns:** By accessing growth opportunities in private markets, real estate, and infrastructure, NPS subscribers could potentially achieve higher compounded returns on their retirement savings over the long term.
*   **Portfolio Diversification:** These assets can offer a valuable diversification tool. Their performance is often driven by different economic factors than those affecting public equities or bonds, thereby reducing overall portfolio risk.
*   **Inflation Protection:** Assets like real estate and infrastructure have shown a tendency to perform well during inflationary periods, offering a potential hedge against the erosion of purchasing power for retirement savings.
*   **Access to New Growth Sectors:** Investing in InvITs, for instance, provides exposure to India's burgeoning infrastructure development, a sector with significant long-term growth potential.

### Associated Risks
*   **Illiquidity:** AIFs, by their very nature, are often illiquid investments with long lock-in periods. While REITs and InvITs are listed, their trading volumes might not always be as high as large-cap equities, potentially making it harder to exit positions quickly without impacting prices.
*   **Market Volatility:** While offering diversification, these assets are not immune to market fluctuations. Real estate and infrastructure sectors can be sensitive to economic downturns, interest rate changes, and regulatory shifts.
*   **Valuation Challenges:** Valuing private assets within AIFs can be complex and less transparent than valuing publicly traded stocks.
*   **Regulatory Changes:** The regulatory landscape for alternative investments is still evolving, and changes could impact their performance or the ability of pension funds to invest in them.
*   **Concentration Risk:** If a significant portion of the portfolio is allocated to a few specific alternative assets, it could lead to concentration risk if those assets underperform.

PFRDA currently allows pension funds to invest up to 5% of their corpus in 'Alternative Assets' (Scheme A). Any expansion of this limit would be carefully considered, balancing the potential for higher returns with the need to protect subscribers' retirement savings.

## Regulatory Framework and Future Outlook

The Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA) is the governing body for NPS and plays a critical role in defining the investment guidelines for pension funds. Under the current regulations, pension funds are permitted to invest a limited portion of their assets in 'Alternative Assets' (Asset Class A). This limit is currently capped at 5% of the total corpus for subscribers opting for the 'Active Choice'.

The PFRDA's approach is typically cautious, prioritising the safety and long-term growth of subscribers' retirement savings. Any decision to increase the allocation to AIFs, REITs, or InvITs would involve thorough deliberation, considering market conditions, liquidity aspects, risk management frameworks, and the overall benefit to subscribers. Such a move would likely be preceded by detailed consultations with pension funds, market participants, and other stakeholders.

The potential expansion of this limit reflects a broader trend in global pension management towards incorporating alternative assets to enhance portfolio efficiency and achieve better risk-adjusted returns. For NPS subscribers, this could mean access to a more diversified and potentially higher-performing retirement portfolio in the future, aligning with the evolving investment landscape in India.

## A real example

Let's consider **Meera, 35, ₹25L CTC, Mumbai**, who has been investing in NPS for 5 years. Her current NPS corpus is ₹15 lakhs. She uses the 'Active Choice' with an aggressive allocation: 75% Equity (E), 20% Corporate Bonds (C), and 5% Government Securities (G). Let's assume her current portfolio generates an average annual return of 10%.

Now, imagine PFRDA increases the 'Alternative Assets' (A) limit to 15%, and Meera decides to reallocate her portfolio: 65% Equity, 15% Corporate Bonds, 5% Government Securities, and 15% Alternative Assets (AIFs, REITs, InvITs).

Let's assume:
*   Her existing portfolio (75% E, 20% C, 5% G) yields 10% annually.
*   Her new portfolio (65% E, 15% C, 5% G, 15% A) yields 11.5% annually, thanks to the potentially higher returns from alternative assets.
*   She continues to contribute ₹1.5 lakhs annually to NPS.

**Scenario 1: Current Allocation (No significant Alternative Assets)**
*   Current corpus: ₹15,00,000
*   Annual contribution: ₹1,50,000
*   Assumed annual return: 10%
*   Expected corpus at age 60 (25 years later): Approximately ₹2.49 crores

**Scenario 2: Increased Allocation to Alternative Assets**
*   Current corpus: ₹15,00,000
*   Annual contribution: ₹1,50,000
*   Assumed annual return: 11.5% (due to higher allocation to alternatives)
*   Expected corpus at age 60 (25 years later): Approximately ₹3.23 crores

By strategically allocating a portion of her NPS corpus to alternative assets, Meera could potentially see her retirement corpus grow by an additional ₹74 lakhs (₹3.23 crores - ₹2.49 crores) over 25 years, demonstrating the significant impact of even a moderate increase in returns compounded over a long period. This example highlights the potential for wealth creation through diversification into these asset classes, assuming the alternative assets indeed deliver higher returns.

## FAQ

### What are AIFs, REITs, and InvITs?
AIFs (Alternative Investment Funds) are privately pooled funds investing in diverse assets like private equity or venture capital. REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) allow investment in income-generating real estate. InvITs (Infrastructure Investment Trusts) enable investment in infrastructure projects like roads and power lines.

### Are these investments currently available in NPS?
Yes, NPS currently allows a limited allocation (up to 5% for 'Active Choice' subscribers) to 'Alternative Assets' under Asset Class A. The ongoing discussion is about potentially increasing this limit.

### What are the risks involved with these alternative investments?
Key risks include illiquidity (difficulty in quickly selling units), market volatility specific to real estate or infrastructure sectors, and complexities in valuation compared to publicly traded assets.

### How do these differ from traditional mutual funds?
While both are pooled investment vehicles, AIFs typically invest in less traditional, often private assets and cater to sophisticated investors. REITs and InvITs specifically focus on real estate and infrastructure assets, respectively, and are structured to distribute income regularly.

### Can NRIs invest in these through NPS?
Yes, Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are eligible to subscribe to NPS, subject to PFRDA regulations. Any investment options available to resident Indians in NPS would generally also be available to NRIs.

### What is the maximum allocation to Alternative Assets in NPS?
Currently, for subscribers opting for 'Active Choice', the maximum allocation permitted to 'Alternative Assets' (Scheme A) is 5% of the total NPS corpus.
