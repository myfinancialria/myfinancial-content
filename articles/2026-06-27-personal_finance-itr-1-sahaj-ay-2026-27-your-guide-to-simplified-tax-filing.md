# ITR-1 (Sahaj) AY 2026-27: Your Guide to Simplified Tax Filing

**TL;DR**
- ITR-1 (Sahaj) is the simplest income tax return form, designed for resident individuals with a total income up to ₹50 lakhs.
- It covers income primarily from salary, one house property, and other sources like interest and family pension.
- If you have income from more than one house property or any capital gains, you are generally not eligible to file ITR-1.
- The online filing process for ITR-1 is user-friendly, with pre-filled data available on the Income Tax e-filing portal.
- Ensure all your income details and deductions are accurately reported to avoid future discrepancies.

## What this means in plain terms

For many salaried individuals and pensioners in India, filing income tax returns can seem daunting. However, the Income Tax Department offers a simplified form called ITR-1, also known as Sahaj, which translates to "easy." This form is specifically designed for a large segment of taxpayers whose financial affairs are relatively straightforward.

Essentially, if your primary income comes from your job or pension, and you might also earn some interest from fixed deposits or rent from a single property you own, ITR-1 is likely the form for you. It streamlines the reporting process, allowing you to declare your income, claim eligible deductions, and pay your taxes without navigating complex schedules. Understanding its eligibility criteria is the first step towards a smooth tax filing experience for Assessment Year 2026-27.

## Eligibility Criteria for ITR-1 (Sahaj)

ITR-1 (Sahaj) is specifically tailored for resident individuals whose total income does not exceed ₹50 lakhs during the financial year. This form simplifies the tax filing process for a significant portion of the Indian taxpayer base.

To be eligible for ITR-1, your income sources must fit within certain categories:
*   **Income from Salary or Pension:** This is the most common source of income for ITR-1 filers. It includes your basic salary, allowances, perquisites, and any pension received.
*   **Income from One House Property:** You can declare income from a single house property, which can be self-occupied, let-out, or deemed to be let-out. If you own more than one house property from which you derive income, or if you have carried forward losses from house property, you will not be eligible for ITR-1.
*   **Income from Other Sources:** This category typically includes interest income from savings accounts, fixed deposits, recurring deposits, family pension, and dividend income.
*   **Agricultural Income:** If your agricultural income is up to ₹5,000, you can still file ITR-1. Beyond this limit, you would need to file a different ITR form.

**Who cannot file ITR-1 (Sahaj)?**

It's equally important to understand who is *not* eligible to file ITR-1, as using the wrong form can lead to your return being deemed defective. You cannot file ITR-1 if you are:
*   A Non-Resident (NR) or a Resident Not Ordinarily Resident (RNOR).
*   An individual with a total income exceeding ₹50 lakhs.
*   An individual with income from more than one house property.
*   An individual with income from capital gains (e.g., from selling shares, mutual funds, or property).
*   An individual with income from business or profession.
*   A Director in a company.
*   An individual who has held unlisted equity shares at any time during the financial year.
*   An individual with income from foreign assets or foreign sources.
*   An individual claiming relief for foreign tax paid.

## Navigating Key Disqualifications for ITR-1 (AY 2026-27)

The simplicity of ITR-1 comes with specific limitations regarding the types and sources of income that can be reported. For Assessment Year 2026-27, it's crucial to understand these disqualifications clearly, especially concerning property income and capital gains.

### The Two-House Property Rule and ITR-1 Eligibility

A common point of confusion arises when an individual owns more than one house property. For ITR-1 eligibility, the rule is clear: you can only declare income from **one house property**. This means if you own, for instance, a self-occupied home and a second property that is let out or even deemed to be let out (as per income tax rules if it's not self-occupied), you become ineligible for ITR-1. In such scenarios, you would typically need to file **ITR-2**. This form allows for the reporting of income from multiple house properties, including the calculation of net annual value and deductions.

### Capital Gains Income: A Strict Disqualification

Another significant disqualifier for ITR-1 is the presence of **any capital gains income**. Capital gains arise from the sale of capital assets, such as:
*   **Equity Shares or Mutual Funds:** Both long-term and short-term capital gains from these investments.
*   **Property:** Gains from the sale of residential or commercial property.
*   **Gold, Jewellery, or Other Assets:** Gains from the sale of personal assets that qualify as capital assets.

Even a small amount of capital gains income, regardless of whether it's taxable or exempt, immediately disqualifies you from using ITR-1. If you have realised any capital gains during the financial year, you must opt for **ITR-2** or **ITR-3**, depending on whether you also have business or professional income. This is a strict rule, and there is no "capital gains limit" that allows you to file ITR-1; any such income necessitates a different form.

## Step-by-Step Online Filing of ITR-1 (Sahaj)

Filing ITR-1 online is a streamlined process facilitated by the Income Tax Department's e-filing portal. For Assessment Year 2026-27, the steps remain largely consistent, focusing on verification and accurate data entry.

### Pre-requisites for Filing
Before you begin, ensure you have the following documents and information ready:
*   **PAN and Aadhaar number.**
*   **Form 16** from your employer(s).
*   **Bank statements** for interest income.
*   **Investment proofs** for deductions under Section 80C, 80D, etc.
*   **Rent receipts** (if you are a tenant and claiming HRA) or details of rental income from your property.
*   **Form 26AS, Annual Information Statement (AIS), and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS)** to reconcile your income and tax deducted at source (TDS) details. These can be accessed from the e-filing portal.

### The Online Filing Process
1.  **Log in to the e-filing portal:** Visit the official Income Tax Department website (incometax.gov.in) and log in using your PAN/Aadhaar and password. If you are a new user, you will need to register first.
2.  **Select Assessment Year and ITR Form:** On your dashboard, click on "e-File" -> "Income Tax Returns" -> "File Income Tax Return." Select the Assessment Year 2026-27. Choose "Online" as the mode of filing and then select "ITR-1."
3.  **Choose Filing Reason:** Indicate why you are filing the return (e.g., "Taxable income is more than basic exemption limit").
4.  **Validate Pre-filled Data:** The portal will often pre-fill many details from your Form 26AS, AIS, and TIS, including personal information, salary details, and TDS. Carefully review all these pre-filled sections. Make any necessary corrections or additions. It is crucial to match these details with your Form 16 and other records.
5.  **Enter Income Details:**
    *   **Salary/Pension Income:** Verify or enter details from your Form 16.
    *   **House Property Income:** If applicable, enter details of your one house property, including gross rent received (if let out), municipal taxes paid, and interest on home loan (if any). The portal will automatically calculate the standard deduction and net annual value.
    *   **Income from Other Sources:** Declare all other income such as interest from savings accounts, fixed deposits, and dividends.
6.  **Claim Deductions:** Enter details of all eligible deductions under Chapter VI-A, such as:
    *   Section 80C (LIC, PPF, EPF, ELSS, home loan principal, etc.)
    *   Section 80D (Health insurance premium)
    *   Section 80E (Interest on education loan)
    *   Section 80G (Donations)
    *   Section 80TTA/80TTB (Interest on savings account for individuals below/above 60 years)
7.  **Tax Computation and Payment:** The system will automatically compute your tax liability based on the income and deductions provided. If there is any tax payable, you can pay it online using the Challan 280 facility. If there is a refund due, it will be processed after your return is verified.
8.  **Preview and Submit:** Carefully review your entire return for any errors or omissions. Once satisfied, proceed to submit the return.
9.  **E-Verify Your Return:** This is the final and crucial step. Your ITR is not considered complete until it is e-verified. You can e-verify using Aadhaar OTP, net banking, EVC through bank account, or by sending a signed ITR-V to CPC, Bengaluru. E-verification via Aadhaar OTP or net banking is the quickest method.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 35, earning ₹22L CTC, based in Chennai**. She needs to file her ITR for Assessment Year 2026-27.

**Priya's Income Details:**
*   **Salary Income:** ₹22,00,000 (as per Form 16)
*   **Interest from Fixed Deposits:** ₹40,000
*   **Rental Income from one Let-out Property:** ₹30,000 per month (₹3,60,000 annually). She paid municipal taxes of ₹15,000.
*   **Agricultural Income:** ₹3,000 (well within the ₹5,000 limit for ITR-1)

**Priya's Deductions:**
*   **Section 80C:** ₹1,50,000 (EPF, ELSS, Life Insurance Premium)
*   **Section 80D:** ₹25,000 (Health insurance premium for self and family)
*   **Standard Deduction (Salary):** ₹50,000

**Calculation:**

1.  **Gross Salary:** ₹22,00,000
2.  **Less: Standard Deduction (Salary):** ₹50,000
    **Income from Salary:** ₹21,50,000

3.  **Income from House Property:**
    *   Gross Annual Value (GAV): ₹3,60,000
    *   Less: Municipal Taxes Paid: ₹15,000
    *   Net Annual Value (NAV): ₹3,45,000
    *   Less: Standard Deduction @ 30% of NAV: 30% of ₹3,45,000 = ₹1,03,500
    *   **Income from House Property:** ₹2,41,500

4.  **Income from Other Sources:**
    *   Interest from Fixed Deposits: ₹40,000
    *   Agricultural Income: ₹3,000
    *   **Total Income from Other Sources:** ₹43,000

5.  **Gross Total Income (GTI):**
    *   ₹21,50,000 (Salary) + ₹2,41,500 (House Property) + ₹43,000 (Other Sources) = ₹24,34,500

6.  **Less: Deductions under Chapter VI-A:**
    *   Section 80C: ₹1,50,000
    *   Section 80D: ₹25,000
    *   **Total Deductions:** ₹1,75,000

7.  **Total Taxable Income:**
    *   ₹24,34,500 (GTI) - ₹1,75,000 (Deductions) = **₹22,59,500**

Priya is eligible to file ITR-1 (Sahaj) because her total income is less than ₹50 lakhs, she has income from only one house property, and no capital gains or business income. She would then calculate her tax liability based on the applicable income tax slabs for AY 2026-27 on her taxable income of ₹22,59,500.

## What to do this week

To prepare for a smooth ITR-1 filing for Assessment Year 2026-27, consider these actionable steps:

1.  **Gather Your Documents:** Collect your Form 16 from your employer, bank statements showing interest income, and any other income proofs. Also, compile all investment proofs for deductions under Sections like 80C and 80D.
2.  **Verify Pre-filled Data Sources:** Log in to the Income Tax e-filing portal (incometax.gov.in) and download your Form 26AS, Annual Information Statement (AIS), and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS). Cross-verify all entries against your personal records to ensure accuracy and identify any discrepancies.
3.  **Confirm ITR-1 Eligibility:** Based on your income sources and total income, re-confirm that you meet all the eligibility criteria for ITR-1 (Sahaj). Ensure you do not have income from more than one house property, capital gains, or other disqualifying sources.
4.  **Set Up E-Verification Method:** If you haven't already, ensure your Aadhaar is linked to your PAN, and your mobile number is updated with both. This will allow for quick e-verification using Aadhaar OTP, which is the easiest way to complete your filing.

## FAQ

### Who is eligible to file ITR-1 (Sahaj)?
ITR-1 (Sahaj) is for resident individuals with a total income up to ₹50 lakhs, whose income sources include salary/pension, income from one house property, and income from other sources (like interest and family pension). Agricultural income up to ₹5,000 is also permitted.

### Can ITR-1 be filed if I have income from two house properties?
No, if you have income from more than one house property, you are not eligible to file ITR-1. You would need to file ITR-2 instead, which accommodates income from multiple house properties.

### What if I have capital gains income? Can I use ITR-1?
No, any income from capital gains, whether short-term or long-term, disqualifies you from filing ITR-1. Individuals with capital gains must file either ITR-2 or ITR-3, depending on their other income sources.

### Is it mandatory to file ITR-1 online?
While you have the option to file ITR-1 offline (by generating an XML file and uploading it), the online mode through the Income Tax e-filing portal is generally recommended for its ease of use and pre-filled data features.

### What documents do I need to file ITR-1?
You will need your PAN, Aadhaar, Form 16, bank statements for interest income, investment proofs for deductions (e.g., 80C, 80D), and details of any other income. It's also advisable to check Form 26AS, AIS, and TIS.

### What is the income limit for filing ITR-1?
The total income limit for filing ITR-1 (Sahaj) is ₹50 lakhs for the Assessment Year 2026-27. If your total income exceeds this threshold, you will need to file a different ITR form, typically ITR-2.
