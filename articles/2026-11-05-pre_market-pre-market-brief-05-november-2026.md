# Pre-Market Brief — 05 November 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets closed with a positive bias overnight, led by strong gains in US indices and Hang Seng, while Nikkei dipped.
*   GIFT Nifty is signalling a significant gap-up for the Indian markets, indicating a strong positive open.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were aggressive net buyers yesterday, continuing their positive trend over the last few sessions, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers.
*   The outcome of US midterm elections, leading to a decline in crude oil prices and a rally in Indian bonds, is a key positive driver.
*   While specific Nifty technical levels are unavailable, GIFT Nifty's strong positive lead suggests a bullish start.
*   The day's main risk factors include the sustainability of global crude price stability and the consistency of FII inflows.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Global markets displayed a largely positive sentiment overnight. US indices — the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 — all closed significantly higher, reflecting optimism. European markets also ended in the green. In Asia this morning, while Japan's Nikkei 225 saw a dip, Hong Kong's Hang Seng surged, and China's Shanghai Composite posted modest gains. This broad positive global tone, especially from the US, sets a favourable backdrop for the Indian market open.

**Interpretation:** The strong positive close in major US and European markets, coupled with a significant jump in the Hang Seng, indicates a risk-on sentiment globally. This positive momentum is likely to provide a strong tailwind for the Indian equity markets at open today.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) strengthened slightly against the US Dollar, closing at 94.50 with a 0.26% gain. The Dollar Index saw a minor dip of 0.05% to 101.46. Crude oil prices showed an upward movement, with Brent Crude rising 1.07% to $95.63 and WTI Crude up 0.25% to $88.34. Gold prices surged over 1.63% to $4139.40, while Silver also gained 1.36% to $52.83. The US 10-Year Yield declined by 1.17% to 4.89%.

**Interpretation:**
*   **USD/INR:** A strengthening Rupee is generally positive for the economy, potentially easing import costs and reducing inflationary pressures. It might be a slight headwind for export-oriented sectors like IT and Pharma, but overall, it signals stability.
*   **Dollar Index:** A marginally weaker Dollar Index can support emerging market currencies, including the Rupee.
*   **Crude Oil (Brent/WTI):** The rise in crude oil prices, despite recent declines, is a point of caution for India, a major oil importer. Sustained higher crude prices could put pressure on Oil Marketing Companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors, and potentially reignite inflation concerns.
*   **Gold/Silver:** The significant jump in precious metal prices suggests some safe-haven buying or inflation hedging, which could reflect underlying global economic uncertainties despite the equity rally.
*   **US 10Y Yield:** A decline in US bond yields typically makes emerging markets, including India, more attractive for foreign investors, potentially encouraging FII inflows into Indian equities and debt.

## GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling
The GIFT Nifty is trading at 24592.50, showing a significant gain of +2.68%.

**Interpretation:** This strong positive movement in GIFT Nifty clearly indicates a substantial gap-up open for the Nifty 50 today, suggesting a very bullish start to the trading session.

## FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape
Yesterday, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers in the Indian cash equity segment, injecting a substantial ₹7,284 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), on the other hand, were net sellers, offloading equities worth ₹3,712 crore. This marks a continuation of FIIs being net buyers over the last four out of five sessions, while DIIs have largely been net sellers.

| Date         | FII Net (₹ crore) | DII Net (₹ crore) |
| :----------- | :---------------- | :---------------- |
| 2026-11-04   | +7,284            | -3,712            |

**Interpretation:** The consistent and strong buying by FIIs is a significant positive for the market, indicating renewed foreign investor confidence and liquidity inflow. While DIIs have been booking profits, the sheer volume of FII buying suggests that foreign funds are currently driving the market's upward momentum.

## Option-chain signals (OI) — where support & resistance sit
Option-chain Open Interest (OI) data for Nifty, Bank Nifty, and Sensex, including highest call-OI strikes, highest put-OI strikes, and PCR, is unavailable for today's session.

**Interpretation:** The lack of Option Chain data means we cannot identify immediate psychological support and resistance levels based on derivative positioning. This makes it harder to gauge where option sellers expect the market to find a ceiling (Call OI) or a floor (Put OI), and the overall market sentiment from the Put-Call Ratio (PCR). Traders will need to be extra vigilant and use price action and the GIFT Nifty's strong signal as primary indicators for today's session.

## Stock & sector news that matters

**EPL Q2 Results: Profit rises 4% YoY to Rs 111 crore**
EPL (formerly Essel Propack) reported a 4.52% increase in net profit to ₹110.9 crore for the September quarter of FY27, with revenues climbing 30% year-on-year to ₹1,567.4 crore.
**Why it matters:** This is positive news for EPL, indicating healthy growth in both top and bottom lines. Investors will be watching how the market reacts to these results, potentially influencing sentiment for other packaging or consumer discretionary related stocks.

**India bonds climb in step with Treasuries as oil sinks**
Indian government bonds rallied for the fourth consecutive session following a Democratic win in the US midterm elections, which led to a decline in crude oil prices and eased inflation concerns.
**Why it matters:** A rally in Indian government bonds signifies easing interest rate concerns and improved investor sentiment towards debt. The decline in crude oil is a major positive for India's macroeconomic stability, potentially reducing the import bill and inflationary pressures, which is broadly beneficial for the equity market.

**Qualcomm Earnings: 5 key takeaways from Q4 results**
Qualcomm's revenue declined 8% amid weak smartphone chip demand, though automotive sales surged. Rising costs pressured margins, while AI data centres offer diversification and future growth opportunities.
**Why it matters:** While a US-based company, Qualcomm's performance offers insights into the global semiconductor and smartphone market. A decline in smartphone chip demand could have implications for Indian companies in the electronics manufacturing and consumer durables space, while the focus on AI data centres highlights a broader tech trend.

**Alphabet stock jumps 5%: Why Google Parent shares rallied**
Alphabet shares rallied nearly 5% amid shifting US regulatory expectations, strong Google Cloud growth, and investor focus on massive AI infrastructure spending.
**Why it matters:** Alphabet's strong performance, driven by Google Cloud and AI investments, reflects a positive sentiment in the global tech sector. This could spill over to Indian IT services companies, especially those with exposure to cloud computing and AI projects, potentially boosting their outlook.

**Bharat Electronics among 5 stocks showing White Marubozu pattern**
Bharat Electronics, Adani Enterprises, Titan Company, Jio Financial Services, and other Nifty 50 stocks featured in the White Marubozu bullish scanner on November 4, indicating strong buying interest.
**Why it matters:** A White Marubozu is a strong bullish candlestick pattern. This technical signal suggests significant positive momentum and buying conviction in these specific stocks, including a defence sector major (BEL) and a large conglomerate (Adani Enterprises), which could see continued upward movement today.

**Oil cools, FPIs return: Is Dalal Street ready to run?**
Falling crude prices and hopes of easing tensions in West Asia are improving the outlook for Indian equities, but a resilient AI investment cycle could limit foreign fund inflows.
**Why it matters:** This news highlights two critical factors for the Indian market: the positive impact of cooling crude prices and the return of FIIs. These are strong tailwinds. However, the mention of the AI investment cycle potentially diverting global funds suggests that FII inflows, while currently strong, need to be monitored for sustainability.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
*   **Global Crude Oil Prices:** The movement in Brent and WTI crude oil prices will be closely watched. Historically, a sustained rise in crude prices tends to be a negative for Indian markets due to import dependency, while a decline is generally positive, easing inflation and current account deficit concerns.
*   **FII Flow Sustainability:** The strong FII buying trend observed over the past few sessions will be a key monitorable. Markets typically react positively to consistent FII inflows, providing liquidity and confidence, while any reversal can lead to profit booking and volatility.
*   **US Economic Data/Central Bank Commentary (if any):** While no specific US data is listed for today, any global economic releases or commentary from major central banks (like the US Fed) can influence global risk sentiment and, by extension, FII flows into India. Markets tend to react with volatility around such announcements.

## What to watch in today's session
1.  **GIFT Nifty's strong positive open:** Observe if the Nifty 50 opens with a significant gap-up as indicated and how it sustains the initial momentum.
2.  **FII vs DII activity:** Monitor the FII and DII cash market activity for any shift in their stance, especially after yesterday's aggressive FII buying and DII selling.
3.  **Crude Oil Price Action:** Keep an eye on global crude oil prices. Any sharp reversal upwards could dampen sentiment, while continued stability or decline would be supportive.
4.  **Corporate Actions Impact:** Watch the price action of companies going ex-dividend (e.g., SUNTV, SANOFI) and the stock split (GNRL) to see how they adjust and trade.
5.  **Sectoral Performance:** Observe sectors that could be impacted by crude oil prices (OMCs, aviation, paints) and those benefiting from positive global tech sentiment (IT).
6.  **Specific Stock Momentum:** Stocks like Bharat Electronics and Adani Enterprises, which showed strong technical signals yesterday, will be in focus for continued momentum.

## Bottom line
The Indian market is poised for a strong positive open today, largely driven by favourable global cues, particularly from the US, and a significant gap-up signal from GIFT Nifty. The consistent and robust buying by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) is a key supportive factor. While the decline in US yields and cooling crude prices are positive, the sustainability of these trends and FII inflows will be crucial. Traders should watch the initial momentum closely, especially given the unavailability of specific technical and option chain levels for guidance.
