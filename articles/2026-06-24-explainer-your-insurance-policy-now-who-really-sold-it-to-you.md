# Your Insurance Policy: Now, Who Really Sold It To You?

**TL;DR**
- India's insurance regulator, IRDAI, is proposing big changes to how insurance is sold.
- Every insurance policy might soon be digitally linked to the specific salesperson who sold it.
- This aims to make agents more accountable and protect you from mis-selling.
- Intermediaries like banks and agents will face stricter rules and much higher penalties for violations.
- These changes follow a new law, the "Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025."

## What's this about?
The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) has proposed a major revamp of insurance regulations. These new proposals aim to bring more transparency and accountability to how insurance policies are sold across the country. The core idea is to protect you, the customer, better and ensure that insurance agents and companies are more responsible.

## Why it's in the news right now
This is fresh news! IRDAI released these proposals, including three exposure drafts and three consultation papers, in June 2026. These changes come after the "Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha (Amendment of Insurance Laws) Act, 2025" was enacted, which is a big step towards "Insurance for All by 2047." So, the regulator is now updating the specific rules to match this new law and vision.

## How it actually works
The proposed changes focus on several key areas to make the insurance buying process clearer and safer:

1.  **Digital Tagging of Salespersons:** Every insurance policy sold will be digitally linked to the individual salesperson responsible for that sale. This includes specific persons working for corporate agents like banks.
2.  **Lifelong Registrations for Intermediaries:** Insurance agents and other intermediaries might soon have lifelong registrations, instead of needing regular renewals. This aims to provide more stability in services like claims processing. However, they would need to pay an annual fee to keep their registration active.
3.  **Stricter Rules for Corporate Agents:** Banks and other large retail networks that sell insurance (corporate agents) will now need to have at least one qualified and specified person in every branch dedicated to selling insurance.
4.  **Higher Penalties:** To ensure these rules are followed, IRDAI plans to significantly increase the maximum penalty for violations. It could go up to ₹10 crore, a sharp jump from the previous ₹1 crore.
5.  **Enhanced Consumer Protection at Point of Sale:** The proposals introduce stricter measures to safeguard consumers right when they are buying a policy. This means clearer information and processes to prevent mis-selling.

## A simple way to think about it
Imagine you're buying groceries from your local kirana store. Currently, if you have a problem with a product, it can sometimes be hard to pinpoint exactly who helped you or who is accountable. These new insurance rules are like making sure that every time you buy something, the shopkeeper or assistant helping you puts their unique stamp on the bill. If there's an issue later, you know exactly who was involved, and they know they are responsible. This makes everyone more careful and honest in their dealings.

## Why it matters to you
1.  **Reduced Mis-selling:** Knowing that a specific person is accountable for your policy means they are less likely to push unsuitable products or give incomplete information. This protects your hard-earned money.
2.  **Easier Grievance Redressal:** If you face an issue with your policy or feel you were misled, it will be clearer who to hold responsible, potentially making your complaint resolution faster and more effective.
3.  **Better Advice:** With increased accountability and stricter training requirements for agents, you can expect more knowledgeable and ethical advice when choosing an insurance product.
4.  **More Transparent Transactions:** The entire process of buying insurance, from understanding the policy to knowing who sold it, is set to become more transparent.
5.  **Financial Security:** Ultimately, these changes aim to build a more trustworthy insurance sector, helping you make better decisions for your financial security and future.

## Common confusions cleared up
*   **Myth:** All existing insurance policies will change immediately.
    *   **Fact:** These are *proposed* changes. Once finalised, they will apply to new policies or impact the conduct of agents for existing policies. The core terms of your existing policy will not change overnight.
*   **Myth:** Insurance will become more expensive because of these new rules.
    *   **Fact:** The rules focus on transparency and accountability, not directly on premium costs. While compliance costs for companies might increase, the primary goal is to improve consumer protection and market efficiency, not to raise prices.
*   **Myth:** I won't be able to buy insurance from my bank anymore.
    *   **Fact:** Banks can continue to sell insurance as corporate agents. The new rules simply mandate that they have qualified personnel in their branches for this purpose, ensuring you get proper guidance.

## FAQ
### Will I get a digital receipt with the salesperson's name on it?
The exact format is still in proposal, but the intent is to digitally link each policy to the salesperson, making their involvement traceable for accountability.

### What if my agent leaves the company?
The proposal for "lifelong registrations" (with annual fees) aims to ensure continuity of service. Even if an individual agent changes roles, the system is designed for ongoing accountability and service stability.

### Are these rules only for life insurance?
The proposals are part of a sweeping overhaul of insurance regulations, implying they will cover various types of insurance, including life, health, and general insurance, where sales practices are relevant.

### What if I bought my policy online without an agent?
These rules primarily address sales made through individual agents and corporate agents (like banks). Online direct purchases usually have different disclosure and grievance mechanisms already in place.
