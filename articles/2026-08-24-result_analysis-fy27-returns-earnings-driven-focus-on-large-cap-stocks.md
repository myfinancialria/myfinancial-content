# FY27 Returns: Earnings-Driven Focus on Large-Cap Stocks

**TL;DR**
- Kotak Institutional Equities predicts FY27 market returns will primarily be driven by corporate earnings.
- Large-cap stocks, which have lagged recently, are expected to regain investor attention.
- This shift is attributed to their relatively lower earnings growth in previous quarters.
- The market is anticipated to move towards fundamentals, valuing consistent profitability.
- Investors may need to recalibrate their portfolios, potentially favouring stability and established businesses.

## What this means in plain terms

When financial experts talk about "earnings-driven returns," they mean that the growth in stock prices will largely depend on how well companies actually perform and increase their profits, rather than just market sentiment or hype. For a while, smaller and mid-sized companies saw their stock prices rise significantly, partly because of strong investor interest. However, Kotak Institutional Equities, a prominent research firm, suggests that this trend might reverse for the financial year 2027.

According to Pratik Gupta, CEO and Co-Head of Kotak Institutional Equities, large-cap companies – India's biggest and most established firms – are now poised to attract more investor interest. These companies had seen slower earnings growth compared to their smaller counterparts. Now, the expectation is that their earnings performance will improve, making them more attractive and driving their stock prices upwards based on solid business fundamentals.

## The Shift Towards Earnings-Driven Returns for FY27

The outlook from Kotak Institutional Equities highlights a crucial pivot in market dynamics for the Financial Year 2027: a return to earnings-driven returns. This signifies a maturation of market expectations, where investor sentiment is less likely to be the primary propellant for stock prices. Instead, the focus will squarely be on the fundamental profitability and growth trajectory of companies. For investors, this means that speculative plays or companies with high valuations but weak earnings may find it challenging to sustain their upward momentum. The market, in essence, is expected to become more discerning, rewarding businesses that demonstrate robust financial performance, efficient operations, and sustainable profit growth. This emphasis on earnings underscores the importance of fundamental analysis over technical trends or broader market euphoria.

### Understanding Earnings-Driven Growth

Earnings-driven growth implies that a company's stock price appreciation is directly correlated with its net profit and revenue growth. In such an environment, companies that consistently deliver on their earnings estimates or even surpass them are likely to see their stock values rise. Conversely, those that miss expectations or show stagnant profits may face downward pressure, irrespective of their market positioning or sector. This scenario typically favours established businesses with strong competitive moats, predictable cash flows, and a proven track record of converting revenue into profits. It also suggests that valuation multiples might become more rational, with investors willing to pay a premium only for demonstrably superior earnings quality and growth prospects.

## Large Caps Poised for Renewed Investor Attention

A key takeaway from Kotak Institutional Equities' outlook is the anticipated resurgence of large-cap stocks. For several quarters, these market behemoths had taken a backseat as small- and mid-cap companies delivered superior returns, often fuelled by higher growth expectations and, at times, speculative interest. Pratik Gupta notes that this underperformance of large caps was largely due to their relatively lower earnings growth during that period. However, the narrative is now expected to change, with large caps "set to capture investor attention."

### Reasons for the Expected Rebound

Several factors could contribute to this shift. Firstly, large-cap companies often possess greater financial stability, broader market reach, and more resilient business models, making them attractive during periods of economic uncertainty or when investors seek 'safety' in quality. Secondly, their valuations might have become more reasonable after a period of underperformance, presenting a compelling entry point for institutional investors and long-term wealth creators. Thirdly, if their earnings growth indeed picks up, as anticipated, it would naturally draw capital. Furthermore, large caps tend to be more liquid, which is a significant advantage for institutional investors managing substantial funds. The sheer scale of these companies often means they are better positioned to weather economic headwinds and capitalise on broad economic recoveries.

## Management Commentary and Guidance from Kotak Institutional Equities

Pratik Gupta, Chief Executive and Co-Head of Kotak Institutional Equities, has clearly articulated the firm's perspective on the market's direction for FY27. His commentary underscores a fundamental re-evaluation of investment strategies, moving away from the broad-based rally seen in smaller segments towards a more selective, earnings-focused approach. This guidance is crucial for investors, as it signals a potential shift in the factors that will drive market performance.

### Implications of Kotak's Outlook

Kotak Institutional Equities' view suggests that investors should prioritise companies with strong, sustainable earnings power. This implies a deeper dive into financial statements, understanding business models, and assessing management quality. The firm's focus on large caps indicates a preference for established businesses that are likely to deliver consistent, albeit potentially moderate, growth. This guidance could influence how fund managers allocate capital, potentially leading to increased flows into large-cap oriented mutual funds and direct investments in such stocks. For individual investors, it serves as a reminder to not chase momentum blindly but to align investments with fundamental value and earnings potential.

## Market Context: Large Caps vs. Broader Market Segments

The anticipated shift towards large caps in FY27 needs to be understood within the broader market context. Over the past few quarters, small-cap and mid-cap segments of the Indian equity market have delivered exceptional returns, often outperforming their large-cap counterparts by a significant margin. This outperformance was driven by a combination of factors, including higher growth expectations, relatively lower starting valuations (in some cases), and robust retail investor participation. However, such periods of sustained outperformance in smaller segments often lead to stretched valuations and increased volatility.

### Contrasting Performance Trends

While small and mid-caps offered rapid growth potential, they also carried higher risks. Large caps, on the other hand, known for their stability and resilience, saw comparatively subdued returns. Kotak's outlook suggests a normalisation or even a reversal of this trend. If large-cap earnings growth accelerates, and their valuations remain attractive relative to their earnings potential, they could draw capital away from the more expensive small and mid-cap segments. This doesn't necessarily mean a crash in small and mid-caps, but rather a potential moderation of their returns and a reallocation of investor interest towards the larger, more stable companies. This rebalancing could lead to a more balanced market, where quality and earnings drive performance across the board, rather than just segment-specific momentum.

## A real example

Meet **Sanjay, 42, ₹35L CTC, Mumbai**. Sanjay has been investing in direct equities and mutual funds for the past decade. Over the last two years, he saw significant gains in his small-cap and mid-cap mutual fund holdings, which constitute about 40% of his equity portfolio, with large caps making up the remaining 60%.

Upon reading Kotak's outlook, Sanjay decides to re-evaluate his strategy.

1.  **Reviewing his portfolio:** Sanjay checks the current allocation to large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap funds. He notices that his small-cap funds have indeed seen a sharp run-up, and their Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratios look quite high compared to historical averages and even some of his large-cap holdings.
2.  **Analysing large-cap holdings:** He looks at the earnings growth trajectory of the large-cap stocks and funds he holds. He identifies a few large-cap companies that have shown consistent, albeit moderate, earnings growth but whose stock prices haven't fully reflected this due to the broader focus on smaller caps.
3.  **Considering reallocation:** Sanjay decides against a drastic overhaul but plans a tactical reallocation. He sets up a Systematic Withdrawal Plan (SWP) from one of his highest-performing small-cap funds, withdrawing ₹50,000 per month.
4.  **Investing the proceeds:** He then uses these proceeds to start a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) of ₹50,000 per month into a Nifty 50 index fund and a couple of actively managed large-cap funds that align with his long-term investment goals and have a good track record of earnings-driven growth.
5.  **Long-term perspective:** Sanjay understands that market cycles shift. By gradually increasing his exposure to large caps now, he aims to capitalise on their anticipated earnings-driven growth in FY27 while also booking some profits from his high-performing small-cap investments, thereby rebalancing his risk.

This measured approach allows Sanjay to align his portfolio with the anticipated market shift without making impulsive decisions, focusing on the underlying earnings potential of his investments.

## What to do this week

1.  **Review your equity portfolio allocation:** Check the current split between large-cap, mid-cap, and small-cap funds or direct stock holdings. Note down the percentages.
2.  **Assess recent performance and valuations:** Look at the one-year and three-year returns of your small and mid-cap investments. Compare their current valuations (e.g., P/E ratios) with historical averages and large-cap counterparts.

## FAQ

### What does "earnings-driven returns" mean for me as an investor?
It means that the growth in your investments will largely depend on the actual profits and revenues of the companies you invest in, rather than just market sentiment. Focus on companies with strong fundamentals and consistent earnings growth.

### Why are large-cap stocks expected to be in focus for FY27?
Large-cap stocks previously underperformed due to lower earnings growth. Kotak Institutional Equities suggests they are now poised for a rebound in earnings, making them more attractive to investors seeking stability and fundamental value.

### Should I sell all my small-cap and mid-cap investments immediately?
Not necessarily. A sudden, drastic move is rarely advisable. Instead, consider gradually rebalancing your portfolio. You could pause new SIPs in these segments or strategically book some profits to redeploy into large caps, aligning with your risk appetite and financial goals.

### How can I identify good large-cap investment opportunities?
Look for large-cap mutual funds (both active and passive, like index funds), or individual large-cap stocks with a strong track record of consistent earnings, robust balance sheets, and leadership positions in their respective industries. Research reports and financial news portals can be helpful.

### Where can I find reliable information on market outlooks and investment strategies?
Reputable financial news outlets, research reports from established brokerage firms like Kotak Institutional Equities, and official regulatory bodies like SEBI and AMFI India are good starting points. Always cross-reference information and consider consulting a financial professional.

### Will this shift affect my existing SIPs in diversified equity funds?
Diversified equity funds typically have a mix of large, mid, and small-cap stocks. If your fund manager also believes in this shift, they might gradually increase their large-cap allocation. However, it's wise to review your fund's portfolio composition and performance regularly.

### Is this outlook a guarantee of large-cap outperformance?
No, market outlooks are projections based on current information and analysis; they are not guarantees. Economic conditions, geopolitical events, and unexpected corporate developments can always influence market performance. It serves as a guide for informed decision-making.
