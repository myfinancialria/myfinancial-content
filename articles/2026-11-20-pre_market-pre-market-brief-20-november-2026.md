# Pre-Market Brief — 20 November 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets saw a broad-based sell-off overnight, with US and Asian indices declining sharply.
- GIFT Nifty is signalling a positive start for Indian markets, despite the weak global cues.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their strong buying spree, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers.
- A sharp appreciation of the Indian Rupee could significantly impact IT companies' earnings for FY27.
- Key Nifty technical levels for the day are unavailable, suggesting traders should watch price action closely.
- The main risk today is potential profit-booking by FIIs after consistent inflows, coupled with global market weakness.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Global markets witnessed a significant downturn overnight, with major US indices closing sharply lower. The Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all registered declines exceeding 2%, indicating a strong risk-off sentiment. Asian markets followed suit this morning, with Japan's Nikkei 225 plunging over 5% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng also seeing a notable drop. This broad weakness across global equities suggests a cautious mood among international investors.

**Interpretation:** The widespread negative sentiment from US and Asian markets typically signals a weak opening for Indian equities. However, the positive indication from GIFT Nifty suggests a potential divergence or a resilient domestic mood, which could lead to a gap-up opening despite the global headwinds.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee showed significant strength against the US Dollar, appreciating by 0.86% to 92.66. The Dollar Index also saw a marginal dip of 0.07% to 103.83. In commodities, crude oil prices experienced a notable decline, with Brent Crude falling 4.44% to $87.90 and WTI Crude down 2.56% to $81.08. Gold and Silver also saw sharp corrections, dropping over 3% and 4% respectively. US 10-year Treasury yields eased slightly by 0.28% to 5.05%.

**Interpretation:**
- **USD/INR:** The sharp appreciation of the Rupee (USD/INR down) is a positive for importers as their costs decrease, but it could be a headwind for export-oriented sectors like IT and pharmaceuticals, potentially impacting their earnings.
- **Dollar Index:** A slightly weaker Dollar Index generally suggests a supportive environment for emerging market currencies, including the Rupee.
- **Brent/WTI Crude:** A significant drop in crude oil prices is a major positive for India, which is a net oil importer. This could ease inflationary pressures, reduce the current account deficit, and benefit sectors like paints, aviation, and oil marketing companies (OMCs).
- **Gold/Silver:** The fall in precious metals could indicate a shift away from safe-haven assets, possibly reflecting some risk-on appetite or profit-booking in the global markets.
- **US 10Y Yield:** A slight dip in US Treasury yields can reduce the attractiveness of dollar-denominated assets, potentially encouraging FII flows into emerging markets like India, though the current yield remains elevated.

## FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were strong net buyers yesterday, pumping in a substantial ₹7,411 crore into Indian equities. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers, offloading ₹1,357 crore. This trend of FII buying and DII selling has been consistent over the last five sessions.

## Stock & sector news that matters
- **Sharp rupee appreciation may cut IT firms' FY27 earnings by up to 13%: Report**
  A new report suggests that the strengthening Indian Rupee could lead to a significant reduction in earnings for Indian IT companies in FY27.
  **Why it matters:** This is a direct headwind for the large and influential IT sector. A stronger rupee makes Indian IT services more expensive for international clients, potentially impacting their profitability and revenue growth. Investors in IT stocks should watch this closely.
- **Sebi plans consultation paper to remove FPI constraints absent in other markets**
  SEBI is preparing to release a consultation paper aimed at simplifying regulations for foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) by removing certain compliance restrictions.
  **Why it matters:** This is a positive development for attracting more foreign capital into India. Easier FPI regulations could enhance India's appeal as an investment destination, potentially leading to increased FII inflows in the future and supporting overall market sentiment.
- **Indian finance hub eyes dual share model to kickstart public offers: Report**
  Indian regulators are considering a proposal to allow companies to list with dual-class shares in both domestic markets and GIFT City, aiming to boost public offerings.
  **Why it matters:** This move could make Indian markets more attractive for tech and founder-led companies seeking to list, potentially increasing the pipeline of IPOs and offering new investment opportunities for domestic and international investors, especially in GIFT City.
- **IndiGo, Tata Motors PV among 10 Nifty stocks facing earnings downgrades**
  Elara Securities has identified 10 Nifty 50 companies, including IndiGo and Tata Motors PV, that are facing the sharpest earnings estimate downgrades for FY27 and FY28.
  **Why it matters:** Earnings downgrades for Nifty 50 constituents can weigh on overall market sentiment and specific sector outlooks. Investors holding these stocks, particularly in aviation (IndiGo) and auto (Tata Motors PV), should be aware of potential pressure on valuations.
- **AI trade rethink: Will Indian largecaps get first dibs on FPI flows?**
  There's speculation that a potential shift of global capital away from the concentrated AI trade could benefit Indian large-cap stocks, which have seen FPI selling recently.
  **Why it matters:** This highlights a potential thematic shift in global FPI allocation. If the AI trade cools globally, Indian large-caps could become a preferred destination for FPIs, potentially reversing the trend of recent selling and providing a significant boost to the broader market.
- **Anthropic's debut rally fades as shares crack 7% in US pre-market trade**
  Shares of AI company Anthropic fell significantly in US pre-market trading on its second day, following a global market sell-off and concerns about the broader AI trade.
  **Why it matters:** This news reflects a cooling sentiment around the high-flying AI sector globally. While not directly an Indian company, it could influence the broader risk appetite of FIIs towards growth stocks and potentially reinforce the 'AI trade rethink' narrative, impacting sectors perceived as beneficiaries of global tech trends.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
- **Global Market Sentiment:** The significant downturn in US and Asian markets will be a primary driver today. Markets typically react with caution and often mimic global trends in the opening hours, though domestic factors can lead to divergence.
- **FII Flow Continuation:** The consistent strong FII buying is a key support. Any sign of FIIs slowing down or turning net sellers could lead to profit-booking and increased volatility.
- **Rupee Movement:** The sharp appreciation of the Rupee is a notable event. Markets will watch if this trend continues and how it impacts export-oriented sectors, particularly IT, which could see selling pressure.
- **SEBI's Consultation Paper on FPIs:** While not an immediate market mover, the upcoming consultation paper on simplifying FPI regulations is a positive long-term development. Such regulatory reforms are generally viewed favorably by markets, attracting sustained foreign interest.
- **Crude Oil Prices:** The significant drop in crude prices is beneficial for India. Markets typically react positively to lower crude, as it eases inflation concerns and improves macroeconomic indicators.
