# Your Bank and NBFC Team Up: Understanding Co-Lending

**TL;DR**
- Banks and Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) are now working together more closely to offer loans.
- This partnership, called co-lending, helps expand who can get loans across India.
- Banks bring lower-cost funds, while NBFCs offer wider reach and faster processes.
- The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has updated rules to make co-lending more transparent and structured.
- This model is especially helping small businesses and individuals in smaller towns get credit.

## What's this about?
Co-lending is a way for two regulated financial institutions to jointly give out a single loan to a customer. Typically, a bank and a Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) team up. The bank provides a larger portion of the loan amount, often around 80%, while the NBFC funds the remaining part, usually 20%. Both share the risk and rewards from the loan in a pre-agreed way. This model helps combine the strengths of both types of lenders to offer credit more efficiently.

## Why it's in the news right now
Co-lending is a big topic because the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) introduced new, comprehensive guidelines for these arrangements, which became effective from January 1, 2026. These updated rules aim to bring more clarity, transparency, and fairness to how banks and NBFCs work together. Industry leaders are actively discussing how these evolving norms are expanding market opportunities and improving credit access across India. Reports in 2026 indicate a significant growth in co-lent loans, with some estimates suggesting over ₹100,000 crore in outstanding co-lent loans. Many banks and NBFCs are actively forming and expanding their co-lending partnerships.

## How it actually works
1.  **Customer Applies for a Loan:** An individual or a small business approaches an NBFC (or sometimes a bank) for a loan, like a personal loan, home loan, or business loan.
2.  **NBFC Processes Application:** The NBFC, known for its quick customer onboarding and local market understanding, handles the initial application, verification, and assessment of the borrower.
3.  **Joint Funding Agreement:** Once approved, the NBFC and a partner bank agree to co-lend. They decide on their respective shares, with the bank typically providing the majority of the funds (e.g., 70-80%) and the NBFC providing the rest (e.g., 20-30%).
4.  **Minimum Retention:** Under the RBI's 2026 guidelines, each lending partner (both the bank and the NBFC) must keep at least 10% of every individual loan on its own books. This ensures that both entities share the risk and responsibility.
5.  **Unified Loan Terms:** The borrower receives a single loan agreement and pays a blended interest rate, which is a weighted average of the rates offered by both the bank and the NBFC.
6.  **Escrow Account for Transactions:** All loan disbursements and repayments typically flow through a joint escrow account to ensure transparency and proper allocation of funds between the lenders.
7.  **Loan Servicing:** The NBFC usually manages the customer interaction throughout the loan's tenure, including collections and customer support, acting as the primary point of contact for the borrower. However, both regulated entities remain accountable.

An **NBFC (Non-Banking Financial Company)** is a company registered under the Companies Act, involved in the business of loans and advances, acquisition of shares/stocks/bonds/debentures/securities issued by government or local authority or other marketable securities of a like nature, leasing, hire-purchase, insurance business, chit business. It does not include institutions whose principal business is agriculture activity, industrial activity, purchase or sale of any goods (other than securities) or providing any services and sale/purchase/construction of immovable property. Unlike banks, NBFCs cannot accept demand deposits.

## A simple way to think about it
Imagine you want to start a small snack shop in your neighbourhood. You need money for ingredients, equipment, and rent. Your family has some savings, but not enough for everything. Your neighbour, who runs a successful chain of grocery stores, has more funds and can get money at a lower cost.

In co-lending, your neighbour (the Bank) provides most of the capital because they have access to cheaper funds. You (the NBFC) contribute a smaller part, but you have the local knowledge, know the best suppliers, understand what snacks people in your area like, and handle the day-to-day running of the shop and customer interactions. Both of you share the profits (interest) and the risk if the shop doesn't do as well as planned. This way, the shop gets enough funding, benefits from your local expertise, and offers competitive prices to customers.

## Why it matters to you
1.  **Easier Access to Loans:** This model helps extend credit to more people, especially in smaller cities, towns, and rural areas, and to segments like small and medium enterprises (MSMEs) that might find it harder to get loans directly from big banks.
2.  **Potentially Faster Approvals:** NBFCs are often more agile and use digital processes, which can lead to quicker loan application processing and disbursement compared to traditional banking channels.
3.  **More Competitive Interest Rates:** By partnering with banks that have lower costs of funds, co-lending can result in a blended (combined) interest rate that is more affordable for borrowers than if they borrowed solely from an NBFC.
4.  **Wider Range of Loan Products:** Co-lending allows for a greater variety of loan products to be offered, catering to diverse needs, from personal loans to business loans and even consumer durable loans.
5.  **Increased Transparency and Borrower Protection:** The new RBI guidelines emphasize clear disclosures, a single point of contact for the borrower, and a mandatory Key Facts Statement (KFS) to ensure you understand all terms, charges, and responsibilities.

## Common confusions cleared up
-   **Myth:** Co-lending means getting two separate loans from two different lenders.
    **Fact:** It's a single loan, jointly funded by a bank and an NBFC, with unified terms and a blended interest rate. You sign one agreement.

-   **Myth:** The NBFC is just an agent for the bank in co-lending.
    **Fact:** Both the bank and the NBFC share a portion of the loan and its associated risks. The NBFC plays a crucial role in customer sourcing, assessment, and servicing, and has "skin in the game" by retaining at least 10% of the loan.

-   **Myth:** Co-lending is only for large corporate loans.
    **Fact:** While it can be used for various loan types, co-lending is actively used for retail loans like personal loans, consumer durable loans, and loans to MSMEs, helping to expand credit access to these segments.

## FAQ

### What kind of loans are typically co-lent?
Co-lending is used for a variety of loans, including personal loans, home loans, vehicle loans, and especially loans to Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) and priority sector lending.

### Who do I repay the loan to?
You will typically make repayments to the NBFC, which acts as the primary point of contact and handles the loan servicing. The NBFC then distributes the respective shares to the bank.

### Is my data safe with two financial entities?
Yes, both the bank and the NBFC are regulated entities, and they must adhere to strict data privacy and security norms set by the RBI. The loan agreement specifies how data is shared and managed.

### What if there's a problem or grievance with my co-lent loan?
The loan agreement will clearly state which entity is your main point of contact for grievances. You should first approach the NBFC, but both the bank and the NBFC are ultimately responsible for addressing your concerns.

### Does co-lending make the loan riskier for me as a borrower?
No, co-lending does not make the loan riskier for you. In fact, the RBI's new guidelines aim to make the process more transparent and ensure clear responsibilities, providing better protection for borrowers.
