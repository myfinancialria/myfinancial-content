# Don't Miss Your Advance Tax Deadline: What You Need to Know

**TL;DR**
- Advance tax is income tax paid in installments throughout the financial year.
- If your estimated tax liability is ₹10,000 or more after accounting for TDS, you likely need to pay advance tax.
- The deadline for the second installment for FY 2026-27 is September 15, 2026, requiring 45% of your total estimated tax.
- Missing deadlines or underpaying can lead to interest penalties under Sections 234B and 234C.
- You can pay advance tax easily online through the Income Tax Department's e-filing portal.

## What's this about?
Advance tax is simply paying your income tax in parts throughout the year, instead of waiting to pay a lump sum at the very end or when you file your Income Tax Return (ITR). It's a "pay as you earn" system where you estimate your income for the financial year (April 1 to March 31) and pay tax on it periodically. This helps the government collect revenue steadily and prevents taxpayers from facing a huge tax bill at once.

## Why it's in the news right now
The deadline for the second installment of advance tax for the current financial year (FY 2026-27) is **September 15, 2026**. This means anyone liable to pay advance tax must ensure they have paid at least 45% of their total estimated tax liability by this date. With this important date approaching, many individuals, self-employed professionals, and businesses are reviewing their income and tax obligations to avoid penalties.

## How it actually works

1.  **Estimate Your Income:** First, you need to estimate your total income for the entire financial year (April 1, 2026, to March 31, 2027). This includes income from salary (if applicable), business or profession, capital gains, house property, and other sources like interest from fixed deposits or savings accounts.
2.  **Calculate Total Tax Liability:** Based on your estimated income, apply the current income tax slab rates and any special rates for capital gains to calculate your total tax payable for the year.
3.  **Adjust for TDS/TCS:** From this total tax, subtract any Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) or Tax Collected at Source (TCS) that has already been or will be deducted from your income during the year.
4.  **Check the Threshold:** If your remaining estimated tax liability (after adjusting for TDS/TCS) is ₹10,000 or more, then you are required to pay advance tax.
5.  **Pay in Installments:** If you meet the threshold, you must pay your advance tax in specific installments throughout the financial year. For most taxpayers, these deadlines and minimum percentages are:
    *   **By June 15:** At least 15% of your total advance tax.
    *   **By September 15:** At least 45% of your total advance tax (cumulatively, including the June payment).
    *   **By December 15:** At least 75% of your total advance tax (cumulatively).
    *   **By March 15:** 100% of your total advance tax (cumulatively).
6.  **Online Payment:** Advance tax can be paid conveniently online through the official e-filing portal of the Income Tax Department. You select "e-Pay Tax," choose "Income Tax," then "Advance Tax (100)," and specify the Assessment Year (which would be AY 2027-28 for FY 2026-27).

## A simple way to think about it
Imagine you're running a small kirana shop. Instead of waiting until the end of the year to pay all your suppliers at once, you pay them in smaller amounts every few months as you receive goods. This helps you manage your cash flow better and avoids a huge payment crunch later. Advance tax works similarly for your income tax – you pay the government in installments as you earn, rather than a single large payment at the year-end.

## Why it matters to you
1.  **Avoid Penalties:** The biggest reason is to avoid interest penalties. If you don't pay advance tax when required, or pay less than the prescribed percentages by the due dates, you could face interest charges under Sections 234B and 234C of the Income Tax Act. This interest is typically 1% per month on the unpaid amount.
2.  **Better Financial Planning:** Paying tax in installments helps you spread out your tax burden throughout the year, making it easier to manage your finances. It prevents a sudden, large outflow of cash at the time of ITR filing.
3.  **Stay Compliant:** It ensures you comply with income tax laws, saving you from potential notices or complications from the tax department.
4.  **Re-evaluate Your Income:** The installment system encourages you to periodically review your income and expenses, helping you make more accurate tax estimations as the year progresses.

## Common confusions cleared up
*   **Myth:** Only businesses and freelancers need to pay advance tax.
    *   **Fact:** While self-employed individuals and businesses are definitely liable, salaried individuals might also need to pay advance tax if they have other significant income sources not fully covered by TDS. This could include rental income, capital gains from stocks or property, interest from fixed deposits, or freelancing income.
*   **Myth:** I can just pay all my tax on March 15th.
    *   **Fact:** While any tax paid by March 31st is treated as advance tax, paying the full amount only on March 15th (or later) can still attract interest penalties for the earlier missed installments. The law requires payments to be made throughout the year as per the specified percentages.
*   **Myth:** Senior citizens are completely exempt from advance tax.
    *   **Fact:** Resident senior citizens (aged 60 years or above) are exempt from paying advance tax *only if they do not have any income from business or profession*. If they have business or professional income, they are liable to pay advance tax if their total tax liability exceeds ₹10,000.

## FAQ

### Who is exempt from paying advance tax?
Resident senior citizens (aged 60 years or above) who do not have income from business or profession are exempt. Also, if your estimated tax liability after TDS is less than ₹10,000, you don't need to pay advance tax.

### What happens if I miss the September 15th deadline?
If you miss the September 15th deadline or pay less than 45% of your total estimated tax by then, you may be charged interest under Section 234C of the Income Tax Act for deferment of advance tax.

### How do I calculate my advance tax?
You need to estimate your total income for the financial year, calculate the tax on it, and then subtract any TDS/TCS. The remaining amount, if ₹10,000 or more, is your advance tax liability, which you then divide into installments.

### Can I revise my advance tax estimate during the year?
Yes, absolutely. If your income situation changes (e.g., unexpected capital gains, new income source, or higher/lower business profits), you should re-estimate your tax liability and adjust your subsequent advance tax installments accordingly. This helps avoid underpayment penalties.

### What is the Assessment Year (AY) for FY 2026-27?
The Assessment Year (AY) for the Financial Year (FY) 2026-27 will be AY 2027-28. When paying advance tax online, ensure you select the correct Assessment Year.
