# Pre-Market Brief — 30 September 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets show a mixed picture, with US indices closing lower and European markets facing monthly declines, while Asian peers largely gained.
- GIFT Nifty signals a slightly negative open for the Indian benchmark.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong support.
- Nifty shorts have hit a six-month high, marking the worst September F&O series in 25 years.
- For Nifty, the 22700 level is a crucial pivot, with 23000 acting as strong resistance.
- The day's main risk remains sustained FII selling and the impact of rising global bond yields.

European shares are set for their first monthly decline in six months amidst rising yields and energy prices. In Asia this morning, markets largely traded positively, with Japan's Nikkei 225 seeing a significant gain and Chinese factory activity returning to growth.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) strengthened slightly against the US Dollar, closing at ₹95.81, while the Dollar Index also saw a minor dip. Brent crude oil experienced a significant drop of nearly 6%, settling at $96.58, though WTI crude saw a marginal increase. Gold and Silver both registered gains. The US 10-Year Yield continued its upward trajectory, rising by 0.29% to 5.26%.

**Interpretation:** A stronger Rupee and a dip in the Dollar Index are generally positive for India. The sharp fall in Brent crude is a significant relief, potentially easing inflationary pressures and benefiting oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors. However, the continued rise in the US 10-Year Yield is a key concern, as higher global yields can make emerging markets less attractive to foreign investors and put pressure on equities.

## Stock & sector news that matters
- **Re-rating on cars for HAL shares as Tejas Mk1A aircraft deliveries near: Jefferies**
  Jefferies believes that the Tejas Mk1A aircraft, which accounts for 43% of HAL’s order book, could be a key re-rating trigger for the stock as deliveries gain visibility. HAL CMD Ravi Kota expects to deliver 10 aircraft this year.
  **Why it matters:** This news is a significant positive for Hindustan Aeronautics Ltd (HAL), potentially boosting investor sentiment and stock performance in the defence sector.
- **Nifty shorts hit a six-month high in worst September F&O series in 25 years. Time for extra caution?**
  The Nifty’s September derivatives series saw the index fall 6.7%, with foreign investors sharply increasing their bearish bets, pushing FII index shorts to a six-month high.
  **Why it matters:** This indicates strong bearish sentiment among FIIs and suggests potential for continued volatility or downside pressure as the new F&O series begins. It highlights the need for caution, especially for traders.
- **Indian markets are not vulnerable: Sebi chief Tuhin Kanta Pandey remains optimistic even as Sensex, Nifty bleed**
  Sebi Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey expressed optimism about the resilience of Indian markets despite global headwinds like elevated oil prices and bond yields, citing strong macroeconomic fundamentals.
  **Why it matters:** This is a reassuring statement from the regulator, aiming to calm investor nerves amidst recent market corrections and global uncertainties. It suggests confidence in India's long-term growth story.
- **Global Market: European shares head for first monthly decline in six months as yields rise**
  European shares are set for their first monthly decline in half a year, driven by Middle East tensions, higher energy prices, and rising bond yields, which have heightened inflation and interest rate concerns.
  **Why it matters:** This reflects a broader global trend of risk aversion due to macroeconomic concerns. Rising yields globally can divert capital from equities, including Indian markets, making FII flows more challenging.
- **BSE share price dips 3% as Goldman Sachs, BNP Paribas sell 1.7% stake worth ₹2186 crore**
  BSE Limited's shares dropped nearly 3% after Goldman Sachs and BNP Paribas sold a 1.7% stake in the exchange for approximately ₹2186 crore through block deals.
  **Why it matters:** This significant stake sale by institutional investors could put short-term pressure on BSE's share price and might be viewed negatively by other investors, indicating profit-taking or a re-evaluation of the stock.
- **Q2 earnings: Can India Inc repeat Q1, or will Q3 erase the base-effect tailwind?**
  Analysts forecast a potential slowdown in India Inc's Q2 earnings growth to roughly 13-14% compared to Q1, citing rising costs and margin pressures as key contributors.
  **Why it matters:** This outlook suggests that the upcoming earnings season might not be as strong as the previous one, which could lead to stock-specific volatility and impact overall market sentiment if results disappoint.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
- **Start of Q2 Earnings Season:** While no large-cap results are today, the broader Q2 earnings season is underway. Markets typically react to individual company results, leading to stock-specific movements, and the overall trend of earnings growth can set the mood for sectors.
- **Global Bond Yield Movements:** The surge in US 10-Year Treasury bond yields to multi-year highs is a critical factor. Historically, rising global bond yields make equities less attractive, often leading to FII outflows and pressure on equity markets, especially in emerging economies like India.
- **F&O Expiry Implications:** The news of Nifty shorts hitting a six-month high in the September F&O series suggests that the rollover into the new series will be closely watched. F&O expiry periods often see increased volatility and position adjustments, which can lead to sharp moves in the underlying indices.
