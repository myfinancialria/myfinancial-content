# Pre-Market Brief — 14 July 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets presented a mixed picture, with US indices closing lower, Asian markets mixed, and South Korea's Kospi experiencing a sharp decline.
*   GIFT Nifty is signalling a slightly negative opening bias for the Indian market.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers yesterday, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support with net buying.
*   Escalating US-Iran tensions and a significant crash in South Korea's Kospi (amidst AI frenzy concerns) are key global developments.
*   Nifty's key pivot is around 24096, with immediate resistance at 24100 and support at 24000.
*   Geopolitical risks and rising crude oil prices remain the primary concerns for today's session.

European markets were largely flat, while Asian markets this morning are showing a mixed trend, notably with South Korea's Kospi experiencing a significant crash. ## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) depreciated by 0.84% against the US Dollar, closing at ₹96.13. The Dollar Index, measuring the dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies, saw a slight dip of 0.09% to 101.19. Crude oil prices witnessed a notable surge, with Brent Crude up 1.90% to $84.88 and WTI Crude rising 2.15% to $79.82, driven by escalating US-Iran tensions. Gold and Silver also moved higher, gaining 0.76% and 0.93% respectively, reflecting increased safe-haven demand. The US 10-year Treasury Yield climbed 0.88% to 4.61%.

**Interpretation:** A depreciating Rupee (USD/INR up) can be a tailwind for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters, boosting their rupee earnings. However, it also makes imports more expensive, potentially impacting companies reliant on imported raw materials. The significant rise in crude oil prices is a major concern for India, as it is a net oil importer. This could fuel inflation, put pressure on oil marketing companies (OMCs), aviation, and paint sectors, and potentially lead to higher current account deficits. Rising gold and silver prices indicate a flight to safety amidst global uncertainties. The uptick in US 10-year yields could make emerging markets like India less attractive for FII flows.

## Option-chain signals (OI) — where support & resistance sit
**NIFTY:** The highest Call Open Interest (OI) is observed at the 24100 strike (30,872,205 contracts), followed by 24200 (28,845,635 contracts), indicating strong resistance at these levels where option sellers expect the market to face a ceiling. The highest Put OI, acting as immediate support, is at the 24000 strike (44,180,175 contracts), followed by 24050 (27,995,110 contracts). The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) stands at 1.05.

**BANK NIFTY:** For Bank Nifty, the 59000 strike holds the highest Call OI (2,154,900 contracts), making it a significant resistance level, followed by 58000 (1,465,890 contracts). On the support side, the 59000 strike also has the highest Put OI (1,259,130 contracts), followed by 58000 (1,149,660 contracts) and 57000 (789,750 contracts). The PCR is 0.89.

**SENSEX:** Sensex sees the highest Call OI at 77500 (1,147,960 contracts) and 78500 (1,022,820 contracts), acting as resistance. Strong support is visible at 77000 (1,329,260 contracts) and 76000 (862,660 contracts) due to high Put OI. The PCR is 1.03.

**Interpretation:** For Nifty, the PCR above 1 (1.05) leans towards a neutral-to-supportive sentiment, but the concentration of Call OI at 24100 and 24200 suggests that these levels will be tough to breach. The 24000 Put OI provides a solid floor. Bank Nifty's PCR below 1 (0.89) indicates a slightly cautious sentiment, with 58000-59000 acting as a critical zone for both resistance and support. For Sensex, a PCR of 1.03 suggests a neutral-to-supportive bias, with 77000 as key support and 77500 as immediate resistance. Combining these with technical levels, Nifty's range today is likely to be between 24000 and 24150-24200, while Bank Nifty might oscillate around 57500-58000.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
*   **US June CPI data (awaited)**: Investors are awaiting the U.S. June CPI data. Inflation data from major economies like the US is closely watched as it influences global central bank policy, particularly the US Federal Reserve's stance on interest rates. Higher-than-expected inflation can lead to fears of aggressive rate hikes, typically causing market volatility and a flight from riskier assets.
*   **Geopolitical Tensions (US-Iran conflict)**: Escalating US-Iran tensions have contributed to rising oil prices and negatively impacted investor sentiment. Geopolitical events often introduce uncertainty and can trigger sharp, short-term market reactions, especially in commodities like crude oil, which then has a cascading effect on inflation and corporate costs.
*   **F&O Expiry (nearest expiry)**: While not explicitly stated for today, the option chain data refers to the "nearest expiry." F&O expiry days typically see increased volatility and unwinding of positions, leading to significant churn in specific stocks and indices.
