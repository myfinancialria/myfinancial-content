# Market Wrap — 23 October 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian equity markets witnessed a broad-based sell-off today, extending losses for the fourth consecutive session.
- Global cues, particularly a significant sell-off in US markets, coupled with heavy FII selling, were the primary drivers of the decline.
- International Gemological Institute Ltd. (IGIL) was the standout gainer, surging nearly 8%.
- Food delivery giant Swiggy Ltd. emerged as the biggest loser, plummeting over 16%.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers for the day, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided some support.
- Futures & Options (F&O) build-up data for today is unavailable and will be populated from the next session.

## How the market behaved today

Indian markets closed deep in the red today, with benchmark indices Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty registering significant cuts. The Nifty 50 ended the session at 22,600.00, down 0.39%, after trading in a wide range between 22,787.25 and 22,372.00. The Bank Nifty also saw substantial selling pressure, closing at 54,287.35, a decline of 1.16%. The broader market witnessed even sharper corrections, with the Nifty Midcap 100 falling 2.21% and the Nifty Smallcap 100 plunging 2.94%, indicating a widespread bearish sentiment across segments.

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, surged by 6.98% to 16.86, reflecting increased nervousness and uncertainty among investors.

## What moved the market — the factors

The Indian market's downturn today was primarily driven by a confluence of negative global cues and persistent selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs). Overnight, US stocks experienced a significant sell-off, with the Dow Jones crashing over 700 points due to an "AI selloff" and "SpaceX fears," which dampened investor sentiment globally and heavily influenced the opening of Indian markets.

FIIs continued their selling spree, offloading ₹3,459 crore from the cash segment, which put considerable pressure on the benchmarks. While Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided some counter-balance with net buying of ₹2,891 crore, it wasn't enough to stem the overall decline.

Heavyweight stocks also contributed to the market's weakness. Infosys ADRs crashed over 6% after the company cut its FY27 revenue growth guidance, despite beating September-quarter revenue estimates. This news weighed heavily on the IT sector, which, despite being the "best performing" today, still closed in the red. Several Sensex stocks, including Bharat Electronics, NTPC, Power Grid Corporation, and Hindustan Unilever, hit fresh 52-week lows, indicating broad-based weakness in large-cap names as well.

Sector-wise, Metal, Realty, and PSU Bank sectors bore the brunt of the selling, reflecting concerns over valuations and rising borrowing costs. The surge in India VIX also signaled heightened volatility, making investors cautious. Reports also highlighted that only 15% of midcap stocks were trading above their 50-DMA, with JM Financial warning of weakening leadership in midcap and smallcap segments, suggesting that "buying the dip" could be getting riskier.

## Sectors — winners and losers

Today, all sectors closed in negative territory, reflecting the broad market weakness. However, some sectors showed relative resilience compared to others.

- **FMCG:** -0.99% (Often considered a defensive sector, it saw less severe declines).

**Top 3 losers:**

- **REALTY:** -2.38% (Faced selling pressure amidst broader market weakness and potential concerns over rising interest rates).
- **PSU BANK:** -2.26% (Witnessed declines in line with the broader financial services sector).

## News that moved stocks

- **Jio IPO breaks 12 records: Rs 4.5 lakh crore bids, highest applications and more**
  Mukesh Ambani’s Jio Platforms IPO attracted bids exceeding ₹4.5 lakh crore, setting 12 records, including over 1.25 crore applications, with strong institutional and retail participation. This news highlights robust investor appetite for new listings but did not significantly impact the broader market today, which was dominated by negative sentiment.
- **Why US stocks are falling: Dow Jones crashes over 700 points as AI selloff, SpaceX fears hit markets**
  US stocks experienced a significant decline as AI-related shares continued to sell off, with the Dow Jones Industrial Average falling markedly. This global cue heavily influenced negative sentiment in Indian markets.
- **Infosys ADRs crash 6% after Q2 results as revenue guidance cut spooks investors**
  Infosys ADRs fell 6.3% after the company lowered its FY27 revenue growth guidance to 1-2%, despite September-quarter revenue beating estimates. This news negatively impacted Infosys shares and contributed to the IT sector's overall decline.
- **SBI Cards Q2 profit jumps 75% YoY to Rs 778 crore as bad loan costs fall sharply**
  SBI Cards reported a strong 75% year-on-year profit increase to ₹778 crore in Q2 FY27, driven by lower credit costs and improved asset quality. This positive earnings report could provide some support for SBI Cards in the coming sessions, though the broader financial sector faced selling pressure today.
- **Dr Reddy’s Q2 Results: Net profit falls 36% YoY to Rs 913 crore, revenue declines marginally**
  Dr Reddy’s Laboratories reported a 36% year-on-year profit decline to ₹913.4 crore in Q2 FY27, with revenue slipping 1.5%. Weakness in North America pressured performance. This negative earnings report weighed on Dr Reddy's stock.
- **Only 15% of midcap stocks above 50-DMA: Is buying the dip getting riskier? JM Financial spots signs of leadership shift**
  JM Financial warned that midcap and smallcap leadership might weaken as market breadth deteriorates sharply, with largecaps showing early recovery signals. This analysis contributed to the significant underperformance of the Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100 indices today.

## Market breadth

The market breadth was overwhelmingly negative today, with a stark advance/decline ratio of 39 advances to 456 declines out of a total of 495 stocks traded. This indicates that the selling pressure was widespread across the market, affecting almost all stocks.

## FII / DII flows

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued to be net sellers in the Indian equity market today, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided some buying support.

| Investor Type | Net Flow (₹ crore) |
|---|---|

Looking at the 5-day trend, FIIs have been net sellers in three out of the last five sessions, with significant outflows today and on October 15th. DIIs, on the other hand, have been consistent net buyers in four out of the last five sessions, absorbing some of the FII selling pressure. The recent FII selling trend, particularly the substantial outflow today, suggests a cautious stance from foreign investors, possibly due to global uncertainties and rising bond yields.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market

Today's session was primarily driven by negative global cues and persistent FII selling. The significant overnight sell-off in US markets, fueled by an "AI selloff" and "SpaceX fears," set a bearish tone for Indian equities from the start. This global sentiment was compounded by FIIs offloading a substantial ₹3,459 crore from the Indian market.

Key corporate earnings also played a role. Infosys's decision to cut its FY27 revenue growth guidance after its Q2 results led to a sharp fall in its ADRs, impacting the IT sector. Conversely, SBI Cards reported a strong 75% jump in Q2 profit, but this positive news was overshadowed by the broader market weakness. Dr Reddy's Laboratories' 36% profit decline in Q2 also contributed to stock-specific pressure. The RBI Governor's statement that the central bank does not target a specific rupee level but focuses on curbing volatility was a macro-level input, but its immediate impact on market direction was limited amidst the strong global and FII-led selling. The overall sentiment was further dampened by warnings from JM Financial about weakening leadership in midcap and smallcap stocks, which saw these segments underperform significantly.

## Setup for tomorrow

Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will continue to be crucial. The US Dollar Index closed higher at 101.74 (+0.13%), and the USD/INR pair also strengthened to 94.93 (+0.65%), indicating potential pressure on the rupee. The US 10-year yield also rose to 4.99% (+0.79%), which could keep FIIs on edge. While Brent Crude saw a slight dip, WTI Crude rose, and precious metals like Gold and Silver continued their slide due to higher yields reducing their appeal. Investors will be watching for any further developments in US markets overnight and the trajectory of FII flows. Key levels for Nifty 50 to watch would be today's low of 22,372 as a crucial support, while 22,700 could act as an immediate resistance.
