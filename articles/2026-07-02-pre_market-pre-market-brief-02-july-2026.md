# Pre-Market Brief — 02 July 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets closed mixed, with Asian indices showing weakness while US and European markets saw minor declines.
- FIIs continued their selling streak, offloading ₹1,140 crore, while DIIs provided strong support with net buying of ₹3,159 crore.
- Crude oil prices declined, which is a positive for India's import bill and inflation outlook.
- Auto sales data for June showed strong performance across segments, potentially boosting auto stocks.
- Indian bond markets defied US Treasury sell-off, supported by foreign inflows and lower oil prices.

Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight, with most major indices closing in negative territory. US markets saw minor declines, with the Nasdaq leading losses. European markets also ended slightly lower. Asian markets were largely weak, with the Nikkei 225 taking a significant hit, though the Hang Seng managed to post gains.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) depreciated slightly by 0.38% to 95.28 against the US Dollar, indicating some pressure on the domestic currency. The Dollar Index, which measures the dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies, saw a minor dip of 0.10% to 101.29.

In commodities, crude oil prices softened, with Brent Crude falling 1.16% to $70.74 per barrel and WTI Crude down 1.21% to $67.75 per barrel. This decline in crude oil is generally positive for India, as it helps alleviate import bill concerns and inflationary pressures. Gold prices saw a modest gain of 0.33% to $4081.60, while Silver also rose by 0.57% to $60.42, suggesting some safe-haven demand or inflation hedging interest.

The US 10-Year Treasury Yield declined by 0.46% to 4.37%. A falling US yield can sometimes make emerging markets like India more attractive to foreign investors, potentially leading to increased capital inflows.

## Recent FII/DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling trend, offloading Indian equities worth ₹1,140 crore yesterday. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, provided strong counter-support, pumping in ₹3,159 crore into the market. This pattern of DII buying offsetting FII selling has been consistent over the last five trading sessions.

## Overnight news that matters
**June auto sales data: Commercial vehicle turns consensus player; brokerages list stocks to buy**
Indian automakers reported strong performance in June 2026 across passenger vehicles, two-wheelers, commercial vehicles, and tractors. This matters to Indian markets as strong auto sales data signals robust domestic demand and could lead to positive sentiment and potential buying interest in auto sector stocks.

**KPIT outlook disappoints, analysts push growth recovery to FY28**
KPIT Technologies shares tumbled nearly 17% after issuing a weaker-than-expected outlook for FY27, citing profitability pressures on European automakers. This is significant for Indian markets, particularly the IT sector, as it highlights potential headwinds from global client spending and could impact other IT services companies with similar exposure.

**Foreign inflows, lower oil prices help Indian bond traders look past US Treasury selloff**
Indian government bonds saw gains early Thursday, defying a rise in U.S. Treasury yields, fueled by falling oil prices and increasing foreign investor interest. This is positive for Indian markets as it suggests resilience in the domestic bond market, lower borrowing costs for the government, and continued confidence from foreign investors despite global yield movements.

**India's textile stocks become market standouts on trade deals**
Indian textile exporters are experiencing a significant stock market surge, outperforming the broader market due to new trade deals with the UK, EU, and potential US agreements. This matters for Indian markets as it indicates a potential growth driver for the textile sector, making these stocks attractive to investors based on improved export competitiveness and market access.

## What to watch in today's session
1.  **Auto Sector Performance:** Following strong June sales data, watch for how auto stocks (Passenger Vehicles, Two-wheelers, Commercial Vehicles, Tractors) react at the open and during the session.
2.  **IT Sector Sentiment:** Keep an eye on IT stocks, especially after KPIT Technologies' disappointing outlook, as global tech spending concerns could weigh on the sector.
3.  **FII/DII Flow Impact:** Monitor how the continued FII selling and DII buying dynamics influence market breadth and specific large-cap movements.
4.  **Crude Oil Price Movement:** Any further significant movement in crude oil prices, either up or down, will be crucial for India's macroeconomic outlook and related sectors.
5.  **Corporate Action Stocks:** Stocks like GANGAFORGE, LLOYDSENT, GUJENERGY, and CHEMBOND will be in focus due to their ex-date corporate actions today.

## Bottom line
The Indian market is likely to open on a cautious note, influenced by mixed global cues and continued FII outflows. However, strong domestic institutional buying, positive auto sales data, and falling crude oil prices could provide underlying support. Investors should remain vigilant about global developments and sector-specific news.
