# DSP Pension Fund NPS Review: Returns, Portfolio & Fees (2026)

## What this means in plain terms

The National Pension System (NPS) is a government-backed retirement savings scheme designed to help individuals build a corpus for their post-retirement years. When you invest in NPS, your contributions are managed by a Pension Fund Manager (PFM) like DSP Pension Fund. These PFMs invest your money across different asset classes – primarily equities (E), corporate bonds (C), and government securities (G) – based on your chosen asset allocation strategy.

DSP Pension Fund, being a newer entrant, manages these investments with a specific approach, which includes a notable preference for corporate bonds rated AA. Understanding their performance, especially compared to other PFMs, and their investment choices is crucial for subscribers. This review aims to provide a clear picture of DSP Pension Fund's standing in the NPS landscape, helping you make informed decisions about your retirement savings.

## Understanding DSP Pension Fund in NPS

The National Pension System (NPS) operates under the regulatory oversight of the Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA). It allows individuals to contribute regularly into a pension account, which is then invested by professional fund managers. DSP Pension Fund is one such PFM, entrusted with managing the retirement savings of NPS subscribers. As one of the newer players in the NPS ecosystem, DSP Pension Fund aims to deliver competitive returns while adhering to the investment guidelines set by the PFRDA.

Subscribers have the flexibility to choose their preferred PFM and asset allocation. Under the Active Choice option, you can decide the proportion of your investment across E, C, and G asset classes, with certain caps on equity exposure. Alternatively, the Auto Choice option automatically adjusts your asset allocation based on your age, gradually reducing equity exposure as you get older. DSP Pension Fund, like other PFMs, offers schemes under both these choices.

## Analysing DSP Pension Fund's Returns

When evaluating a Pension Fund Manager, returns are a critical factor, though past performance is not indicative of future results. Based on recent analyses, DSP Pension Fund has shown a particular trend in its performance. As of early 2026, DSP Pension Fund recorded the weakest 1-year equity return among its peers in the NPS. This suggests that its equity investment strategy, in the short term, may have lagged behind other fund managers.

However, a comprehensive review requires looking beyond just 1-year equity returns. It's essential to examine its performance across other asset classes (corporate bonds and government securities) and over longer time horizons (3-year, 5-year, and since inception returns). A PFM's ability to consistently generate stable returns across different market cycles, particularly in debt instruments, can be a significant differentiator. While the immediate equity performance might be a point of consideration, investors should also assess DSP's long-term track record once more data becomes available, especially for its debt-oriented funds. The PFRDA regularly publishes performance data of all PFMs, which can be accessed on the NPS Trust website.

## DSP Pension Fund's Portfolio Strategy and Asset Allocation

A PFM's portfolio strategy reflects its investment philosophy and risk management approach. DSP Pension Fund's investment strategy, as observed, includes a notable inclination towards corporate bonds with an AA rating. The fund holds the most AA-rated corporate bonds among its peers. This indicates a preference for corporate debt instruments that are considered to have a good credit quality, balancing returns with a degree of safety.

### Asset Allocation Choices

NPS subscribers can opt for one of two approaches for asset allocation:

*   **Active Choice:** This allows subscribers to actively decide the percentage of their contributions to be invested in E (equities), C (corporate bonds), and G (government securities). For non-government sector subscribers, the maximum allocation to Equity (E) is capped at 75% till age 50, gradually reducing thereafter. However, under the Multiple Scheme Framework (MSF) introduced in October 2025, subscribers can opt for up to 100% equity allocation in specific Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) within the NPS.
*   **Auto Choice (Lifecycle Funds):** This is a default option where the asset allocation is automatically adjusted based on the subscriber's age. As the subscriber gets older, the exposure to equity and corporate bonds is progressively reduced, and the allocation to government securities increases. There are three types of Auto Choice funds: Aggressive (LC75), Moderate (LC50), and Conservative (LC25), each with a different initial equity exposure.

DSP Pension Fund manages portfolios under both these choices, deploying its specific investment strategies within the PFRDA-mandated guidelines. Their focus on AA-rated corporate bonds would primarily impact the 'C' component of both Active and Auto Choice portfolios.

## A real example

Let's consider Anjali, 35, earning ₹20 lakh CTC annually, based in Gurugram. She decided to invest in NPS to build a retirement corpus and avail tax benefits. She opts for an annual contribution of ₹1.5 lakh, which is the maximum eligible for deduction under Section 80C and 80CCD(1B) combined. Anjali chooses DSP Pension Fund as her PFM and selects the Active Choice with an asset allocation of 50% Equity (E), 30% Corporate Bonds (C), and 20% Government Securities (G).

**Annual Contribution:** ₹1,50,000

**Asset Allocation:**
*   Equity (E): ₹75,000 (50% of ₹1,50,000)
*   Corporate Bonds (C): ₹45,000 (30% of ₹1,50,000)
*   Government Securities (G): ₹30,000 (20% of ₹1,50,000)

Let's assume the following average annual returns for DSP Pension Fund's respective asset classes over a long term (these are illustrative and not guaranteed):
*   Equity (E): 10% per annum
*   Corporate Bonds (C): 7% per annum
*   Government Securities (G): 6% per annum

**Calculation for one year:**
*   Return from Equity: ₹75,000 * 10% = ₹7,500
*   Return from Corporate Bonds: ₹45,000 * 7% = ₹3,150
*   Return from Government Securities: ₹30,000 * 6% = ₹1,800
*   Total estimated return for the year: ₹7,500 + ₹3,150 + ₹1,800 = ₹12,450
*   Total corpus after one year (approx.): ₹1,50,000 + ₹12,450 = ₹1,62,450

If Anjali continues this contribution and asset allocation until her retirement at age 60 (25 years), her corpus would grow significantly due to compounding. For instance, with an average blended return of approximately 8.5% per annum (based on the assumed individual asset class returns and allocation), her projected corpus at retirement could be over ₹1.1 crore. This calculation does not account for changes in asset allocation over time, fee deductions, or potential increases in contributions. The final corpus is subject to market performance and actual returns generated by the PFM.

## FAQ

### Can I change my Pension Fund Manager (PFM) in NPS?
Yes, NPS subscribers have the flexibility to change their PFM once in a financial year. This can be done online through the Central Recordkeeping Agency (CRA) portal where your NPS account is maintained.

### What are the tax benefits of investing in NPS?
NPS offers tax benefits under Section 80C, 80CCD(1B) for an additional deduction of up to ₹50,000, and 80CCD(2) for employer contributions, which is deductible up to 10% of salary (Basic + DA) without any monetary limit.

### How does 'Auto Choice' work with DSP Pension Fund?
Under Auto Choice, DSP Pension Fund automatically adjusts your asset allocation across E, C, and G funds based on your age. As you grow older, the exposure to riskier assets like equity is gradually reduced, and allocation to safer government securities increases.

### What are the fees associated with DSP Pension Fund in NPS?
The fees for Pension Fund Managers in NPS are regulated by the PFRDA and are generally very low. They include an investment management fee, which is a small percentage of assets under management, and other transaction charges.

### How often can I change my asset allocation with DSP Pension Fund?
If you have opted for Active Choice, you can change your asset allocation up to four times in a financial year. For subscribers under Auto Choice, the asset allocation is automatically managed and does not require manual changes.

### Is DSP Pension Fund suitable for conservative investors?
DSP Pension Fund, like other PFMs, offers various schemes including those focused on corporate bonds and government securities. Conservative investors might find their debt-oriented funds appealing, especially given their higher allocation to AA-rated corporate bonds.

### Where can I find the latest performance data for DSP Pension Fund?
The latest performance data, including returns across different asset classes and timeframes for all Pension Fund Managers, is regularly published on the NPS Trust website and the PFRDA website.
