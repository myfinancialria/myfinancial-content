# Market Wrap — 06 October 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian equities extended their rebound for a second consecutive session, with benchmark indices closing higher.
- Positive global cues, particularly from US markets, and falling crude prices were key drivers.
- Nifty 50 gained nearly 1%, while Midcap and Smallcap indices outperformed with stronger gains.
- Trent Ltd. was the standout gainer, surging over 12%, while Coal India Ltd. was the biggest loser.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong buying support.
- F&O positioning data is unavailable for today's wrap, but FPIs were noted to be paring Bank Nifty shorts.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market's rebound today was primarily driven by a confluence of positive global cues and sustained domestic buying interest. US stocks, particularly the S&P 500 and Nasdaq, hit all-time highs, buoyed by falling Treasury yields and easing oil prices, which fueled an AI rally. This positive sentiment spilled over into Indian equities. Falling crude prices also provided a tailwind, easing concerns about inflation and current account deficit.

Domestically, strong Q2 business updates from key companies like Honasa Consumer and Titan provided stock-specific momentum. While Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) stepped in with robust buying, effectively cushioning the market and driving it higher.

Sector-wise, Pharma, FMCG, and Energy led the gains, indicating a broad-based recovery in defensive and consumption-oriented sectors. Conversely, IT and Realty sectors witnessed some profit-booking, acting as minor drags on the overall market. The significant drop in India VIX also suggested increasing investor confidence and reduced uncertainty.

- **Indian Equities Rebound on Global Cues and Q2 Updates:** Indian equities extended gains for a second session, with Nifty rising over 220 points, driven by falling crude prices, strong Q2 business updates, and positive global cues. This broad positive sentiment helped lift the overall market.

This indicates some strategic positioning in the banking sector.

## F&O build-up — where traders are positioning
OI build-up needs the previous session's OI — it will populate from the next run.

**Interpretation:**
Long build-up, where both price and Open Interest (OI) increase, typically signals fresh buying with conviction, suggesting that traders expect the upward trend to continue. Short build-up, characterized by a price drop and an increase in OI, indicates fresh selling pressure, implying a bearish outlook. Short covering, where the price rises while OI decreases, suggests that traders who had previously sold are now buying back to close their positions, leading to a bounce. This doesn't always reflect genuine underlying strength. Lastly, long unwinding, where both price and OI decrease, indicates that traders who had bought earlier are now selling their positions, often due to profit-taking or a weakening outlook.
