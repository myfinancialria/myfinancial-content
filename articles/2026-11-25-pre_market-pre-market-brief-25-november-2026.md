# Pre-Market Brief — 25 November 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global cues are mixed, with US markets closing mixed and most Asian indices trading lower.
- GIFT Nifty indicates a slightly cautious to flat opening for the Indian market.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remain strong net buyers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continue to book profits.
- The Indian Rupee has strengthened significantly, hitting an 8-month high, driven by persistent FII inflows and declining crude oil prices.
- With Nifty technical levels unavailable, traders should watch the GIFT Nifty level of 25881.50 as an early indicator.
- The approaching RBI policy decision and sustained DII selling pose key risks for the day.

Overnight, US markets presented a mixed picture, with the Dow Jones closing higher by 0.85%, while the Nasdaq and S&P 500 ended in negative territory, down 1.48% and 0.50% respectively. This morning, Asian markets are largely in the red, with Japan's Nikkei 225 down 2.78%, Hong Kong's Hang Seng falling 1.14%, and China's Shanghai Composite declining by 2.03%. Europe's FTSE 100, however, closed positively, up 0.38%.

While the Dow's positive close offers some support, the broader sentiment from Asia and the tech-heavy Nasdaq's decline could weigh on market opening.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) has shown significant strength, appreciating by 0.75% to 92.22 against the US Dollar. The Dollar Index, which measures the greenback against a basket of major currencies, saw a modest rise of 0.21% to 104.03. In the energy markets, Brent Crude declined by 3.61% to $88.10, and WTI Crude was down 0.72% to $81.46. Gold prices dipped slightly by 0.29% to $3551.70, and Silver also saw a decline of 0.69% to $40.48. The US 10-Year Yield eased by 0.28% to 4.90%.

**Interpretation:** A stronger Rupee (USD/INR down) is a positive for the Indian economy, potentially easing import costs and making Indian assets more attractive to foreign investors. Falling crude oil prices are a significant tailwind for India, benefiting oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors by reducing input costs. A softer US 10-year yield can also be supportive for emerging markets like India, potentially encouraging FII inflows.

-   **Rupee scales 8-month high as foreign flows to Indian equities persist**
    The Indian rupee has shown significant strength against the US dollar, propelled by robust foreign portfolio investments, closing at 92.23 per dollar.
    **Why it matters:** A strong rupee is generally positive for the Indian economy, making imports cheaper and potentially attracting more foreign capital.     **Why it matters:** Lower bond yields indicate higher bond prices, which is generally positive for the debt market. The mention of the RBI verdict suggests potential volatility in bond markets around the policy announcement, which could also spill over into equity market sentiment.

This could lead to a rally in RMC Switchgears and potentially create positive sentiment for other companies in the infrastructure and power distribution sectors.

    Four Nifty500 stocks, including APL Apollo Tubes, closed more than 2% below their respective Volume Weighted Average Prices (VWAPs) on November 23.
    **Why it matters:** Closing significantly below VWAP can indicate selling pressure and a bearish sentiment for these specific stocks. Traders might interpret this as a sign of weakness, potentially leading to further downside or consolidation for APL Apollo Tubes and the other mentioned stocks.

Investors holding these stocks may consider reviewing their positions, while traders might look for shorting opportunities or avoid these names.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
-   **RBI Policy Decision (approaching):** The bond market news mentions the Reserve Bank of India's policy decision approaching. Central bank policy announcements are typically high-impact events that can introduce significant volatility across equity, debt, and currency markets. Markets usually react to changes in interest rates, liquidity measures, and the central bank's commentary on inflation and growth outlook.
-   **FII/DII Flow Trends:** The continued strong FII inflows versus DII selling will be closely watched. Sustained FII buying typically provides underlying support, while DII profit-booking can cap rallies or lead to intraday corrections.
-   **Global Crude Oil Prices:** The recent decline in Brent and WTI crude prices is a positive for India. Any significant reversal in this trend could impact inflation expectations and corporate margins, especially for sectors like OMCs, paints, and aviation.
