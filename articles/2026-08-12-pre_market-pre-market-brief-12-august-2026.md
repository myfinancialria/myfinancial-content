# Pre-Market Brief — 12 August 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight, with US indices closing lower while Asian markets showed varied performance.
*   GIFT Nifty is signalling a cautious start for Indian equities, pointing to a potential gap-down open.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were both net buyers in the cash segment yesterday, offering some support.
*   Rising crude oil prices, nearing $90 a barrel due to geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, remain a significant concern.
*   Nifty 50 faces immediate resistance around the 24,500 mark, with 24,400 acting as key support.
*   The primary risk for today's session is the continued upward trajectory of crude oil and its potential to fuel inflation and impact corporate margins.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Global markets displayed a mixed sentiment overnight. US indices, the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500, all closed in the red, reflecting a cautious mood. In Asia, the Nikkei 225 saw gains, boosted by optimism around AI infrastructure demand, while the Hang Seng declined. The Shanghai Composite, however, managed to close higher. This mixed global backdrop suggests that Indian markets might open with some uncertainty, influenced by the negative cues from the US but potentially finding some resilience from specific Asian sectors.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) saw a slight depreciation against the US Dollar, trading at ₹95.42. The Dollar Index also edged up marginally to 99.88. Crude oil prices continued their upward march, with Brent Crude rising by 1.10% to $89.89 and WTI Crude by 1.08% to $84.10, nearing the critical $90 mark. Gold prices surged by 1.71% to $4458.00, and Silver also gained 1.02% to $65.43, likely driven by safe-haven demand amidst geopolitical concerns and ahead of US CPI data. The US 10-year Treasury Yield saw a slight dip to 4.68%.

**Interpretation:** A depreciating Rupee (USD/INR ↑) can be a tailwind for IT and pharmaceutical exporters but puts pressure on importers. Rising crude oil prices (Brent/WTI ↑) are a significant headwind for India, potentially increasing inflation, widening the trade deficit, and impacting sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation. Higher gold prices (Gold ↑) suggest increased safe-haven buying, indicating market uncertainty. A slight dip in US 10-year yield (US 10Y Yield ↓) might offer some relief from FII outflow concerns, but the overall commodity price action is a negative for India.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
*   **Middle East Geopolitical Tensions**: The ongoing conflict in the Middle East is directly impacting crude oil prices. Markets typically react with increased volatility and risk aversion when geopolitical tensions escalate, particularly when they threaten global energy supplies. Rising crude prices can lead to inflationary concerns and pressure on import-dependent economies like India.
*   **US CPI Data (scheduled later)**: The anticipation of US Consumer Price Index (CPI) data is a key global event. Markets typically react with heightened volatility around such major economic data releases, as they influence expectations for the US Federal Reserve's monetary policy. Stronger-than-expected inflation could lead to fears of higher interest rates, potentially impacting global liquidity and FII flows.
*   **Ongoing IPOs (LEAP India, Milky Mist)**: While not market-moving for the broader indices, the subscription status and grey market premiums (GMP) of ongoing IPOs like LEAP India and Milky Mist will be watched by retail investors for listing day performance. Strong subscription numbers can indicate healthy investor appetite in the primary market.
