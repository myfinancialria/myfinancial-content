# Your Income Tax Return (ITR) for FY 2025-26: Deadlines & What's New

**TL;DR**
- The deadline to file your Income Tax Return (ITR) for the Financial Year (FY) 2025-26 (Assessment Year 2026-27) is approaching.
- Most salaried individuals and pensioners need to file by July 31, 2026.
- If you have business income but don't need an audit, your deadline is August 31, 2026.
- New ITR forms for AY 2026-27 are out, with some expanded eligibility for ITR-1 and ITR-4.
- Filing on time helps you avoid penalties, interest, and ensures smooth processing of refunds.

## What's this about?
Every year, individuals and businesses in India need to tell the government about their income and pay any taxes due. This is called filing your Income Tax Return (ITR). It's a mandatory financial exercise where you declare your earnings, claim deductions, and calculate your tax liability for a specific financial year. The current period for which you'll be filing your ITR is Financial Year (FY) 2025-26, which ran from April 1, 2025, to March 31, 2026. The year in which you file this return is called the Assessment Year (AY), which is 2026-27.

## Why it's in the news right now
The Income Tax Department has recently notified the new ITR forms for Assessment Year 2026-27, and the online filing utilities for some forms (like ITR-1, ITR-2, and ITR-4) are already available. As we are now in June, the ITR filing season is officially kicking off, with key deadlines just around the corner in July and August. Many news outlets are reminding taxpayers about these important dates and the changes introduced for this assessment year, including staggered deadlines and updated form eligibilities. Employers are also legally required to issue Form 16, a crucial document for salaried individuals, by June 15, 2026, making it timely to start preparing.

## How it actually works

Filing your ITR involves a few key steps:

1.  **Gather Your Documents:** Before you start, collect all necessary documents. This includes your Form 16 (from your employer, expected by June 15, 2026), Form 26AS, Annual Information Statement (AIS), Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS) (these show your tax credits and financial transactions), investment proofs (like PPF, ELSS, LIC, home loan certificates), and bank statements.
2.  **Choose the Correct ITR Form:** There are different ITR forms (ITR-1 to ITR-7), and the one you use depends on your income sources and total income. For example, ITR-1 (Sahaj) is for resident individuals with income up to ₹50 lakh from salary, one or two house properties, pension, or other specified sources. ITR-4 (Sugam) is for individuals, HUFs, and firms (other than LLPs) opting for the presumptive taxation scheme with income up to ₹50 lakh.
3.  **Calculate Your Income and Tax:** Add up all your income from salary, house property, capital gains, business/profession, and other sources. Then, subtract eligible deductions (like those under Section 80C, 80D, etc.) to arrive at your taxable income. Calculate the tax based on the applicable tax slabs. Remember, the new tax regime is now the default, so you'll need to explicitly choose the old regime if you want to claim its deductions.
4.  **Pay Any Due Tax:** If, after all calculations, you find you owe more tax, you must pay it before or during filing. This is often called 'self-assessment tax'.
5.  **Fill and Submit the Form:** You can fill out your ITR form online directly on the Income Tax Department's e-filing portal or use offline utilities. Once filled, review it carefully, verify the details, and submit it electronically.
6.  **Verify Your Return:** After submission, it's crucial to verify your ITR. This can be done online using Aadhaar OTP, Net Banking, or by sending a signed ITR-V (acknowledgement form) to the Income Tax Department's CPC in Bengaluru. Without verification, your ITR is considered invalid.

## A simple way to think about it
Think of filing your ITR like preparing a detailed monthly budget report for your household. At the end of the month, you list all the money that came in (salaries, rent from a spare room, interest from savings) and all the money you spent on certain approved expenses (like school fees, medical insurance premiums). You then calculate how much money is left over, and based on that, you set aside a certain amount for a "community fund" (your tax). If you overpaid during the year, the community fund returns the extra money to you. If you underpaid, you need to contribute more.

## Why it matters to you

1.  **Avoid Penalties and Interest:** Missing the deadline can lead to penalties. For instance, if your total income is above ₹5 lakh, you might pay a late fee of ₹5,000; if it's below ₹5 lakh, the penalty is ₹1,000. You might also have to pay interest on any unpaid tax.
2.  **Claim Refunds:** If you've paid more tax than required (through TDS or advance tax), filing your ITR on time is the only way to get your refund back. Early filing often means faster refunds.
3.  **Carry Forward Losses:** If you have capital losses (e.g., from selling shares) or business losses, you can only carry them forward to offset future income if you file your ITR by the due date.
4.  **Loan and Visa Applications:** Your ITR is an important document for proving your income and financial standing. Banks often ask for ITR copies when you apply for loans (home loans, car loans), and embassies may require them for visa applications.
5.  **Financial Discipline:** Timely filing ensures you maintain a clear and transparent financial record, which is good for your overall financial health.

## Common confusions cleared up

**Myth:** Only people who owe tax need to file an ITR.
**Fact:** Even if you don't owe any tax, or if tax has already been deducted from your salary (TDS), you might still be required to file an ITR if your gross total income (before deductions) exceeds the basic exemption limit. Also, filing helps you claim refunds and carry forward losses.

**Myth:** All individual taxpayers have the same ITR filing deadline.
**Fact:** Not anymore. For AY 2026-27, deadlines are staggered. Most salaried individuals and pensioners have until July 31, 2026. However, individuals with business income not requiring an audit have until August 31, 2026. Businesses requiring an audit have even later deadlines.

**Myth:** If I make a mistake, I can't correct my ITR.
**Fact:** You can file a "revised return" if you discover errors after submitting your original ITR. The deadline for filing a revised return for AY 2026-27 is March 31, 2027. There's also an "updated return" (ITR-U) option for certain situations, which allows you to update your return even later, though it comes with additional tax and conditions.

## FAQ

### What is the basic exemption limit for individuals for FY 2025-26?
Under the old tax regime, the basic exemption limit for individuals below 60 years is ₹2.5 lakh. For those aged 60 to 80, it's ₹3 lakh, and for those above 80, it's ₹5 lakh. Under the new tax regime, the basic exemption limit is ₹3 lakh for all individuals.

### What is Form 16 and why is it important?
Form 16 is a certificate issued by your employer that provides details of the salary paid to you and the tax (TDS) deducted from your salary during the financial year. It is crucial for salaried individuals to file their ITR as it contains income and TDS details.

### Can I file my ITR after the due date?
Yes, you can file a "belated return" after the due date, but it will attract a late filing fee under Section 234F (₹1,000 or ₹5,000, depending on your income). You also lose the ability to carry forward certain losses and may face interest on unpaid tax.

### What if I choose the old tax regime this year?
The new tax regime is the default from the Income Tax Act 2025. If you wish to opt for the old tax regime to claim various deductions and exemptions, you must explicitly choose it while filing your ITR. For those with business income, this might involve submitting Form 10-IEA.

### Where can I find the official ITR forms?
The official ITR forms for AY 2026-27 will be available on the Income Tax Department's e-filing portal (www.incometax.gov.in). It's important to use the latest forms for the correct assessment year.
