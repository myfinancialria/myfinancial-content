# Pre-Market Brief — 23 October 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets saw US tech stocks decline significantly, while Asian markets were mixed.
*   GIFT Nifty is signalling a notable gap-down open for the Indian benchmark Nifty 50.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers yesterday, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support.
*   Broader markets, especially smallcap and midcap indices, experienced a sharp selloff yesterday, raising valuation concerns.
*   Nifty's CPR pivot at 22519.92 will be a crucial level to watch for market direction.
*   The ongoing global tech selloff and FII outflows remain key risks for today's session.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Overnight, US markets closed mixed, with technology stocks leading the decline. The Nasdaq plunged over 2.5%, and the S&P 500 also fell, reflecting concerns about macroeconomic instability and market volatility. The Dow Jones, however, managed a marginal gain. In Asia this morning, markets are showing a mixed picture, with the Nikkei 225 slightly down, while the Hang Seng and Shanghai Composite posted gains. This global backdrop suggests a cautious to negative start for Indian equities, particularly with the tech sector under pressure.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee weakened against the US Dollar, with USD/INR rising by 0.64% to 94.92. The Dollar Index also saw a slight increase of 0.18% to 101.80. In commodities, Brent Crude declined over 3% to $109.35, while WTI Crude edged up by 1.02% to $100.48. Gold prices fell by 0.68% to $4032.70, and Silver also dropped by 1.30%. The US 10-Year Yield decreased by 3.75% to 4.95%.

**Interpretation:** A rising USD/INR could provide a tailwind for Indian IT and pharmaceutical exporters but puts pressure on importers. The fall in Brent crude is generally positive for Indian oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors, potentially easing input cost pressures. The decline in the US 10-year yield is typically seen as positive for equity markets globally, as it can reduce the attractiveness of bonds and potentially ease FII outflow concerns.

**Interpretation:** For Nifty, the highest Call Open Interest (OI) at 23000 suggests that option sellers expect this level to act as a strong ceiling for the index. Conversely, the highest Put OI at 22000 indicates that option buyers are defending this level as a potential floor. A PCR of 0.91 for Nifty leans slightly cautious, suggesting more call writing than put writing. Bank Nifty and Sensex, with PCRs above 1 (1.07 and 1.20 respectively), show a more supportive or neutral-to-bullish bias from the options market. Combining with technicals, Nifty's immediate resistance could be around 22500-22600 (CPR Top/R1 and Put OI at 22500), while strong support is likely around 22000.

## Stock & sector news that matters
*   **Broader markets crash! Small, midcap indices tumble up to 4%. Why analysts are sounding the alarm**
    Smallcap and midcap stocks plunged up to 4% yesterday, extending a sharp weekly selloff. **Why it matters:** This indicates a significant correction in the broader market, driven by concerns over stretched valuations. Investors should exercise caution in these segments, as analysts warn that current valuations might be difficult to justify.
*   **Nasdaq CEO flags IPO slowdown amid market volatility**
    The IPO market is facing challenges due to macroeconomic instability and fluctuating market conditions, causing many firms to delay or abandon their public offerings. **Why it matters:** This reflects a broader global risk-off sentiment and could impact investor appetite for new listings in India as well, potentially affecting companies like Infinx Technology Solutions which is planning a $300 million IPO.
*   **Cyient shares rally 9% after Q2 revenue rises 25%; board declares Rs 12/sh interim dividend**
    Cyient shares surged yesterday after the company reported a 25% year-on-year rise in consolidated revenue for Q2 FY27, despite a decline in net profit. The board also declared an interim dividend of ₹12 per share. **Why it matters:** This positive reaction to revenue growth and dividend declaration could provide some support to the IT services sector, despite the broader tech selloff.
*   **Nippon Silver ETF, other silver ETFs slide up to 4% as silver prices tumble. Here’s why**
    Silver ETFs, including Nippon Silver ETF, fell up to 4% as India permitted 6,500 metric tonnes of silver imports over six months, easing domestic supply tightness. **Why it matters:** This news directly impacts silver prices and related ETFs, suggesting potential for further price adjustments due to increased supply and broader market factors like higher interest rates.
*   **Rs 9,000 crore wiped out in a day: Why Swiggy investors are losing patience with Instamart**
    Swiggy shares plunged yesterday, wiping out around ₹9,000 crore in market value, as widening losses from its Instamart quick commerce business overshadowed growth in food delivery. **Why it matters:** This highlights investor scrutiny on profitability, especially for high-growth, loss-making new-age companies. It could impact sentiment for other listed or upcoming tech/startup IPOs.
*   **Havells India stock drops to a new 52-week low after Q2 results. What’s worrying investors?**
    Havells India shares fell about 6% yesterday to a new 52-week low as investors reacted to margin pressures from rising competition in cables and wires, continued losses at its Lloyd consumer durables segment, and higher input costs. **Why it matters:** This indicates sector-specific challenges within consumer durables and electricals, with competitive pressures and input costs impacting profitability.
