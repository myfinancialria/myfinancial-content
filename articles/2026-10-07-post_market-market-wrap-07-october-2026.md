# Market Wrap — 07 October 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed lower today, snapping a two-session rally.
- The market's decline was largely influenced by the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) policy rate hike and its stance on 'calibrated tightening'.
- Chennai Petroleum Corporation Ltd. emerged as the top gainer in the Nifty 500 pack, surging over 14%.
- National Aluminium Co. Ltd. was the biggest loser among Nifty 500 stocks, dropping nearly 5%.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers in the cash market, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong buying support.
- F&O positioning data is unavailable for today's wrap and will be included from the next session.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equity markets witnessed a downward trend today, with the Nifty 50 closing below the 22,600 mark and the Bank Nifty also ending in the red. The indices opened lower and largely traded with a negative bias throughout the session, reflecting investor caution following the RBI's policy announcement.

| Index | Close | Change | High | Low |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nifty 50 | 22603.05 | -0.76% | 22717.65 | 22546.30 |
| Bank Nifty | 55055.55 | -0.13% | 55340.70 | 54636.20 |
| Nifty Midcap 100 | 59382.60 | -0.63% | 59718.55 | 59200.05 |
| Nifty Smallcap 100 | 19506.95 | +0.30% | 19578.10 | 19358.80 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, rose by 2.06% to 13.89, indicating an increase in expected market fluctuations.

## What moved the market — the factors
The primary driver behind today's market decline was the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) decision to raise the policy rate for the first time since February 2023 and its shift to a stance of 'calibrated tightening'. This move, aimed at stabilizing the 'undervalued' Rupee and addressing inflation concerns, led to investor apprehension, causing the Nifty 50 to drop over 173 points and the Sensex to fall 429 points, snapping a two-session rally.

Global cues also played a significant role, with a global bond market decline seeing UK 30-year gilt yields hit a 28-year high, driven by inflation and government spending. In the US, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq retreated from record highs amidst rising Treasury yields and oil prices, adding to the cautious sentiment.

FIIs continued their selling spree in the Indian cash market, offloading ₹2,961 crore, which further pressured the indices. However, DIIs provided robust support, pumping in ₹5,089 crore, cushioning a steeper fall. Sectorally, the Metal index was the biggest drag, falling 2.33%, with heavyweights like National Aluminium and Adani Enterprises among the top losers. Despite a rating upgrade, Adani Enterprises also saw a decline, reflecting broader market weakness.

## News that moved stocks
**RBI's Policy Stance and Rupee Intervention**
- **RBI says it will ensure 'undervalued' Rupee finds its correct level**: The Reserve Bank of India announced its intention to stabilize the undervalued rupee, which recently hit a five-month low. This comes after the RBI raised its policy rate for the first time since February 2023, signaling a calibrated tightening approach to monetary policy. This news impacted the overall market sentiment and currency markets.

**Corporate Developments**
- **Ola Electric board approves rights issue at 26% discount; shareholders to pay in two calls**: Ola Electric announced a rights issue at ₹27 per share, a significant discount to its market value, aiming to raise up to ₹1,000 crore. Investors will pay 60% upfront, with the issue opening on October 8. This news is significant for Ola Electric shareholders.
- **Adani Enterprises gets rating upgrade from CARE Ratings to AA; Stable; shares up 21% in 2026**: CARE Ratings upgraded Adani Enterprises' long-term rating to CARE AA; Stable, citing stronger financial flexibility. While a positive long-term development, the stock still ended as a top loser today amidst broader market weakness.
- **Sun Pharma to consider ₹15,000 crore NCD issue; board meeting on 12 October**: Sun Pharmaceutical Industries will hold a board meeting on October 12 to approve the issuance of non-convertible debentures worth up to ₹15,000 crore to fund a bridge loan for acquiring Organon. This news is important for Sun Pharma investors.

**Global Market Concerns**
- **UK 30-year gilt yields hit 28-year high in global selloff**: British 30-year bond yields surged to their highest level since January 1998, driven by ongoing inflation and substantial government borrowing. This global bond market decline contributed to the cautious sentiment in Indian markets.
- **Gold slides to two-month low as robust dollar, yields add pressure**: Gold prices fell to a two-month low due to a strong US dollar and rising Treasury yields. This reflects a broader shift in global asset markets, impacting commodity-related stocks and investor sentiment.

**Regulatory Caution**
- **NSE cautions investors over pricey overseas ETFs as demand surges**: The NSE has warned investors about the increasing risks associated with international exchange-traded funds (ETFs) due to steep premiums. This caution highlights potential risks for retail investors venturing into overseas investment avenues.

Looking at the 5-day history, FIIs have been consistent net sellers, with outflows ranging from ₹2,961 crore to ₹10,148 crore daily. Conversely, DIIs have consistently provided strong buying support, with daily inflows between ₹5,089 crore and ₹11,272 crore. This trend suggests that domestic investors are actively absorbing the selling pressure from foreign funds.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's market session was largely dominated by the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) monetary policy actions. The RBI raised its policy rate for the first time since February 2023 and articulated a stance of 'calibrated tightening'. This move was aimed at addressing the 'undervalued' Rupee, which had reached a five-month low, and managing inflationary pressures.

The market reacted negatively to this development, with benchmark indices like the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing lower. Investors interpreted the rate hike and the tightening stance as a signal of higher borrowing costs and a potentially slower economic growth outlook, leading to profit-booking across various sectors. The Metal, Realty, and Auto sectors were particularly hit, experiencing significant declines, while PSU Banks showed some resilience. The overall cautious sentiment was also exacerbated by global factors such as rising bond yields and a strong US dollar.
