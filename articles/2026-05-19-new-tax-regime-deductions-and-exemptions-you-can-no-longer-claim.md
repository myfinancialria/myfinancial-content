# New Tax Regime: Deductions and Exemptions You Can No Longer Claim

**TL;DR**
- India's income tax system offers two regimes: the old, with higher rates but many deductions, and the new, with lower rates but fewer deductions.
- The new regime, introduced in 2020 and made default from FY 2023-24, simplifies taxation by removing most common exemptions and deductions.
- Key benefits like Section 80C, House Rent Allowance (HRA), Leave Travel Allowance (LTA), and the standard deduction are unavailable in the new regime.
- Interest paid on housing loans (Section 24b) and employer contributions to NPS (Section 80CCD(2)) are also largely impacted or restricted.
- Choosing between regimes requires a careful comparison of your gross income, potential deductions, and the tax liability under each.

## What this means in plain terms

For years, Indian taxpayers used a system where they could reduce their taxable income by claiming various deductions and exemptions. These included investments in provident funds, insurance premiums, home loan interest, and allowances like HRA. This system, now referred to as the 'old tax regime,' allowed individuals to significantly lower their tax burden if they planned their finances well.

However, in 2020, the government introduced an alternative – the 'new tax regime'. This regime aimed to simplify taxation by offering lower income tax rates across various slabs. The trade-off? Most of the popular deductions and exemptions that taxpayers relied on were removed. From the financial year 2023-24, the new tax regime has become the default option for individuals, meaning if you don't explicitly choose the old regime, you will automatically be taxed under the new one. This shift necessitates a clear understanding of what you gain in lower rates versus what you lose in tax-saving opportunities.

## Understanding the Two Tax Regimes

The core difference between the old and new tax regimes lies in their philosophy: saving through investments versus lower base rates.

Under the **old tax regime**, individuals pay tax at higher slab rates but can significantly reduce their taxable income by claiming a multitude of deductions and exemptions. These include popular sections like 80C (for investments in PPF, ELSS, life insurance premiums, etc.), 80D (health insurance premiums), 24b (interest on home loans), HRA, LTA, and the standard deduction for salaried employees. This regime encourages specific savings and spending patterns to avail tax benefits.

The **new tax regime**, on the other hand, offers a simplified structure with lower tax rates across more income slabs. For instance, the number of slabs was reduced, and rates were adjusted downwards. The primary intention behind this regime was to encourage taxpayers to move away from tax-driven investments and instead focus on financial decisions based purely on their merit. While it promises lower tax outgo for those with minimal deductions, it fundamentally changes how many Indians plan their taxes and investments. From FY 2023-24, the new regime is the default, but taxpayers retain the flexibility to choose the old regime if it proves more beneficial. This choice can be made annually for salaried individuals and individuals with no business income. For those with business income, the choice is generally a one-time decision, with limited options to switch back. More details can be found on the Income Tax India website (https://incometax.gov.in).

## Key Deductions and Exemptions Lost in the New Regime

The most significant aspect of switching to the new tax regime is foregoing a substantial list of tax-saving avenues. Here are the major ones that are no longer available:

### Section 80C, 80CCC, 80CCD(1)
This is arguably the biggest loss for most Indian taxpayers. Section 80C allows deductions up to ₹1.5 lakhs for various investments and expenses, including:
-   Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) contributions
-   Public Provident Fund (PPF) contributions
-   Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS) mutual funds
-   Life insurance premiums
-   Children's tuition fees
-   Principal repayment of home loan
-   National Savings Certificates (NSC)
-   Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)
-   Senior Citizen's Savings Scheme (SCSS)
Sections 80CCC (for pension funds) and 80CCD(1) (for employee's contribution to NPS) are also unavailable.

### Standard Deduction
Salaried individuals and pensioners can no longer claim the standard deduction of ₹50,000. This was a straightforward deduction available without any specific investment proof.

### House Rent Allowance (HRA)
For those living in rented accommodation, HRA is a significant exemption that reduces taxable income. This exemption is not available in the new regime.

### Leave Travel Allowance (LTA)
The exemption for LTA, which allows employees to claim tax benefits on travel expenses for themselves and their families, is also removed.

### Interest on Housing Loan (Section 24b)
Interest paid on a housing loan for a self-occupied property, up to ₹2 lakhs annually, cannot be claimed as a deduction under Section 24b in the new regime. For let-out properties, the interest deduction is also restricted.

### Section 80D (Health Insurance Premiums)
Premiums paid for health insurance for self, family, and parents, which could be deducted under Section 80D, are not allowed.

### Other Notable Deductions/Exemptions Lost:
-   **Section 80E:** Interest on education loan.
-   **Section 80G:** Donations to certain charities.
-   **Section 80TTA/80TTB:** Interest from savings accounts for individuals below 60/senior citizens.
-   **Children's Education Allowance & Hostel Expenditure Allowance:** These minor allowances are also removed.
-   **Professional Tax:** The deduction for professional tax paid is no longer available.

## Limited Deductions Still Available in the New Regime

While the new regime significantly curtails deductions, a few key ones remain, primarily aimed at social security and specific allowances:

### Employer's Contribution to NPS (Section 80CCD(2))
This is a crucial exception. While your own contribution to NPS (under 80CCD(1)) is not deductible, the employer's contribution to your NPS account, up to 10% of your basic salary + Dearness Allowance (DA), remains deductible under Section 80CCD(2) in the new tax regime. For central government employees, this limit is 14%. This is a significant benefit for salaried individuals whose employers contribute to their NPS. The PFRDA website (https://pfrda.org.in) provides more details on NPS.

### Agniveer Corpus Fund (Section 80CCH)
Contributions made to the Agniveer Corpus Fund are deductible under Section 80CCH in the new tax regime. This is a specific provision for individuals enrolled in the Agnipath Scheme.

### Transport Allowance for Persons with Disability
Transport allowance is still exempt for employees who are blind or orthopaedically handicapped.

### Conveyance Allowance for Performance of Duties
Allowance granted to meet the expenditure on conveyance in performing duties of an office or employment of profit, to the extent it is incurred for that purpose.

### Daily Allowance
Allowance granted to meet the ordinary daily charges incurred by an employee on account of absence from his normal place of duty.

These limited deductions mean that for most taxpayers, the decision to opt for the new regime will hinge primarily on whether the lower tax rates outweigh the loss of the extensive deductions available under the old regime.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 35, a salaried professional in Gurugram, earning ₹28 Lakhs CTC per year.** She pays ₹30,000 monthly rent.
Her salary breakdown and potential deductions under the old regime:
-   **Basic + DA:** ₹14,00,000
-   **HRA:** ₹7,00,000
-   **Special Allowance:** ₹6,00,000
-   **EPF Contribution (employee's):** ₹1,68,000 (12% of Basic+DA)
-   **Home Loan Interest:** ₹2,00,000 (for a self-occupied property)
-   **Health Insurance Premium (80D):** ₹25,000
-   **Other 80C investments (ELSS, PPF):** ₹1,00,000
-   **Professional Tax:** ₹2,500

**Calculation under Old Tax Regime:**

1.  **Gross Salary:** ₹28,00,000
2.  **Less: Exemptions**
    *   HRA Exemption: Minimum of (Actual HRA: ₹7,00,000, Actual Rent Paid - 10% of Basic+DA: ₹3,60,000 - ₹1,40,000 = ₹2,20,000, 50% of Basic+DA: ₹7,00,000). So, HRA exemption = ₹2,20,000.
3.  **Less: Standard Deduction:** ₹50,000
4.  **Less: Professional Tax:** ₹2,500
5.  **Gross Taxable Income (after HRA, SD, PT):** ₹28,00,000 - ₹2,20,000 - ₹50,000 - ₹2,500 = ₹25,27,500
6.  **Less: Chapter VI-A Deductions**
    *   80C (EPF + Other investments): Max ₹1,50,000 (Priya's total is ₹1,68,000 + ₹1,00,000 = ₹2,68,000, but capped at ₹1,50,000)
    *   80D (Health Insurance): ₹25,000
    *   24b (Home Loan Interest): ₹2,00,000
    *   **Total Deductions:** ₹1,50,000 + ₹25,000 + ₹2,00,000 = ₹3,75,000
7.  **Net Taxable Income (Old Regime):** ₹25,27,500 - ₹3,75,000 = ₹21,52,500

**Tax Calculation (Old Regime - FY 2023-24 rates):**
-   Up to ₹2,50,000: Nil
-   ₹2,50,001 - ₹5,00,000: 5% on ₹2,50,000 = ₹12,500
-   ₹5,00,001 - ₹10,00,000: 20% on ₹5,00,000 = ₹1,00,000
-   ₹10,00,001 - ₹21,52,500: 30% on ₹11,52,500 = ₹3,45,750
-   **Total Income Tax:** ₹12,500 + ₹1,00,000 + ₹3,45,750 = ₹4,58,250
-   Add 4% Health & Education Cess: ₹4,58,250 * 4% = ₹18,330
-   **Total Tax Payable (Old Regime):** ₹4,58,250 + ₹18,330 = **₹4,76,580**

**Calculation under New Tax Regime (Default from FY 2023-24):**

1.  **Gross Salary:** ₹28,00,000
2.  **Less: Standard Deduction (not allowed):** ₹0
3.  **Less: HRA Exemption (not allowed):** ₹0
4.  **Less: Other Deductions (80C, 80D, 24b, PT not allowed):** ₹0
5.  **Net Taxable Income (New Regime):** ₹28,00,000

**Tax Calculation (New Regime - FY 2023-24 rates):**
-   Up to ₹3,00,000: Nil
-   ₹3,00,001 - ₹6,00,000: 5% on ₹3,00,000 = ₹15,000
-   ₹6,00,001 - ₹9,00,000: 10% on ₹3,00,000 = ₹30,000
-   ₹9,00,001 - ₹12,00,000: 15% on ₹3,00,000 = ₹45,000
-   ₹12,00,001 - ₹15,00,000: 20% on ₹3,00,000 = ₹60,000
-   Above ₹15,00,000 (i.e., ₹15,00,001 - ₹28,00,000): 30% on ₹13,00,000 = ₹3,90,000
-   **Total Income Tax:** ₹15,000 + ₹30,000 + ₹45,000 + ₹60,000 + ₹3,90,000 = ₹5,40,000
-   Add 4% Health & Education Cess: ₹5,40,000 * 4% = ₹21,600
-   **Total Tax Payable (New Regime):** ₹5,40,000 + ₹21,600 = **₹5,61,600**

In Priya's case, with significant deductions (HRA, 80C, 24b), the **old tax regime results in a lower tax liability of ₹4,76,580** compared to ₹5,61,600 under the new regime. This is a difference of ₹85,020, clearly indicating that for individuals with substantial deductions, the old regime remains more beneficial.

## What to do this week

1.  **Gather Your Documents:** Collect all proofs of income, investments, and expenses for the current financial year. This includes salary slips, rent receipts, home loan statements, health insurance premium receipts, and investment proofs for 80C.
2.  **Calculate Your Deductions:** List out all the deductions and exemptions you are eligible for under the old tax regime. Sum up your potential claims under 80C, HRA, home loan interest, 80D, and the standard deduction.
3.  **Run a Tax Comparison:** Use an online tax calculator (many available on financial portals or the Income Tax India website) to compare your estimated tax liability under both the old and new regimes. Input your gross income and then apply your deductions for the old regime scenario.
4.  **Inform Your Employer:** Based on your comparison, inform your employer about your preferred tax regime choice for the current financial year. This ensures accurate TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) deductions from your salary.
5.  **Review Financial Goals:** Reflect on whether your current investment strategy is primarily tax-driven. If the new regime makes more sense for you, consider if you still want to continue certain tax-saving investments or if you'd rather invest in options that align purely with your financial goals, irrespective of tax benefits.

## FAQ

### ### Is the new tax regime mandatory for everyone?
No, the new tax regime is the default option from FY 2023-24, but taxpayers can still choose to opt for the old tax regime if it is more beneficial for them.

### ### Can I switch between the old and new tax regimes every year?
Yes, salaried individuals and those without business income can choose between the old and new regimes each financial year when filing their Income Tax Return.

### ### What is the main benefit of the new tax regime?
The main benefit is lower income tax rates across various slabs and a simplified tax filing process due to the absence of most deductions and exemptions.

### ### Does the new tax regime impact my employer's contribution to NPS?
No, the employer's contribution to your NPS account (up to 10% of Basic+DA, or 14% for central government employees) remains deductible under Section 80CCD(2) even in the new tax regime.

### ### Is the standard deduction available in the new tax regime?
No, the standard deduction of ₹50,000 for salaried individuals and pensioners is not available under the new tax regime.

### ### What if I have a home loan? Which regime is better?
If you have a significant home loan, especially for a self-occupied property where you claim up to ₹2 lakhs interest under Section 24b, the old tax regime is usually more beneficial, as this deduction is not allowed in the new regime.

### ### Does the new regime affect tax on capital gains?
No, the new tax regime primarily impacts tax on income from salary, house property, and other sources. Capital gains taxation rules remain largely unchanged and are independent of the choice between old and new tax regimes.
