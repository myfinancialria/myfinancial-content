# Pre-Market Brief — 18 November 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets ended mixed, with US indices closing lower while Asian markets showed a mixed trend.
*   GIFT Nifty signals a cautious to negative open for Indian equities.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continue their strong buying spree, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) have been net sellers.
*   Government is reportedly weighing tax tweaks to boost cash equity trading and curb derivatives.
*   Nifty 50's CPR indicates a narrow range, suggesting potential for a trending day, with 25250 as a key pivot.
*   The primary risk for today remains global market sentiment and potential policy shifts on taxation.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Overnight, major US indices closed in the red, reflecting a cautious sentiment among investors. The Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all registered declines. In Asia this morning, markets presented a mixed picture, with Japan's Nikkei 225 showing a slight gain, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng dipped, and Shanghai Composite saw a marginal rise. This mixed global backdrop suggests a lack of strong directional cues from international markets for the Indian open.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) strengthened against the US Dollar, while the Dollar Index saw a slight uptick. Crude oil prices, both Brent and WTI, witnessed significant declines, which is a positive development. Gold and Silver also saw considerable drops. The US 10-year Treasury yield eased slightly.

*   **USD/INR:** 93.44 (-0.56%)
*   **Dollar Index:** 103.77 (+0.27%)
*   **Brent Crude:** 89.37 (-4.05%)
*   **WTI Crude:** 83.28 (-3.17%)
*   **Gold:** 3772.70 (-2.37%)
*   **Silver:** 45.67 (-3.41%)
*   **US 10Y Yield:** 5.10 (-0.35%)

**Interpretation:** A stronger Rupee (USD/INR down) is generally positive for importers and could ease imported inflation concerns. The sharp fall in crude oil prices is a significant tailwind for India, potentially reducing input costs for sectors like paints, aviation, and oil marketing companies, while also easing inflationary pressures and improving the current account deficit. Lower US 10-year yields could reduce the attractiveness of dollar assets, potentially supporting FII flows into emerging markets like India, and also contribute to a rise in Indian bond prices.

1.  **SpiceJet board approves up to Rs 5,500 crore fundraise amid operational challenges; stock hits 5% upper circuit**
    SpiceJet's board has approved a plan to raise up to ₹5,500 crore, leading to the stock hitting its upper circuit for the sixth consecutive session.
    **Why it matters:** This significant fundraise is crucial for the airline's operational stability and expansion plans, potentially boosting investor confidence in the company and the broader aviation sector.
2.  **Govt weighs tax tweaks to boost stock trading, curb derivatives: Report**
    The government is reportedly considering proposals to reduce the Securities Transaction Tax (STT) on cash equities by 50% while increasing levies on derivatives.
    **Why it matters:** This potential policy shift could significantly impact trading volumes and strategies. A reduction in STT on cash equities might encourage more long-term investing, while higher taxes on derivatives could curb speculative activity, affecting brokers and high-frequency traders.
3.  **India bonds rise tracking easing crude oil, US Treasury yields**
    The Indian bond market is experiencing a positive trend, with bond yields decreasing due to falling crude oil prices and lower US Treasury yields.
    **Why it matters:** Lower bond yields generally indicate a more favourable interest rate environment. This is positive for government finances, corporate borrowing costs, and could attract more foreign investment into Indian debt markets.
4.  **Global money rushes back into Indian stocks at record pace**
    Foreign investors have poured a record $9.61 billion into Indian equities so far in November, reversing months of selling, driven by improving earnings prospects and easing crude prices.
    **Why it matters:** This strong resurgence of FII inflows is a major positive for the Indian market, providing liquidity and confidence. It suggests a renewed global interest in Indian growth story, supported by domestic fundamentals and macro factors.
5.  **V2 Retail slides 15% to 2-year low, then stages sharp rebound. What's driving the wild swings in this beaten-down stock?**
    V2 Retail reported a 5.7% year-on-year rise in Q2 net profit to ₹18.21 crore, with revenue increasing to ₹905.59 crore, despite climbing expenses.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

*   **Government's discussion on tax tweaks (STT):** Any official confirmation or further details on the proposed changes to Securities Transaction Tax (STT) could lead to significant market reactions. Markets typically react with volatility to policy changes, with cash market participants potentially seeing it as a positive and derivative traders as a negative.
*   **Crude Oil Price Movement:** The sharp decline in crude oil prices yesterday is a major positive. Continued softening of crude could further boost sentiment for oil-sensitive sectors and the broader economy. Markets generally react positively to falling crude prices, as it eases inflation and improves trade balances.
*   **FII Flow Momentum:** The sustained strong FII inflows are a key support. Any significant shift in this trend, either an acceleration or a slowdown, could influence market direction. Markets tend to rally on strong FII buying and correct on sustained selling.
*   **Global Market Cues:** The mixed global sentiment, particularly the negative close in US markets, will be closely watched. Any further weakness in global indices could trigger cautious trading in India. Markets often mirror the sentiment of major global peers, especially the US.

The Indian market is poised for a cautious to potentially negative open, influenced by weaker US cues and GIFT Nifty's signal.
