# Market Wrap — 12 August 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian benchmark indices witnessed a mixed closing, with Nifty 50 declining marginally while Bank Nifty posted solid gains.
- Weakness in the IT and FMCG sectors, notably due to a significant fall in TCS, weighed on the broader market.
- PSU Banks and Media sectors emerged as the top performers, providing support to the banking index.
- National Aluminium Co. Ltd. was the standout gainer, while Godrej Consumer Products Ltd. was the biggest loser.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net buyers for the day, alongside Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs).
- F&O positioning data is unavailable for today, will be populated from the next session.

## How the market behaved today
Indian markets concluded a mixed session today, with the Nifty 50 closing slightly in the red, while the Bank Nifty posted a healthy gain. The Nifty 50 opened lower and traded with a negative bias for most of the day, finding some support in the latter half but ultimately closing at 24435.95, down 0.15%. In contrast, the Bank Nifty showed strong momentum, closing at its day's high of 57885.85, up 0.77%. Midcap and Smallcap indices also saw a mixed trend.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nifty 50 | 24435.95 | -0.15% | 24473.30 | 24265.95 |
| Bank Nifty | 57885.85 | +0.77% | 57885.85 | 57254.00 |
| Nifty Midcap 100 | 64024.40 | +0.28% | 64026.20 | 63529.10 |
| Nifty Smallcap 100 | 19821.65 | -0.18% | 19898.75 | 19786.00 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, eased by 1.43% to 11.69, indicating a slight reduction in expected market swings.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market's mixed performance today was primarily driven by a tug-of-war between strong sectoral gains and significant individual stock-specific weakness. The Nifty 50's marginal dip can be largely attributed to the sharp decline in the IT sector, particularly due to TCS shares tumbling nearly 4% following the news of N Chandrasekaran's resignation as Tata Sons chairman. This wiped out ₹35,000 crore from its market value and exerted considerable downward pressure on the index. The FMCG sector also contributed to the negative sentiment, with Godrej Consumer Products Ltd. experiencing a substantial fall.

Conversely, the Bank Nifty surged, propelled by robust performance in PSU Banks and the broader banking sector. FIIs and DIIs both remained net buyers today, providing some underlying support to the market, though their net buying figures were relatively modest compared to previous sessions. Global cues also played a role, with investors cautiously awaiting key US CPI data, which kept broader market sentiment subdued across Asian and European markets. Renewed geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and rising oil prices also added to the cautious mood.

## News that moved stocks
- **Corporate Leadership Change Impacts IT Major:** **TCS shares tumble 4% after N Chandrasekaran resigns.** TCS shares fell sharply, wiping out ₹35,000 crore from its market value, following the resignation of N Chandrasekaran as Tata Sons chairman. This news significantly impacted the IT sector and the broader Nifty 50.
- **Strong Q1 Earnings Boost Pharma and Consumer Tech:** **Marksans Pharma shares rally over 9% after Q1 profit spikes 174% YoY.** Marksans Pharma saw its shares surge after reporting a 174% YoY jump in Q1 FY27 profit to ₹159 crore. Similarly, **Lenskart Q1 Results: Profit zooms 182% YoY to Rs 228 crore; revenue up 34%**, showing strong growth in the consumer tech space.
- **Automotive Sector Earnings:** **Tata Motors CV Q1 Results: Net profit soars 83% YoY to Rs 2,560 cr on one-time gain from Tata Capital; revenue jumps 19%.** Tata Motors Commercial Vehicles reported a substantial increase in net profit for Q1 FY27, driven by a one-time gain from Tata Capital.
- **Market Share Gains for Fintech Platform:** **Groww shares climb 2% after July client additions; market share rises to 28.88%.** Groww's shares gained after the company announced adding over 70,000 clients in July, increasing its market share.
- **REITs and InvITs in Focus:** **Embassy Office Parks REIT to enter Nifty 500, become only investment trust in Nifty Midcap 150.** This inclusion highlights the growing prominence of REITs in Indian indices. Additionally, **Investcorp-backed NDR InvIT Trust files draft paper with Sebi for Rs 750 crore IPO**, signaling more activity in the infrastructure investment trust space.

The market breadth was negative today, with 220 advances against 274 declines out of a total of 494 stocks. This indicates that more stocks declined than advanced, suggesting a cautious underlying sentiment despite the mixed index performance.

## FII / DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers in the cash market today, infusing ₹259 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) also recorded net buying of ₹25 crore.

|---|---|

Over the past five trading sessions, FIIs have largely been net buyers, with net inflows on four out of five days, totaling ₹4,217 crore (excluding today). DIIs have shown a more mixed trend, with significant buying on some days and selling on others. Today's modest net buying from both institutional categories suggests continued, albeit cautious, interest in Indian equities.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was primarily driven by a combination of significant corporate news and global cautiousness ahead of key data. The major event impacting the market was the news of N Chandrasekaran's resignation as Tata Sons chairman, which led to a sharp decline in TCS shares and consequently weighed heavily on the IT sector and the Nifty 50. On the earnings front, positive Q1 results from companies like Marksans Pharma and Tata Motors Commercial Vehicles provided some stock-specific and sectoral boosts. However, the overall market remained subdued as investors globally, including in India, awaited the crucial US CPI data, leading to a cautious stance and impacting bond yields and crypto markets. Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and rising crude oil prices also contributed to the cautious sentiment.

## Setup for tomorrow
Tomorrow's market direction will likely be influenced by the overnight developments, especially the US CPI data, which is a key global cue. US and European markets traded cautiously today ahead of this data, and its outcome could set the tone for Asian markets tomorrow. Investors will also keep an eye on crude oil prices, which saw a slight uptick today, and the movement of the US Dollar Index. Sectors like IT and FMCG will be watched for any follow-up moves after today's declines, while banking and PSU bank stocks could continue to show resilience. Key levels for Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty will be crucial to monitor for directional cues.
