# Pre-Market Brief — 28 May 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets presented a mixed picture, with US indices closing marginally higher, while key Asian markets like Nikkei and Hang Seng saw declines.
- Geopolitical tensions, particularly between the US and Iran, escalated overnight, impacting global sentiment and leading to a sharp fall in Gift Nifty futures.
- Crude oil prices surged significantly, with WTI Crude up nearly 3%, potentially impacting India's import bill and inflation outlook.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, offloading over ₹1,000 crore, but Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong counter-support, buying over ₹3,800 crore.
- Retail investors showed strong confidence in Indian equities, increasing exposure to major stocks like Reliance Industries and Infosys in Q4 2026.

## Where global markets closed
Global markets closed with a mixed sentiment yesterday. US indices saw marginal gains, with the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all inching up. European markets also closed slightly positive, with the FTSE 100 recording a modest increase. However, Asian markets showed weakness, as the Nikkei 225 declined, and the Hang Seng index saw a more significant drop of over 1.3%. The Shanghai Composite, in contrast, managed a slight gain. The overall global tone appears cautious, influenced by geopolitical developments and varying regional performances.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) strengthened slightly against the US Dollar, closing at ₹95.68, a gain of 0.23%. This could offer some relief on the import front. Conversely, the Dollar Index, which measures the dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies, saw a modest increase of 0.17% to 99.38.

In the commodities space, crude oil prices witnessed a significant rally. Brent Crude rose by 0.65% to $94.90, while WTI Crude surged by a substantial 2.97% to $91.31. This sharp rise in oil prices is a key concern for India, a net importer of crude, as it could impact the country's current account deficit and potentially fuel inflationary pressures.

Precious metals, however, saw a decline. Gold fell by 0.85% to $4409.50, and Silver dropped by 2.01% to $73.10. This could be attributed to a shift in investor sentiment or profit-booking after recent rallies.

The US 10-Year Yield decreased by 0.27% to 4.48%. A falling US yield typically makes emerging markets like India more attractive to foreign investors, as it reduces the appeal of US dollar-denominated safe-haven assets.

## Overnight news that matters
**Gift Nifty tumbles nearly 2% after US-Iran exchange strikes in latest escalation**
Fresh US strikes on an Iranian military site have intensified geopolitical tensions, causing Gift Nifty 50 futures to fall sharply. This matters to Indian markets as it suggests a likely significantly lower opening today, reflecting heightened global risk aversion and concerns over potential disruptions to oil supplies.

**Bitcoin trades at $73K amid US-Iran tensions; ETF outflows top $2.5 billion in 2 weeks**
Bitcoin and Ethereum experienced significant drops as rising US-Iran tensions and substantial crypto liquidations impacted investor sentiment, with over $700 million in crypto liquidated in a single day. This news highlights a broader risk-off sentiment in global markets, where even alternative assets like cryptocurrencies are feeling the heat of geopolitical instability, which could spill over into equity markets.

**Reliance Industries, Infosys, among 10 stocks which saw highest buying by retail investors in Q4**
Despite volatile markets, rising crude prices, rupee weakness, and geopolitical tensions, retail investors increased their exposure to major Indian stocks during Q4 2026, signaling confidence in long-term prospects. This indicates robust domestic investor confidence in large-cap Indian companies, which could provide a cushion against FII outflows and support market stability.

**Why India's mid & smallcaps are outrunning Nifty 50 & where to invest now? Rajesh Kothari explains**
Indian mid and smallcap stocks are surging ahead of largecaps, driven by robust earnings, with many firms now debt-free and profitable. This trend suggests a strong underlying health in a broader segment of the Indian market, indicating potential opportunities for investors looking beyond the frontline indices and highlighting the importance of stock-specific analysis.

**Commodity upcycle: Why copper and aluminium could explode in coming quarters. Should Indians invest?**
Market attention is shifting towards industrial commodities like copper and aluminium, with analysts believing structural supply shortages, booming AI, and energy-transition demand could drive significant price increases. This is relevant for Indian markets as it could impact input costs for various industries, benefit companies involved in mining or processing these metals, and potentially signal broader inflationary pressures.

## What to watch in today's session
1.  **Geopolitical Developments:** The escalating US-Iran tensions will be a primary focus. Any further developments could significantly influence global and Indian market sentiment, particularly crude oil prices.
2.  **Crude Oil Prices:** The sharp rise in Brent and WTI crude is a critical factor. Investors should monitor crude price movements as sustained high prices could impact India's economy and corporate earnings.
3.  **FII/DII Flows:** The ongoing trend of FII selling against strong DII buying will be crucial. Continued domestic support is essential to absorb foreign outflows and maintain market stability.
4.  **Mid & Smallcap Performance:** Given the recent outperformance, investors should watch the momentum in the mid and smallcap segments for potential stock-specific opportunities and broader market breadth.
5.  **Global Cues:** While US markets closed slightly positive, the mixed Asian cues and the impact of geopolitical events will set the tone for the Indian open.

## Bottom line
The Indian market is likely to open with a cautious to negative bias today, primarily influenced by the overnight escalation in US-Iran tensions and the sharp fall in Gift Nifty futures. While domestic retail and institutional investor confidence remains robust, the surge in crude oil prices and continued FII selling present headwinds. Investors should remain vigilant regarding geopolitical developments and their potential impact on global commodity markets.
