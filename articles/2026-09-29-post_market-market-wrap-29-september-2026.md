# Market Wrap — 29 September 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed lower today, extending the negative sentiment from a volatile monthly expiry.
- Persistent foreign institutional investor (FII) outflows, coupled with elevated US Treasury yields and crude oil volatility, were key factors weighing on the market.
- Kirloskar Oil Eng Ltd. emerged as the top gainer in the Nifty 500 pack, surging over 12%.
- PB Fintech Ltd. was the biggest loser among Nifty 500 stocks, declining over 6%.
- FIIs remained net sellers in the cash market, while domestic institutional investors (DIIs) provided crucial buying support.
- Data for F&O positioning signal is unavailable for today's session.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equity markets concluded the trading day in the red, with both benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, registering declines. The session saw significant volatility, particularly during the closing auction on monthly expiry, where Nifty's indicative price briefly plunged 2% before recovering. Broader markets also faced selling pressure, with Midcap and Smallcap indices underperforming the benchmarks.

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, eased slightly by 1.69% to close at 13.41, suggesting a marginal reduction in immediate fear, although overall market sentiment remained cautious.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market's downward trajectory today was primarily influenced by a confluence of global and domestic factors. Persistent outflows from Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued to be a significant drag, with FIIs selling over ₹5,300 crore in the cash segment. This selling pressure was exacerbated by elevated US Treasury yields, which make riskier emerging market assets like Indian equities less attractive. Crude oil volatility also played a role in shaping investor sentiment.

Domestically, the market witnessed a volatile monthly expiry session, with the Nifty experiencing a sharp, albeit brief, dip during the closing auction. This contributed to the overall cautious mood. Sectorally, defensive sectors like Metal and Pharma saw some buying interest, while interest rate-sensitive and growth-oriented sectors such as Realty, IT, and Auto faced considerable selling pressure. Heavyweight stocks like Adani Enterprises and Adani Ports provided some support, while financial services stocks like PB Fintech and Kfin Technologies saw significant corrections. The broader market's weak breadth, with declines significantly outnumbering advances, indicated widespread selling across segments.

## News that moved stocks
- **Nifty's Volatile Expiry and Worst September Series in 25 Years:** The Nifty 50 experienced a sharp 2% plunge in its indicative price during the closing auction on monthly expiry, before recovering. This volatility underscored the bearish sentiment that led to Nifty posting its worst September derivatives series in 25 years, falling 6.7% due to foreign selling, elevated crude oil prices, a weak rupee, and rising bond yields.
- **China Rolls Out Measures to Bolster Property Sector:** China introduced new measures to support its struggling property sector and economy, including the central bank lowering its interest rates. This news could have a mixed impact on global markets, potentially easing concerns about China's growth but also highlighting underlying economic challenges.
- **US Market Slips as Rising Treasury Yields Offset AI-Driven Tech Gains:** US stock indices, including the Dow Jones Industrial Average, S&P 500, and Nasdaq Composite, reported losses as increasing government bond yields dampened investor sentiment. This global trend of higher yields impacted Indian markets as well, contributing to FII outflows.
- **India 10-year Bond Snaps Four-Day Losing Run:** Indian government bonds rebounded today, ending a four-day losing streak, as US Treasury yields eased and crude oil prices declined. This provided some relief to the domestic bond market, though concerns about inflation persist.
- **CoinSwitch Swings to Profitability in FY26:** Crypto platform CoinSwitch achieved profitability based on Adjusted EBITDA for FY26, with revenue climbing 150% to ₹324.19 crore. This news highlights the growing maturity of the crypto sector in India.
- **Lloyds Enterprises Settles Listing Rule Violations Case with Sebi:** Sebi settled a case with Lloyds Enterprises and its former executives after a payment of ₹4.16 crore, resolving alleged disclosure and accounting violations.

## FII / DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree in the Indian equity market today, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net buyers, providing crucial support.

| Investor Type | Net Flow (₹ crore) |
|---|---|
| FII | -5,353 |
| DII | 5,189 |

Over the past five trading sessions, FIIs have been net sellers for four out of five days, indicating a consistent withdrawal of foreign capital. In contrast, DIIs have been net buyers every day during this period, actively absorbing the selling pressure from foreign investors. This trend suggests that domestic liquidity is playing a vital role in cushioning the market from sharper declines amidst global headwinds.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was largely shaped by the monthly expiry of derivative contracts, which contributed to heightened volatility, particularly in the closing hour. The Nifty's indicative price saw a sudden 2% drop before recovering, reflecting the unwinding and rolling over of positions.

The persistent negative sentiment was further fueled by significant FII outflows of ₹5,353 crore, extending a trend observed over the past few sessions. These outflows were likely influenced by global cues, including the elevated US 10-year Treasury yields (closing at 5.27%) and the strength of the US Dollar Index (closing at 101.50), which make emerging markets less attractive. Crude oil prices, particularly Brent, saw a sharp decline of over 8%, which is generally positive for India as an oil importer, but the overall global risk-off sentiment dominated.

Specific sectors like Realty, IT, and Auto bore the brunt of selling pressure, while Metal and Pharma sectors showed some resilience. News from China regarding new measures to bolster its property sector and economy also contributed to the global narrative, though its direct impact on Indian markets today was likely indirect through overall sentiment.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will continue to be a significant determinant for the Indian market. US markets slipped today due to rising Treasury yields, which could set a cautious tone for Asian markets tomorrow morning. The US 10-year yield remains elevated at 5.27%, and the Dollar Index is firm at 101.50, suggesting continued pressure from foreign capital outflows. Brent crude's sharp decline to $96.84 could offer some relief on the inflation front, but the overall global risk appetite remains subdued.

Domestically, investors will be watching for any follow-through from the volatile monthly expiry. Key levels for Nifty 50, according to some analysts, would be a decisive breach above the 23100 zone to establish conviction. Sectors like Auto might see renewed interest ahead of the festive season, as highlighted by positive outlooks for Maruti Suzuki, TVS Motor, and Mahindra. Individual stocks like Tata Steel, KSB, Power Mech, and TCI are also expected to be in focus due to specific company developments.
