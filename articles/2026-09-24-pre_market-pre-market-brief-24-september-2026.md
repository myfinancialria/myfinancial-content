# Pre-Market Brief — 24 September 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets closed mixed to negative, with US indices down and Asian markets showing a mixed trend.
- GIFT Nifty is signalling a cautious to negative start for Indian equities, pointing to a potential gap-down.
- Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained strong net buyers, providing crucial support, while FIIs were net buyers yesterday after a volatile week.
- PB Fintech shares crashed 26% after IRDAI proposed significant changes to insurance commission structures, impacting the broader financial sector.
- Nifty 50's CPR pivot is at 23209.47, with key resistance at 23400 and strong support at 23000 based on Option Chain data.
- The surge in US 10-year Treasury yields to 5.11% could pose a risk to FII flows and global equity sentiment.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Global markets presented a mixed picture overnight. US indices closed lower, with the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all registering declines, indicating a cautious sentiment in Western markets. In Asia, the Nikkei 225 posted gains, while the Hang Seng and Shanghai Composite traded lower, reflecting concerns over US-China trade talks. This mixed global tone, coupled with a negative bias from GIFT Nifty, suggests a subdued opening for Indian markets.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee weakened against the US Dollar, with USD/INR trading at 95.90, up 0.22%. The Dollar Index remained largely stable, down a marginal 0.03% at 101.07. In the commodities space, Brent Crude saw a significant decline of 4.68% to $98.26, while WTI Crude edged up 0.07% to $92.22. Gold prices were nearly flat, down 0.02% at $4317.60, and Silver gained 0.45% to $64.67. A notable move was the US 10-year Treasury Yield, which surged by 3.04% to 5.11%.

**Interpretation:** A rising USD/INR could be a tailwind for IT and Pharma exporters but puts pressure on importers. The sharp drop in Brent crude is positive for India, potentially easing inflationary pressures and benefiting oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors. However, the significant jump in US 10-year yields is a concern, as higher global yields can make emerging markets less attractive, potentially leading to FII outflows from Indian equities.

## GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling
The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 23203.50, down 0.48%. This indicates a negative bias and points towards a potential gap-down opening for the Nifty 50 today.

## FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape
Yesterday, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers in the Indian cash market, injecting ₹1,617 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their strong buying streak, adding ₹2,341 crore. Over the last five sessions, DIIs have consistently been net buyers, providing significant support to the market, while FII flows have been volatile, with three out of five sessions showing net selling.

## Option-chain signals (OI) — where support & resistance sit
**Nifty:** The Put-Call Ratio (PCR) for Nifty is 0.82, indicating a slightly cautious to neutral sentiment. The highest Call Open Interest (OI), acting as immediate resistance, is observed at 23400 (14.19 million contracts), followed by 23500. On the support side, the highest Put OI is at 23000 (11.60 million contracts), with 23200 also showing significant Put OI.
**Bank Nifty:** The PCR for Bank Nifty stands at 0.9, suggesting a similar cautious-to-neutral stance. Resistance is seen at 57000 (1.51 million contracts) and 56500. Key support levels based on Put OI are at 55000 (0.92 million contracts) and 56000.
**Sensex:** Sensex PCR is 0.7, leaning towards a more cautious or bearish sentiment. Resistance is strong at 74200 (7.11 million contracts) and 74500. Support is identified at 74000 (4.46 million contracts) and 73800.

**Interpretation:** For Nifty, the 23400-23500 zone will act as a strong ceiling, while 23000 is a critical floor that buyers are defending. A PCR below 1 for all indices suggests that call writing (expecting prices to fall or stay below a certain level) is more aggressive than put writing (expecting prices to rise or stay above a certain level). Combined with technical levels, Nifty needs to respect the 23000-23400 range today, with a break on either side potentially dictating the day's trend. Bank Nifty's 55000-57000 range and Sensex's 73800-74500 range will be important to monitor.

## Stock & sector news that matters
- **PB Fintech loses ₹22,700 crore from market value as stock crashes 26% after IRDAI’s reform plans.**
    - PB Fintech shares plunged 26%, wiping out nearly ₹22,703 crore from its market capitalisation after IRDAI proposed changes to insurance commission structures. Jefferies flagged risks to distributors.
    - **Why it matters:** This is a significant negative development for the entire insurance distribution sector and financial services companies with exposure to insurance. The proposed IRDAI reforms could impact revenue models and profitability, leading to continued pressure on stocks like PB Fintech, HDFC Bank, and other financial entities mentioned in the Livemint report as witnessing wealth erosion.
- **NSE IPO: India's biggest exchange lists at ₹1,800 per share; gains 5% after flat debut.**
    - NSE shares rallied after a muted listing at ₹1,800, pushing the exchange into India’s ninth-most valued company with a market cap of ₹4.63 lakh crore.
    - **Why it matters:** The successful listing and subsequent gain of India's largest exchange, NSE, highlights investor appetite for institutional-grade financial infrastructure. While not directly impacting the broader market indices, it reflects confidence in India's capital markets and could draw attention to other listed exchange-related entities.
- **Sebi board meeting: PMS rules overhaul, FPI commodity trades among key decisions to watch out.**
    - Sebi's upcoming board meeting aims to discuss significant changes to portfolio management services (PMS) and settlement regulations. Proposed revisions could allow discretionary portfolio managers to make new decisions.
    - **Why it matters:** Any changes to PMS rules could impact how high-net-worth individuals' portfolios are managed, potentially affecting flows into specific segments. Allowing FPIs into commodity trades could open up new avenues for foreign investment and increase liquidity in commodity derivatives. Investors will watch for outcomes from this meeting.
- **NYSE, Blockchain.com explore tokenised versions of US-listed stocks.**
    - NYSE and Blockchain.com are exploring blockchain-based versions of US-listed stocks and ETFs, as financial institutions accelerate tokenisation efforts while regulators address questions around ownership.
    - **Why it matters:** This global development signals a potential shift towards blockchain integration in traditional finance. While a long-term play, it highlights the ongoing evolution of market infrastructure and could eventually influence how Indian exchanges and financial institutions approach digital assets.
- **China, Hong Kong stocks decline as Trump-Xi talks face investor scepticism.**
    - China and Hong Kong stocks dropped on Thursday as investors doubted a meeting between US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping would yield key breakthroughs.
    - **Why it matters:** Geopolitical tensions and trade talks between the US and China continue to influence global market sentiment. Any negative developments or lack of progress can weigh on Asian markets, including India, due to interconnected global trade and investment flows.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
- **Sebi Board Meeting:** Scheduled to discuss significant changes to PMS rules and FPI commodity trades. Markets typically react to policy changes with increased volatility, especially if the changes are perceived to have a broad impact on market participants or investment avenues.
- **US 10-Year Treasury Yields:** The sharp rise in US 10Y yields to over 5% is a critical global event. Historically, higher US yields tend to make dollar-denominated assets more attractive, potentially leading to FII outflows from emerging markets like India and putting pressure on equity valuations.
- **Bitcoin Options Expiry ($16 billion):** A large Bitcoin options expiry on September 25th could lead to increased volatility in the cryptocurrency market. While not directly impacting Indian equities, significant movements in global crypto can sometimes reflect broader risk sentiment among global investors.
- **Upcoming IPOs:** The primary market is bustling with four IPOs opening for public subscription on September 25th. While individual IPOs don't move the broader market, strong subscription numbers can indicate healthy investor sentiment for new listings.
