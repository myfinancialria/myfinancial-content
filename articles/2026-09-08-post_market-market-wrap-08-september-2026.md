# Market Wrap — 08 September 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed in negative territory today.
- Global cues, particularly rising US Treasury yields and elevated crude oil prices due to Middle East tensions, weighed on investor sentiment.
- Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100 indices, however, showed resilience, closing with modest gains.
- GE Vernova T&D India Ltd. was the standout gainer, while The New India Assurance Company Ltd. was the biggest loser among Nifty 500 stocks.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers today, alongside Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), providing some support.
- F&O positioning data is unavailable for today's wrap and will be included from the next session.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equities witnessed a mixed session today, with frontline indices Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing in the red, while broader markets showed strength. The Nifty 50 opened flat and remained under pressure throughout the day, closing at 23635.10, down 0.61%. Similarly, the Bank Nifty also traded negatively, ending at 56777.55, a decline of 0.54%. In contrast, the Nifty Midcap 100 and Nifty Smallcap 100 indices managed to eke out gains, indicating a preference for broader market segments.

| Index | Close | Change | High | Low |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nifty 50 | 23635.10 | -0.61% | 23758.95 | 23623.10 |
| Bank Nifty | 56777.55 | -0.54% | 57044.00 | 56720.45 |
| Midcap 100 | 62915.80 | +0.21% | 62976.60 | 62580.00 |
| Smallcap 100 | 20133.75 | +0.17% | 20142.30 | 20027.25 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, edged up slightly by 0.63% to 11.23, suggesting a minor increase in expected market fluctuations.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market's performance today was largely influenced by a combination of global cues and sector-specific movements. The primary drag on large-cap indices like Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty came from concerns over rising US Treasury yields, which are creeping towards 5%, raising anxieties about corporate borrowing costs and stock valuations globally. Additionally, renewed Middle East tensions pushed crude oil prices to over a six-week high, adding to inflation worries and impacting sentiment.

Despite these global headwinds, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained strong net buyers, injecting ₹567 crore into the market, while Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) also turned net buyers with ₹280 crore, after two consecutive days of selling. This institutional support likely cushioned the fall in benchmark indices and contributed to the positive performance of midcap and smallcap segments.

Sectorally, the banking and IT sectors faced selling pressure, with Bank Nifty and Nifty IT both closing in the red. Financial Services stocks like The New India Assurance Company Ltd., IDBI Bank Ltd., and IFCI Ltd. were among the top Nifty 500 losers, reflecting the weakness in the broader financial space. Conversely, the Media and Pharma sectors saw buying interest, with stocks like PVR INOX Ltd. performing well. The Capital Goods sector also saw significant gains, with GE Vernova T&D India Ltd., Data Patterns (India) Ltd., and Finolex Cables Ltd. featuring among the top Nifty 500 gainers.

## Market breadth
The market breadth was slightly negative today, with 219 advances and 273 declines out of a total of 492 stocks. This indicates that while the broader indices showed some gains, the overall participation in the rally was not widespread, and more stocks ended in the red than in the green.

## FII / DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned net buyers today, injecting ₹280 crore into the Indian equity market. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their buying spree, with a net inflow of ₹567 crore.

| Investor Type | Net Flow (₹ crore) |
|---|---|
| FII | 280 |
| DII | 567 |

Looking at the 5-day history, FIIs have shown a mixed trend, turning net buyers today after two consecutive days of significant selling (₹-3,112 crore on Sep 4 and ₹-2,346 crore on Sep 3). Prior to that, they were strong buyers. DIIs, on the other hand, have been consistent net buyers throughout the last five sessions, providing crucial support to the market. This consistent DII buying, coupled with FIIs turning positive today, suggests underlying domestic confidence despite global uncertainties.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was primarily driven by a confluence of global macroeconomic factors rather than a single defining event. The rising US 10-year Treasury yields, nearing the critical 5% mark, created an overhang on global equity markets, including India. This concern was exacerbated by escalating Middle East tensions, which pushed crude oil prices higher, fueling inflation fears. These global headwinds particularly impacted large-cap indices like Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, which closed in negative territory.

However, the market also saw sector-specific movements and strong domestic institutional buying. The real estate sector, for instance, received positive news regarding its potential GDP contribution and new project launches, indicating a robust outlook. The Media and Pharma sectors also witnessed buying interest, suggesting some rotation of capital. The overall market breadth, though slightly negative, saw midcap and smallcap indices holding their ground, highlighting selective buying in broader market segments.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will continue to be a significant factor. The movement in US Treasury yields and crude oil prices, influenced by geopolitical developments, will be closely watched. The US markets closed mixed today, with the Dow Jones falling, S&P 500 flat, and Nasdaq edging up, reflecting the uncertainty. The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) closed weaker at 94.81, and Brent crude is elevated at $97.98, which could add to inflationary pressures. Investors will also monitor the performance of Asian markets overnight. Key sectors to watch include financials and IT, given their underperformance today, and the resilient midcap and smallcap segments.
