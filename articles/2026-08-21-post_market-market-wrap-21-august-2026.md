# Market Wrap — 21 August 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian equity benchmarks, Nifty 50 and Sensex, ended largely flat today, extending gains for a second straight session but remaining subdued for the week.
- The market's performance was influenced by persistent global concerns, including elevated crude oil prices, rising global bond yields, and Middle East uncertainties.
- Welspun Corp Ltd. emerged as the top Nifty 500 gainer, soaring over 15%.
- Himadri Speciality Chemical Ltd. was the biggest Nifty 500 loser, declining over 7%.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equity benchmarks closed largely flat on Friday, marking a second consecutive session of minor gains despite a subdued weekly performance. The Nifty 50 settled at 24252.00, up a marginal 0.08%, after trading in a narrow range between 24206.80 and 24284.05. The Bank Nifty outperformed slightly, closing at 57761.95 with a gain of 0.46%, maintaining a positive momentum throughout the day.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market today navigated a landscape dominated by global headwinds, leading to a largely flat close for the benchmark indices. Key drivers included persistent concerns over elevated crude oil prices and rising global bond yields, which weighed on investor sentiment. Middle East uncertainties also contributed to the cautious mood.

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers, pulling out ₹583 crore from the cash market, which added to the pressure. However, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong counter-support, injecting a substantial ₹3,538 crore, helping to cushion the market's fall.

Sectoral rotation was evident, with Metal, Banking, and Realty sectors showing strength, while defensive sectors like FMCG and Auto faced selling pressure. Heavyweight stocks within these sectors played a role in their respective index movements. For instance, the Bank Nifty's positive close was supported by gains in banking stocks, while the drag on the Nifty 50 was partly due to declines in FMCG majors like Britannia.

## Market breadth
The market breadth today leaned towards declines, with 267 stocks falling compared to 224 advances out of a total of 491 traded stocks. This suggests that while the benchmark indices ended largely flat, the underlying sentiment was slightly negative, with more stocks losing ground than gaining.

## FII / DII flows
Today, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the Indian cash market, offloading equities worth ₹583 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide strong buying support, with a net inflow of ₹3,538 crore.

| Investor Type | Net Flow (₹ crore) |
|---|---|
| FII | -583 |
| DII | 3,538 |

Looking at the 5-day trend, FIIs have shown mixed activity, with two days of net buying and three days of net selling. Their net selling today marks a shift from their net buying on August 18. DIIs, however, have been consistent net buyers throughout the past five sessions, demonstrating robust domestic support for the market. This consistent DII buying has been a crucial factor in absorbing FII outflows and preventing sharper market corrections.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session lacked a single defining event, with the market's subdued performance largely driven by a confluence of broad global flows and persistent cues. Elevated crude oil prices, fueled by renewed Gulf tensions, continued to be a significant concern, impacting the Indian rupee and overall economic sentiment. Rising global bond yields also contributed to the cautious mood, as seen in European markets. The FII outflows, despite strong DII buying, reflected this global apprehension. No specific Indian economic data release or major corporate event significantly swayed the market today; instead, it was a day of consolidation amidst external pressures.
