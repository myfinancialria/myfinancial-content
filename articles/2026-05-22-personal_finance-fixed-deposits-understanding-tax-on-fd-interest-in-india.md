# Fixed Deposits: Understanding Tax on FD Interest in India

**TL;DR**
- Interest earned on regular Fixed Deposits (FDs) is fully taxable as 'Income from Other Sources' under Indian tax law.
- This interest income is added to your total annual income and taxed according to your applicable income tax slab rate.
- Banks deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) if your FD interest exceeds ₹40,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens) in a financial year.
- You can submit Form 15G or 15H to your bank to avoid TDS if your total income is below the taxable limit.
- Tax-saving FDs under Section 80C offer a deduction on the principal invested, but the interest earned on them remains taxable.

## What this means in plain terms

Fixed Deposits (FDs) are a popular savings option in India, known for their safety and guaranteed returns. However, many depositors are often surprised by how the interest earned on these FDs is treated for income tax purposes. Simply put, the interest you receive from your FDs is not tax-free. It's considered income, just like your salary or business profits, and the government expects its share.

This interest gets added to your total income for the financial year. Depending on which income tax bracket you fall into, a portion of this interest will be paid as tax. This applies whether you opt for cumulative FDs (where interest is reinvested) or non-cumulative FDs (where interest is paid out periodically). Understanding these rules is crucial for effective financial planning, especially for individuals in higher tax brackets or those relying significantly on FD income.

## Taxation of FD Interest: The Basics

Interest earned from Fixed Deposits falls under the head "Income from Other Sources" as per the Income Tax Act, 1961. This means it's not treated as business income, capital gains, or salary. Instead, it's clubbed with any other miscellaneous income you might have, such as rental income from property not used for business, lottery winnings, or interest from savings accounts. The total of all such income, including your FD interest, is then added to your gross total income.

Once your total income is calculated, it is taxed at the slab rates applicable to you for that financial year. For instance, if you are in the 30% tax bracket, every ₹100 of FD interest you earn will attract a tax of ₹30. This applies to both resident Indians and Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), though NRIs have specific TDS rules. It's important to note that the tax liability arises in the year the interest is accrued, even if it's a cumulative FD where you receive the lump sum at maturity. For detailed information, you can refer to the official Income Tax India website ([https://incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in)).

### Cumulative vs. Non-Cumulative FDs and Tax

Whether your FD is cumulative (interest compounded and paid at maturity) or non-cumulative (interest paid monthly, quarterly, or annually), the tax treatment remains the same. The interest is taxable on an accrual basis. This means that even if you haven't physically received the interest from a cumulative FD, it is considered earned and taxable each financial year. Banks typically provide an annual interest certificate detailing the accrued interest for tax filing purposes. For non-cumulative FDs, the interest is taxable in the year it is received.

## TDS on Fixed Deposits: What You Need to Know

Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is a mechanism where the tax is deducted by the payer (in this case, the bank) at the time of making certain payments, including FD interest. For resident individuals, banks are mandated to deduct TDS if the total interest earned from all FDs held with that bank exceeds a certain threshold in a financial year.

Currently, this threshold is ₹40,000 for regular depositors and ₹50,000 for senior citizens (aged 60 years or above). If your interest income crosses this limit, the bank will deduct TDS at a rate of 10% if you have provided your PAN. If you have not provided your PAN, the TDS rate increases to 20%. This TDS amount is then deposited with the government on your behalf. You can claim credit for this TDS when you file your income tax return, adjusting it against your final tax liability. For more on TDS, refer to the CBDT notifications available on the Income Tax India website ([https://incometaxindia.gov.in](https://incometaxindia.gov.in)).

### Avoiding TDS with Form 15G/15H

If your total estimated income for the financial year is below the basic exemption limit (i.e., you have no tax liability), you can submit Form 15G (for non-senior citizens) or Form 15H (for senior citizens) to your bank. By submitting these forms, you declare that your total income is below the taxable threshold and request the bank not to deduct TDS on your FD interest. It's crucial to ensure your declaration is accurate, as providing false information can lead to penalties. These forms need to be submitted annually, typically at the beginning of the financial year or when you open a new FD.

## Tax-Saving FDs: A Different Category

While regular FD interest is fully taxable, there's a specific type of FD known as a "Tax-Saving Fixed Deposit" that offers a benefit under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act. These FDs come with a mandatory lock-in period of five years. The principal amount invested in these FDs, up to a maximum of ₹1.5 lakhs in a financial year, qualifies for a deduction from your gross total income.

However, it's vital to understand a key distinction: only the principal amount invested is eligible for the Section 80C deduction. The interest earned on these tax-saving FDs is still fully taxable as per your income tax slab, just like interest from any other regular FD. This is a common point of confusion for many investors. The benefit lies in reducing your taxable income by the principal invested, not in making the interest tax-free. Premature withdrawal is generally not allowed for tax-saving FDs due to their five-year lock-in.

## Minimising Your FD Tax Burden

For individuals in higher tax brackets, the tax on FD interest can significantly eat into their returns. However, there are legitimate strategies to potentially minimise this burden:

### 1. Splitting FDs Across Family Members

If you have a substantial amount to invest, consider splitting FDs across family members who are in lower income tax brackets or have no taxable income. For instance, you could invest in FDs in the name of your spouse or adult children who have lower income, thereby reducing the overall tax liability on the interest earned. This strategy works best if the funds genuinely belong to them or if you are gifting the funds legally.

### 2. Choosing Non-Cumulative Interest Payouts

For those who rely on regular income from FDs, opting for non-cumulative FDs with monthly or quarterly interest payouts can spread the tax liability over the years. While the interest is still taxable annually on accrual, receiving it periodically might align better with your cash flow needs and allow for better tax planning compared to a large lump sum at maturity.

### 3. Considering Tax-Efficient Alternatives

For investors in the highest tax brackets, FDs might not be the most tax-efficient option. Alternatives like debt mutual funds, especially those held for more than three years, offer indexation benefits which can significantly reduce the effective tax rate on capital gains. Another option could be tax-free bonds issued by government entities, though these are less common now. For long-term goals, Public Provident Fund (PPF) offers EEE (Exempt-Exempt-Exempt) status, meaning contributions, interest, and maturity amounts are all tax-free, though it has a lower interest rate and a 15-year lock-in. Consulting a financial planner can help you explore options best suited for your risk profile and tax bracket.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 42, ₹25L CTC, Mumbai**.
Priya has accumulated ₹15 lakhs from her savings and wants to invest it in a Fixed Deposit for a year at 7% p.a. She is in the 30% income tax slab.

1.  **FD Interest Calculation:**
    *   Principal: ₹15,00,000
    *   Interest Rate: 7% p.a.
    *   Annual Interest: ₹15,00,000 * 7% = ₹1,05,000

2.  **TDS Deduction:**
    *   Since her interest of ₹1,05,000 exceeds the ₹40,000 threshold, the bank will deduct TDS.
    *   TDS @ 10%: ₹1,05,000 * 10% = ₹10,500
    *   Net interest received after TDS: ₹1,05,000 - ₹10,500 = ₹94,500

3.  **Final Tax Liability:**
    *   Priya's total income includes her salary and this FD interest.
    *   Her FD interest of ₹1,05,000 is added to her taxable income.
    *   Since she is in the 30% slab, the actual tax on this interest is: ₹1,05,000 * 30% = ₹31,500.

4.  **Adjusting for TDS:**
    *   Priya has already paid ₹10,500 as TDS.
    *   Her remaining tax liability on this FD interest will be: ₹31,500 - ₹10,500 = ₹21,000.
    *   She will have to pay this additional ₹21,000 when filing her income tax return.

Now, if Priya also decides to invest ₹1.5 lakhs in a 5-year tax-saving FD under Section 80C at 7% p.a.:
*   The ₹1.5 lakhs principal will be deducted from her gross total income, saving her (₹1,50,000 * 30%) = ₹45,000 in tax on her salary income.
*   However, the interest earned on this tax-saving FD (₹1,50,000 * 7% = ₹10,500) will still be added to her total income and taxed at her 30% slab rate, incurring a tax of ₹3,150 on this interest.

This example clearly shows that while tax-saving FDs help with Section 80C deductions, the interest component is always taxable.

## FAQ

### ### Is FD interest fully tax-free if I submit Form 15G/15H?
No, submitting Form 15G/15H only prevents the bank from deducting TDS. The interest earned is still considered taxable income and must be declared in your income tax return. You will pay tax on it if your total income exceeds the basic exemption limit.

### ### What happens if I don't provide PAN to the bank for my FD?
If you do not provide your PAN, the bank will deduct TDS at a higher rate of 20% on your FD interest, instead of the standard 10%, once the threshold is crossed. This can result in a larger upfront tax deduction.

### ### Is interest from tax-saving FDs also taxable?
Yes, while the principal amount invested in a tax-saving FD qualifies for deduction under Section 80C, the interest earned on it is fully taxable as per your income tax slab.

### ### Can NRIs save tax on FD interest in India?
NRIs are subject to TDS on FD interest at a rate of 30% (plus surcharge and cess) in India. However, they may be able to claim tax relief under Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAA) between India and their country of residence.

### ### Do all banks deduct TDS on FD interest?
Yes, all banks and financial institutions offering Fixed Deposits are legally mandated to deduct TDS if the interest paid or accrued exceeds the prescribed limit (₹40,000 for regular citizens, ₹50,000 for senior citizens) in a financial year.

### ### Is there any tax benefit for senior citizens on FD interest?
Yes, senior citizens (aged 60 and above) get a higher TDS threshold of ₹50,000 on FD interest. Additionally, they can claim a deduction of up to ₹50,000 on interest income from FDs and savings accounts under Section 80TTB of the Income Tax Act.
