# Market Wrap — 02 October 2026

**TL;DR**
-   Indian equity benchmarks, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed lower today, marking a broad-based decline across the market.
-   Aggressive selling by Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) for the fifth consecutive session was a significant drag on market sentiment.
-   Schneider Electric Infrastructure Ltd. and INOX India Ltd. were among the top Nifty 500 gainers, both from the Capital Goods sector.
-   PB Fintech Ltd. and Bajaj Auto Ltd. emerged as the biggest Nifty 500 losers, with Bajaj Auto contributing to the Auto sector's sharp decline.
-   FIIs remained net sellers, pulling out ₹9,484 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided crucial support with net buying of ₹10,042 crore.
-   F&O build-up data is unavailable for this session and will be populated from the next wrap.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equities witnessed a broad-based decline today, with the benchmark Nifty 50 closing down by 0.88% at 22,421.95. The Bank Nifty also ended in the red, shedding 0.33% to settle at 54,450.75. The session saw the indices open lower and trend downwards for most of the day, reflecting cautious sentiment and sustained selling pressure. Midcap and Smallcap indices also mirrored the broader market weakness.

| Index | Close | Change | High | Low |
| :---------------- | :---------- | :------- | :--------- | :--------- |
| Nifty 50 | 22421.95 | -0.88% | 22610.60 | 22217.30 |
| Bank Nifty | 54450.75 | -0.33% | 55091.40 | 54066.60 |
| Nifty Midcap 100 | 58732.00 | -1.01% | 59384.60 | 58006.90 |
| Nifty Smallcap 100 | 19058.65 | -0.97% | 19256.40 | 18797.70 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, surged by 7.19% to 14.46, indicating an increase in market nervousness and uncertainty among participants.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market's decline today was primarily driven by persistent selling pressure from Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs), who offloaded a significant ₹9,484 crore from Indian equities. This continued a trend of heavy FII outflows, with September alone seeing them pull out ₹35,860 crore. Despite positive global cues from the US market, where softer jobs data cooled rate-hike bets and pushed the Nasdaq to a record high, FIIs continued to exit Indian markets, likely due to global uncertainties and rising US interest rates.

Domestically, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided a strong counter-balance by buying ₹10,042 crore, their efforts were not enough to absorb the FII selling entirely, leading to a net negative impact. Sectorally, the IT sector showed resilience, gaining over 2%, likely benefiting from the global tech optimism and potentially a weaker rupee. However, heavyweights like the Auto sector, which declined by 3.46% with Bajaj Auto being a significant loser, along with Metal and Media sectors, dragged the broader indices down. The rise in India VIX also reflected heightened investor caution.

## Sectors — winners and losers
The market witnessed a mixed bag today, with the IT sector standing out while others faced significant selling pressure.

**Top 3 gainer sectors:**
-   **IT:** +2.17% – This sector showed strong resilience, likely buoyed by global tech optimism and potentially benefiting from a weaker rupee.
-   **BANK:** -0.33% – While technically in the red, the banking sector showed comparatively less weakness than the broader market.
-   **PHARMA:** -0.48% – Similar to banks, the pharma sector experienced a modest decline, indicating some defensive buying or less selling pressure.

**Top 3 loser sectors:**
-   **AUTO:** -3.46% – This sector was the biggest drag on the market, experiencing heavy selling pressure.
-   **METAL:** -2.35% – Metal stocks faced significant declines, possibly due to global commodity price fluctuations or demand concerns.
-   **MEDIA:** -2.33% – The media sector also saw substantial losses, contributing to the broader market weakness.

## News that moved stocks
-   **Bets on US Federal Reserve rate hike in October fall as job market cools**: The US economy added only 29,000 jobs last month, significantly below forecasts, and the unemployment rate rose to 4.2%. This softer jobs data has led to reduced bets on a US Federal Reserve rate hike in October, which generally provides a positive global sentiment for equities.
-   **Why US stocks are rising: Nasdaq hits record after weaker jobs data cools rate-hike bets**: Following the disappointing jobs data, US stocks rose, with the Nasdaq Composite hitting a record high. Lower Treasury yields and declining oil prices also contributed to this positive market sentiment, which could influence FII flows in the future.
-   **FPIs turn net sellers, pull out Rs 35,860 cr in Sep**: Foreign portfolio investors shed a staggering ₹35,860 crore from Indian equities in September, driven by global uncertainties and rising US interest rates. This selling trend has persisted into October and was a major factor behind today's market weakness.
-   **Sebi penalises SMC Global Securities for compliance lapses**: Sebi levied a penalty of ₹1 lakh on SMC Global Securities for regulatory infractions, specifically the mishandling of client order-placement records. This highlights ongoing regulatory scrutiny in the financial services sector.
-   **Office leasing at record 66.4 mn sq ft in Jan-Sep across top 9 cities, driven by GCCs: CBRE**: Leasing of office spaces in nine major Indian cities reached a record 66.4 million square feet from January to September 2026, an 8% year-on-year increase, primarily driven by Global Capability Centres (GCCs). This positive news indicates robust demand in the real estate sector.
-   **Keventer, Saroj Poddar groups launch Rs 400-cr real estate debt fund**: Keventer Group and Saroj Poddar Group have launched a ₹400-crore investment fund for real estate ventures, focusing on secured debt financing. This development further underscores activity and investment in the real estate sector.

## Market breadth
The market breadth was overwhelmingly negative today, with 382 declines against only 111 advances out of a total of 493 traded stocks. This indicates that the selling pressure was widespread across the market, affecting a large number of stocks beyond just the benchmark indices.

## FII / DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree today, offloading Indian equities worth a net ₹9,484 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, provided strong buying support, with net inflows of ₹10,042 crore.

| Investor Type | Net Flow (₹ crore) |
| :------------ | :----------------- |
| FII | -9,484 |
| DII | 10,042 |

Looking at the 5-day history, FIIs have been consistent net sellers, pulling out funds for five consecutive sessions, with today's outflow being the highest in this period. Conversely, DIIs have consistently been net buyers, absorbing a significant portion of the FII selling, which has helped cushion the market from a sharper decline. This trend suggests that while foreign investors are exiting, domestic money is actively stepping in to support the market.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was primarily driven by a combination of persistent FII selling and mixed global cues. There was no single defining domestic event that dictated market direction. The significant outflow of ₹9,484 crore by FIIs, continuing their trend of selling for the fifth straight day, was the most impactful factor on the Indian market. Globally, the US saw positive sentiment with the Nasdaq hitting a record high after weaker jobs data cooled rate-hike bets, but this positive sentiment did not translate into FII buying in India. Instead, the market reacted to the broad FII outflows, leading to a decline across most sectors, particularly Auto, Metal, and Media, while the IT sector showed some resilience. The rise in India VIX also indicated increased caution among market participants.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead, the Indian market will continue to watch global cues closely. While US markets reacted positively to softer jobs data, the persistent FII selling in India remains a key concern. Overnight, the US Dollar Index saw a slight dip, while crude oil prices (Brent and WTI) also declined, which could be a positive for India's import bill. However, the USD/INR closed higher, indicating a weaker rupee. Investors will monitor the direction of FII flows and global market sentiment, particularly how Asian markets open. Key levels for Nifty 50 around the 22,200 zone will be crucial to watch for support, as suggested by market experts.
