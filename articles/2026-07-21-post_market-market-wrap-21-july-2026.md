# Market Wrap — 21 July 2026

**TL;DR**
-   Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed in the red, extending losses for the day.
-   Heavyweight stocks like HDFC Bank, Reliance Industries, and SBI weighed down the benchmarks.
-   Karur Vysya Bank Ltd. was the top Nifty 500 gainer, surging over 13%.
-   Aditya Birla Sun Life AMC Ltd. emerged as the top Nifty 500 loser, declining over 6.7%.
-   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide support with net buying.

## How the market behaved today
Indian benchmark indices witnessed a day of consolidation with a negative bias, as the Nifty 50 closed below the 24,200 mark. The session saw a largely range-bound movement after a flat open, with benchmarks unable to sustain any upward momentum. While the frontline indices struggled, the broader market showed resilience, with midcap and smallcap indices posting gains.

| Nifty 50 | 24187.70 | -0.21% | 24262.20 | 24135.65 |
| Bank Nifty | 57835.35 | -0.19% | 58228.65 | 57803.85 |
| Nifty Midcap 100 | 62987.60 | +0.30% | 63026.10 | 62749.80 |
| Nifty Smallcap 100 | 19428.05 | +0.53% | 19441.95 | 19335.65 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, eased by 2.93% to 12.60, indicating a slight reduction in market apprehension.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market today was a tale of two halves: benchmarks under pressure while the broader market showed strength. The Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty were dragged down primarily by heavyweight stocks such as HDFC Bank, Reliance Industries, and SBI, which extended losses. Global cues also played a significant role, with escalating Middle East tensions impacting oil prices and creating uncertainty. The Euro zone banks tightening credit standards amid geopolitical risks, as per the ECB survey, added to the cautious sentiment globally.

Despite these headwinds, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their buying spree, providing crucial support to the market and absorbing the selling pressure from Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs). This DII buying, coupled with positive momentum in specific sectors like Realty, Auto, and Metal, helped the Midcap and Smallcap indices close in the green, indicating underlying strength in the broader market.

## News that moved stocks
-   **Adani Group Companies Announce Q1 Results:** Adani Total Gas reported a 14% year-on-year (YoY) decline in consolidated net profit to ₹142 crore, despite a 27% jump in revenue. In contrast, Adani Energy Solutions saw its profit zoom 124% YoY to ₹1,149 crore, with revenue increasing 42%, driven by strong performance in transmission and distribution segments. These contrasting results led to varied reactions in the respective Adani group stocks.
-   **TVS Motor Shares Jump on Strong Q1 Earnings:** TVS Motor Company's shares surged over 6% after the company reported a robust 67% YoY increase in net profit to ₹1,019 crore for Q1, with revenue from operations also rising 33%. This strong performance provided a significant boost to the auto sector.
-   **SBI Funds Management IPO Lists with Premium:** SBI Funds Management listed at ₹613.30 on the National Stock Exchange, a 6.85% premium over its IPO price of ₹574 per share. SBI chairman Setty stated there are no plans for further stake sales in SBI Funds after the IPO.
-   **Indian Rupee Gains Amid Easing Oil Tensions:** The Indian rupee strengthened slightly today, buoyed by the prospect of improved diplomatic relations between the U.S. and Iran, which helped reduce tensions in the oil market. This, coupled with strong capital inflows, supported the currency.
-   **Global Geopolitical Tensions and Market Impact:** The US dollar wavered as markets grappled with escalating Middle East tensions, though hopes for a ceasefire provided some counter-balance. Euro zone banks tightened lending standards due to geopolitical uncertainty, and bond yields ticked higher as elevated oil prices threatened inflation outlook.

The market breadth leaned towards advances today, with 276 stocks advancing against 219 declines out of a total of 495 traded stocks. This indicates a relatively positive sentiment in the broader market, despite the benchmark indices closing lower.

## FII / DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers in the Indian cash equity market today, offloading shares worth ₹376 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their supportive role, injecting ₹1,018 crore into the market.

| Investor Category | Net Activity (₹ crore) |
|:---|:---|
| FII | -376 |
| DII | +1,018 |

Looking at the 5-day history, FIIs have been consistent net sellers, with outflows totalling over ₹9,120 crore in the last five sessions. DIIs, on the other hand, have been consistent net buyers, providing strong counter-support with inflows exceeding ₹9,800 crore over the same period. This trend highlights the domestic institutions' continued confidence and their role in cushioning the market against foreign outflows.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's market activity was largely influenced by a mix of corporate earnings and evolving global geopolitical dynamics. On the corporate front, **TVS Motor Company's strong Q1 results** stood out, with a significant jump in profit and revenue, leading to a notable rally in its stock and providing a boost to the broader auto sector. Conversely, **Adani Total Gas reported a decline in Q1 profit**, which likely weighed on investor sentiment for that particular stock, while **Adani Energy Solutions' impressive profit growth** provided a positive counterpoint within the Adani group. The successful listing of **SBI Funds Management IPO** also garnered attention, indicating investor appetite for new offerings.

Globally, the persistent **Middle East tensions** continued to be a key concern, impacting crude oil prices and contributing to a cautious stance among international investors. The slight gain in the Indian rupee, attributed to hopes of US-Iran diplomacy easing oil market tensions, offered some relief. Overall, while specific earnings provided stock-specific movements, the broader market's performance, particularly the underperformance of benchmarks, was largely driven by the cautious global environment and sustained FII selling.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead, global cues will continue to be crucial for tomorrow's session. US stock futures are showing a rebound, particularly in chip stocks, which could provide some positive momentum. However, the ongoing Middle East tensions and their impact on oil prices (Brent Crude at $90.32, WTI Crude at $83.53) will remain a key watch factor. The Dollar Index is stable at 101.00, while the Indian rupee gained slightly against the dollar, closing at 96.24. Investors will also monitor the US 10-year yield, which ticked higher to 4.60%. Domestically, the performance of sectors like Realty and Auto, which gained today, will be watched, along with any further news on corporate earnings. Key levels for Nifty 50 will be around 24,100 on the downside and 24,250 on the upside.
