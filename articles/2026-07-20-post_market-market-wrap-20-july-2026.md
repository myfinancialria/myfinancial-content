## How the market behaved today
Indian equities witnessed a mixed trading session today, with benchmark indices Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing in the red, while the broader market indices, Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100, managed to post gains. The Nifty 50 closed at 24238.50, down 0.39%, after trading within a narrow range for most of the day. The Bank Nifty saw a sharper decline, ending at 57945.00, down 0.98%. The day's trading saw a generally negative bias for the frontline indices, largely influenced by banking sector weakness.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market today was primarily influenced by a combination of global geopolitical tensions, rising crude oil prices, and significant selling pressure in the banking sector. Escalating Iran-US tensions and surging crude prices dampened investor sentiment, leading to a cautious approach. This global uncertainty also saw the Indian rupee hit a two-month low as oil prices hovered around ninety dollars per barrel.

Heavyweight banking stocks, particularly private sector lenders like Axis Bank and HDFC Bank, experienced notable declines, acting as a major drag on the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty. Conversely, PSU banks showed strong buying interest, helping the PSU Bank index emerge as the top sectoral gainer. Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, offloading ₹376 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided crucial support by net buying ₹1,018 crore, cushioning the market's fall. Despite the weakness in large caps, the broader market, represented by the Nifty Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100, demonstrated resilience, suggesting underlying domestic strength and selective buying interest.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was largely driven by a confluence of global geopolitical concerns and their impact on commodity prices, particularly crude oil. News of escalating Iran-US tensions and the subsequent rise in crude oil prices dampened investor sentiment across the board. This also led to the Indian rupee depreciating to a two-month low against the dollar.

The direct impact was most visible in the banking sector, where major private banks like Axis Bank and HDFC Bank saw significant selling, contributing heavily to the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty's decline. The rise in crude oil prices also unsettled bond markets globally, with US yields climbing. Despite these headwinds, the broader market showed resilience, with midcap and smallcap indices posting gains, and PSU banks emerging as strong performers. The Reserve Bank of India reportedly intervened by unloading dollars to stabilize the rupee amidst the oil price concerns.

Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues will continue to play a significant role. US stock futures gained today as easing oil prices offset lingering Middle East tensions, which could provide some positive momentum. However, the ongoing geopolitical situation and crude oil price movements will remain key factors to watch. The US 10-year yield closed higher at 4.57%, while Brent Crude was up at $88.56. Sectors like banking, especially the performance of private versus PSU banks, and oil-sensitive sectors will be in focus.
