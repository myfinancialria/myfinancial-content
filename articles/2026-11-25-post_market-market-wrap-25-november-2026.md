# Market Wrap — 25 November 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed significantly higher, shrugging off earlier global concerns.
- Strong buying interest from Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) was the primary driver for the market's upward momentum.
- Jupiter Wagons Ltd. emerged as the top gainer in the Nifty 500, surging by 20%.
- APL Apollo Tubes Ltd. was the biggest laggard, falling over 11%.
- FIIs continued their robust buying streak, injecting ₹5,673 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net sellers.
- F&O build-up data is unavailable for today and will be populated from the next session.

## How the market behaved today
The Indian equity market displayed remarkable resilience today, with the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing strongly in the green. After opening with some caution, both indices trended upwards through the day, recovering from early global jitters. The Nifty 50 ended at 25819.25, gaining 1.85%, while the Bank Nifty closed at 63703.25, up 1.79%. The broader market saw a mixed performance, with the Nifty Midcap 100 registering a modest gain, while the Nifty Smallcap 100 ended in negative territory.

India VIX, the volatility gauge, eased slightly by 0.17% to 17.67, indicating a marginal reduction in market anxiety.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market's strong performance today was primarily driven by a combination of robust FII inflows and a calming of global fears, particularly from the US markets. Despite earlier reports of a sharp selloff in AI-related shares and the Nasdaq 100 briefly entering bear-market territory, US markets pared their early losses, which provided a positive cue for Indian equities. Falling US Treasury yields also lifted Indian government bonds, creating a more favourable environment for domestic assets.

Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their strong buying spree, pumping in a significant ₹5,673 crore into the Indian market, effectively counteracting the ₹2,760 crore net selling by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs). This consistent FII support has been a key pillar for the market's upward trajectory.

Sector-wise, there was a clear rotation towards defensive sectors like Fast Moving Consumer Goods (FMCG) and Pharma, which saw substantial gains of 3.79% and 2.38% respectively. Heavyweights like Dabur India and Hindustan Unilever from the FMCG sector contributed significantly to the Nifty's rise. The Banking sector also performed well, aligning with the overall positive sentiment. Conversely, sectors like Media and Metal faced selling pressure, with Media being the biggest laggard, down 4.44%. News of copper prices slumping to an 11-month low likely weighed on metal stocks.

Regulatory developments also played a role, with SEBI easing KYC norms to simplify the client onboarding process, a move that could improve investor sentiment. Discussions around a potential 25 basis points interest rate hike by the RBI in December, despite easing oil prices, also kept market participants watchful.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was largely driven by a combination of global cues and strong FII flows. Early concerns stemming from a sharp selloff in US AI-related stocks and the Nasdaq 100 briefly entering bear-market territory were mitigated as US markets pared their losses. This easing of global fears, coupled with falling US Treasury yields which boosted Indian bonds, set a positive tone.

The consistent and robust buying by FIIs, totaling ₹5,673 crore, provided significant upward momentum, particularly to large-cap indices like the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty. Sectorally, there was a clear rotation, with defensive sectors like FMCG and Pharma witnessing strong buying, while Metal and Media sectors faced headwinds. News regarding copper prices slumping to an 11-month low likely impacted metal stocks. Regulatory news from SEBI, easing KYC norms, was also a positive for market sentiment, suggesting efforts to improve investor participation. Discussions around a potential RBI rate hike in December also kept participants on their toes, but did not deter the overall bullish sentiment.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead, global cues will continue to be important. While US markets pared losses today, concerns about inflation and the Federal Reserve's stance on interest rates remain. The US 10-year yield closed lower at 4.85%, which is a positive for emerging markets. Brent Crude eased to $90.47, while WTI Crude saw a gain. The Dollar Index closed slightly down at 103.67, and the USD/INR strengthened to 92.22.

Investors will be watching for further developments on global inflation data and any comments from central bank officials. Domestically, the consistent FII flows will be key. Sectors like FMCG and Pharma, which showed strength today, might continue to be in focus, while the performance of Metal and Media sectors will depend on global commodity trends and any specific company news. Key levels for the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty will be crucial to monitor for directional cues.
