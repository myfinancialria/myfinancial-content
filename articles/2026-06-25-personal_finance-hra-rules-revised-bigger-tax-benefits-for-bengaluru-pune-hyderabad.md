# HRA Rules Revised: Bigger Tax Benefits for Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad

**TL;DR**
- The Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) has expanded the list of cities considered "metro" for House Rent Allowance (HRA) exemption purposes.
- Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Ahmedabad are now included in the metro city category.
- This revision allows eligible salaried individuals in these cities to claim HRA exemption up to 50% of their salary, an increase from the previous 40%.
- These new HRA rules are effective from Financial Year 2026-27, meaning they apply to income earned from April 1, 2026, onwards.
- It is crucial to remember that HRA exemption benefits are exclusively available under the Old Tax Regime, not the New Tax Regime.

## What this means in plain terms

For decades, only four Indian cities – Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai – were classified as "metro" for the purpose of calculating House Rent Allowance (HRA) exemption under the Income Tax Act. This meant that salaried individuals residing in these cities could claim a higher HRA exemption, up to 50% of their basic salary, compared to 40% for those in all other cities.

However, the economic landscape and rental markets in India have evolved significantly. Cities like Bengaluru, Hyderabad, and Pune have emerged as major economic hubs, attracting a large salaried workforce and experiencing soaring rental costs, often comparable to, or even exceeding, those in the traditional metro cities. Despite these high living expenses, taxpayers in these cities were previously limited to the 40% HRA exemption cap, leading to a smaller portion of their HRA being tax-free.

Recognising this disparity, the CBDT has introduced a significant change. Under the Income Tax Rules, 2026, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Ahmedabad have now been officially added to the list of metro cities for HRA exemption. This expansion means that from Financial Year 2026-27 (i.e., for income earned from April 1, 2026, onwards), eligible salaried individuals living in these four newly added cities can now claim HRA exemption based on 50% of their salary, potentially leading to substantial tax savings and a higher take-home pay. This benefit, however, is exclusively for those who opt for the Old Tax Regime.

## Understanding HRA Exemption Basics

House Rent Allowance (HRA) is a crucial component of a salaried employee's compensation package, designed to help them meet their rental accommodation expenses. Under Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (now Rule 279 of the Income Tax Rules, 2026), a portion of the HRA received can be exempt from tax, provided certain conditions are met. This exemption significantly reduces an individual's taxable income.

### The "Least of Three" Rule

The HRA exemption amount is calculated as the *lowest* of the following three conditions:

1.  **Actual HRA received:** The amount of HRA that your employer pays you.
2.  **Rent paid minus 10% of salary:** This calculates the excess of rent paid over 10% of your basic salary.
3.  **A percentage of salary:**
    *   50% of salary, if you reside in a metro city.
    *   40% of salary, if you reside in any other city.

The amount that is the least among these three figures is the HRA exemption you can claim. The remaining HRA, if any, becomes part of your taxable income.

### What "Salary" Means for HRA

For the purpose of HRA calculation, "salary" is defined as the aggregate of Basic Salary, Dearness Allowance (if it forms part of your retirement benefits), and any commission received as a percentage of turnover. This definition is important as it directly impacts the 10% and 40%/50% calculations.

## The Expanded Definition of Metro Cities for HRA

The recent changes by the CBDT are a welcome relief for millions of salaried professionals across India, especially those in fast-growing urban centres.

### New Cities, New Opportunities

Historically, only Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai were categorised as metro cities for the purpose of the 50% HRA exemption. This definition, which had remained unchanged for over two decades, failed to account for the skyrocketing rental costs in other major Indian cities.

With the latest revision, the list of metro cities eligible for the 50% HRA exemption has been expanded to eight. The four new additions are:

*   **Bengaluru**
*   **Hyderabad**
*   **Pune**
*   **Ahmedabad**

This means that if you are residing in any of these eight cities – Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, or Ahmedabad – you can now factor in 50% of your salary when calculating your HRA exemption, provided you meet the other conditions. This change is particularly impactful for mid-to-high income employees who pay substantial rents in these cities, as it allows a larger portion of their HRA to be tax-free.

### Effective Date: FY 2026-27 Onwards

It is crucial for taxpayers to note the effective date of these new rules. The expanded metro city list and the corresponding 50% HRA exemption apply from **Financial Year 2026-27 onwards**, which commenced on April 1, 2026.

This means:

*   **For filing Income Tax Returns (ITR) for FY 2025-26 (due by July 31, 2026):** The old rules still apply. Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Ahmedabad will be treated as non-metro cities, and the 40% of salary cap will be used for HRA exemption calculation.
*   **For income earned from April 1, 2026 (FY 2026-27) onwards:** The new rules come into effect. If you reside in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, or Ahmedabad, you can use the 50% of salary cap for your HRA exemption calculation.

## HRA and Your Tax Regime Choice

The Indian income tax system currently offers taxpayers two distinct regimes: the Old Tax Regime and the New Tax Regime. Your choice between these two significantly impacts the deductions and exemptions you can claim, including HRA.

### Old vs. New Tax Regime

A fundamental point to understand is that the **HRA exemption is available exclusively under the Old Tax Regime**. The New Tax Regime, introduced to simplify taxation with lower tax rates, foregoes most exemptions and deductions, including HRA, Leave Travel Allowance (LTA), and deductions under Section 80C, 80D, etc.

Therefore, even with the expanded metro city list, if you have opted for or plan to opt for the New Tax Regime, you will not be able to claim any HRA exemption, regardless of your city of residence.

### Re-evaluating Your Option

For salaried individuals in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Ahmedabad, this change in HRA rules makes the choice between the Old and New Tax Regimes even more critical. If you were previously leaning towards the New Tax Regime due to its simplicity, the increased HRA exemption under the Old Tax Regime might now make the latter more financially advantageous.

It is advisable to perform a fresh comparison of your potential tax liability under both regimes, taking into account the higher HRA benefit if applicable, along with other deductions and exemptions you are eligible for under the Old Tax Regime. This exercise will help you make an informed decision that maximises your tax savings.

## Navigating Compliance and Documentation

While the expanded HRA benefits are welcome, the CBDT has also tightened compliance requirements to ensure genuine claims. Proper documentation is key to a smooth HRA claim process.

### Essential Documents

To successfully claim HRA exemption, you must maintain accurate records and submit necessary documents to your employer or present them during tax assessment. These include:

*   **Rent Agreement:** A valid rent agreement between you and your landlord is essential. It should clearly state the monthly rent, period of tenancy, and details of both parties.
*   **Rent Receipts:** You must obtain monthly rent receipts from your landlord. These receipts should be properly stamped if the annual rent exceeds ₹5,000.
*   **Proof of Rent Payments:** Keep records of your rent payments, such as bank statements showing NEFT/IMPS/UPI transfers or digital payment records. Cash payments, while permissible, require more stringent documentation.

### Landlord's PAN and Relationship Disclosure

The compliance requirements have become more stringent to prevent fraudulent claims:

*   **Landlord's PAN:** If the aggregate annual rent paid exceeds ₹1 lakh (i.e., more than ₹8,333 per month), it is mandatory to furnish your landlord's Permanent Account Number (PAN). If the landlord does not have a PAN, you must obtain a declaration from them to that effect, along with their name and address.
*   **Relationship Disclosure:** From April 1, 2026, if you are paying rent to a family member (e.g., parents, spouse), you may need to clearly disclose this relationship when claiming HRA. This is to ensure that the transaction is legitimate and not merely a way to reduce taxable income without genuine rental expense.

Ensuring all documentation is in order well in advance will help you avoid any issues during your tax filing process.

## A real example

Let's consider **Aisha, 35, earning ₹24L CTC, residing in Bengaluru.**
Her salary structure for FY 2026-27 (effective April 1, 2026) is:
*   Basic Salary: ₹10,00,000 per annum (₹83,333 per month)
*   Dearness Allowance (part of retirement benefits): ₹2,00,000 per annum (₹16,667 per month)
*   HRA received: ₹5,00,000 per annum (₹41,667 per month)
*   She pays a monthly rent of ₹35,000, totalling ₹4,20,000 per annum.

For HRA calculation, Aisha's "salary" is Basic + DA = ₹10,00,000 + ₹2,00,000 = ₹12,00,000 per annum.

**Scenario 1: Under Old Rules (as applicable for FY 2025-26, before April 1, 2026)**
Bengaluru was considered a non-metro city, so 40% of salary would apply.
1.  Actual HRA received: ₹5,00,000
2.  Rent paid minus 10% of salary: ₹4,20,000 - (10% of ₹12,00,000) = ₹4,20,000 - ₹1,20,000 = ₹3,00,000
3.  40% of salary: 40% of ₹12,00,000 = ₹4,80,000

The least of these three is ₹3,00,000.
**HRA Exemption (Old Rules): ₹3,00,000**
Taxable HRA: ₹5,00,000 - ₹3,00,000 = ₹2,00,000

**Scenario 2: Under New Rules (effective from FY 2026-27, from April 1, 2026)**
Bengaluru is now considered a metro city, so 50% of salary will apply.
1.  Actual HRA received: ₹5,00,000
2.  Rent paid minus 10% of salary: ₹4,20,000 - (10% of ₹12,00,000) = ₹4,20,000 - ₹1,20,000 = ₹3,00,000
3.  50% of salary: 50% of ₹12,00,000 = ₹6,00,000

The least of these three is ₹3,00,000.
**HRA Exemption (New Rules): ₹3,00,000**
Taxable HRA: ₹5,00,000 - ₹3,00,000 = ₹2,00,000

In Aisha's specific case, even with the new rules, her HRA exemption remains ₹3,00,000. This is because the "Rent paid minus 10% of salary" condition (₹3,00,000) is the lowest of the three and acts as the limiting factor. The increase in the percentage cap from 40% to 50% only benefits those whose HRA received or 40% of salary was previously the limiting factor.

Let's modify Aisha's scenario slightly: What if Aisha paid a higher rent of ₹45,000 per month (₹5,40,000 per annum)?

**Modified Scenario (Rent ₹45,000/month):**
1.  Actual HRA received: ₹5,00,000
2.  Rent paid minus 10% of salary: ₹5,40,000 - ₹1,20,000 = ₹4,20,000

**Under Old Rules (40% of salary):**
3.  40% of salary: ₹4,80,000
The least is ₹4,20,000.
**HRA Exemption (Old Rules): ₹4,20,000**
Taxable HRA: ₹5,00,000 - ₹4,20,000 = ₹80,000

**Under New Rules (50% of salary):**
3.  50% of salary: ₹6,00,000
The least is ₹4,20,000.
**HRA Exemption (New Rules): ₹4,20,000**
Taxable HRA: ₹5,00,000 - ₹4,20,000 = ₹80,000

Even in this modified scenario, the "Rent paid minus 10% of salary" remains the lowest. This highlights that the increased 50% cap is beneficial when the 40% cap was previously the lowest factor. For Aisha, with her current HRA and rent, the "rent paid minus 10% of salary" is the limiting factor.

Let's consider another example where the 50% rule makes a difference:
**Rahul, 38, ₹28L CTC, Bengaluru.**
*   Basic Salary: ₹12,00,000 per annum (₹1,00,000 per month)
*   Dearness Allowance: ₹3,00,000 per annum (₹25,000 per month)
*   HRA received: ₹6,00,000 per annum (₹50,000 per month)
*   Monthly rent paid: ₹48,000, totalling ₹5,76,000 per annum.

Rahul's "salary" for HRA = Basic + DA = ₹12,00,000 + ₹3,00,000 = ₹15,00,000 per annum.

**Scenario: For FY 2025-26 (Old Rules, Bengaluru as non-metro)**
1.  Actual HRA received: ₹6,00,000
2.  Rent paid minus 10% of salary: ₹5,76,000 - (10% of ₹15,00,000) = ₹5,76,000 - ₹1,50,000 = ₹4,26,000
3.  40% of salary: 40% of ₹15,00,000 = ₹6,00,000

The least of these three is ₹4,26,000.
**HRA Exemption (FY 2025-26): ₹4,26,000**
Taxable HRA: ₹6,00,000 - ₹4,26,000 = ₹1,74,000

**Scenario: For FY 2026-27 (New Rules, Bengaluru as metro)**
1.  Actual HRA received: ₹6,00,000
2.  Rent paid minus 10% of salary: ₹5,76,000 - (10% of ₹15,00,000) = ₹5,76,000 - ₹1,50,000 = ₹4,26,000
3.  50% of salary: 50% of ₹15,00,000 = ₹7,50,000

The least of these three is ₹4,26,000.
**HRA Exemption (FY 2026-27): ₹4,26,000**
Taxable HRA: ₹6,00,000 - ₹4,26,000 = ₹1,74,000

In this specific case, the "Rent paid minus 10% of salary" is the lowest, so the change from 40% to 50% does not directly increase Rahul's HRA exemption. The benefit of the 50% cap becomes evident when the 40% of salary was previously the limiting factor. For instance, if Rahul's rent was much higher, such that "Rent paid minus 10% of salary" was, say, ₹5,50,000, then under the old rules, his exemption would be capped at ₹6,00,000 (40% of salary), but under new rules, it would be ₹5,50,000 (Rent paid minus 10% of salary, as 50% of salary is ₹7,50,000).

The key takeaway is that the 50% cap provides more headroom, benefiting those with higher HRA components and substantial rental payments where the old 40% cap was restrictive.

## FAQ

### Is HRA exemption available under the New Tax Regime?
No, HRA exemption is not available under the New Tax Regime. It is an exemption exclusively offered under the Old Tax Regime.

### Which cities are now considered metro for HRA purposes?
From Financial Year 2026-27, eight cities are classified as metro for HRA exemption: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Ahmedabad.

### When do these new HRA rules become effective?
The expanded metro city list for HRA exemption is effective from Financial Year 2026-27, meaning it applies to income earned from April 1, 2026, onwards.

### Do I need my landlord's PAN for HRA claims?
Yes, if your annual rent paid exceeds ₹1 lakh, it is mandatory to provide your landlord's PAN. If they don't have one, you need a declaration from them.

### Can I claim HRA if I pay rent to my parents?
Yes, you can claim HRA if you pay rent to your parents, provided the transaction is genuine and properly documented with a rent agreement and receipts. From April 1, 2026, you may also need to disclose the relationship.

### What does "salary" mean for HRA calculation?
For HRA calculation, "salary" refers to the sum of your Basic Salary, Dearness Allowance (if it forms part of your retirement benefits), and any commission received as a percentage of turnover.
