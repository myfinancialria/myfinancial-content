# New Tax Regime: Key Deductions & Exemptions You Can No Longer Claim

**TL;DR**
- The new income tax regime, introduced from FY 2020-21, offers lower tax rates across various income slabs.
- However, opting for this regime means foregoing over 70 common tax deductions and exemptions available under the old regime.
- For most salaried individuals, the standard deduction, HRA exemption, and Section 80C benefits are the most significant deductions lost.
- Since FY 2023-24, the new regime is the default option, though taxpayers can still choose the old regime annually.
- Careful calculation is essential to determine which regime results in a lower tax liability based on your income and eligible investments.

## What this means in plain terms

India's income tax system now offers taxpayers a choice between two distinct regimes: the 'old' regime and the 'new' regime. The old regime, which has been in place for decades, allows you to claim numerous deductions and exemptions for specific investments and expenses, effectively reducing your taxable income. This means you pay tax on a lower amount, even if the tax rates themselves are higher.

The new regime, on the other hand, operates on a simpler principle. It offers significantly lower tax rates across different income brackets, but in exchange, you must forgo almost all the deductions and exemptions that were central to the old system. This trade-off means that while your tax rate might be lower, you'll be paying tax on a much larger portion of your gross income. Understanding which regime works best for you depends entirely on your income level and how many deductions you typically claim.

## Major Deductions and Exemptions You Forfeit

When you choose the new tax regime, you consciously opt out of a vast array of tax benefits that have traditionally been central to Indian financial planning. For many, these deductions were key motivators for saving and investing. Here are some of the most significant ones you'll lose:

### Section 80C Benefits
This is arguably the most popular and widely used deduction, allowing individuals to reduce their taxable income by up to ₹1.5 lakhs annually. Under the new regime, you cannot claim any of the following:
*   **Provident Fund (PF) contributions:** Both employee and employer contributions to EPF or VPF.
*   **Public Provident Fund (PPF):** Investments made into a PPF account.
*   **Life Insurance Premiums:** Premiums paid for life insurance policies.
*   **Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS):** Investments in these tax-saving mutual funds.
*   **Home Loan Principal Repayment:** The principal portion of your home loan EMIs.
*   **Children's Tuition Fees:** Fees paid for up to two children's education.
*   **National Savings Certificates (NSC):** Investments in NSCs.
*   **Senior Citizen's Savings Scheme (SCSS):** Investments in this scheme.
*   **Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY):** Contributions to an SSY account.

### Standard Deduction for Salaried Individuals
For salaried taxpayers, a standard deduction of ₹50,000 was introduced in lieu of transport allowance and medical reimbursement. This automatic deduction significantly reduces taxable income for all employees. Under the new regime, this benefit is not available. However, a standard deduction of ₹50,000 *is* now available for salaried individuals and pensioners under the new tax regime from FY 2023-24 (AY 2024-25) onwards, as per the Finance Act 2023. This is a crucial update to consider.

### House Rent Allowance (HRA) Exemption
Salaried individuals who live in rented accommodation and receive HRA as part of their salary package can claim an exemption for a portion of their rent paid. This can be a substantial benefit, especially in high-rent cities. This exemption is not available in the new regime.

### Leave Travel Allowance (LTA) Exemption
Employees can claim an exemption for travel expenses incurred during leave within India, twice in a block of four calendar years. This popular benefit is also not available under the new tax regime.

## Other Important Deductions and Exemptions Lost

Beyond the major ones, several other valuable deductions and exemptions that cater to specific financial situations are also foregone when you opt for the new tax regime.

### Interest on Housing Loan (Section 24(b))
If you have a self-occupied property and are repaying a home loan, you can claim a deduction of up to ₹2 lakhs on the interest paid. This significantly reduces the taxable income for many homeowners. Under the new regime, this deduction is not permitted for self-occupied property. For let-out property, the interest can still be set off against rental income, but the limit on set-off against other income (₹2 lakhs) is removed.

### Section 80D: Health Insurance Premiums
Premiums paid for health insurance for yourself, your family, and dependent parents are deductible under Section 80D. This includes preventive health check-ups. The limits range from ₹25,000 to ₹1 lakh, depending on age and family composition. This crucial deduction for healthcare expenses is unavailable in the new regime.

### Section 80TTA and 80TTB: Interest on Savings Accounts
Section 80TTA allows individuals and HUFs (other than senior citizens) a deduction of up to ₹10,000 on interest earned from savings bank accounts. Senior citizens can claim up to ₹50,000 on interest from savings and fixed deposits under Section 80TTB. Both these deductions are not available under the new regime.

### Professional Tax and Entertainment Allowance
Salaried employees often pay professional tax to their state government, which is deductible from their income. Government employees can also claim an exemption for entertainment allowance. Both these are foregone in the new regime.

### Section 80CCD(1B): NPS Contributions
While employer contributions to NPS (under Section 80CCD(2)) remain deductible in both regimes, the additional self-contribution of up to ₹50,000 to NPS under Section 80CCD(1B) is not available as a deduction in the new regime. This impacts those who use NPS as a voluntary retirement savings tool.

## Navigating the Choice: Who Benefits?

The choice between the old and new tax regimes is not one-size-fits-all. Generally, the new regime tends to be more beneficial for individuals who:
*   Have fewer investments and expenses that qualify for deductions (e.g., young professionals just starting their careers).
*   Don't own a home or have completed their home loan repayments.
*   Don't have significant health insurance premiums or other major deductible expenses.
*   Prefer a simpler tax filing process without the need to track numerous investment proofs.

Conversely, the old regime is usually more advantageous for those who:
*   Make substantial investments under Section 80C (PPF, ELSS, home loan principal).
*   Pay significant HRA or have a home loan with interest payments (Section 24(b)).
*   Pay health insurance premiums (Section 80D) and contribute to NPS (Section 80CCD(1B)).
*   Have a higher income and can effectively utilise a wide range of deductions to bring down their taxable income.

The key is to perform a comparative calculation using a tax calculator, factoring in all your potential deductions under the old regime versus the lower tax rates of the new regime. The Income Tax Department provides a helpful tax calculator on its official portal to assist with this comparison (refer to [incometax.gov.in](https://incometax.gov.in)).

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 35, ₹22L CTC, Hyderabad**, a salaried professional.

**Priya's Income & Deductions (Old Regime):**
*   Gross Salary: ₹22,00,000
*   Standard Deduction: ₹50,000
*   HRA Exemption: ₹1,50,000 (based on rent paid)
*   Section 80C Investments: ₹1,50,000 (PPF, ELSS, life insurance)
*   Section 80D (Health Insurance): ₹25,000
*   Professional Tax: ₹2,400

**Calculation under Old Regime:**
1.  Gross Salary: ₹22,00,000
2.  Less: Standard Deduction: ₹50,000
3.  Less: HRA Exemption: ₹1,50,000
4.  Less: Professional Tax: ₹2,400
5.  Gross Taxable Income: ₹22,00,000 - ₹50,000 - ₹1,50,000 - ₹2,400 = ₹19,97,600
6.  Less: Section 80C: ₹1,50,000
7.  Less: Section 80D: ₹25,000
8.  **Net Taxable Income (Old Regime):** ₹19,97,600 - ₹1,50,000 - ₹25,000 = **₹18,22,600**

**Tax Calculation (Old Regime Rates - FY 2023-24):**
*   Up to ₹2,50,000: Nil
*   ₹2,50,001 - ₹5,00,000 (5%): ₹12,500
*   ₹5,00,001 - ₹10,00,000 (20%): ₹1,00,000
*   ₹10,00,001 - ₹18,22,600 (30% on ₹8,22,600): ₹2,46,780
*   Total Tax: ₹12,500 + ₹1,00,000 + ₹2,46,780 = ₹3,59,280
*   Add 4% Health & Education Cess: ₹14,371
*   **Total Tax Payable (Old Regime): ₹3,73,651**

**Calculation under New Regime (Default from FY 2023-24):**
1.  Gross Salary: ₹22,00,000
2.  Less: Standard Deduction: ₹50,000 (This is now allowed in the new regime from FY 2023-24)
3.  **Net Taxable Income (New Regime):** ₹22,00,000 - ₹50,000 = **₹21,50,000**

**Tax Calculation (New Regime Rates - FY 2023-24):**
*   Up to ₹3,00,000: Nil
*   ₹3,00,001 - ₹6,00,000 (5%): ₹15,000
*   ₹6,00,001 - ₹9,00,000 (10%): ₹30,000
*   ₹9,00,001 - ₹12,00,000 (15%): ₹45,000
*   ₹12,00,001 - ₹15,00,000 (20%): ₹60,000
*   ₹15,00,001 - ₹21,50,000 (30% on ₹6,50,000): ₹1,95,000
*   Total Tax: ₹15,000 + ₹30,000 + ₹45,000 + ₹60,000 + ₹1,95,000 = ₹3,45,000
*   Add 4% Health & Education Cess: ₹13,800
*   **Total Tax Payable (New Regime): ₹3,58,800**

In Priya's case, with her current level of deductions, the **New Regime results in a lower tax payable of ₹3,58,800** compared to ₹3,73,651 under the Old Regime. This difference of ₹14,851 highlights that even with significant deductions, the lower slab rates of the new regime, coupled with the new standard deduction, can sometimes be more beneficial. This example underscores the importance of individual calculation.

## FAQ

### Is the new tax regime mandatory for everyone?
No, the new tax regime is not mandatory. While it is the default option from FY 2023-24, individual taxpayers can still choose to opt for the old tax regime annually.

### Can I switch between the old and new tax regimes every year?
Yes, salaried individuals can opt for the old or new tax regime each financial year when filing their Income Tax Return. However, individuals with business income have more restrictive rules for switching.

### What are the main benefits of the new tax regime?
The main benefits are lower tax slab rates and a simpler tax structure, as it eliminates the need to track and claim numerous deductions and exemptions. A standard deduction of ₹50,000 is also available for salaried individuals and pensioners.

### Are there *any* deductions allowed in the new tax regime?
Yes, a few deductions are still allowed. These include the standard deduction of ₹50,000 for salaried individuals and pensioners, employer's contribution to NPS (Section 80CCD(2)), and certain deductions related to Agniveer Corpus Fund.

### Who benefits most from the new tax regime?
Individuals with lower incomes, those who don't make many tax-saving investments, or those who don't claim significant deductions like HRA or home loan interest, often find the new regime more beneficial due to its lower tax rates.

### What happens if I don't choose a regime?
If you are a salaried individual and do not explicitly communicate your choice to your employer, the new tax regime will be applied by default for TDS purposes. However, you can still choose your preferred regime when filing your Income Tax Return.
