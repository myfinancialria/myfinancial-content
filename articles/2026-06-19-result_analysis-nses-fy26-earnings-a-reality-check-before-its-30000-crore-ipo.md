# NSE's FY26 Earnings: A Reality Check Before Its ₹30,000-Crore IPO

**TL;DR**
- The National Stock Exchange (NSE) reported a significant 16% drop in net profit for FY26, falling to ₹10,302 crore from ₹12,188 crore in FY25.
- Operating margins also sharply declined to 67% in FY26 from 74% a year ago, indicating weakening core profitability.
- This slowdown is primarily attributed to the Securities and Exchange Board of India's (SEBI) crackdown on speculative derivatives trading and substantial legacy legal settlements.
- NSE's revenue also saw a 3% decline year-on-year, reaching ₹16,601 crore, mainly due to reduced income from transaction charges.
- These results present a crucial question for investors ahead of the anticipated ₹30,000-crore IPO: whether FY26 is an anomaly or a new normal for the exchange.

## What this means in plain terms

The National Stock Exchange, India's largest stock exchange, is gearing up for a massive Initial Public Offering (IPO) that could be worth ₹30,000 crore. However, its latest financial results for the fiscal year 2026 have thrown a curveball. The exchange's profits have taken a notable hit, dropping by 16%, and its operating margins have also shrunk.

This dip in performance is largely due to two main factors: stricter rules from the market regulator, SEBI, aimed at curbing excessive speculation in derivatives trading, and significant payouts for old legal disputes. For potential investors looking at the upcoming IPO, these numbers are a "reality check." They raise important questions about NSE's future growth trajectory and profitability, especially since the exchange has historically seen explosive profit growth.

## Decoding NSE's FY26 Performance

The National Stock Exchange reported a net profit of ₹10,302 crore for the financial year 2026, marking a 16% year-on-year decline from ₹12,188 crore in FY25. This significant drop comes alongside a 3% reduction in total revenue, which stood at ₹16,601 crore in FY26 compared to ₹17,141 crore the previous year.

A key indicator of the exchange's core business health, the operating margin, also fell sharply to 67% in FY26 from approximately 74% in FY25. This suggests a weakening in the profitability of its primary operations. The decline in revenue was largely driven by a reduced share of income from transaction charges, which fell 4% year-on-year to ₹13,057 crore from ₹13,636 crore. Clearing and settlement income also saw a steeper decline of 22%, dropping to ₹251 crore from ₹321 crore. This moderation in market activity, particularly in equity cash and derivatives Average Daily Turnover (ADT), played a direct role in the financial results.

## The Impact of Regulatory Shifts and Legal Settlements

The primary drivers behind NSE's moderated performance are clearly identified as SEBI's regulatory interventions and substantial legal settlements.

### SEBI's Derivatives Crackdown
SEBI has introduced several measures to curb speculative trading in equity derivatives, which has directly impacted trading volumes. Options trading alone contributed over 60% of NSE's operating revenue in FY26, highlighting its heavy dependence on this segment. The regulatory tightening, which began with measures in July 2024, has led to a steady decline in futures and options (F&O) trading activity. NSE itself acknowledged in its IPO papers that these measures have "moderated trading activity across both cash and derivatives segments, affecting trading revenues during FY26." Further regulatory actions, higher transaction taxes, or shifts in investor preferences could continue to hurt trading volumes and profitability.

### Legacy Legal Settlements and Provisions
NSE's profitability was further aggravated by significant provisions for legacy legal settlements. A major contributor was a provision of ₹1,391.21 crore relating to SEBI orders connected to the co-location facility and dark fibre connectivity. While NSE had proposed revised settlement terms of ₹1,491.21 crore to SEBI for the prolonged co-location case, the outcome of these applications was still uncertain at the time of the DRHP filing. Earlier, in October 2024, NSE paid over ₹643 crore to settle proceedings linked to its Trading Access Point (TAP) architecture and network connectivity. These substantial one-time charges have directly impacted the net profit figures for FY26.

## Management Outlook and IPO Implications

NSE has been attempting to go public for nearly a decade, and the current IPO, estimated to be worth ₹30,000 crore, could be India's largest ever. The exchange's draft red herring prospectus (DRHP) explicitly flags risks from regulatory changes, technology failures, cyberattacks, and its heavy reliance on derivatives trading for revenue.

The management's perspective, as reflected in the DRHP, indicates an awareness of the challenges. The company has invested significantly in technology, with tech spending jumping nearly 29% to ₹1,315 crore, to bolster infrastructure and cyber resilience. Despite the FY26 performance, NSE remains the world's largest equity derivatives exchange, handling 51.18% of global equity derivatives contracts in FY26. This strong market position is a key aspect for investors. However, the question remains whether FY26's performance is an aberration or a "new normal" for the exchange, with some experts believing its era of explosive profit growth might be over. The IPO will be an Offer for Sale (OFS), meaning existing shareholders will divest shares, and NSE itself will not receive any fresh funds from the public issue.

## Broader Market Context and Investor Considerations

NSE operates in a duopoly with BSE, but it holds a dominant market share, especially in equity derivatives. In FY26, NSE's net profit was over four times that of BSE, and its revenue was more than 3.5 times higher. However, this dominance in derivatives also makes NSE more susceptible to regulatory tightening in that segment, as highlighted by its recent results.

For investors, the IPO valuation, currently estimated to be over ₹5 trillion, will be critical. While NSE boasts world-class financial metrics such as a high Profit After Tax (PAT) margin of 55.05% and Return on Equity (ROE) of 32.98% in FY26, the recent decline in profitability introduces a new layer of consideration. The IPO is expected to deepen institutional participation and could even lead to NSE's inclusion in benchmark indices like Nifty 50 or Sensex, creating mechanical buying. However, large IPOs can also absorb significant market liquidity, potentially leading to temporary selling pressure on existing stocks. Investors will need to carefully evaluate the final valuation, ongoing regulatory developments, and the exchange's risk factors, including its reliance on a concentrated group of top 10 trading members who accounted for nearly 47% of its operating revenue in FY26.

## A real example

Meet Anjali, 42, a software engineer with ₹35 lakh CTC in Hyderabad, who has been keenly following the NSE IPO news. She's a long-term investor, typically allocating funds to blue-chip stocks and diversified mutual funds.

*   **Net Profit Drop:** A 16% fall from ₹12,188 crore to ₹10,302 crore is significant. This isn't just a small blip; it affects the earnings per share (EPS) and, consequently, the P/E ratio at which the IPO might be valued.
*   **Margin Erosion:** The operating margin declining from 74% to 67% suggests that the core business is becoming less profitable. This directly impacts future earnings potential.
*   **Regulatory Headwinds:** The derivatives crackdown by SEBI is a structural change, not a temporary one. Anjali understands that if SEBI continues to tighten rules (which it has indicated it might), NSE's most profitable segment could face ongoing pressure.
*   **Legal Settlements:** The ₹1,432 crore settlement charge that impacted profits in FY26 is a one-off, but it highlights past governance issues that delayed the IPO for years. While resolved, it underscores regulatory scrutiny.

Anjali decides to adjust her strategy. Instead of blindly investing for a "listing pop," she will now:
1.  **Re-evaluate Valuation:** She will wait for the final IPO price band to be announced. If the valuation is still very high despite the reduced profitability, she might reconsider her allocation, or even wait for the stock to stabilise post-listing.
2.  **Focus on Long-Term Growth Drivers:** She will look for management commentary on how NSE plans to diversify revenue streams beyond derivatives or adapt to the new regulatory environment to ensure sustained growth.
3.  **Consider the "New Normal":** Anjali will factor in the possibility that the era of "explosive profit growth" might indeed be over for NSE, and adjust her expected returns accordingly. She will compare NSE's revised profitability metrics with global exchanges and BSE to see if the premium valuation is still justified.

For Anjali, the FY26 results are not a deal-breaker, but a prompt to be more cautious and analytical, aligning her investment decision with a realistic outlook rather than past performance or IPO hype.

## FAQ

### What caused NSE's net profit to drop in FY26?
NSE's net profit fell by 16% in FY26 primarily due to SEBI's crackdown on speculative derivatives trading, which impacted trading volumes, and significant provisions for legacy legal settlements, including the co-location case.

### How did SEBI's derivatives crackdown affect NSE?
SEBI's measures to strengthen the equity derivatives framework led to a moderation in trading activity across both cash and derivatives segments, directly impacting NSE's transaction charge revenue, which is a major contributor to its top line.

### What was the impact of legal settlements on NSE's financials?
NSE made substantial provisions for legal settlements, notably ₹1,391.21 crore related to the co-location case, which significantly impacted its net profit for FY26. Other settlements, like ₹643 crore for the Trading Access Point architecture, also contributed to expenses.

### Is the ₹30,000-crore NSE IPO still on track?
Yes, NSE has filed its Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) for an IPO estimated at ₹30,000 crore, which could be India's largest. The FY26 earnings provide a crucial data point for investors to consider ahead of its listing.

### What does the decline in operating margin signify for NSE?
The sharp fall in NSE's operating margin from 74% to 67% indicates a weakening in the profitability of its core operations. This suggests that the cost of generating revenue has increased, or revenue growth has slowed more than expenses.

### Will NSE receive funds from its IPO?
No, the NSE IPO is entirely an Offer for Sale (OFS). This means existing shareholders will be selling their shares, and the National Stock Exchange itself will not receive any fresh capital from the public issue.

### What are the main risks highlighted by NSE in its IPO papers?
NSE has flagged several risks, including regulatory changes in derivatives markets, technology failures, cyberattacks, AI-related challenges, and a high concentration of revenue from its top 10 trading members.
