# LIC Q1 Results Explained: What Investors Need to Know

**TL;DR**
- LIC reported a robust 22.8% year-on-year increase in net profit, reaching ₹13,492 crore for Q1 FY27.
- The Value of New Business (VNB) surged by 61.3% to ₹3,136 crore, indicating improved profitability from new policies.
- VNB margin expanded significantly by 750 basis points to 22.9%, driven by a strategic shift towards higher-value non-participating products.
- Net Premium Income (NPI) saw a modest 6.75% growth to ₹1.27 lakh crore, reflecting steady business expansion.
- LIC maintained its market leadership with an overall market share of 60.10% in First-Year Premium Income, despite a slight decline from the previous year.

## What this means in plain terms

When a large insurer like Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) declares its quarterly results, it offers a glimpse into the health of its business and, by extension, the broader insurance sector in India. For investors, these numbers are more than just figures; they represent the company's ability to attract new customers, manage its existing policyholders, and generate profits. This quarter's results for Q1 FY27 (April-June 2026) show a mixed but largely positive picture.

The headline profit growth is impressive, but for an insurance company, it's crucial to look beyond just net profit. Metrics like Value of New Business (VNB) and VNB margin provide deeper insights into the quality and future profitability of the business being written. LIC's significant jump in VNB and its margin suggests that the company is not just selling more policies, but it's selling *better* policies – those that are expected to be more profitable over their lifetime. This strategic shift is a key takeaway for anyone tracking LIC's performance.

## Headline Numbers & Estimates

LIC's Q1 FY27 performance showcased strong growth in key profitability metrics, exceeding market expectations in several areas. The insurer reported a consolidated net profit of ₹13,492 crore for the quarter ended June 30, 2026, marking a substantial 22.8% increase compared to ₹10,986 crore in the corresponding period last year. This robust profit growth was supported by healthy premium collections and improved new business performance.

Net Premium Income (NPI) for the quarter rose by 6.75% year-on-year to ₹1.27 lakh crore, up from ₹1.19 lakh crore in Q1 FY26. While premium growth was moderate compared to profit growth, it indicates a steady expansion of LIC's core insurance business. Total income for the quarter also saw an improvement, reaching ₹2,37,848 crore as against ₹2,22,864 crore in the year-ago period.

### Value of New Business (VNB) and Margin Expansion

Perhaps the most significant highlight of LIC's Q1 FY27 results was the remarkable surge in its Value of New Business (VNB). VNB, which represents the expected future profit from new policies written during a period, jumped by an impressive 61.3% year-on-year to ₹3,136 crore, compared to ₹1,944 crore in Q1 FY26. This sharp increase signals that LIC is generating significantly more economic value from its new business.

Accompanying the VNB growth, the VNB margin expanded sharply by 750 basis points, reaching 22.9% from 15.4% in the year-ago period. This expansion reflects a notable improvement in the product mix and stronger profitability from the new business written during the quarter. The management has attributed this improvement to its product diversification and distribution strategy, particularly the focus on higher-margin products.

## Segmental Performance & Product Mix

LIC's performance in Q1 FY27 also provided insights into its various business segments and the ongoing shift in its product strategy. The insurer continued to maintain its market leadership, holding an overall market share of 60.10% in First-Year Premium Income (FYPI) for the quarter. However, this was a slight dip from 63.51% in Q1 FY26, primarily due to a decline in the group insurance business market share from 76.54% to 70.90%. Interestingly, LIC's individual business market share marginally increased from 38.76% to 38.89%.

### Individual and Group Business Growth

In the individual segment, LIC sold 31.02 lakh policies during Q1 FY27, a modest 2.06% increase from 30.40 lakh policies sold in the same period last year. However, individual new business premium grew at a faster pace of 14.5% year-on-year to ₹14,351 crore, indicating that customers are purchasing policies with higher premium values. First-year premium income for the overall business rose 22.5% to ₹9,217 crore. Renewal premium income increased by 3.3% to ₹61,833 crore, and single premium collections climbed 8.7% to ₹56,369 crore.

A significant trend is LIC's increasing focus on non-participating (non-par) products within its individual business. The Annualised Premium Equivalent (APE) from individual non-par products increased by 14.2% to ₹2,447 crore in Q1 FY27. The contribution of non-par products to individual APE also rose from 30.34% to 32.49%, highlighting a deliberate shift towards more profitable offerings. This strategic move is a key factor behind the improved VNB margins.

## Financial Health & Solvency

Beyond premium and profit figures, LIC's financial health is also reflected in its solvency ratio and Assets Under Management (AUM). The solvency ratio, a critical indicator of an insurer's ability to meet its long-term obligations, improved to 2.42 in Q1 FY27 from 2.17 in the year-ago period. This comfortably exceeds the regulatory requirement set by IRDAI (Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India), underscoring the company's strong capital position. [cite: IRDAI]

Assets Under Management (AUM) also witnessed healthy growth, increasing by 4.10% year-on-year to ₹59.39 lakh crore as of June 30, 2026, compared to ₹57.05 lakh crore last year. This indicates effective management of its vast investment portfolio. On the persistency front, which measures policy retention, the 13th-month persistency ratio edged slightly lower by 50 basis points to 70.4% from 70.9%. However, the longer-term 61st-month persistency ratio showed a significant improvement, rising by 300 basis points to 61.3% from 58.3%, indicating better policy retention over the long run.

## Management Commentary and Outlook

LIC's management expressed satisfaction with the Q1 FY27 performance, particularly highlighting the leadership in both individual and group businesses despite increasing competition. R Doraiswamy, CEO & MD, noted that the 61% growth in VNB and the 7.5% expansion in VNB margin to 22.90% were direct outcomes of the company's product diversification and distribution strategy.

The focus remains on profitable growth, with a continued emphasis on non-participating products. The management also indicated that the government is unlikely to undertake another stake sale in LIC over the next 18 months, providing some stability regarding ownership. Furthermore, LIC is preparing for the proposed Risk Based Capital framework and exploring strategic investments in fintech companies, signaling a forward-looking approach to business and technology.

## FAQ

### What is Value of New Business (VNB)?
VNB represents the expected future profits from all new insurance policies sold by a company during a specific period. It's a key metric for insurance companies, indicating the profitability and quality of their new business.

### Why is VNB margin important for an insurance company?
VNB margin shows how much value an insurer generates relative to the new business it writes. A higher VNB margin indicates that the company is selling more profitable policies, often due to a better product mix or efficient operations.

### What are "participating" (par) and "non-participating" (non-par) policies?
Participating (par) policies allow policyholders to share in the insurer's profits, typically through bonuses or dividends. Non-participating (non-par) policies offer guaranteed benefits without profit sharing, often with more predictable returns for the insurer.

### How does the solvency ratio impact policyholders?
The solvency ratio indicates an insurer's financial strength and its ability to meet its obligations to policyholders. A higher solvency ratio, like LIC's 2.42, means the company has sufficient capital to pay out claims, providing greater security to policyholders.

### What does the increase in non-par products mean for LIC's future?
LIC's increasing focus on non-par products suggests a strategic shift towards higher-margin and more predictable profit streams. This can lead to more stable earnings and improved profitability over the long term, benefiting shareholders.

### What is First-Year Premium Income (FYPI)?
First-Year Premium Income (FYPI) is the total premium collected by an insurance company from new policies sold during a financial year. It's a key indicator of an insurer's ability to attract new customers and expand its business.
