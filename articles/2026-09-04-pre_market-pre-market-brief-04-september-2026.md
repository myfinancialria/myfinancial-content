# Pre-Market Brief — 04 September 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets show a mixed to positive bias, with Asian and European indices mostly higher, but US Fed rate cut expectations tempering sentiment.
- GIFT Nifty indicates a slightly negative opening for the Indian benchmark indices.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers yesterday, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide strong support as net buyers.
- The biggest news is the sharp fall in US Fed rate cut bets below 50% following Governor Chris Waller’s comments, suggesting a cautious global interest rate outlook.
- Nifty 50's immediate range to watch is between 23900 (strong Put OI support) and 24000 (highest Call OI resistance).
- The main risk for the day remains global interest rate uncertainty and sustained FII outflows.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) saw a marginal appreciation against the US Dollar, closing at ₹94.48, a -0.01% change. The Dollar Index (DXY) weakened by -0.54% to 99.03. Crude oil prices edged higher, with Brent Crude at $95.77 (+0.15%) and WTI Crude at $91.50 (+0.54%). Gold prices saw a significant jump of +3.45% to ₹4516.80, while Silver also rallied by +3.99% to ₹67.31. The US 10-Year Treasury Yield remained unchanged at 4.80%.

**Interpretation:** A stable to slightly stronger Rupee is generally positive for the economy, easing import costs. The weakening Dollar Index could also be supportive. However, rising crude oil prices, if sustained, could put pressure on oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint manufacturers, and aviation stocks due to higher input costs. The sharp rise in gold and silver suggests increased safe-haven demand or inflation concerns globally. The unchanged US 10-Year Yield, while stable, needs to be watched closely given the global interest rate uncertainty, as any significant rise could make emerging markets less attractive for FII flows.

**Why it matters:** This news signals a more hawkish stance from the US Fed than previously anticipated, which could lead to higher global interest rates for longer. This typically makes emerging markets like India less attractive for foreign investors, potentially leading to FII outflows and impacting overall market sentiment.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react

1.  **US Fed Governor Chris Waller's comments:** The market reacted to his comments by reducing rate cut expectations. Such statements from key central bank officials often introduce volatility as traders re-price future monetary policy.
2.  **US Payrolls Data:** The market is keenly awaiting the US payrolls report. This crucial economic data point is a significant indicator of the health of the US labour market and can heavily influence the Federal Reserve's monetary policy decisions. Strong payrolls data typically reduces the likelihood of rate cuts, potentially strengthening the dollar and impacting global equity flows, while weak data could increase rate cut expectations.
3.  **India's Weekly Debt Supply:** Indian government bond yields are pausing ahead of the weekly debt sale. Government bond auctions are important for gauging market demand for sovereign debt and can influence bond yields, which in turn affect borrowing costs for companies and the government. Markets usually react with some stability or minor adjustments around these events, depending on the demand and pricing.
