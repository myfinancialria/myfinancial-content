# Market Wrap — 19 June 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian benchmark indices, Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty, closed in negative territory.
- Midcap and Smallcap indices showed resilience, ending the day with gains.
- The IT sector was the biggest drag, led by a significant fall in Infosys.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided support.
- SEBI announced key reforms, including the reintroduction of open market buybacks.
- Jio Platforms filed its Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) for a major IPO.

## How the market closed
The Indian equity benchmarks witnessed a subdued session today, with the Nifty 50 declining by 0.64% to close at 24,013.10, while the Bank Nifty also ended lower by 0.48% at 57,685.75. Midcap and Smallcap indices, however, managed to buck the trend, showing positive momentum.

| Smallcap 100 | 18784.45 | +0.42% | 18826.65 | 18638.30 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, increased by 2.37% to settle at 12.97, indicating a slight uptick in expected market fluctuations.

## Market breadth
The market breadth was largely neutral today, with 250 advances against 251 declines out of 501 total stocks. This indicates an almost equal number of stocks moving up and down, suggesting a lack of broad-based directional conviction in the market.

## FII / DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the Indian equity market today, offloading shares worth ₹1,025 crore. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), however, provided strong support by injecting ₹3,517 crore into the market.

| Investor Type | Net Cash Position (₹ crore) |
| :------------ | :-------------------------- |
| FII | -1,025 |

Looking at the 5-day history, FIIs have largely been net sellers, with significant outflows on June 18th and June 12th, interspersed with minor inflows. DIIs, on the other hand, have consistently been net buyers, providing crucial support to the market over the past week.

## What moved markets today

**SEBI Reforms and IPO Buzz**
- **Sebi board meeting: Open market buyback is back and intraday borrowing for MFs; 5 key decisions investors should know**. The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) approved several reforms, including the reintroduction of exchange-based buybacks for companies, effective August 1. Other key decisions include allowing intraday borrowing for mutual funds and faster AIF launches, aimed at improving market efficiency and investor protection.
- **AGS Health, PGP Glass, 2 others get Sebi nod to float IPOs**. Four Indian companies, AGS Health, PGP Glass, Shreni Shares, and SRIT India, received SEBI's approval to launch their Initial Public Offerings (IPOs), indicating a robust pipeline for new listings. Separately, SBI Mutual Fund is also preparing to launch its IPO by early July, with SEBI approval expected soon.

**Jio Platforms IPO Takes Center Stage**
- **Jio IPO filed: 10 crucial things investors must know about Ambani's $3 billion plan**. Jio Platforms has filed its Draft Red Herring Prospectus (DRHP) with SEBI for a substantial IPO, comprising a fresh issue of up to 27 crore shares. This offering aims to reduce debt, fund growth, and unlock value for shareholders.
- **What Mukesh Ambani said as Jio IPO becomes Reliance's 'most important milestone' of 2026**. Reliance Industries Chairman Mukesh Ambani announced that the Jio Platforms IPO is a significant value creation milestone for the year, assuring shareholders of its importance. He also detailed plans to allocate ₹27,500 crore from the IPO proceeds to prepay debt and invest in future technologies like 6G and AI.

**Global Cues Impact Bond Market**
- **India 10-year yield snaps 6-day falling streak as US-Iran talks stall**. India's benchmark 10-year bond yield saw a pause in its recent decline today. This shift was attributed to stalled talks between the U.S. and Iran, which led to an uptick in oil prices and negatively impacted bond market sentiment.

## Setup for tomorrow
Tomorrow's session will likely see investors reacting to the full implications of SEBI's reforms, particularly the reintroduction of open market buybacks, which could provide a boost to corporate actions. The ongoing news flow around the Jio Platforms IPO will also remain a key market driver. Global cues, especially crude oil prices and the dollar movement, will be closely watched.
