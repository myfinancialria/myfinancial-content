# Market Wrap — 19 August 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian equities closed lower for the seventh consecutive session, with Nifty 50 shedding 0.32% and Bank Nifty down 0.04%.
- The market was primarily weighed down by persistent high crude oil prices, rising US Treasury yields, and global uncertainties.
- Glaxosmithkline Pharmaceuticals Ltd. emerged as the top Nifty 500 gainer, surging 5.39%.
- Netweb Technologies India Ltd. was the biggest Nifty 500 loser, declining 5.41%.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net buyers with ₹1,652 crore, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) also bought ₹2,579 crore in the cash segment today.
- F&O build-up data for today is unavailable and will populate from the next session.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equity benchmarks extended their losing streak for the seventh straight session on Wednesday, with the Nifty 50 closing below the 24,100 mark. The market opened flat and largely traded with a negative bias throughout the day, experiencing a gradual decline as global headwinds continued to weigh on investor sentiment. The Bank Nifty also ended marginally lower, while broader markets, represented by the Midcap 100 and Smallcap 100, saw sharper cuts.

| Index | Close | Change | High | Low |
| :------------------ | :---------- | :------- | :---------- | :---------- |
| Nifty 50 | 24078.30 | -0.32% | 24172.85 | 24025.65 |
| Bank Nifty | 57239.75 | -0.04% | 57356.85 | 57001.75 |
| Nifty Midcap 100 | 63408.75 | -0.21% | 63593.00 | 63198.40 |
| Nifty Smallcap 100 | 19707.15 | -0.51% | 19820.20 | 19678.65 |

The India VIX, a measure of market volatility, eased slightly by 0.61% to 11.32, indicating a marginal reduction in immediate market uncertainty despite the downtrend.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market's continued decline was largely influenced by a confluence of global and domestic factors. High crude oil prices, with Brent crude nearing $92 a barrel, remained a significant concern, fueling inflation worries and impacting the Energy sector negatively. The Indian rupee also reflected this pressure, hitting a three-week low against the US dollar, though RBI intervention helped prevent a sharper fall.

Globally, rising US Treasury yields continued to pose a risk to the US economy by increasing borrowing costs, which in turn kept global investors cautious. European markets were largely flat amidst Middle East tensions and anticipation of the Federal Reserve's July meeting minutes. Adding to the domestic sentiment, a Bank of America survey indicated that India has replaced Indonesia as Asia's least preferred stock market, citing global uncertainties and weak domestic growth.

Despite FIIs turning net buyers today, the broader sentiment remained weak, reflected in the negative market breadth where declines significantly outnumbered advances. The Energy sector was particularly hit, while the IT sector showed some resilience.

The day saw a mixed sectoral performance, with defensive sectors showing some strength while others faced selling pressure.

- **PSU BANK:** -0.01% - Public Sector Banks ended almost flat, indicating relative stability.

**Top 3 Loser Sectors:**
- **ENERGY:** -1.18% - This sector was the biggest drag, likely impacted by the sustained high crude oil prices and concerns over their implications.
- **MEDIA:** -0.91% - The Media sector witnessed significant selling pressure today.
- **FMCG:** -0.55% - Fast-Moving Consumer Goods stocks also ended in the red, contributing to the market's decline.

## News that moved stocks
- **Shiprocket and Behari Lal Engineering make strong market debuts:** Shiprocket shares listed at a 35% premium over its IPO price, while Behari Lal Engineering shares rallied 14% after listing at a 63% premium. These strong debuts indicate robust investor appetite for new listings, despite the broader market weakness.
- **Rupee hits three-week low amid high oil prices:** The Indian rupee touched its lowest point since late July, primarily driven by elevated oil prices and sustained demand for the dollar. The Reserve Bank of India's interventions helped to moderate the fall. This development impacts import-heavy sectors and could fuel inflation.
- **JM Financial's $80 Brent call favors Oil India and ONGC:** Analysts at JM Financial expect Brent crude to remain near $80 a barrel for the next twelve months, favoring Oil India and ONGC due to anticipated earnings growth and Oil India's refinery capacity expansion plans. This outlook provides a positive catalyst for these specific oil exploration and production companies.
- **Sebi to roll out AI rules with a ‘kill switch’ for stock market:** The market regulator will soon issue guidelines for the responsible use of AI and machine learning in India’s securities market. These rules will include mandatory safeguards like a "kill switch," human oversight, and data controls, aiming to ensure market integrity and investor protection in the evolving technological landscape.
- **IDFC FIRST Bank raises $500 million through global bond issuance:** IDFC FIRST Bank successfully raised $500 million through its maiden international bond issuance, marking its entry into global debt markets. The three-year senior notes attracted anchor investors like BlackRock and Capital Group, signaling confidence in the bank's financial health and growth prospects.

## Market breadth
The market breadth was overwhelmingly negative today, with 346 declines significantly outnumbering 152 advances out of a total of 498 traded stocks. This indicates that the selling pressure was broad-based across the market, rather than concentrated in a few specific stocks or sectors.

## FII / DII flows
Today, both Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) and Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were net buyers in the Indian equity cash market.

| Investor Category | Net Activity (₹ crore) |
| :---------------- | :--------------------- |
| FII net | +1,652 |
| DII net | +2,579 |

Looking at the 5-day history, FIIs have been net sellers over the past five sessions, with today's buying breaking a trend of net outflows. In contrast, DIIs have consistently been net buyers, providing crucial support to the market and absorbing FII selling pressure. Over the last five trading sessions, FIIs have net sold ₹2,388 crore, while DIIs have net bought a substantial ₹18,231 crore. This consistent DII buying has been a key factor in preventing a sharper market correction.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was largely driven by a continuation of global concerns and their domestic repercussions. The most prominent factor was the persistent rise in crude oil prices, which kept inflation worries alive and directly impacted the Energy sector, making it the biggest sectoral loser. This also put pressure on the Indian Rupee, which hit a three-week low against the US Dollar, despite intervention from the RBI.

Globally, the ongoing sell-off in US Treasuries, pushing yields higher, contributed to a cautious sentiment, as it signals increased borrowing costs and potential economic headwinds. While there were no major domestic economic data releases or policy decisions today, the cumulative effect of these global cues, coupled with a survey indicating India as Asia's least preferred market, led to broad-based selling and a negative market breadth.

## Setup for tomorrow
As we head into Thursday, global cues will remain critical. Investors will closely watch the Federal Reserve's July meeting minutes for insights into future monetary policy direction, which could impact global bond yields and currency movements. The trajectory of crude oil prices will also continue to be a key determinant for market sentiment, especially for the Energy sector and the Rupee. US and European markets' overnight performance, particularly any shifts in bond yields, will set the tone for Asian markets. Domestically, the market will look for signs of FII buying continuity and any fresh triggers to break the current losing streak. Key levels for Nifty 50 to watch would be the 24,000 mark for support and 24,150-24,200 for resistance.
