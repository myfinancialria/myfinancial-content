# Pre-Market Brief — 24 November 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets saw a strong rally, especially in US tech, driven by AI optimism.
*   GIFT Nifty indicates a flat to slightly negative open for the Indian market.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remain strong net buyers, driving market momentum.
*   The AI boom and its potential impact on inflation are key themes globally, influencing Fed rate hike bets.
*   FII flows continue to be the primary support for the Indian market rally.
*   Inflation concerns and the unpredictability of tax policies (LTCG debate) pose underlying risks.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Global markets witnessed a significant upswing overnight, largely propelled by renewed optimism around artificial intelligence (AI) and a strong performance in US technology stocks. The Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all closed with substantial gains, with Nasdaq leading the charge. In Asia this morning, the Nikkei 225 surged, reflecting the positive global sentiment, particularly in AI-linked stocks. However, the FTSE 100 in the UK and the Hang Seng in Hong Kong showed minor declines, while Shanghai posted a modest gain.

**Interpretation:** The strong positive close in the US, especially in tech, and a significant rally in Japan suggest a generally positive global tone. However, the mixed performance in other Asian markets and a slightly negative GIFT Nifty could temper the enthusiasm for the Indian open, pointing to a potentially flat to cautious start despite the strong global tailwinds.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) strengthened slightly against the US Dollar, closing at 92.90, a 0.24% increase. The Dollar Index, which measures the dollar's strength against a basket of major currencies, saw a minor decline of 0.36% to 103.94. In the crude oil market, Brent crude futures fell by 2.73% to $86.93, while WTI crude remained largely flat with a marginal gain of 0.05% at $80.21. Gold prices dipped by 0.47% to $3634.20, and Silver also saw a notable decline of 2.04% to $42.26. The US 10-year Treasury yield edged up by 0.12% to 4.92%.

**Interpretation:** A slightly stronger Rupee against a weakening Dollar Index is generally positive for India, potentially easing import costs. The fall in Brent crude prices is a significant relief, as lower crude oil prices can reduce India's import bill, ease inflationary pressures, and benefit oil marketing companies (OMCs), paint, and aviation sectors. However, the rise in the US 10-year yield could make US assets more attractive, potentially impacting foreign institutional investor (FII) flows into emerging markets like India. The dip in gold and silver prices reflects a shift away from safe-haven assets, possibly due to increased risk appetite in equity markets.

## GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling
The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 25490.00, showing a marginal decline of -0.02%.

**Interpretation:** This indicates a flat to slightly negative opening bias for the Nifty 50 today, suggesting that despite strong overnight global cues, the Indian market might open cautiously or consolidate.

## FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were significant net buyers yesterday, pumping in ₹5,673 crore into the Indian equity market. Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), on the other hand, were net sellers, offloading equities worth ₹-2,760 crore. This trend of FII buying and DII selling has been consistent over the last five sessions.

DIIs have largely been booking profits or rebalancing portfolios against this FII influx. This FII buying spree has been a key factor in the market's recent upward momentum.

## Stock & sector news that matters

*   **AI boom looms over Warsh's Fed as inflation worries grow**
    Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh highlighted the transformative impact of artificial intelligence on the economy, expressing optimism about its benefits but also cautioning about growing inflation concerns.
    **Why it matters:** This news sets the global macroeconomic tone, suggesting that while AI is driving market enthusiasm (as seen in US tech rallies), central banks like the Fed are closely watching its potential inflationary effects. This could influence future interest rate decisions, impacting global liquidity and FII flows into India.

*   **RBI DG asks primary dealers to bid responsibly in G-Sec underwriting auctions**
    RBI Deputy Governor Rohit Jain has urged primary dealers to ensure responsible bidding in government securities (G-Sec) auctions, citing instances of volatile pricing that disrupt orderly market conditions.
    **Why it matters:** This intervention signals the RBI's focus on maintaining stability in the bond market. Any volatility in G-Sec yields can impact borrowing costs for the government and corporations, and also influence overall liquidity and investor sentiment in the financial system.

*   **IIFCL IPO: Govt plans to sell part of its stake, company to issue fresh shares**
    The government plans to partially divest its stake in India Infrastructure Finance Company Ltd (IIFCL) through an Initial Public Offering (IPO) by the end of the current fiscal year.
    **Why it matters:** This indicates the government's continued push for divestment and its focus on infrastructure financing. An IPO of a major infrastructure finance company could bring attention to the infrastructure sector and potentially create opportunities for investors interested in this space.

*   **Investors mint Rs 34.61 lakh cr in November as markets rally nearly 11%**
    Indian equity markets have seen a substantial surge in November, with the BSE Sensex climbing nearly 11 percent, adding ₹34.61 lakh crore to investor wealth, largely driven by foreign institutional investors.
    **Why it matters:** This highlights the strong positive momentum in the Indian market this month, primarily attributed to FII buying. It underscores the current bullish sentiment and the significant wealth creation happening, but also points to the market's reliance on foreign flows.

*   **HDFC Bank, Reliance Industries among 10 stocks with highest DII holding in Q2**
    Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) increased their overall net investment to ₹1.69 lakh crore in Q2, with their share rising to an all-time high. HDFC Bank and Reliance Industries are among the top 10 stocks with the highest DII holdings.
    **Why it matters:** This shows where domestic money is being deployed and the conviction of DIIs in large-cap, fundamentally strong companies. High DII holding in these bellwether stocks can provide a degree of stability and indicate domestic confidence in their long-term prospects.

*   **The LTCG debate: Why markets need tax stability and not ad-hoc fixes**
    Concerns are rising among Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) about the unpredictability of India's tax policies, particularly regarding Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax, leading to calls for reconsideration.
    **Why it matters:** This is a critical issue for FII sentiment. Tax policy stability is crucial for attracting and retaining foreign capital. Any uncertainty or adverse changes in LTCG tax could deter FIIs, potentially impacting future foreign investment flows and market stability.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
*   **Global AI Developments and Fed Commentary:** Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh's comments on AI and inflation will be closely watched. Markets typically react to central bank commentary with increased volatility, especially if it hints at changes in monetary policy or economic outlook. Strong AI-related news can drive tech stocks.
*   **RBI's Stance on G-Sec Auctions:** The RBI Deputy Governor's remarks on responsible bidding in G-Sec auctions indicate the central bank's vigilance over bond market stability. Any further communication or actions from the RBI regarding bond yields can impact banking stocks, bond prices, and overall liquidity.
*   **FII Flow Trends:** The continued strong FII buying is a major market driver. Any significant shift in this trend, either a slowdown or reversal, could lead to market consolidation or correction. Markets tend to follow the direction of sustained institutional flows.
*   **LTCG Tax Debate:** The ongoing discussion around Long-Term Capital Gains tax policy is a key sentiment indicator for foreign investors. Uncertainty here can lead to cautious FII participation, while clarity or favourable policy changes could boost confidence.

## What to watch in today's session
1.  **Global Cues and AI Momentum:** Monitor how global markets, especially US tech and Asian indices, react to the ongoing AI narrative.
2.  **FII Flows:** Keep a close eye on FII buying activity. Their sustained support is crucial for market direction.
3.  **Crude Oil Prices:** The recent dip in Brent crude is positive. Further movements will impact inflation expectations and sectors like OMCs, aviation, and paints.
4.  **USD/INR Movement:** A stronger Rupee is beneficial, but any sharp depreciation could signal broader economic concerns.
5.  **RBI's Bond Market Vigilance:** Watch for any further developments or statements from the RBI regarding G-Secs, as it impacts interest rate sensitive sectors.
6.  **LTCG Tax Debate:** The ongoing discussion around tax stability for FIIs is a key sentiment driver and could influence foreign investment decisions.

## Bottom line
The Indian market is likely to open flat to slightly negative today, as indicated by GIFT Nifty, despite a strong overnight rally in global markets, particularly in US tech. FIIs remain the primary drivers of the current market momentum with consistent buying, while DIIs have been net sellers. Investors should remain cautious about underlying inflation concerns and the ongoing debate around tax policy stability, which could influence foreign capital flows.
