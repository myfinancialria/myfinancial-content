# ITR Deadline: 31 October for Audited Accounts & Penalties

**TL;DR**
- The Income Tax Return (ITR) filing deadline for Financial Year 2025-26 (Assessment Year 2026-27) is 31 October 2026 for certain categories of taxpayers.
- This deadline primarily applies to companies and individuals, HUFs, firms, or LLPs whose accounts are required to be audited.
- Missing this deadline incurs a late filing fee of up to ₹5,000 under Section 234F, along with interest under Section 234A on any unpaid tax.
- Beyond monetary penalties, late filing can also lead to the inability to carry forward certain losses to future assessment years.
- Even if the original deadline is missed, a belated return can generally be filed by 31 December 2026, though with applicable penalties.

## What this means in plain terms

For most salaried individuals, the Income Tax Return (ITR) deadline for a financial year typically falls on 31 July. However, the Income Tax Department sets a later deadline for specific categories of taxpayers who have more complex financial affairs, particularly those whose accounts need to be formally audited. This extended deadline is usually 31 October.

Essentially, if you run a business or profession that requires an audit under the Income Tax Act, or if you are a company, then your ITR for the Financial Year 2025-26 (which covers income earned from 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026) must be filed by 31 October 2026. Failing to meet this date can result in financial penalties and other disadvantages, even if you eventually file your return.

## Who must file by 31 October?

The Income Tax Act, 1961, specifies different due dates for filing Income Tax Returns based on the taxpayer's category and the nature of their income. The 31 October deadline for Assessment Year 2026-27 (covering Financial Year 2025-26) applies to the following:

### Companies
All companies, whether private or public, are required to get their accounts audited under the Companies Act, 2013. Consequently, they must file their ITR by 31 October of the relevant assessment year.

### Taxpayers whose accounts are required to be audited
This category includes individuals, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), firms (including LLPs), and co-operative societies who are mandated to get their accounts audited under the Income Tax Act or any other law. The audit requirement typically kicks in if their gross receipts or turnover exceeds certain thresholds. For instance, for businesses, the threshold for audit is generally ₹1 crore, and for professionals, it's ₹50 lakhs, though these limits can be higher under specific conditions (e.g., if opting for presumptive taxation and declaring income below the presumptive rate).

### Partners of a firm whose accounts are required to be audited
Even if an individual partner's personal accounts do not require an audit, if the partnership firm they are a part of needs to get its accounts audited, then all partners of that firm also have a 31 October ITR filing deadline. This ensures consistency in financial reporting related to the firm's operations.

## Understanding Penalties for Late Filing

Missing the 31 October ITR deadline can lead to several financial consequences as per the Income Tax Act. These penalties are designed to encourage timely compliance.

### Late Filing Fee (Section 234F)
If you fail to file your ITR by the due date, a late filing fee is levied under Section 234F of the Income Tax Act.
*   **₹5,000:** This fee applies if your total income exceeds ₹5 lakhs.
*   **₹1,000:** This reduced fee applies if your total income is ₹5 lakhs or less.
It's important to note that this fee is applicable even if you have no tax payable, as it is a penalty for the delay in filing the return itself, not for unpaid tax.

### Interest for Default in Furnishing Return (Section 234A)
Beyond the late filing fee, if there is any tax outstanding, interest is charged under Section 234A.
*   **Rate:** Interest is charged at 1% per month or part of a month.
*   **Calculation:** This interest is calculated on the amount of tax remaining unpaid, starting from the original due date (31 October in this case) until the actual date the return is filed.
This interest is levied on the net tax payable after deducting any advance tax paid, TDS (Tax Deducted at Source), TCS (Tax Collected at Source), and any relief under Section 90/90A/91.

### Interest for Default in Advance Tax (Sections 234B and 234C)
If you are liable to pay advance tax and have either not paid it or paid less than 90% of your total tax liability by the financial year-end, interest under Section 234B may be levied. Similarly, if advance tax instalments are not paid on time or are insufficient, Section 234C interest applies. These interests are calculated from the respective advance tax due dates until the date of payment or until the date of filing the return, whichever is earlier. While these are not directly penalties for missing the ITR deadline, they often become relevant for taxpayers who miss the 31 October deadline as they are typically also liable for advance tax.

## Beyond Monetary Fines: Other Consequences

The repercussions of not filing your ITR by the due date extend beyond just financial penalties. There are several non-monetary disadvantages that can impact your financial planning and future dealings.

### Loss of Ability to Carry Forward Certain Losses
One of the most significant consequences of late filing is the inability to carry forward certain types of losses to future assessment years. This includes:
*   **Business Losses:** Losses from a business or profession (other than unabsorbed depreciation) cannot be carried forward.
*   **Capital Losses:** Both short-term and long-term capital losses cannot be carried forward.
This means you lose the opportunity to set off these losses against future income, which could have otherwise reduced your tax liability in subsequent years.

### Delayed Refunds
If you are eligible for a tax refund, filing your ITR late will inevitably delay the processing of your refund. The Income Tax Department will only process the refund once the return is filed and assessed. Moreover, the interest on refunds (under Section 244A) is generally calculated from the date of filing the belated return, not from the original due date, further reducing your potential interest income.

### Difficulty in Visa Applications and Loan Approvals
ITR acknowledgements are often required as proof of income and financial stability when applying for visas, especially for countries like the USA, Canada, or Schengen nations. Similarly, banks and financial institutions typically ask for ITR copies for the last few years when you apply for loans (home loans, car loans, personal loans, etc.). A missing or belated ITR can complicate these processes, potentially leading to delays or even rejection of applications.

### Prosecution
While less common for simple delays, repeated or intentional failure to file ITRs, especially when there is a significant tax liability, can lead to prosecution under the Income Tax Act. This can involve imprisonment for a term ranging from three months to seven years, along with a fine, depending on the amount of tax evaded.

## A real example

Let's consider **Meera, 45, who runs a proprietorship firm in Mumbai with an annual turnover of ₹1.2 crores.** Her firm's accounts are required to be audited. For FY 2025-26, her total tax liability after TDS was ₹3.5 lakhs. She had paid ₹3 lakhs as advance tax but still had ₹50,000 outstanding tax. The ITR due date for her was 31 October 2026. However, due to unforeseen circumstances, she managed to file her ITR only on 15 January 2027.

Let's calculate the penalties she would face:

1.  **Late Filing Fee (Section 234F):**
    *   Meera's total income would likely exceed ₹5 lakhs (given her turnover and tax liability).
    *   Therefore, she would be liable for a late filing fee of **₹5,000**.

2.  **Interest for Default in Furnishing Return (Section 234A):**
    *   Outstanding tax: ₹50,000.
    *   Period of delay:
        *   November 2026: 1 month
        *   December 2026: 1 month
        *   January 2027: Part of a month (even 1 day counts as a full month) = 1 month
        *   Total delay = 3 months.
    *   Interest rate: 1% per month.
    *   Interest = ₹50,000 * 1% * 3 months = **₹1,500**.

3.  **Total Penalty and Interest:**
    *   Late Filing Fee: ₹5,000
    *   Interest u/s 234A: ₹1,500
    *   Total additional amount payable = ₹5,000 + ₹1,500 = **₹6,500**.

In addition to this, if Meera had defaulted on her advance tax payments, she would also be liable for interest under Sections 234B and 234C. For instance, if her ₹50,000 outstanding tax was due to underpayment of advance tax, she would have accrued interest on that amount from the respective advance tax due dates.

## What to do this week

1.  **Confirm Your Due Date:** Verify if your specific income profile or business structure requires you to file by 31 October. If your accounts need an audit, this is your deadline.
2.  **Gather Documents:** Start compiling all necessary financial documents, including bank statements, balance sheets, profit & loss statements, audit reports, TDS certificates (Form 16A/16), and any other income proofs.
3.  **Contact Your Auditor/CA:** If you haven't already, reach out to your Chartered Accountant or tax professional immediately to ensure your accounts are audited and the report is ready well before the deadline.

5.  **Plan for E-filing:** Familiarise yourself with the e-filing portal (https://incometax.gov.in) and ensure your digital signature certificate (DSC) is valid, as it's often required for filing by companies and audited entities.

## FAQ

### Who exactly needs an audit for ITR filing?
An audit is generally required for businesses with a turnover exceeding ₹1 crore, or professionals with gross receipts over ₹50 lakhs. Higher thresholds may apply under presumptive taxation schemes if certain conditions are met. Companies always require an audit.

### What happens if I file a belated return after 31 October?
If you miss the 31 October deadline, you can still file a "belated return" generally until 31 December of the assessment year (i.e., 31 December 2026 for FY 2025-26). However, this will incur a late filing fee under Section 234F and interest under Section 234A, if applicable.

### Can I revise my ITR if I make a mistake after filing by 31 October?
Yes, if you discover an error or omission after filing your original return, you can file a "revised return" under Section 139(5). This can generally be done until 31 December of the assessment year (31 December 2026 for FY 2025-26) or before the completion of assessment, whichever is earlier.

### Is there any way to waive off the late filing fee or interest?
Generally, no. The late filing fee and interest under Section 234A are mandatory if the return is not filed by the due date or tax is not paid on time. In rare, exceptional circumstances, the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) may allow condonation of delay, but this is not a common occurrence.

### What if I am a salaried employee, but my employer's accounts are audited?
If you are a salaried employee and do not have any business or professional income that requires an audit, your ITR due date remains 31 July. The 31 October deadline applies to the entity whose accounts are audited, not necessarily its employees.

### How is the 1% interest under Section 234A calculated if I file on, say, 15 November?
The interest is calculated for each month or part of a month. So, if you file on 15 November, it will be counted as one full month of delay (from 31 October to 30 November), and interest for that month will be charged. If you file on 1 December, it would be two months.
