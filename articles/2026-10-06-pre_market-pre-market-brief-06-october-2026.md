# Pre-Market Brief — 06 October 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets ended mostly positive overnight, with Asian peers also trading in the green this morning.
*   GIFT Nifty indicates a positive opening bias for the Indian benchmark indices.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued to provide strong buying support.
*   Strong Q2 business updates from Trent and Dabur could bring specific stocks into focus.
*   Nifty's 22700 level is a crucial pivot, acting as both immediate resistance and support based on option chain data.
*   The continued FII selling trend and rising US 10-year yields remain key risks for the market.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Global equities largely closed higher overnight, with US markets showing a positive trend led by technology stocks. The Nasdaq advanced by 1.05%, while the S&P 500 gained 0.66% and the Dow Jones rose 0.18%. In Asia this morning, markets are also trading positively, with Japan's Nikkei 225 up 1.05% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng gaining 1.00%. The Shanghai Composite also saw a modest rise of 0.31%, and the UK's FTSE 100 closed 0.89% higher.

**Interpretation:** The broadly positive sentiment from global markets, particularly the strong showing in the US and Asian indices, suggests a favourable backdrop for the Indian market open today.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) saw a slight depreciation against the US Dollar, trading at ₹96.39, up 0.07%. The Dollar Index, however, eased slightly by 0.15% to 102.02. In the energy markets, crude oil prices declined, with Brent Crude falling by 1.23% to $99.09 and WTI Crude down 1.59% to $88.01. Gold prices rose by 0.59% to $4181.20, while Silver also gained 0.98% to $61.47. The US 10-year Treasury yield increased by 0.64% to 5.31%.

**Interpretation:** A slightly weaker Rupee could offer a tailwind for export-oriented sectors like IT and Pharmaceuticals, but it also increases import costs. The dip in crude oil prices is a positive for India, potentially easing inflationary pressures and benefiting sectors like oil marketing companies, paints, and aviation. The rise in the US 10-year yield is a point of caution, as higher global yields can make emerging markets less attractive, potentially impacting FII flows.

## GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling

**Interpretation:** This indicates a positive opening for the Indian benchmark Nifty 50, suggesting a gap-up start to today's trading session.

## FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape
Yesterday, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) remained net sellers in the Indian equity cash segment, offloading shares worth ₹-4,699 crore. In contrast, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their strong buying spree, injecting ₹5,182 crore into the market. This trend of FII selling being largely absorbed by DII buying has been consistent over the last five trading sessions.

## Option-chain signals (OI) — where support & resistance sit
For the Nifty, the highest Call Open Interest (OI) is concentrated at the 22700 strike (33,467,720 contracts), followed by 22800 (30,489,290 contracts), indicating strong resistance levels. Conversely, the highest Put OI is also at 22700 (40,327,430 contracts), followed by 22600 (39,833,690 contracts), suggesting robust support. The Nifty PCR (Put-Call Ratio) stands at 1.3, which leans towards a supportive or bullish-to-neutral sentiment.

For Bank Nifty, the 56000 strike holds the highest Call OI (1,212,750 contracts), acting as a significant resistance. The 55000 strike also has substantial Call OI (776,760 contracts). On the Put side, 55000 has the highest OI (1,047,480 contracts), serving as immediate support, followed by 56000 (755,400 contracts). The Bank Nifty PCR is 1.02, indicating a neutral-to-slightly supportive outlook.

For Sensex, the 73000 strike has the highest Call OI (1,082,400 contracts), followed by 74000 (1,078,820 contracts), marking key resistance zones. The highest Put OI is at 72500 (1,721,440 contracts), followed by 72000 (1,234,720 contracts), providing strong support. The Sensex PCR is 1.25, suggesting a supportive bias.

**Interpretation:** The Nifty's 22700 level is a critical battleground, with both highest call and put OI, suggesting it will be a key level to respect today. A sustained move above or below this level could dictate the day's trend. For Bank Nifty, 55000 is immediate support, while 56000 acts as strong resistance. Sensex finds strong support at 72500 and faces resistance at 73000. PCR values above 1 for all indices generally indicate that market participants are writing more puts than calls, suggesting a supportive undertone or expectation of limited downside.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
*   **Start of Q2FY27 Earnings Season:** While no major Nifty 500 results are today, the news flow around Q2 business updates (like Trent, Dabur) and analyst expectations for sectors like banking indicate the earnings season is kicking off. Markets typically show stock-specific volatility and sector rotation around earnings announcements, with strong results leading to rallies and weak results causing corrections.
*   **Global Central Bank Commentary (BOJ):** Expectations of a potential December rate hike from the Bank of Japan (BOJ) could influence global bond yields and currency movements. Central bank policy shifts often lead to increased volatility in global markets, and India, as an emerging economy, can experience ripple effects on FII flows and currency strength.
*   **FII Flow Trend:** The continued selling by FIIs is a key factor to monitor. Persistent outflows can put pressure on benchmark indices, while any signs of moderation or reversal could provide a boost.

## Bottom line
The Indian market is poised for a positive open today, largely influenced by favourable global cues and a supportive GIFT Nifty. The Nifty's 22700 level will serve as a crucial point of contention, determining the immediate directional bias. While domestic institutional buying continues to provide a strong cushion, the persistent selling by foreign investors and rising US bond yields remain factors necessitating caution.
