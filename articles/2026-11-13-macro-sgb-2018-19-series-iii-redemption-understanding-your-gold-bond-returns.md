## How Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) Work

Sovereign Gold Bonds are government securities denominated in grams of gold. They are issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on behalf of the Government of India. When you invest in an SGB, you are essentially buying gold in a paperless form. Each unit of SGB represents one gram of gold, and the issue price is fixed based on the simple average of the closing price of 999 purity gold, published by the India Bullion and Jewellers Association (IBJA) Ltd., for the last three business days of the week preceding the subscription period.

A key feature of SGBs is the fixed interest rate of 2.50% per annum, paid semi-annually on the initial investment amount. This interest income is deposited directly into the investor's bank account. The bonds have a maturity period of eight years, though an early exit option is available after the fifth year, exercisable on interest payment dates.

SGBs eliminate the risks and costs associated with holding physical gold, such as storage charges, purity concerns, and making charges. They can be held in a demat account, making them easy to trade on stock exchanges, although liquidity can sometimes be a concern. The government backing ensures the safety of the investment, making it a secure alternative to physical gold or gold ETFs.

## SGB 2018-19 Series III: Issue Price, Redemption, and Returns

The SGB 2018-19 Series III was issued in November 2018 at an issue price of ₹3,183 per gram. After completing its full eight-year tenure, the bond is now being redeemed on November 13, 2026. The redemption price for SGBs is calculated based on the simple average of the closing price of 999 purity gold, published by the India Bullion and Jewellers Association (IBJA) Ltd., for the last three business days preceding the date of redemption.

For investors in this specific series, the returns have been quite remarkable. As per reports, investors stand to earn capital gains of up to 348% on their initial investment, excluding the interest income. This significant appreciation reflects the substantial increase in gold prices over the past eight years.

In addition to these capital gains, investors also received a steady stream of interest income. At 2.50% per annum on the initial issue price of ₹3,183 per gram, this translates to ₹79.575 per gram each year. Over the eight-year tenure, the total interest earned per gram would be ₹636.60 (₹79.575 x 8). This combination of capital appreciation and regular interest payments underscores the attractive nature of SGBs as a long-term investment in gold.

## Tax Implications of SGB Redemption

One of the most appealing aspects of investing in Sovereign Gold Bonds is their favourable tax treatment, particularly upon maturity. For individual investors, the capital gains arising from the redemption of SGBs after the full eight-year maturity period are completely exempt from income tax. This is a significant advantage compared to other forms of gold investment, where capital gains are typically taxable.

However, it is important to understand the tax implications for different scenarios:
*   **Redemption at Maturity (8 years):** For individuals, the long-term capital gains (LTCG) are exempt from tax. This applies when the bond is redeemed directly with the RBI after its full tenure.
*   **Early Redemption (after 5 years):** If an investor opts for early redemption after the fifth year but before maturity, the capital gains are still considered long-term. In this case, the gains are taxable at 20% after indexation benefits.
*   **Sale on Stock Exchange:** If SGBs are sold on a stock exchange before the eight-year maturity:
    *   If sold within three years of purchase, any gains are considered Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) and are taxed at the investor's applicable income tax slab rate.
    *   If sold after three years but before eight years, any gains are considered Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) and are taxed at 20% with indexation benefits.

The interest income earned on SGBs, which is paid semi-annually, is taxable. This interest is added to the investor's total income and taxed at their applicable income tax slab rate. However, no Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is applicable on the interest paid on SGBs. Investors are responsible for declaring this income and paying the appropriate tax.

## SGBs vs. Other Gold Investment Avenues

Sovereign Gold Bonds offer a distinct set of advantages when compared to other popular methods of investing in gold, such as physical gold, Gold Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs), and Gold Mutual Funds.

**Physical Gold:** Investing in physical gold (jewellery, coins, bars) comes with several practical challenges. There are concerns about purity, often accompanied by making charges which reduce the effective gold content for investment purposes, and Goods and Services Tax (GST) on purchase. Additionally, storing physical gold requires secure arrangements, which can incur costs or risks. SGBs eliminate these issues entirely, offering gold in a dematerialised form without purity concerns or storage costs.

**Gold ETFs and Gold Mutual Funds:** These instruments offer a convenient way to invest in gold digitally and are traded on stock exchanges. They provide liquidity and eliminate purity concerns. However, Gold ETFs and Mutual Funds typically come with expense ratios, which are recurring charges that eat into your returns over time. More importantly, capital gains from these investments are generally taxable, either as short-term or long-term capital gains depending on the holding period, without the specific exemption available for SGBs redeemed at maturity.

**SGB Advantages:** Beyond the tax exemption on maturity for individuals, SGBs provide a fixed interest income (2.50% p.a. on the initial investment), which is not available with physical gold or gold ETFs/mutual funds. They are backed by the Government of India, offering a high degree of safety. While SGBs have an 8-year lock-in with an exit option after 5 years, this long-term horizon is often aligned with gold's role as a strategic asset. The absence of an expense ratio further enhances their appeal for long-term investors seeking exposure to gold.

## A real example

Let's consider Priya, 42, a self-employed consultant earning ₹35 lakhs per annum in Mumbai. In November 2018, Priya invested in 10 grams of SGB 2018-19 Series III.

*   **Initial Investment:**
    *   Issue Price per gram: ₹3,183
    *   Total Investment for 10 grams: ₹3,183 * 10 = ₹31,830

*   **Interest Earned:**
    *   Annual interest rate: 2.50% on the issue price
    *   Annual interest per gram: 2.50% of ₹3,183 = ₹79.575
    *   Total annual interest for 10 grams: ₹79.575 * 10 = ₹795.75
    *   Total interest over 8 years: ₹795.75 * 8 = ₹6,366

*   **Redemption Value:**
    *   As per reports, investors earned up to 348% in capital gains on this series.
    *   Capital gain per gram: 348% of ₹3,183 = ₹11,073.24
    *   Redemption Price per gram: Issue Price + Capital Gain = ₹3,183 + ₹11,073.24 = ₹14,256.24
    *   Total Redemption Value for 10 grams: ₹14,256.24 * 10 = ₹1,42,562.40

*   **Total Returns for Priya:**
    *   Total Capital Gain: ₹1,42,562.40 (Redemption Value) - ₹31,830 (Initial Investment) = ₹1,10,732.40
    *   Total Return (Capital Gain + Interest): ₹1,10,732.40 + ₹6,366 = ₹1,17,098.40

*   **Tax Implications:**
    *   The capital gain of ₹1,10,732.40 is completely exempt from tax for Priya as an individual investor, since the bond was held till maturity.
    *   The interest income of ₹6,366, received over 8 years, would have been added to Priya's taxable income each year and taxed at her applicable slab rate.

This example illustrates how Priya's initial investment of ₹31,830 grew significantly, yielding a tax-free capital gain and consistent interest income over the eight-year period.

## FAQ

### What is the maturity period for Sovereign Gold Bonds?
Sovereign Gold Bonds have a fixed maturity period of eight years. However, investors have an option to exit the bond early after the fifth year, on interest payment dates.

### How is the SGB redemption price calculated?
The redemption price of an SGB is based on the simple average of the closing price of 999 purity gold, published by the India Bullion and Jewellers Association (IBJA) Ltd., for the last three business days preceding the date of redemption.

### Is the interest earned on SGBs taxable?
Yes, the interest income from Sovereign Gold Bonds, paid semi-annually, is taxable. It is added to your total income and taxed as per your applicable income tax slab rate. However, there is no TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) on this interest.

### Are capital gains on SGBs taxable at maturity?
For individual investors, the capital gains arising from the redemption of SGBs after holding them for the full eight-year maturity period are fully exempt from income tax. This is a significant tax benefit.

### Can I sell my SGBs before maturity?
Yes, SGBs are tradable on stock exchanges. You can sell them before maturity, but if sold within three years, gains are short-term taxable. If sold after three years but before eight years, gains are long-term taxable with indexation benefits.

### What happens if I don't have a demat account for SGBs?
If you did not hold your SGBs in a demat account, you would have received a Holding Certificate. Upon maturity, the redemption proceeds will be credited directly to the bank account linked to your SGB application, as per RBI records.
