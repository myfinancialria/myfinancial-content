# Pre-Market Brief — 03 August 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets saw a mixed close, with US indices ending higher on tech optimism, while Asian markets, particularly Japan, declined.
- GIFT Nifty is signalling a strong positive opening for Indian equities, up over 1%.
- Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continue to be net buyers, providing crucial support, while Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were marginal net buyers yesterday.
- A coordinated US-Japan intervention to support the Yen and China's pledge for timely policy adjustments are key global developments.
- Nifty 50's immediate resistance is seen around 24600-24625, with strong support at 24500.
- The sharp divergence in crude oil prices (Brent up, WTI down) and specific stock reactions to Q1 results will be in focus.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Overnight, US markets closed firmly in the green, driven by renewed optimism in artificial intelligence and strong tech earnings, leading to significant inflows into US equity funds. However, Asian markets this morning are largely trading lower, with Japan's Nikkei experiencing a notable decline due to a stronger Yen impacting exporters and a confirmed US-Japan intervention in currency markets. China's central bank has also pledged timely policy adjustments to support its economy. This mixed global sentiment suggests a cautious yet potentially positive start for Indian markets, influenced by the strong GIFT Nifty signal.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee strengthened significantly against the US Dollar, climbing to a one-month peak at ₹95.22, a drop of 0.48%. This was aided by a decline in oil prices and increased foreign inflows. The Dollar Index also saw a slight dip of 0.12% to 99.68. In commodities, Brent Crude rose by 1.22% to $90.12 per barrel, while WTI Crude experienced a sharp decline of 5.85% to $79.72. Gold prices moved higher by 1.68% to $4117.30, and Silver also gained 0.97% to $58.15. The US 10-Year Yield increased by 1.76% to 4.74%.

**Interpretation:** A stronger Rupee (USD/INR down) is generally positive for India's import bill and helps curb imported inflation, potentially benefiting sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paints, and aviation. However, it could be a slight headwind for export-oriented sectors like IT and pharmaceuticals. The divergence in crude prices, with Brent (more relevant for India) rising, could put some pressure on companies sensitive to crude input costs. Rising US 10-year yields could make US assets more attractive, posing a potential risk to FII flows into emerging markets like India.

## GIFT Nifty — what it's signalling
The GIFT Nifty is currently trading at 24676.00, up by 1.19%. This indicates a significant gap-up opening for the Nifty 50 index today.

**Interpretation:** The strong positive signal from GIFT Nifty suggests that Indian markets are poised for a firm start, likely opening higher than yesterday's close. This could build on the momentum from yesterday's rally.

## FII/DII flows — who's driving the tape
Yesterday, Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were marginal net buyers in the Indian equity cash segment, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their strong buying trend. Over the last five sessions, FIIs have largely been net buyers, with DIIs consistently providing significant support.

## Stock & sector news that matters

-   **Zee shares tumble 12% after Sebi action against Subhash Chandra, Punit Goenka**
    Zee Entertainment shares plunged over 12% after Sebi barred founder Subhash Chandra and CEO Punit Goenka from the securities market for one year over an unauthorised property pledge case.
    **Why it matters:** This news is highly negative for Zee Entertainment Enterprises Ltd. (ZEEL) and could lead to further selling pressure on the stock. It also raises concerns about corporate governance in the media sector.

-   **GAIL India shares tumble 5% despite Q1 net profit doubling to Rs 4,665 crore.**
    GAIL India shares fell 5% despite the Maharatna PSU reporting a near doubling of Q1 FY27 consolidated net profit to ₹4,665 crore. The decline was attributed to one-off gains in the earnings beat.
    **Why it matters:** This indicates that strong headline numbers may not always translate to positive stock performance if the quality of earnings is questioned or if one-off gains are driving the profit. It could impact sentiment for other PSU stocks with similar earnings characteristics.

-   **Rupee climbs to one-month peak of 95.13 vs USD on oil slump, stronger inflows**
    The Indian rupee reached a one-month high as oil prices declined and foreign inflows increased. The Reserve Bank of India is expected to maintain current interest rates this week.
    **Why it matters:** A strengthening rupee is positive for India's macroeconomic stability, potentially easing inflation pressures and reducing the import bill. This could be beneficial for import-heavy sectors and could attract further foreign capital.

-   **Blue Dart Express shares surge 7% after Q1 results.**
    Blue Dart Express shares jumped nearly 7% after the logistics company reported a strong Q1 FY27 performance, driven by robust profit growth and margin expansion.
    **Why it matters:** This positive reaction highlights the importance of strong earnings performance and could bring the logistics sector into focus. It suggests that companies delivering on growth and margins are being rewarded by the market.

-   **HFCL shares rebound 5% on Rs 523 crore order win.**
    HFCL shares rose sharply after the company secured an international order worth ₹522.73 crore, extending investor optimism around the telecom equipment maker.
    **Why it matters:** A significant order win provides a clear growth trigger for HFCL and could boost sentiment for other companies in the telecom equipment and infrastructure sector.

-   **Global Market: Japan, US confirm coordinated yen intervention to stem currency slide**
    Japan's finance ministry confirmed that Tokyo and Washington jointly intervened in currency markets to support the yen for the first time since 2011.
    **Why it matters:** This is a major global currency market event, indicating significant efforts by major economies to manage currency volatility. While directly impacting the Yen, such interventions can have ripple effects across global currency and equity markets, influencing risk appetite.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
-   **RBI Monetary Policy (expected this week):** The rupee news mentions the RBI is expected to maintain current interest rates this week. Central bank policy decisions are always key market movers. Markets typically react with volatility around policy announcements, especially if there are unexpected changes in rates or guidance on inflation and growth.
-   **Global Crude Oil Price Volatility:** The sharp divergence between Brent and WTI crude prices, with Brent rising, will be closely watched. Significant movements in crude oil prices can influence inflation expectations, the Rupee, and the profitability of oil-sensitive sectors like OMCs, aviation, and chemicals.
-   **FII/DII Flow Trends:** The continuation of DII support and FII participation will be crucial for sustaining market momentum. Consistent strong flows typically provide underlying strength, while sudden reversals can lead to market corrections.

Indian markets are poised for a positive start today, largely driven by the strong GIFT Nifty signal and continued domestic institutional support.
