# Pre-Market Brief — 26 May 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets closed mostly positive, with US indices gaining, while Asian markets showed a mixed trend.
- Crude oil prices saw a significant decline overnight, potentially easing inflationary pressures for India.
- The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, indicating a positive sentiment for the local currency.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turned net buyers yesterday after a period of outflows, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their strong buying support.
- Geopolitical tensions in West Asia remain a key concern, impacting global sentiment and leading to delayed IPOs in India.

## Where global markets closed
Global markets presented a largely positive picture overnight, with major US indices closing in the green. The Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 all registered gains, reflecting a generally optimistic sentiment in the Western economies. In Asia, the performance was mixed, with Japan's Nikkei 225 seeing a slight dip, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng posted modest gains. China's Shanghai Composite, however, ended the session lower. This mixed global tone suggests a cautious optimism, with investors weighing positive economic data against ongoing geopolitical uncertainties.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee strengthened against the US Dollar, with the **USD/INR** pair closing at ₹95.43, down by 0.30%. A stronger Rupee is generally positive for the Indian economy as it makes imports cheaper and can help curb imported inflation. The **Dollar Index** stood at 99.09, with its percentage change data unavailable.

In the commodities market, crude oil prices witnessed a significant decline. **Brent Crude** fell by 8.04% to $95.22 per barrel, while **WTI Crude** dropped by 5.12% to $91.65 per barrel. This sharp fall in oil prices is a substantial positive for India, a major oil importer, as it can alleviate concerns about inflation, reduce the import bill, and potentially provide more headroom for the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on monetary policy.

**Gold** prices saw a modest increase of 0.31% to $4534.80, while **Silver** gained 1.24% to $76.83. The slight uptick in precious metals could indicate some safe-haven buying amidst ongoing global uncertainties, despite the overall positive equity market sentiment.

The **US 10-Year Treasury Yield** declined by 0.61% to 4.56%. A fall in US bond yields can make emerging markets like India more attractive to foreign investors, as the relative yield differential becomes more appealing. This could potentially support FII inflows into Indian debt and equity markets.

## Overnight news that matters

**Markets brace for a long conflict as investors reassess global equity risks: Andrew Freris**
West Asian tensions are creating market uncertainty, with investors facing prolonged volatility for months or years. Some Asian markets like Singapore and Taiwan are outperforming the US.
*Why it matters to Indian markets:* This expert view highlights that geopolitical risks, particularly from West Asia, are not transient but could be a persistent factor. This sustained uncertainty can lead to continued volatility in Indian markets, influence FII flows, and prompt investors to re-evaluate risk premiums.

**Water purifier maker Kent RO delays IPO as Mideast war dents sentiment**
Kent RO Systems has postponed its stock market debut, citing global market volatility worsened by the Iran conflict and rising raw material costs.
*Why it matters to Indian markets:* This is a direct impact of geopolitical tensions on India's primary market. An IPO delay by a prominent company like Kent RO indicates that investor sentiment for new listings is fragile, and broader market confidence is being affected by global events. This could lead to a cautious approach for other upcoming IPOs.

**Policy stability, relief on STT and LTCG key to winning back FPIs: Sudip Bandyopadhyay**
Foreign investors are pulling money from Indian markets, and experts urge India to rethink taxes like STT and LTCG, emphasizing policy consistency as a major concern for global funds.
*Why it matters to Indian markets:* This news directly addresses the persistent FII outflows seen in recent times. Suggestions for tax reforms (STT, LTCG) and policy stability are crucial for attracting and retaining foreign capital, which is vital for market depth and liquidity. Any movement on these fronts could significantly impact FII sentiment and future inflows.

**Indian bonds choppy as oil reverses course putting focus back on US-Iran issues**
Indian government bond yields edged higher in early trading Tuesday, influenced by renewed U.S.-Iran tensions that pushed oil prices up. This development reversed the support from softer Treasury yields.
*Why it matters to Indian markets:* While crude oil prices closed significantly lower overnight (as per our data), this news highlights how sensitive Indian bond markets are to geopolitical developments and their potential impact on oil prices. Even the *perception* of rising oil prices due to US-Iran tensions can cause volatility in bond yields, influencing borrowing costs for the government and corporations, and impacting inflation expectations.

## What to watch in today's session
1.  **Geopolitical Developments:** Keep a close eye on any further news or escalation regarding West Asian tensions. These events have a direct bearing on global crude oil prices and overall market sentiment, as evidenced by the delayed IPOs and expert commentary.
2.  **Crude Oil Price Action:** Following the significant overnight drop in Brent and WTI crude, monitor how these prices behave today. Sustained lower oil prices would be a major positive for India, potentially boosting sectors sensitive to fuel costs and easing inflationary pressures.
3.  **FII/DII Flows:** While FIIs turned net buyers yesterday, it's crucial to see if this trend continues or if it was a one-off. Consistent FII buying, coupled with DII support, would provide a strong foundation for the market.
4.  **Impact of Ex-Dividend Stocks:** For investors holding shares in DHAMPURSUG, GPTINFRA, HATSUN, and IRB, today is the ex-dividend date. Monitor how these stocks react to this corporate action, as prices typically adjust downwards to reflect the dividend payout.
5.  **Bond Market Movement:** Watch Indian government bond yields, especially in light of the overnight crude oil price drop and continued global geopolitical concerns. Yield movements can signal shifts in inflation expectations and RBI policy outlook.

## Bottom line
The Indian market is likely to open with a cautiously optimistic bias today, primarily supported by the significant drop in crude oil prices and the return of FII buying yesterday. However, the persistent geopolitical tensions in West Asia continue to cast a shadow, leading to market uncertainty and impacting primary market sentiment. Investors should remain vigilant, focusing on crude oil movements and global news flow, while DII support continues to be a crucial stabilising factor.
