# Earning FD Interest? Why You Should Submit Form 121 — TDS Provisions Explained

**TL;DR**
- Banks deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on Fixed Deposit (FD) interest if it exceeds certain thresholds in a financial year.
- For most individuals, this threshold is ₹50,000; for senior citizens, it is ₹1 lakh.
- Form 121 is a self-declaration form to prevent TDS deduction if your total tax liability for the year is nil.
- This form has replaced the earlier Forms 15G and 15H, effective from April 1, 2026, for the Tax Year 2026-27 onwards.
- Even if Form 121 is submitted and no TDS is deducted, you must still declare the FD interest income when filing your Income Tax Return (ITR).

## What this means in plain terms

When you earn interest on your Fixed Deposits (FDs), the bank might deduct a part of it as Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) before crediting the interest to your account. This happens if your total FD interest from that bank crosses a certain limit in a financial year. For most individuals, this limit is ₹50,000, and for senior citizens (those aged 60 and above), it's ₹1 lakh.

However, if your total income for the financial year is below the basic exemption limit, meaning you don't have any tax liability, you can avoid this TDS deduction. This is where Form 121 comes in. By submitting this form to your bank, you declare that your estimated total income for the year is below the taxable limit, and therefore, no tax is payable by you. This prevents the bank from deducting TDS, ensuring you receive the full interest amount. Remember, even if no TDS is deducted, you are still required to report this interest income when you file your Income Tax Return (ITR).

## Understanding TDS on Fixed Deposit Interest

TDS on FD interest is governed by Section 194A of the Income Tax Act, 2025 (earlier Section 194A of the Income Tax Act, 1961). Banks and other financial institutions are mandated to deduct tax at source if the interest paid or credited to a resident individual's account exceeds specified limits in a financial year.

### Current TDS Thresholds and Rates
For the Tax Year 2026-27 onwards, the TDS thresholds for FD interest are:
*   **₹50,000** for individuals below 60 years of age and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs).
*   **₹1,00,000** for senior citizens (individuals aged 60 years or more at any time during the financial year).

If the interest income crosses these limits, the bank will deduct TDS at a rate of 10%. If you do not provide your Permanent Account Number (PAN) to the bank, the TDS rate will be a higher 20%. It's crucial to note that banks with a Core Banking System (CBS) will aggregate interest income from all FDs held by an individual across all branches of that bank to check if the threshold is crossed.

## Form 121: Your Tool to Prevent Unnecessary TDS

Form 121 is a self-declaration form introduced under Section 393(6) of the Income Tax Act, 2025, read with Rule 211 of the Income Tax Rules, 2026. It unifies the provisions of the erstwhile Forms 15G and 15H, simplifying the process for resident individuals and HUFs to avoid TDS on various incomes, including FD interest.

### Who can submit Form 121?
Form 121 can be submitted by:
*   Resident individuals (irrespective of age, including senior citizens).
*   Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs).
*   Certain eligible assessees (like trusts) whose total income is below the taxable limit.

The primary condition for submitting Form 121 is that **your estimated total income for the financial year must be below the basic exemption limit, resulting in a nil tax liability.** This means even if your FD interest crosses the TDS threshold, if your overall taxable income is zero, you can submit this form. Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), companies, and firms are not eligible to file Form 121.

## How and When to Submit Form 121

Submitting Form 121 correctly and on time is essential to ensure that TDS is not deducted from your FD interest.

### Submission Process
Most banks now offer both online and offline methods for submitting Form 121:
*   **Online Submission:** Many banks allow you to submit Form 121 through their internet banking portal or mobile banking app. This is often the most convenient method. You will typically fill in your personal details, PAN, estimated income for the financial year, and details of the income for which you are seeking non-deduction of TDS.
*   **Offline Submission:** You can download Form 121 from the Income Tax Department's website (incometax.gov.in) or obtain it from your bank branch. Fill it accurately and submit it to the bank where you hold the FD.

Ensure you provide your valid PAN, as it is mandatory. The bank will verify the details and assign a Unique Identification Number (UIN) to your declaration.

### Timelines for Submission
It is advisable to submit Form 121 at the beginning of each financial year, or at least before the first interest payment is due. This ensures that the bank does not deduct TDS from the outset. Form 121 is valid for one financial year only, so you must submit it afresh every year if you continue to meet the eligibility criteria.

### Consequences of Not Submitting or Incorrect Submission
*   **TDS Deduction:** If you are eligible but fail to submit Form 121, the bank will deduct TDS as per the applicable rates (10% or 20% if PAN is not provided) once your interest income crosses the threshold.
*   **Claiming Refund:** If TDS is deducted, and your actual tax liability for the year is nil, you will have to claim a refund by filing your Income Tax Return (ITR). This can lead to a delay in receiving your full interest amount.
*   **Penalties:** Providing false or incorrect information in Form 121 can lead to penalties under Section 277 of the Income Tax Act, 2025 (earlier Section 277 of the Income Tax Act, 1961).

## A real example

**Priya, 32, ₹18L CTC, Hyderabad**

Priya works in a tech company in Hyderabad, with an annual CTC of ₹18 lakhs. She has invested ₹8 lakhs in a Fixed Deposit, earning 7.5% interest per annum.

Let's calculate her FD interest and potential TDS scenario for the Tax Year 2026-27:

1.  **Annual FD Interest:** ₹8,00,000 \* 7.5% = ₹60,000
2.  **TDS Threshold for Priya:** Since Priya is below 60 years of age, the TDS threshold for her is ₹50,000.
3.  **TDS Applicability:** Her FD interest of ₹60,000 exceeds the ₹50,000 threshold. Therefore, the bank will deduct TDS.
4.  **TDS Calculation (without Form 121):** The bank will deduct 10% TDS on the entire interest amount of ₹60,000.
    *   TDS = 10% of ₹60,000 = ₹6,000.
    *   Priya would receive ₹60,000 - ₹6,000 = ₹54,000 as net interest.

Now, let's consider her overall tax situation. Priya claims deductions under Section 80C (₹1.5 lakhs) and pays health insurance premiums (₹25,000 under Section 80D). Under the old tax regime, her taxable income might be substantial, and she would likely have a tax liability. In such a scenario, she cannot submit Form 121 because her tax liability is not nil.

However, if Priya had a lower income, say ₹4 lakhs, and after all deductions, her taxable income fell below the basic exemption limit (e.g., ₹3 lakhs under the new tax regime or ₹2.5 lakhs under the old regime), her tax liability would be nil. In this hypothetical situation, even though her FD interest is ₹60,000, she *could* submit Form 121 to prevent the bank from deducting the ₹6,000 TDS. She would still have to declare the ₹60,000 interest in her ITR, but since her total tax liability is nil, she wouldn't pay any tax on it.

## What to do this week

1.  **Check your FD interest income:** Review your bank statements or contact your bank to determine the estimated interest income from all your Fixed Deposits for the current financial year.
2.  **Verify TDS thresholds:** Confirm if your total FD interest from a particular bank is likely to cross the ₹50,000 (for non-senior citizens) or ₹1 lakh (for senior citizens) threshold.
3.  **Assess your tax liability:** Estimate your total taxable income for the current financial year. If your total tax liability is expected to be nil, then you are eligible to submit Form 121.
4.  **Submit Form 121:** If eligible, submit Form 121 to your bank(s) as soon as possible. You can usually do this online via net banking or by submitting a physical form at the branch.
5.  **Keep records:** Retain a copy of the submitted Form 121 or the acknowledgment received from the bank for your records.

## FAQ

### What is Form 121?
Form 121 is a self-declaration form submitted to banks or financial institutions to request non-deduction of TDS on certain incomes, like FD interest, if your total tax liability for the financial year is zero.

### Has Form 121 replaced Form 15G and 15H?
Yes, effective from April 1, 2026, for the Tax Year 2026-27 onwards, Form 121 has replaced both Form 15G and Form 15H under the new Income Tax Act, 2025.

### Who is eligible to submit Form 121?
Resident individuals (of any age) and Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs) whose estimated total income for the financial year is below the basic exemption limit and whose final tax liability will be nil are eligible.

### Do I need to submit Form 121 every year?
Yes, Form 121 is valid for only one financial year. You must submit a fresh form at the beginning of each financial year if you continue to meet the eligibility criteria.

### What happens if I don't submit Form 121 but am eligible?
If you are eligible but don't submit Form 121, the bank will deduct TDS if your interest income crosses the specified threshold. You would then need to claim this TDS back as a refund when filing your Income Tax Return.

### Can NRIs submit Form 121?
No, Form 121 is only for resident taxpayers. Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) are not eligible to submit this form.

### Is FD interest taxable even if TDS is not deducted using Form 121?
Yes, FD interest is always considered taxable income and must be declared in your Income Tax Return, even if no TDS was deducted due to the submission of Form 121.
