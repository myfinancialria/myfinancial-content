# SGB 2019-20 Series VI: Understanding Premature Redemption Gains

- Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) 2019-20 Series VI is currently open for premature redemption, offering significant returns to eligible investors.
- The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has set the premature redemption price at ₹14,213 per gram for this series.
- Investors who purchased at the original issue price of ₹3,840 per gram stand to realise capital gains of approximately 270% on their initial investment.
- These substantial gains are in addition to the fixed interest payments received semi-annually throughout the bond's holding period.
- Understanding the tax implications for these capital gains is crucial for investors considering premature redemption.

## What this means in plain terms

Today, investors who bought the Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB) 2019-20 Series VI have an opportunity to redeem their bonds earlier than the full 8-year maturity period. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has announced a fixed price of ₹14,213 per gram for this premature redemption. This is a considerable jump from the original purchase price of ₹3,840 per gram, meaning investors could see their initial investment grow by over 2.7 times, excluding the regular interest they’ve already received.

This option becomes available after five years from the date of issue, providing liquidity and the chance to book profits if the gold price has appreciated significantly, as it has in this case. While the full maturity period is eight years, this five-year window allows investors to exit and benefit from market movements. However, it's important to consider the tax implications on these gains before making a decision.

## Understanding Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs)

Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) are government securities denominated in grams of gold. They are substitutes for holding physical gold, offering a safe and convenient way to invest in the precious metal without the associated risks of storage or purity concerns. Introduced by the Government of India, these bonds are issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on behalf of the government.

Investors in SGBs receive a fixed interest rate of 2.50% per annum on their initial investment amount, paid semi-annually. The principal amount is linked to the market price of gold, meaning investors benefit from any appreciation in gold prices. These bonds have a maturity period of eight years, with an option for premature encashment after the fifth year from the date of issue. SGBs are also listed on stock exchanges, offering a potential avenue for liquidity, though trading volumes can be thin.

## The Mechanics of Premature Redemption

SGBs come with a maturity period of eight years. However, a key feature is the option for premature redemption, which can be exercised after the fifth year from the date of issue. This window allows investors to exit their investment earlier, providing flexibility and the opportunity to lock in gains if gold prices are favourable. The premature redemption option is typically available on the interest payment dates.

The price for premature redemption is determined by the RBI. It is calculated based on the simple average of the closing price of gold of 999 purity for the last three working days of the week preceding the date of redemption, as published by the India Bullion and Jewellers Association (IBJA) Limited. This mechanism ensures that the redemption price reflects the prevailing market rates of gold, offering a fair value to investors choosing to exit early. For the SGB 2019-20 Series VI, this fixed price is ₹14,213 per gram.

## Calculating the Gains and Taxation on SGBs

For investors in SGB 2019-20 Series VI, the original issue price was ₹3,840 per gram. With the premature redemption price fixed at ₹14,213 per gram, the capital appreciation is substantial. The absolute gain per gram is ₹14,213 - ₹3,840 = ₹10,373. This translates to a percentage gain of approximately 270.13% on the initial investment, excluding the 2.50% annual interest already received.

The tax implications for SGBs vary depending on when they are redeemed.
*   **Interest Income:** The 2.50% annual interest received on SGBs is taxable. It is added to your 'Income from Other Sources' and taxed as per your applicable income tax slab rate. Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) is not applicable on SGB interest, but investors are required to declare this income and pay taxes.
*   **Capital Gains on Premature Redemption:** If you redeem SGBs prematurely after five years but before the eight-year maturity, the capital gains are considered Long Term Capital Gains (LTCG). These gains are subject to a tax of 20% with indexation benefit. Indexation adjusts the purchase price for inflation, thereby reducing the taxable gain.
*   **Capital Gains on Maturity:** The most attractive tax benefit of SGBs is that capital gains arising on redemption at maturity (after 8 years) are fully exempt from income tax for individual investors. This makes SGBs a highly tax-efficient investment for long-term gold exposure.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 42, a software engineer with ₹35L CTC, based in Hyderabad**. Priya invested in the SGB 2019-20 Series VI when it was issued. She decided to purchase 20 grams of gold through this series.

*   **Original Issue Price:** ₹3,840 per gram
*   **Total Initial Investment:** 20 grams * ₹3,840/gram = ₹76,800

Now, five years later, Priya is considering premature redemption.

*   **Premature Redemption Price:** ₹14,213 per gram
*   **Total Redemption Value:** 20 grams * ₹14,213/gram = ₹284,260

**Calculating the Capital Gain:**
*   **Capital Gain:** ₹284,260 (Redemption Value) - ₹76,800 (Initial Investment) = ₹207,460

This represents a percentage gain of (₹207,460 / ₹76,800) * 100 = 270.13%.

**Tax Implications (Illustrative):**
Since Priya is opting for premature redemption after five years, the capital gain of ₹207,460 will be treated as Long Term Capital Gain (LTCG). She will be eligible for indexation benefit on her purchase price. After applying indexation, the taxable gain would likely be lower, and this reduced gain would be taxed at 20%. She would also have received semi-annual interest payments of 2.50% per annum on her initial investment of ₹76,800, which would have been taxed as per her income slab each year.

## FAQ

### What is the maturity period of Sovereign Gold Bonds?
Sovereign Gold Bonds have a maturity period of eight years. However, investors have an option for premature encashment or redemption after the fifth year from the date of issue, typically on interest payment dates.

### How is the premature redemption price of SGBs determined?
The premature redemption price is based on the simple average of the closing price of gold of 999 purity for the last three working days of the week preceding the date of redemption, as published by the IBJA.

### Are the capital gains from SGBs taxable?
Capital gains on SGBs redeemed prematurely (after 5 years but before 8 years) are taxed as Long Term Capital Gains at 20% with indexation benefit. However, capital gains on redemption at maturity (after 8 years) are exempt from tax for individual investors.

### Is the interest received on SGBs taxable?
Yes, the interest of 2.50% per annum paid semi-annually on SGBs is taxable. It is added to your 'Income from Other Sources' and taxed as per your applicable income tax slab.

### Can I sell my SGBs on the stock exchange?
Yes, SGBs are listed on stock exchanges and can be traded. However, liquidity can sometimes be an issue, meaning it might be difficult to find a buyer at your desired price, especially for smaller denominations.

### What is the advantage of holding SGBs until maturity?
The primary advantage of holding SGBs until their full 8-year maturity is that the capital gains realised at redemption are completely exempt from income tax for individual investors, making it a highly tax-efficient investment.
