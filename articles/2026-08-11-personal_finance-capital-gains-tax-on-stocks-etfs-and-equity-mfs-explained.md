# Capital Gains Tax on Stocks, ETFs, and Equity MFs Explained

**TL;DR**
- Capital gains from stocks, equity ETFs, and equity mutual funds are categorised as either short-term or long-term based on their holding period.
- For these assets, a holding period of more than 12 months qualifies for Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax.
- Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) on these assets are taxed at a flat rate of 15% under Section 111A of the Income Tax Act.
- Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) exceeding ₹1 lakh in a financial year are taxed at 10% without indexation under Section 112A.
- It is crucial to accurately track your purchase and sale dates to determine the correct tax liability.

## What this means in plain terms

When you sell an investment like a stock, an Exchange Traded Fund (ETF), or an equity mutual fund, the profit you make is called a capital gain. This gain is subject to tax in India, but how much tax you pay depends on how long you held the investment before selling it. This duration is known as the 'holding period'.

For stocks, equity ETFs, and equity mutual funds, the tax rules differentiate between 'short-term' and 'long-term' gains. If you sell these investments within 12 months of buying them, any profit is considered a Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG). If you hold them for more than 12 months, the profit is a Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG). Each type of gain has a different tax rate, which we will explore further.

## Understanding Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) on Equities

Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG) arise when you sell listed equity shares, equity-oriented mutual funds, or equity ETFs after holding them for more than 12 months. This holding period is critical. For instance, if you buy a stock on January 15, 2023, and sell it on January 16, 2024, it qualifies as a long-term investment.

Under Section 112A of the Income Tax Act, LTCG on the sale of these specified equity assets is taxed at a rate of 10%. However, there's a significant exemption: the first ₹1 lakh of LTCG in a financial year is entirely exempt from tax. This means you only pay 10% tax on the amount of LTCG that exceeds ₹1 lakh. For example, if your total LTCG from stocks and equity MFs in a year is ₹2.5 lakhs, you will pay tax on ₹1.5 lakhs (₹2.5 lakhs - ₹1 lakh exemption) at 10%, which amounts to ₹15,000. It's important to note that indexation benefits, which adjust the purchase cost for inflation, are not available for LTCG on these equity assets.

### Grandfathering Provision

A historical point worth noting is the 'grandfathering' provision for LTCG on equities. When LTCG tax was reintroduced on equity shares and equity-oriented mutual funds from April 1, 2018, gains accrued up to January 31, 2018, were exempt. This means that for assets purchased before February 1, 2018, the cost of acquisition for calculating LTCG is considered to be the higher of the actual purchase price and the fair market value (FMV) as of January 31, 2018 (subject to certain conditions). This provision helps reduce the taxable gain for older investments.

## Navigating Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) on Equities

Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) occur when you sell listed equity shares, equity-oriented mutual funds, or equity ETFs within 12 months of their purchase. The holding period here is 12 months or less. For example, if you buy shares on March 1, 2024, and sell them on November 15, 2024, any profit would be classified as STCG.

STCG on these equity assets, where Securities Transaction Tax (STT) has been paid, is taxed at a flat rate of 15% under Section 111A of the Income Tax Act. This 15% rate is applicable irrespective of your income tax slab. Unlike LTCG, there is no ₹1 lakh exemption for STCG. This makes short-term trading potentially more tax-intensive.

### Impact on Overall Taxable Income

While STCG is taxed at a flat rate, the amount of STCG is still added to your total income when determining your overall tax slab. However, the tax on this particular gain is calculated separately at 15%. This is a crucial distinction to understand for tax planning.

## Key Considerations: Set-off and Carry Forward of Losses

Capital losses can be set off against capital gains, which is an important aspect of tax planning.

### Setting Off Capital Losses

*   **Short-Term Capital Loss (STCL):** An STCL can be set off against both Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG) and Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG). This provides flexibility in managing your tax liability.
*   **Long-Term Capital Loss (LTCL):** An LTCL can only be set off against Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG). It cannot be adjusted against STCG.

### Carrying Forward Unadjusted Losses

If, after setting off losses in the current financial year, you still have unadjusted capital losses, these can be carried forward for up to 8 subsequent assessment years. When carried forward, an STCL can be set off against both STCG and LTCG in future years, while an LTCL can only be set off against LTCG in future years. To carry forward losses, it is mandatory to file your income tax return (ITR) by the due date.

## A real example

Let's consider **Priya, 35, a software engineer with ₹30L CTC, based in Hyderabad.**

Priya made the following equity investments and sales in the Financial Year 2025-26:

1.  **Investment 1 (Stock A):**
    *   Purchased 100 shares of Stock A on May 10, 2024, for ₹1,500 each. Total cost: ₹1,50,000.
    *   Sold 100 shares of Stock A on March 15, 2025, for ₹1,800 each. Total sale value: ₹1,80,000.
    *   Holding period: Less than 12 months.
    *   **Gain:** ₹1,80,000 - ₹1,50,000 = ₹30,000 (STCG)

2.  **Investment 2 (Equity Mutual Fund):**
    *   Purchased units of an equity mutual fund on July 20, 2023, for ₹2,50,000.
    *   Sold units of the same mutual fund on October 5, 2025, for ₹3,80,000.
    *   Holding period: More than 12 months.
    *   **Gain:** ₹3,80,000 - ₹2,50,000 = ₹1,30,000 (LTCG)

3.  **Investment 3 (Stock B):**
    *   Purchased 50 shares of Stock B on June 1, 2025, for ₹2,000 each. Total cost: ₹1,00,000.
    *   Sold 50 shares of Stock B on August 10, 2025, for ₹1,900 each. Total sale value: ₹95,000.
    *   Holding period: Less than 12 months.
    *   **Loss:** ₹95,000 - ₹1,00,000 = -₹5,000 (STCL)

**Calculating Priya's Capital Gains Tax for FY 2025-26:**

*   **Total STCG:** ₹30,000 (from Stock A)
*   **Total LTCG:** ₹1,30,000 (from Equity Mutual Fund)
*   **Total STCL:** ₹5,000 (from Stock B)

**Step 1: Set off STCL.**
Priya has an STCL of ₹5,000. This can be set off against STCG first.
Adjusted STCG = ₹30,000 (STCG) - ₹5,000 (STCL) = ₹25,000.
Remaining STCL = ₹0.

**Step 2: Calculate tax on adjusted STCG.**
Taxable STCG = ₹25,000.
STCG Tax = 15% of ₹25,000 = ₹3,750.

**Step 3: Calculate tax on LTCG.**
Total LTCG = ₹1,30,000.
Exemption for LTCG = ₹1,00,000.
Taxable LTCG = ₹1,30,000 - ₹1,00,000 = ₹30,000.
LTCG Tax = 10% of ₹30,000 = ₹3,000.

**Priya's total capital gains tax liability for FY 2025-26:**
Total Tax = STCG Tax + LTCG Tax = ₹3,750 + ₹3,000 = ₹6,750.

This amount will be added to her overall tax liability, along with any applicable cess and surcharges.

## What to do this week

1.  **Consolidate Investment Statements:** Download and organise all your capital gains statements from your brokerages and mutual fund registrars (like CAMS, KFintech) for the current financial year. This will give you a clear overview of your transactions.
2.  **Identify Holding Periods:** For any recent sales, quickly check the purchase and sale dates to determine if they classify as short-term (less than 12 months) or long-term (more than 12 months) gains or losses.
3.  **Estimate Capital Gains/Losses:** Based on your statements, make a rough estimate of your total STCG, LTCG, STCL, and LTCL for the financial year. This will help you anticipate your tax liability.
4.  **Review Tax Harvest Opportunities (if applicable):** If you have unrealised long-term losses, consider if selling them now makes sense to offset existing long-term gains, keeping the ₹1 lakh LTCG exemption in mind. This is a more advanced step, so proceed with caution.
5.  **Consult a Professional:** If your portfolio is complex or you're unsure about calculations, schedule a brief call or meeting with a Certified Financial Planner or a tax advisor to clarify your specific situation.

## FAQ

### What is the holding period for equity shares and equity mutual funds to qualify as long-term?
For listed equity shares, equity-oriented mutual funds, and equity ETFs, the holding period must be more than 12 months to qualify as a long-term asset.

### Is indexation benefit available for Long-Term Capital Gains on equities?
No, the benefit of indexation is not available for Long-Term Capital Gains arising from the sale of listed equity shares, equity-oriented mutual funds, or equity ETFs.

### Can I adjust capital losses against other income sources?
No, capital losses can only be set off against capital gains. They cannot be set off against other income sources like salary, house property income, or business income.

### What happens if I have Long-Term Capital Losses?
Long-Term Capital Losses (LTCL) can only be set off against Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG). If not fully adjusted in the current year, they can be carried forward for up to 8 subsequent assessment years, provided you file your ITR on time.

### Are dividends from equity mutual funds or stocks taxable?
Yes, dividends received from Indian companies or equity mutual funds are fully taxable in the hands of the recipient at their applicable income tax slab rates.

### Do I need to pay Securities Transaction Tax (STT) on these transactions?
Yes, Securities Transaction Tax (STT) is levied on the purchase and sale of equity shares, equity-oriented mutual funds, and equity ETFs on recognised stock exchanges in India. This is a prerequisite for availing the concessional tax rates under Section 111A and 112A.
