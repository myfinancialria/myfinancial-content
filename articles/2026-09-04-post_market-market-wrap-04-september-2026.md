# Market Wrap — 04 September 2026

**TL;DR**
- Indian equities witnessed a mixed session, with the Nifty 50 closing marginally higher, while the Bank Nifty remained largely flat.
- Global cues, particularly US jobs data fueling rate-hike bets, created a cautious sentiment, though domestic buying provided support.
- The New India Assurance Company Ltd. emerged as the top gainer in the Nifty 500, surging over 17%.
- KEI Industries Ltd. was the biggest loser among Nifty 500 stocks, declining nearly 9%.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers for the day, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) continued their strong buying support.
- F&O positioning data for fresh build-up is currently unavailable and will be included in subsequent wraps.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equity benchmarks closed with a mixed performance today, largely trading in a narrow range. The Nifty 50 managed a marginal gain, closing at 23897.70, up by 0.10%. The Bank Nifty, on the other hand, ended almost flat at 57369.65, with a minor dip of 0.02%. The broader market saw the Nifty Midcap 100 decline by 0.25%, while the Nifty Smallcap 100 showed resilience, rising by 0.22%.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market today navigated a landscape shaped by a combination of global cues and domestic flows. The primary external factor was the subdued sentiment in US stocks, which opened largely flat after stronger-than-expected jobs data boosted expectations that the Federal Reserve could raise interest rates this month. This global caution likely kept Indian benchmarks from making significant upward moves.

Domestically, the market witnessed a clear divergence in institutional investor activity. Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were net sellers, offloading equities worth ₹2,346 crore. However, this selling pressure was largely absorbed by robust buying from Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs), who pumped in ₹4,977 crore, providing crucial support to the market.

Sector-wise, the Metal sector showed strong momentum, leading the gains, while the Realty, Pharma, and IT sectors faced selling pressure. Heavy-weight stocks also played a role, with some metal and banking stocks contributing to a relief rally as concerns over an imminent US Fed rate hike eased slightly during the day. Notably, the Capital Goods sector saw significant declines, with stocks like KEI Industries, R R Kabel, Polycab India, and Finolex Cables featuring prominently among the top losers.

A significant piece of domestic news that garnered attention was the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) approving the initial public offerings (IPOs) of Cosmic PV Power, Monomark Engineering, RKB Global, and most notably, the National Stock Exchange (NSE). This development, particularly the anticipated ₹30,000 crore NSE IPO, signals a potential revival in India’s primary market and could attract significant investor interest in the coming weeks.

## News that moved stocks
- **SEBI approves NSE IPO along with other major public offers:** The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has given its nod to the initial public offerings (IPOs) of Cosmic PV Power, Monomark Engineering, RKB Global, and the highly anticipated public offer from the National Stock Exchange (NSE). This significant development is expected to bring a substantial ₹30,000 crore issue from NSE to the market, marking a pivotal moment for India's primary market.
- **US jobs report fuels rate-hike bets, keeping global markets subdued:** US stocks opened largely flat today after stronger-than-expected jobs data boosted expectations that the Federal Reserve could raise interest rates this month. This global sentiment contributed to the cautious trading observed in Indian equities.
- **US diesel prices hit record high due to Iran conflict:** Diesel prices in the United States soared to unprecedented levels, primarily attributed to the ongoing six-month conflict involving Iran. This rise in costs is expected to push up transportation fees for a wide range of goods, potentially impacting logistics-heavy sectors globally.
- **Swiggy sees 4% equity change hands in block deals:** Shares of the food delivery giant Swiggy rose by 2.53% following block deals that involved a 4% change in its equity. This movement comes ahead of its anticipated removal from the MSCI index on September 7 due to foreign ownership caps.
- **SPML Infra's BESS battery pack clears key global certification tests:** SPML Infra announced that its proprietary 104.4 kWh BESS battery pack has successfully acquired important international safety certifications. This achievement marks a crucial step towards the commercial rollout of their energy storage system, potentially benefiting the company.
- **SEBI and European Markets Authority partner for cooperation on central counterparties:** SEBI and the European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) have signed a memorandum of understanding to enhance cooperation and information exchange regarding central counterparties. This agreement replaces a previous one and aims to strengthen regulatory collaboration.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's session was not dominated by a single defining event but rather by a confluence of factors. The primary driver of caution was the global sentiment stemming from the US jobs report, which strengthened expectations of a potential US Federal Reserve rate hike. This news kept global markets, including India, on edge.

Domestically, the market saw a tug-of-war between FII selling and robust DII buying. While FIIs booked profits, DIIs stepped in with significant capital, providing a floor to the market and preventing a major downturn. Sectorally, the Metal sector found favour, possibly due to a slight easing of global rate hike fears during the day, as noted in some market reports. Conversely, the Capital Goods sector experienced notable selling pressure, with several key stocks in this segment featuring among the top losers.

A notable domestic development was SEBI's approval of the National Stock Exchange's (NSE) long-awaited IPO, alongside other public offers. This news, while not immediately impacting market indices, sets the stage for a significant capital market event in the near future, potentially attracting substantial investor interest.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead to tomorrow's session, global cues will continue to play a crucial role. US stocks were subdued today following the jobs report, and while Brent Crude prices saw a decline, the Dollar Index and US 10-year yields edged higher, suggesting continued caution regarding interest rates. Investors will be watching for further clarity on the global economic outlook and any fresh signals from central banks. Domestically, the sustained DII buying could continue to provide support, but FII flows will be keenly monitored. Traders should watch the Nifty 50's range, with 24000 acting as a psychological resistance and 23800 as immediate support. Sectors like Metal, which showed strength today, and the underperforming Capital Goods sector will be in focus.
