# Pre-Market Brief — 01 September 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Global markets ended in the red overnight, with US indices down and Asian markets showing a mixed-to-negative start.
*   GIFT Nifty indicates a slightly negative opening bias for the Indian benchmark Nifty 50.
*   Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) were significant net sellers yesterday, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) provided strong support.
*   Rising US Treasury yields and high crude oil prices are key global headwinds, pushing India's 10-year bond yield higher.
*   Nifty's immediate resistance is seen around 24100-24150, with strong support at 24000.
*   The primary risks for today include sustained FII outflows, elevated crude oil prices, and rising domestic bond yields.

## How global markets traded — and what it means for India
Global markets closed mostly lower overnight, reflecting a cautious sentiment. US indices, the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500, all ended in negative territory, with declines ranging from 0.58% to 0.72%. European markets also saw a negative close, with the FTSE 100 down 0.18%. In Asia this morning, the Nikkei 225 was down 0.15%, the Hang Seng fell 0.90%, and the Shanghai Composite dipped 0.16%, indicating a broadly subdued start to the trading day across the region.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) strengthened against the US Dollar, closing at ₹94.88, a decline of 0.52%. Conversely, the Dollar Index saw a slight uptick of 0.17% to 99.60. Crude oil prices continued their upward trajectory, with Brent Crude rising 1.59% to $91.93 and WTI Crude gaining 1.68% to $87.20. Gold prices also moved higher, up 0.79% to $4466.30, while Silver increased by 0.83% to $66.77. A significant development was the US 10-year Treasury Yield, which surged 1.84% to 4.76%.

**Interpretation:**
*   **USD/INR:** A strengthening Rupee is generally positive for the Indian economy, potentially easing import costs and reducing inflationary pressures. It could also provide some tailwind for sectors like IT and pharmaceuticals, though the overall impact needs to be seen in context of global flows.
*   **Dollar Index:** A rising Dollar Index indicates broad strength in the US dollar, which can sometimes put pressure on emerging market currencies, including the Rupee, despite its recent appreciation.
*   **Brent/WTI Crude:** The continued rise in crude oil prices is a significant concern for India, a major oil importer. This could lead to higher inflation, increased current account deficit pressure, and potential headwinds for sectors like oil marketing companies (OMCs), paints, and aviation.
*   **Gold/Silver:** Rising precious metal prices often reflect increased safe-haven demand amidst global uncertainties or inflation concerns.
*   **US 10Y Yield:** The sharp rise in US Treasury yields is a major global development. Higher US yields make dollar-denominated assets more attractive, potentially leading to FII outflows from emerging markets like India. It also puts upward pressure on Indian bond yields, increasing borrowing costs for the government and corporations.

## Stock & sector news that matters

*   **US Treasury selloff pushes India 10-year bond yield towards 7%**
    Indian government bonds saw a sharp decline, leading to higher yields, mirroring a significant rise in U.S. Treasury yields due to escalating geopolitical risks and inflation concerns from high oil prices.
    **Why it matters:** Rising bond yields increase borrowing costs for companies and the government, potentially impacting corporate profitability and economic growth. Higher yields also make debt instruments more attractive, which could divert funds from equities, especially from FIIs. This is a significant macroeconomic headwind for the broader market.

*   **Ather Energy’s 130% stock surge leaves Tesla and BYD behind in 2026**
    Shares of Ather Energy have surged nearly 130% in 2026, outperforming global EV stocks, driven by expectations of rising market share and new product launches. The company is backed by Hero MotoCorp.
    **Why it matters:** This news highlights strong investor confidence in the EV sector, particularly in domestic players like Ather Energy. It could bring Hero MotoCorp into focus due to its backing of Ather, and potentially create positive sentiment for other EV-related stocks or auto ancillary companies with EV exposure.

*   **Ace investor Ashish Dhawan picks up 35 lakh shares in Religare Enterprises; stock jumps 5%**
    Veteran investor Ashish Dhawan acquired approximately 35 lakh shares of Religare Enterprises over two sessions, as per NSE bulk deal data, leading to a 5% jump in the stock.
    **Why it matters:** Significant buying by a prominent investor like Ashish Dhawan often signals confidence in the company's future prospects and can attract retail and institutional interest, potentially leading to further upward movement in Religare Enterprises' stock.

*   **Titan Company, Sky Gold, other shares fall up to 6% on PM Modi’s second appeal to avoid gold**
    Jewellery stocks, including Titan Company and Sky Gold, fell by up to 6% after Prime Minister Narendra Modi urged citizens to avoid non-essential gold purchases to conserve foreign exchange.
    **Why it matters:** This direct appeal from the Prime Minister can significantly impact consumer sentiment towards gold purchases, leading to reduced demand for jewellery. This could put continued pressure on jewellery retailers and manufacturers like Titan Company and Sky Gold.

*   **Manufacturing PMI slips to five-year low: Is more pain in store?**
    India's Manufacturing PMI has declined to a five-year low, suggesting a slowdown in manufacturing momentum. This, coupled with margin risks and potential capital expenditure cuts, indicates a challenging second half for the economy despite strong Q1 growth.
    **Why it matters:** A weakening Manufacturing PMI is a crucial economic indicator. A sustained decline suggests a slowdown in industrial activity, which could impact corporate earnings across various manufacturing-dependent sectors and potentially lead to a more cautious outlook for the broader economy.

*   **OMCs set for earnings recovery in Q2 despite oil market uncertainty**
    Oil marketing companies (OMCs) are expected to see a swift earnings recovery in Q2, primarily due to lower crude oil prices in the previous quarter and easing freight and insurance costs, which should help compensate for Q1 losses.
    **Why it matters:** This news offers a positive outlook for OMCs (like IOC, BPCL, HPCL) for their Q2 results, despite the current rise in crude prices. Investors might react positively to these companies, anticipating improved profitability in the near term, though the current crude price surge remains a long-term watch point.

## Events & data to watch — and how markets usually react
*   **Rising Global and Domestic Bond Yields:** The surge in US 10-year Treasury yields and the corresponding upward pressure on India's 10-year bond yield (towards 7%) is a critical factor. Markets typically react to rising yields with caution, as it increases borrowing costs for companies and makes equities less attractive compared to fixed-income investments. This can lead to FII outflows and dampen overall market sentiment.
*   **Crude Oil Prices:** The continued upward trend in Brent and WTI crude oil prices is a significant inflationary concern for India. Historically, higher crude prices have led to increased volatility in Indian markets, putting pressure on sectors like OMCs, airlines, and paint companies, and contributing to broader inflation.
*   **FII Flow Trends:** The consistent FII selling over recent sessions, despite DII support, will be closely watched. Sustained FII outflows can put significant downward pressure on benchmark indices and specific stocks.
*   **Manufacturing PMI Data:** The recent slip in India's Manufacturing PMI to a five-year low is a key economic data point. Markets generally react negatively to weakening economic indicators, as it signals potential slowdowns in corporate earnings and overall economic growth.
*   **Global Central Bank Commentary:** Comments from central bank officials, such as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's remarks on a potential global investment boom, can influence market sentiment by providing insights into future monetary policy direction and economic outlook.

## Bottom line
The Indian market is likely to open with a cautious to slightly negative bias today, influenced by weak global cues and a negative signal from GIFT Nifty. The continued FII selling, coupled with rising global and domestic bond yields and elevated crude oil prices, presents significant headwinds. While DIIs have been providing crucial support, the ability of benchmarks to hold key psychological and technical support levels, particularly Nifty's 24000 mark, will be critical in determining the day's trajectory. Investors should remain vigilant about global macroeconomic developments and FII flow trends.
