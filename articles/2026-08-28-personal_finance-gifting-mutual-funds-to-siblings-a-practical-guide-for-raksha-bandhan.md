# Gifting Mutual Funds to Siblings: A Practical Guide for Raksha Bandhan

**TL;DR**
- Gifting mutual fund units to siblings is a thoughtful way to promote financial well-being.
- The process involves submitting a request to the AMC/RTA, often with a notarised gift deed.
- Both the giver and receiver must be KYC-compliant for the transfer to be successful.
- Gifts to siblings are exempt from income tax for the recipient under Section 56(2)(x) of the Income Tax Act.
- Capital gains tax will apply to the recipient upon redemption, calculated from the original purchase date of the units.

## What this means in plain terms

Instead of traditional gifts, you can now transfer units of mutual funds you own to your sibling. This means you're giving them a share in an investment, which can grow over time and help them build wealth. It's like giving them a small piece of a financial portfolio you've already started.

The process involves informing the mutual fund company or their registrar that you want to transfer specific units. Both you and your sibling need to have completed your Know Your Customer (KYC) formalities, which is a standard verification process. Crucially, while the act of gifting itself to a sibling isn't taxed, any profits your sibling makes when they eventually sell those units will be subject to capital gains tax.

## The Process of Gifting Mutual Fund Units

Gifting mutual fund units is a structured process, ensuring compliance and clear ownership. It's not as simple as handing over a physical item, but it is straightforward once you understand the steps.

### Initiating the Transfer

To begin, you, as the donor, need to approach the respective Asset Management Company (AMC) or their Registrar and Transfer Agent (RTA) – such as CAMS or KFintech – where the mutual fund units are held. You will typically need to fill out a specific 'Off-market Transfer' or 'Gift Deed' form. This form will require details of both the donor (you) and the donee (your sibling), including their PAN and folio numbers.

### Essential Documentation

A key document for gifting mutual funds is a Gift Deed. While not always strictly mandated by AMCs for transfers between specified relatives, it is highly recommended. A notarised Gift Deed clearly establishes the intent of the gift and the relationship between the donor and donee, which is crucial for tax purposes. It should specify the mutual fund scheme name, folio number, ISIN of the units, and the number of units being gifted. Both parties' signatures and witness signatures are usually required. Additionally, copies of PAN cards and address proofs for both donor and donee will be needed.

### Folio Number and Ownership

The gifted units will be transferred from your existing folio to your sibling's existing folio. If your sibling does not have an existing folio with that particular AMC, a new one will be created for them. It's important to note that the gifted units will be held solely in your sibling's name; joint holdings are not permitted for gifted units.

## Key Conditions and Eligibility for Gifting

Before you proceed with gifting mutual fund units, certain conditions must be met by both the giver and the receiver to ensure a smooth and compliant transaction.

### KYC Compliance is Mandatory

Both the donor and the donee must be fully Know Your Customer (KYC) compliant. This is a fundamental requirement for any mutual fund transaction in India, as per SEBI regulations. If either party is not KYC-compliant, the process will halt until this formality is completed. This involves submitting identity proof (like PAN card) and address proof, along with in-person verification (IPV) if required.

### No Joint Holdings for Gifted Units

When you gift mutual fund units, they will be transferred to a folio solely in your sibling's name. You cannot gift units into a joint folio where you are also a holder. This ensures clear ownership and avoids future complications regarding capital gains and redemption. The recipient becomes the sole owner of the gifted units.

### Existing SIPs are Not Transferred

If you have an ongoing Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) in the mutual fund scheme from which you are gifting units, the SIP itself is not transferred. The gift only pertains to the accumulated units held in your folio at the time of transfer. If your sibling wishes to continue investing in the same scheme via SIP, they would need to set up a fresh SIP from their own bank account.

### Lock-in Periods and Charges

If the mutual fund units being gifted are subject to a lock-in period (e.g., ELSS funds), these units cannot be transferred until the lock-in period is over. There are generally no direct charges levied by the AMC or RTA for the off-market transfer of mutual fund units between relatives. However, if you use a professional service for drafting the gift deed or notarisation, those charges would apply.

## Tax Implications of Gifting Mutual Funds

Understanding the tax implications is crucial for both the giver and the receiver of mutual fund gifts. The good news is that gifts to siblings fall into a specific exempt category under Indian tax laws.

### Gift Tax Exemption for Siblings

Under Section 56(2)(x) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, any sum of money or property received without consideration is taxable in the hands of the recipient. However, there are specific exemptions. Gifts received from "relatives" are exempt from tax. The definition of "relative" includes siblings (brother or sister of the individual) and their spouses. Therefore, when you gift mutual fund units to your sibling, your sibling will not have to pay any income tax on the value of the gift itself.

### Capital Gains Tax on Redemption

While the gift itself is tax-free for the recipient, any capital gains arising from the sale or redemption of these gifted units will be taxable in the hands of the recipient. The cost of acquisition for the recipient will be considered as the original cost at which the donor purchased the units. The holding period for calculating capital gains will also start from the original date of purchase by the donor.

*   **Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG):** If the recipient redeems equity-oriented mutual fund units within one year of the original purchase date by the donor, the gains will be taxed as STCG at 15% (plus cess). For debt-oriented mutual funds, if redeemed within three years, gains are added to the recipient's income and taxed at their applicable slab rates.
*   **Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG):** For equity-oriented mutual funds, if redeemed after one year, gains exceeding ₹1 lakh in a financial year are taxed at 10% without indexation. For debt-oriented mutual funds, if redeemed after three years, gains are taxed at 20% with indexation benefit.

It is important for the recipient to keep records of the original purchase details (date, cost) provided by the donor, as these will be needed for calculating capital gains at the time of redemption.

## A real example

Priya, 32, a software engineer in Bengaluru earning ₹22 lakhs per year, wants to gift mutual fund units to her younger brother, Rohan, 28, who is just starting his career and earns ₹8 lakhs per year. For Raksha Bandhan, Priya decides to gift Rohan 500 units of an equity mutual fund (Scheme X) she purchased three years ago.

**Priya's initial purchase details:**
*   Date of purchase: August 15, 2023
*   Number of units: 500
*   Purchase NAV: ₹100 per unit
*   Total investment: ₹50,000

**Current scenario (August 2026):**
*   Current NAV of Scheme X: ₹180 per unit
*   Value of 500 units: ₹90,000

**The Gifting Process:**
1.  Priya ensures both she and Rohan are KYC-compliant.
2.  She drafts a Gift Deed, specifying the 500 units of Scheme X, her folio number, and Rohan's PAN and folio details. She gets it notarised.
3.  Priya submits the Gift Deed along with the off-market transfer form to the RTA (e.g., CAMS).
4.  The RTA processes the request, transferring 500 units from Priya's folio to Rohan's folio.

**Tax Implications:**
*   **For Rohan (Recipient):** The gift of ₹90,000 worth of mutual fund units from his sister is exempt from income tax under Section 56(2)(x) as she is a relative.
*   **Upon Redemption (e.g., Rohan redeems in August 2027):**
    *   Original purchase date (for capital gains calculation): August 15, 2023 (Priya's purchase date).
    *   Holding period: 4 years (August 2023 to August 2027), making it a Long-Term Capital Gain for equity funds.
    *   Cost of acquisition: ₹50,000 (Priya's original cost).
    *   Suppose Rohan redeems the 500 units when the NAV is ₹200 per unit.
    *   Redemption value: 500 units * ₹200 = ₹1,00,000.
    *   Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG): ₹1,00,000 (Redemption Value) - ₹50,000 (Cost of Acquisition) = ₹50,000.
    *   Since LTCG from equity funds up to ₹1 lakh in a financial year is exempt, Rohan will not pay any tax on this ₹50,000 gain. If his total LTCG from equity in that year exceeded ₹1 lakh, the excess would be taxed at 10%.

This example illustrates how Rohan receives a valuable asset without immediate tax liability, and any future gains are taxed based on the original investment details.

## What to do this week

2.  **Identify Units to Gift:** Decide which specific mutual fund scheme and how many units you wish to gift. Ensure they are not under any lock-in period.

## FAQ

### Is a gift deed mandatory for gifting mutual funds to a sibling?
While some AMCs might not strictly demand it for transfers between specified relatives, a notarised Gift Deed is highly recommended. It serves as crucial evidence of the gift and relationship, especially for tax purposes.

### Are there any charges for gifting mutual fund units?
Generally, AMCs or RTAs do not levy any direct charges for the off-market transfer of mutual fund units between relatives. However, you might incur costs for drafting and notarising the Gift Deed.

### What if my sibling is an NRI?
Gifting to an NRI sibling is possible, but it involves additional compliance with FEMA regulations. The NRI sibling must have a valid NRO or NRE account linked to their mutual fund folio, and the transfer usually involves specific declarations.

### Can I gift an ongoing SIP to my sibling?
No, an ongoing SIP cannot be transferred. The gift pertains only to the accumulated units held in your folio. If your sibling wishes to continue investing, they must start a new SIP from their own bank account.

### How is capital gains tax calculated for the recipient of the gift?
The recipient's cost of acquisition is considered as the original cost at which the donor purchased the units. The holding period for calculating capital gains also starts from the donor's original purchase date.

### Are there any limits on the value of mutual fund units I can gift to my sibling?
No, there are no monetary limits on the value of mutual fund units you can gift to your sibling. Gifts to specified relatives are fully exempt from income tax for the recipient, regardless of the amount.
