# India Inc’s Earnings Rebound Faces Commodity Cost Headwinds

**TL;DR**
- Indian companies reported their strongest profit growth in ten quarters during Q2 FY27, with net profit rising 27% year-on-year.
- Net sales also saw robust growth of 22% year-on-year, marking the highest topline expansion in at least three years.
- This rebound was significantly driven by a strong performance from oil marketing companies and select other sectors.
- However, surging commodity prices, including oil, freight, and metals, are expected to put considerable pressure on corporate margins from the December quarter onwards.
- Companies with limited pricing power may struggle to pass on these increased input costs, leading to an uneven earnings landscape in the near future.

## What this means in plain terms

After a period of moderate performance, India Inc. has shown a strong rebound in its financial results for the second quarter of the current fiscal year (Q2 FY27, July-September 2026). Companies collectively saw a significant jump in their profits and sales, indicating a healthier economic environment and improved demand. This growth has been particularly notable in sectors like oil marketing, which experienced a sharp turnaround, contributing substantially to the overall positive figures.

However, this positive momentum is now facing a new challenge: the rising cost of essential raw materials and transportation. Commodities such as crude oil, metals, and freight charges have been increasing, directly impacting the operational expenses of businesses across various sectors. While some companies have managed to increase their product prices to offset these rising costs, many others might not have the same flexibility, potentially leading to a squeeze on their profit margins in the coming months. This means that even with strong sales, the actual profitability could be under threat.

## Q2 FY27 Performance: A Strong Rebound

India Inc. delivered impressive financial results for Q2 FY27, showcasing a significant turnaround in corporate profitability. An analysis of 356 companies' standalone results revealed that net profit surged by 27% year-on-year in the September quarter. This marks the fastest profit growth recorded in over two years, a substantial improvement compared to the 9% increase seen in the same quarter last year. Alongside robust profit growth, net sales also demonstrated considerable strength, rising 22% year-on-year. This represented the strongest topline growth for the cohort in at least three years, indicating healthy demand and business activity across the economy.

The strong performance was not uniform across all sectors. A significant portion of this earnings rebound was attributed to a sharp turnaround in oil marketing companies, which had faced headwinds in previous quarters. Additionally, strong gains in a few select sectors contributed to the overall positive picture. This concentrated growth, however, also highlights potential vulnerabilities as the broader corporate landscape navigates inflationary pressures.

### Margin Pressures Looming Ahead

Despite the strong Q2 performance, the outlook for corporate margins in the immediate future appears challenging. Elevated input costs, driven by surging prices of crude oil, freight, and various metals, are threatening to squeeze profitability from the December quarter (Q3 FY27) onwards. Rating agency ICRA projects that India Inc.'s aggregate operating profit margin (OPM) could contract by 100-150 basis points year-on-year in Q2 FY27, specifically citing elevated raw material, fuel, freight, and packaging costs as key contributors to this pressure.

The ability of companies to absorb or pass on these increased costs varies significantly. While some businesses have already implemented price hikes to protect their margins, their capacity for further increases is limited. This uneven pricing power suggests that the impact of rising commodity costs will be felt differently across industries, potentially leading to a more disparate earnings performance in the coming quarters.

## Sectoral Impact and Divergent Trends

The impact of rising commodity costs and evolving demand dynamics is creating divergent trends across various sectors of India Inc. While some industries are better positioned to handle these pressures, others are likely to face significant headwinds.

### Resilient and Vulnerable Sectors

Sectors heavily reliant on domestic consumption, such as automobiles, retail, consumer durables, and hospitality, are generally expected to outperform export-oriented sectors. This resilience is attributed to relatively stable domestic demand. For instance, the automotive sector, particularly passenger vehicles, is likely to see revenue growth supported by higher volumes and a favourable product mix, even as elevated raw material costs continue to weigh on margins.

Conversely, export-oriented sectors like information technology, apparel/home textiles, and cut and polished diamonds are likely to face challenges due to continued weakness in global demand. The Fast-Moving Consumer Goods (FMCG) sector is also under considerable pressure. Rising input costs, particularly for crude oil and palm oil, are squeezing margins, and companies are struggling to fully pass on these increases to consumers. Elevated food inflation could further impact household purchasing power, affecting demand for packaged foods and personal care products.

### Pricing Power and Cost Management

The ability to maintain or expand margins in the face of rising input costs largely depends on a company's pricing power. Commodity producers, for instance, have benefited from stronger realisations, while many downstream and consumer-facing companies have had to absorb a portion of the cost increases to protect demand and sustain volume growth. Some companies are implementing cost efficiencies and leveraging operating scale to offset gross margin pressures. For example, Tata Consumer Products is expected to see gross margin contraction but EBITDA margin expansion due to operating leverage and cost efficiencies. However, for many, especially those in competitive markets, sustained commodity price inflation will inevitably lead to margin erosion.

## Management Commentary and Future Outlook

Management commentary from various companies indicates a cautious but determined approach to navigating the current economic landscape. While the Q2 FY27 results show strong growth, the focus is shifting towards managing input costs and maintaining profitability in the face of persistent inflationary pressures.

Companies are actively exploring strategies such as calibrated price hikes, operational efficiencies, and a favourable product mix to mitigate the impact of rising costs. For instance, Bajaj Auto expects rupee depreciation to support exports and a favourable product mix along with price hikes to improve average selling prices, even as higher raw material costs impact EBITDA margins. Similarly, Maruti Suzuki anticipates growth aided by a favourable product mix and stronger export volumes, despite elevated raw material costs and supply chain disruptions.

The outlook for the coming quarters remains nuanced. While domestic demand is expected to remain supportive, especially for consumption-linked sectors, global demand weakness continues to be a concern for export-oriented businesses. Furthermore, the monsoon outlook and its potential impact on rural consumption and food inflation are closely monitored. Overall, companies are bracing for a period where cost management and strategic pricing will be crucial determinants of sustained profitability.

## A real example

Let's consider **Ananya, 42, a self-employed furniture manufacturer in Pune, with an annual revenue of ₹60 lakhs.** Her business relies heavily on raw materials like wood, metal, and certain chemicals for finishes, along with significant freight costs for transporting finished goods.

In Q2 FY27, Ananya experienced healthy demand, leading to a 20% year-on-year increase in her revenue. Her raw material costs, however, typically account for 50% of her revenue, and freight costs are another 10%.
*   **Previous Quarter (Illustrative):**
    *   Revenue: ₹15 lakhs (₹60 lakhs / 4 quarters)
    *   Raw Material Cost: ₹7.5 lakhs (50% of revenue)
    *   Freight Cost: ₹1.5 lakhs (10% of revenue)
    *   Other Operating Costs (Fixed): ₹3 lakhs
    *   Profit: ₹15 lakhs - ₹7.5 lakhs - ₹1.5 lakhs - ₹3 lakhs = ₹3 lakhs

Now, with commodity costs rising by an estimated 5-7% and freight costs by 10% from Q3 FY27, let's see the impact on her profitability, assuming she can only pass on a limited 2% price increase to her customers due to competitive pressures.

*   **Q3 FY27 (Projected):**
    *   Original Revenue: ₹15 lakhs
    *   New Revenue with 2% price hike: ₹15 lakhs * 1.02 = ₹15.3 lakhs
    *   Original Raw Material Cost: ₹7.5 lakhs
    *   New Raw Material Cost (7% increase): ₹7.5 lakhs * 1.07 = ₹8.025 lakhs
    *   Original Freight Cost: ₹1.5 lakhs
    *   New Freight Cost (10% increase): ₹1.5 lakhs * 1.10 = ₹1.65 lakhs
    *   Other Operating Costs (Fixed): ₹3 lakhs
    *   Projected Profit: ₹15.3 lakhs - ₹8.025 lakhs - ₹1.65 lakhs - ₹3 lakhs = ₹2.625 lakhs

In this scenario, despite increasing her prices, Ananya's profit drops from ₹3 lakhs to ₹2.625 lakhs, representing a decline of approximately 12.5%. This illustrates how rising input costs, even with some pricing power, can significantly erode profitability for businesses across India Inc.

## FAQ

### What are "commodity costs" in this context?
Commodity costs refer to the prices of basic raw materials and inputs that companies use in their production processes, such as crude oil, natural gas, various metals (steel, aluminium), chemicals, agricultural produce, and even freight charges for transportation.

### How do rising commodity costs affect corporate earnings?
When commodity costs rise, a company's cost of goods sold (COGS) increases. If the company cannot fully pass on these higher costs to consumers through price hikes, its gross profit margins and, subsequently, its net profits will shrink.

### Which sectors are most affected by rising commodity prices?
Sectors that are highly dependent on raw material inputs, such as manufacturing (automobiles, cement, consumer durables), chemicals, and those with limited pricing power, are generally most impacted. Oil marketing companies are also sensitive to crude oil price fluctuations.

### What is "pricing power" and why is it important?
Pricing power is a company's ability to raise its product or service prices without significantly losing customer demand or market share. Companies with strong brands, unique products, or essential offerings tend to have greater pricing power, allowing them to better offset rising input costs.

### Will this impact individual investors?
Yes, indirectly. If corporate earnings are squeezed, it can affect stock valuations and dividend payouts. Investors holding shares in companies with weak pricing power or high commodity exposure might see their investments underperform. It also impacts the prices of consumer goods.

### What is Q2 FY27?
Q2 FY27 refers to the second quarter of the financial year 2026-27. In India, the financial year runs from April 1 to March 31, so Q2 covers the period from July 1 to September 30, 2026.
