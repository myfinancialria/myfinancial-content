# Gifting Money to NRI Child: TCS on LRS & Refund Explained

**TL;DR**
- Gifting money to an NRI child from a resident Indian parent is tax-free for the recipient in India.
- Remittances under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) for gifts are subject to Tax Collected at Source (TCS).
- A 20% TCS applies on gift remittances exceeding ₹10 lakhs in a financial year, on the amount above this threshold.
- Transfers to an NRI's NRO account in India typically do not attract TCS, but transferring funds from an NRO account overseas is subject to LRS rules.
- The remitter (parent) can claim the TCS amount as a credit against their income tax liability or seek a refund when filing their Income Tax Return.

## What this means in plain terms

If you're a resident Indian looking to send money to your child living abroad, it's a generous gesture that typically doesn't attract gift tax for your child in India. This is because gifts received from specified relatives, like parents, are exempt from tax under Indian income tax laws.

However, the act of sending money outside India falls under the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS). This scheme allows resident individuals to remit up to USD 250,000 (approximately ₹2 crores) per financial year for various permissible purposes, including gifts. While the gift itself is tax-free for your child, the remittance process might involve a Tax Collected at Source (TCS) component. This isn't an additional tax, but rather an advance tax that your bank collects on behalf of the government. You can later claim this amount back.

Understanding when and how TCS is applied, especially for gifts, and whether transfers to an NRI's NRO account differ, is crucial for smooth financial planning. These rules have seen recent updates, particularly concerning the threshold and rates, making it important to be aware of the latest provisions.

## Understanding LRS and TCS on Overseas Remittances

The Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS), introduced by the RBI, is a framework that allows resident Indian individuals to send money abroad for various current and capital account transactions. This includes purposes like education, medical treatment, travel, investments, and gifts. The annual limit for such remittances is USD 250,000 per financial year (April to March).

Tax Collected at Source (TCS) is a mechanism under Section 206C(1G) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, where banks or authorised dealers collect a specified percentage of tax at the time of processing certain outward remittances under LRS. This is not a final tax, but an advance tax payment that is deposited with the government. The primary objective of TCS on foreign remittances is to track high-value transactions and ensure compliance.

For financial year 2025-26 onwards, the TCS rules have been updated. For remittances under LRS for purposes other than education or medical treatment (which includes gifts), a TCS of 20% is applicable on the amount exceeding ₹10 lakhs in a financial year. This ₹10 lakh threshold is a combined limit across all LRS remittances made by an individual in a financial year, through all banks and payment modes.

### Key LRS Remittance Categories and TCS Rates (FY 22025-26 onwards):
*   **Education (via specified education loan):** 0% TCS.
*   **Education (self-funded) / Medical Treatment:** 2% TCS on amounts exceeding ₹10 lakhs.
*   **Overseas Tour Packages:** 2% TCS without any amount stipulation. (Note: Some sources mention 5% up to ₹10 lakh and 20% beyond for tour packages, but the latest update from Budget 2026 suggests 2% flat. It's crucial to confirm with your bank for the exact rate at the time of transaction.)
*   **Any Other Purpose (including gifts, investments, maintenance of relatives):** 20% TCS on amounts exceeding ₹10 lakhs.

## Gifting to an NRI: Tax Implications for Giver and Receiver

When a resident Indian gifts money to an NRI child, the tax implications are generally favourable for the recipient under Indian tax laws.

### Tax-Free Gift for the NRI Child
Under Section 56(2)(x) of the Income Tax Act, gifts received by an individual or Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) exceeding ₹50,000 in a financial year are typically taxable as "Income from Other Sources." However, there are specific exemptions. Gifts received from "relatives" are fully exempt from tax, regardless of the amount. The Income Tax Act defines "relative" to include immediate family members such as parents, spouse, siblings, and lineal ascendants or descendants. Therefore, a gift from a parent to their NRI child falls under this exemption, meaning the NRI child will not have to pay income tax on the gift amount in India.

### No Gift Tax for the Giver
India abolished the separate Gift Tax Act in 1998. Therefore, the resident Indian parent making the gift is not liable to pay any gift tax on the amount given. The tax consideration for the remitter primarily revolves around the TCS on the outward remittance under LRS, as discussed above.

## TCS on Gifts to NRIs: Overseas Transfer vs. NRO Account

The application of TCS depends on whether the gift is transferred directly to an overseas bank account or to the NRI child's Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) account in India.

### Direct Transfer to Overseas Bank Account
If you, as a resident Indian, directly transfer funds as a gift to your NRI child's overseas bank account, this transaction falls squarely under the LRS. As established, if the cumulative amount of such remittances (for purposes other than education/medical) exceeds ₹10 lakhs in a financial year, a 20% TCS will be collected on the amount exceeding this threshold. The bank facilitating the transfer will collect this TCS at the time of remittance.

### Transfer to NRO Account in India
Many resident Indians choose to transfer funds to their NRI child's NRO account in India. An NRO account is used by NRIs to manage income earned in India, such as rent, dividends, or pension.
Crucially, transferring a rupee-denominated gift amount from a resident Indian's account to an NRI's NRO account *within India* is generally not subject to TCS. This is because such a transaction is considered a domestic transfer, not an outward remittance under LRS at that stage.

However, if the NRI child later wishes to transfer funds from their NRO account to their overseas bank account, this *repatriation* of funds will be subject to LRS rules and limits. NRIs can repatriate up to USD 1 million per financial year from their NRO account, subject to necessary documentation. While the transfer from NRO to NRE (Non-Resident External) account or directly overseas is generally exempt from TCS for NRIs under Section 206C(1G), it's important to note that the *resident individual* making the initial gift to the NRO account might still be subject to LRS rules if the bank considers it an LRS transaction for the remitter. Most banks currently do not collect TCS on such rupee-denominated gift transfers to NRO accounts. It's always advisable to confirm the bank's specific policy.

## Claiming a Refund for TCS Paid

The Tax Collected at Source (TCS) is not a final tax. It is an advance tax that you, as the remitter, can claim back. Here's how the refund mechanism works:

### TCS as Advance Tax Credit
The amount of TCS collected by your bank is deposited with the Income Tax Department and reflects in your Form 26AS. Form 26AS is an annual consolidated tax statement that provides details of tax deducted or collected at source on your behalf. When you file your annual Income Tax Return (ITR), you can claim this TCS amount as a credit against your total tax liability for that financial year.

### How to Claim the Refund
1.  **Verify TCS Deduction:** Ensure the TCS amount is correctly reflected in your Form 26AS. This can be accessed from the Income Tax e-Filing Portal (incometax.gov.in).
2.  **File Income Tax Return:** When filing your ITR, you will declare the TCS amount under the relevant section (typically in the 'Taxes Paid' or 'TCS' tab, under section 206C(1G)).
3.  **Adjustment or Refund:**
    *   If your total tax liability for the year is higher than the TCS paid, the TCS amount will be adjusted against your tax payable, reducing the amount you need to pay.
    *   If your total tax liability is less than the TCS paid, or if you have no tax liability, the excess TCS amount will be refunded to you. Refunds are typically processed within a few months after e-verification of your ITR.

### Who Claims the Refund?
The TCS is collected from the remitter (the parent in this gifting scenario). Therefore, it is the remitter who is eligible to claim the TCS credit or refund when filing their ITR in India. The NRI child, as the recipient, generally cannot claim this TCS credit as they are not the one from whom the tax was collected.

## A real example

**Prakash, 52, ₹35L CTC, Mumbai**

Prakash wants to gift ₹25 lakhs to his daughter, Neha, 28, who is an NRI residing in Canada. Neha has an overseas bank account in Canada and also an NRO account in India. Prakash is considering two options for sending the money.

**Option 1: Direct Transfer to Neha's Overseas Bank Account**

1.  **LRS Limit:** Prakash is a resident Indian. The remittance of ₹25 lakhs falls within the annual LRS limit of USD 250,000 (approximately ₹2 crores).
2.  **TCS Applicability:** Gifting is categorised under "Any Other Purpose" for LRS. For FY 2025-26 onwards, a 20% TCS applies on amounts exceeding ₹10 lakhs for this category.
3.  **TCS Calculation:**
    *   Total gift amount: ₹25,00,000
    *   TCS-exempt threshold: ₹10,00,000
    *   Amount on which TCS is applicable: ₹25,00,000 - ₹10,00,000 = ₹15,00,000
    *   TCS rate: 20%
    *   TCS amount collected: 20% of ₹15,00,000 = ₹3,00,000
4.  **Total Outflow for Prakash:** Prakash will need to pay ₹25,00,000 (gift amount) + ₹3,00,000 (TCS) = ₹28,00,000 to complete the transfer.
5.  **Refund:** Prakash can claim this ₹3,00,000 TCS as a credit against his income tax liability when filing his ITR. If his tax liability is less than ₹3,00,000, he can claim the excess as a refund.

**Option 2: Transfer to Neha's NRO Account in India**

1.  **Transfer to NRO:** Prakash transfers ₹25 lakhs from his Indian bank account to Neha's NRO account in India.
2.  **TCS Applicability:** As per prevailing banking practice, most banks currently do not collect TCS on rupee-denominated gift transfers from a resident Indian's account to an NRI's NRO account within India. Therefore, Prakash would likely not pay any TCS at this stage.
3.  **Neha's Repatriation:** If Neha later decides to transfer this ₹25 lakhs from her NRO account to her overseas bank account in Canada, this would be a repatriation of funds by an NRI. While NRIs are generally exempt from TCS on repatriation from NRO to NRE or overseas accounts under Section 206C(1G), it's important for Neha to ensure all FEMA guidelines are met for such a transfer. She can repatriate up to USD 1 million per financial year from her NRO account.

In this scenario, Option 2 initially involves no TCS for Prakash, but Neha would need to handle the repatriation from her NRO account, which is typically exempt from TCS for NRIs. Option 1 involves upfront TCS for Prakash, which he can then claim back.

## What to do this week

1.  **Understand LRS Thresholds:** Review your cumulative LRS remittances for the current financial year (April to March) to determine if you are approaching or have exceeded the ₹10 lakh threshold for "Other Purposes" (which includes gifts).
2.  **Consult Your Bank:** Before initiating any large remittance, speak with your bank's forex or NRI desk. Confirm the exact TCS rate applicable for "gifting" to an NRI and clarify their policy on transfers to NRO accounts.
3.  **Gather Documentation:** Ensure you have your PAN card and are prepared to provide any other documents your bank may require for LRS remittances, such as a declaration of purpose.
4.  **Keep Records:** Maintain meticulous records of all remittance transactions, including purpose codes, amounts, and any TCS collected. This will be crucial for filing your Income Tax Return.

## FAQ

### Is a gift from a parent to an NRI child taxable in India?
No, a gift of money from a parent to an NRI child is exempt from income tax in India for the child, as gifts from specified relatives are not considered taxable income under Section 56(2)(x) of the Income Tax Act.

### What is the LRS limit for gifting money abroad?
Resident Indians can remit up to USD 250,000 (approximately ₹2 crores) per financial year under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) for various permissible purposes, including gifts.

### What is the TCS rate on gifting money to an NRI child?
For financial year 2025-26 onwards, a 20% TCS is applicable on gift remittances exceeding ₹10 lakhs in a financial year, on the amount above this threshold.

### Does TCS apply if I transfer money to my NRI child's NRO account in India?
Generally, no. Transfers from a resident Indian's account to an NRI's NRO account within India for gifting purposes are typically not subject to TCS as per prevailing banking practice.

### Can I get a refund for the TCS paid on a gift remittance?
Yes, the TCS collected is an advance tax. The remitter (parent) can claim this amount as a credit against their income tax liability or seek a refund when filing their Income Tax Return in India.

### What if the gift is for education or medical treatment?
Remittances for education (self-funded) or medical treatment are subject to a lower TCS rate of 2% on amounts exceeding ₹10 lakhs, from FY 2025-26 onwards. If education is funded by an approved loan, TCS is 0%. It's crucial to use the correct purpose code.
