# Pre-Market Brief — 25 May 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets closed mostly positive, with Asian indices showing strong gains, indicating a favourable start for Indian equities.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak for the fifth consecutive session, while Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) remained net buyers.
- The Indian Rupee strengthened significantly against the US Dollar, and Brent crude oil prices saw a notable decline, easing inflation concerns.
- Several prominent companies, including TCS, LTM, and Tata Consumer Products, are trading ex-dividend today.
- The RBI's recent dividend payout offers a fiscal buffer, but experts caution about persistent inflation risks from subsidies and fuel excise cuts.

## Where global markets closed
Global markets largely ended in positive territory on Friday, setting a generally optimistic tone for the start of the new trading week. US indices, including the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500, posted moderate gains. European markets also closed higher, with the FTSE 100 registering a positive finish. Asian markets showed particular strength, with Japan's Nikkei 225 surging nearly 3%, and both the Hang Seng and Shanghai Composite recording healthy advances. This broad-based positive sentiment across major global bourses suggests a potentially firm opening for Indian markets.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee showed significant strength against the US Dollar, with **USD/INR** closing at ₹95.28, a decrease of 0.93%. A stronger Rupee is generally beneficial for India as it makes imports cheaper, potentially easing inflationary pressures and reducing the current account deficit.

The **Dollar Index** data is unavailable.

In the commodities market, **Brent Crude** experienced a notable decline, falling by 3.22% to $100.21 per barrel. This is a positive development for India, a major oil importer, as lower crude prices can significantly reduce the country's import bill and help manage domestic fuel inflation. **WTI Crude** remained unchanged at $96.60 per barrel.

**Gold** prices saw a slight uptick of 0.05% to $4523.20, while **Silver** also gained marginally by 0.40% to $76.20. These moves suggest a period of consolidation for precious metals after recent volatility, possibly reflecting underlying safe-haven demand or positioning ahead of key economic data.

The **US 10-Year Yield** declined by 0.61% to 4.56%. A fall in US bond yields can make equities relatively more attractive and might signal easing inflation expectations or a flight to quality amidst global uncertainties. For emerging markets like India, lower US yields can sometimes encourage capital inflows.

## Overnight news that matters

**RBI dividend provides fiscal buffer, but inflation risks loom large: Kanika Pasricha, Union Bank**
The Reserve Bank of India's recent dividend payout offers a modest fiscal cushion to the government. However, the expert warns that government finances face pressure from ongoing fuel excise cuts and rising fertiliser subsidies, which could exacerbate inflation risks.
*Why it matters to Indian markets:* This news directly impacts government fiscal health and the inflation outlook. While the dividend provides immediate relief, persistent inflation could lead to a tighter monetary policy stance from the RBI, affecting interest rate-sensitive sectors and overall economic growth.

**A Tougher Fed? Markets brace for policy shift under Kevin Warsh**
Kevin Warsh has assumed the role of the new U.S. Federal Reserve chair amidst a backdrop of rising inflation, elevated oil prices, and geopolitical tensions. Investors are keenly observing whether his policy approach will prioritise economic growth or focus more on controlling inflation.
*Why it matters to Indian markets:* The US Federal Reserve's monetary policy decisions have significant global ramifications. A "tougher Fed" under Warsh, potentially leading to higher interest rates, could trigger capital outflows from emerging markets like India and strengthen the US Dollar, impacting FII flows and the Rupee.

**Aluminium prices near record high: What’s driving the rally?**
Aluminium prices are trading close to multi-year highs, driven by persistent structural supply tightness rather than temporary factors. Stricter environmental regulations, high energy costs, and production caps are limiting global output.
*Why it matters to Indian markets:* Elevated aluminium prices are a mixed bag for Indian markets. While it benefits domestic aluminium producers like Hindalco and Vedanta, it increases input costs for industries that use aluminium, such as automotive, construction, and packaging, potentially impacting their profitability.

**Sterlite Technologies shares rise 5% after subsidiary bags $1.1 billion hyperscaler AI contract**
Shares of Sterlite Technologies surged nearly 5% after its subsidiary secured a multi-year optical connectivity supply agreement, estimated at $1.11 billion, from a global hyperscaler for AI-ready data centre infrastructure.
*Why it matters to Indian markets:* This is a significant positive development for Sterlite Technologies, highlighting strong demand for advanced optical connectivity solutions driven by the global AI boom. It could boost investor confidence in the company and potentially in other Indian tech infrastructure providers catering to global demand.

**Mid & small-cap stocks firm despite oil shocks and global uncertainty: But will the trend continue?**
Indian mid- and small-cap indices have shown remarkable resilience and delivered strong performance over the past two months, with the Nifty Smallcap 100 surging over 20% from its March 2026 lows, despite global uncertainties and oil price volatility.
*Why it matters to Indian markets:* The sustained outperformance of mid and small-cap segments indicates strong domestic liquidity and investor confidence in broader market themes. However, the question of whether this trend can continue amidst global headwinds is crucial for retail investors, as these segments are typically more volatile.

## What to watch in today's session
1.  **FII and DII Activity**: Continue to monitor the net cash flows of Foreign and Domestic Institutional Investors. Persistent FII selling, if not adequately absorbed by DIIs, could put pressure on benchmark indices.
2.  **Crude Oil Movement**: Given the significant drop in Brent crude prices, watch for its impact on oil marketing companies, paint companies, and other sectors sensitive to crude input costs. Further declines could be a positive for India's macroeconomic stability.
3.  **Metal Sector Performance**: With aluminium prices near record highs, keep an eye on metal stocks, particularly aluminium producers. Any sustained rally or profit-booking in this sector will be important.
4.  **Corporate Actions**: Stocks like TCS, LTM, Tata Consumer Products, and SIYSIL will trade ex-dividend today. Investors should observe their opening prices and subsequent movements as they adjust for the dividend payout.
5.  **Mid and Small-Cap Resilience**: After their recent strong performance, it will be crucial to see if the mid and small-cap segments can maintain their momentum or if profit-booking emerges, especially given the broader global cues.

## Bottom line
Indian markets are likely to open with a positive bias, taking cues from the broadly firm global markets, especially the strong performance in Asian equities. The significant strengthening of the Rupee and the fall in Brent crude prices are positive domestic factors that could support sentiment. However, the continuous selling by FIIs remains a point of caution, and investors should closely watch their activity alongside the resilience of domestic institutions.
