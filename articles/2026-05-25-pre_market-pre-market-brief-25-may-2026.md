# Pre-Market Brief — 25 May 2026

**TL;DR**
- Global markets closed mostly positive, with Asian indices leading gains, though US markets are shut today for Memorial Day.
- The Indian Rupee strengthened significantly against the US Dollar, while Brent crude oil prices saw a notable decline.
- Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling streak, offloading ₹4,440 crore, largely absorbed by Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs).
- The RBI's record ₹2.87 lakh crore dividend transfer to the government provides substantial fiscal support.
- Several prominent companies, including TCS and Tata Consumer Products, have ex-dividend dates today.

## Where global markets closed
Global equity markets largely ended in positive territory on Friday, with Asian indices showing particular strength. Japan's Nikkei 225 surged over 2.8%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng and China's Shanghai Composite also posted healthy gains. European markets, represented by the FTSE 100, closed modestly higher. In the US, the Dow Jones, Nasdaq, and S&P 500 registered gains, albeit smaller, ahead of the Memorial Day holiday. Investors will note that US markets will remain closed today, Monday, May 25, for the holiday. The overall global tone appears cautiously optimistic, despite underlying concerns about inflation and geopolitical tensions.

## Currency, commodities and yields
The Indian Rupee (USD/INR) saw a significant appreciation, strengthening by over 1% to 95.20 against the US Dollar. A stronger rupee generally makes imports cheaper for India, which is a net importer of goods like crude oil, potentially easing inflationary pressures.

In the commodities market, Brent Crude oil prices declined by 3.22% to $100.21 per barrel. A fall in international crude oil prices is a positive development for India, as it reduces the country's import bill and can help manage domestic fuel prices and inflation. WTI Crude remained unchanged.

Gold prices edged up slightly by 0.05% to $4523.20, while Silver saw a 0.40% increase to $76.20. These modest gains could indicate some safe-haven demand or a slight weakening of the dollar, making precious metals more attractive.

The US 10-Year Treasury Yield fell by 0.61% to 4.56%. A decline in US bond yields can sometimes signal easing inflation expectations or increased demand for safer government bonds, which could influence global capital flows and borrowing costs for emerging markets like India.

## Corporate actions today
Today marks the ex-date for several corporate actions, primarily dividends, which will impact shareholders of these companies:

*   **TCS**: The shares will trade ex-dividend for a dividend of ₹31 per share. This means that to be eligible for this dividend, investors must have purchased the shares before today.
*   **LTM**: Shareholders will see the shares trade ex-dividend for a payout of ₹53 per share.
*   **TATACONSUM**: Tata Consumer Products shares will trade ex-dividend for a dividend of ₹10 per share.
*   **SIYSIL**: The company has declared a special dividend of ₹4 per share, and its shares will trade ex-dividend today.

## Recent FII/DII flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) continued their selling trend on Friday, recording a net outflow of ₹4,440 crore from the Indian cash equity market. This persistent selling, observed over the last five sessions, has seen FIIs withdraw over ₹10,385 crore. However, Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) have consistently provided strong counter-support, injecting ₹6,004 crore into the market yesterday, offsetting a significant portion of the FII outflows.

## Overnight news that matters

**RBI transfer of ₹2.87 lakh cr surplus to govt means for Indian economy**
The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has announced a record dividend transfer of ₹2.87 lakh crore to the government.
*Why it matters:* This substantial infusion of funds provides the government with significant fiscal headroom. It can be used to bolster infrastructure spending, reduce the fiscal deficit, or provide targeted economic stimulus, which could have a positive ripple effect across various sectors of the Indian economy.

**Sterlite Technologies shares rise 5% after subsidiary bags $1.1 billion hyperscaler AI contract**
Sterlite Technologies' shares gained after its subsidiary secured a multi-year optical connectivity supply agreement worth an estimated $1.11 billion from a global hyperscaler for AI-ready data centres.
*Why it matters:* This large contract is a significant positive for Sterlite Technologies, indicating strong demand for its optical connectivity solutions in the rapidly expanding AI and data centre infrastructure space. It could boost investor confidence in the company and the broader telecom infrastructure sector.

**A Tougher Fed? Markets brace for policy shift under Kevin Warsh**
Kevin Warsh has taken charge as the new U.S. Federal Reserve chair amid rising inflation, elevated oil prices, and geopolitical tensions.
*Why it matters:* The appointment of a new Fed chair, particularly one perceived as potentially "tougher" on inflation, could signal a shift in US monetary policy. Any hawkish stance by the Fed could lead to higher interest rates globally, impacting capital flows to emerging markets like India and potentially strengthening the US Dollar, which would have implications for the Rupee and FII flows.

**US Stock Market: Are S&P 500, Nasdaq open or shut today for Memorial Day 2026? Check here**
Wall Street will observe the Memorial Day holiday on Monday, May 25, meaning both the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq will be closed.
*Why it matters:* With US markets closed, global trading volumes may be lower, and Indian markets will lack direct cues from Wall Street. This could lead to more domestically driven trading or potentially higher volatility if there are significant local developments without the usual international benchmarks.

**Nifty Pharma, 4 others hit 52-week high since Iran war while Nifty fell 7%. Will the form continue?**
Despite a challenging market backdrop fueled by geopolitical tensions and foreign fund outflows, five Indian sectors—Pharma, Energy, Defence, Capital Markets, and Metals—are showing remarkable strength, hitting 52-week highs.
*Why it matters:* This highlights pockets of resilience and strength within the Indian market, even as the broader Nifty has seen a decline. Investors may continue to focus on these sectors for potential opportunities, driven by factors specific to their industries, such as increased demand, government initiatives, or commodity price movements.

## What to watch in today's session
1.  **FII/DII Flow Dynamics**: Monitor whether FII selling continues and if DIIs maintain their strong buying support, which has been crucial in absorbing foreign outflows.
2.  **Impact of RBI Dividend**: Observe how the market reacts to the RBI's record dividend transfer. Any government announcements regarding the utilisation of these funds could influence sentiment in infrastructure, public sector, or other related sectors.
3.  **Sectoral Performance**: Keep a close watch on sectors that have shown resilience, such as Pharma, Energy, Defence, Capital Markets, and Metals, to see if their upward momentum continues.
4.  **Dividend-paying Stocks**: Companies like TCS, LTM, Tata Consumer Products, and SIYSIL are trading ex-dividend today. Their stock prices might see adjustments reflecting the dividend payout, and investors will be watching for any post-ex-date movements.
5.  **Global Cues (without US markets)**: With US markets closed, Indian markets will primarily draw cues from Asian and European markets, as well as domestic news flow. Any significant developments in crude oil prices or the Rupee's movement will also be key.

## Bottom line
The Indian market is poised for an open influenced by largely positive global cues from Asia and Europe, coupled with a strengthening Rupee and falling crude oil prices, which are favourable for India. However, the continued FII selling remains a point of caution, though it has been effectively counterbalanced by robust DII buying. The significant RBI dividend transfer provides a strong domestic tailwind. Investors should remain vigilant about the ongoing FII activity and the performance of resilient domestic sectors, especially with US markets being closed today.
