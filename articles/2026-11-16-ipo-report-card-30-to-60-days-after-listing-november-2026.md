## Why look 30 to 60 days after listing?
When a company first offers its shares to the public through an Initial Public Offering (IPO), there's often a lot of excitement. The "listing day" (the first day a company's shares are traded on the stock exchange) can see shares "pop" – meaning they trade significantly higher than their "issue price" (the fixed price at which shares were offered in the IPO). This initial jump is often called a "listing gain" (the percentage difference between the IPO price and the listing day closing price).

However, this first-day frenzy doesn't always tell the full story. Many factors, like speculative trading or high initial demand, can push prices up temporarily. For a calmer, more instructive read, it's helpful to look at how these shares perform a month or two later. This 30 to 60-day window is important because the initial excitement often fades, and a key event occurs: the "anchor investor lock-in period" usually ends after 30 days. Anchor investors (large institutional investors who commit to buying a significant portion of shares before the IPO opens to the public) are then free to sell their shares, which can impact the supply and demand dynamics in the market. By this time, the market has had more time to assess the company's actual business and future prospects, leading to a more settled price.
