# Missed FD Income: How to Rectify and What Happens If You Don't

**TL;DR**
- Fixed Deposit (FD) interest income is fully taxable under Indian income tax laws.
- If you missed declaring FD interest in your original Income Tax Return (ITR), you can file an Updated Return (ITR-U).
- ITR-U allows corrections for up to two assessment years from the end of the relevant assessment year.
- Filing an ITR-U incurs an additional tax penalty, which increases with the delay in filing.
- Ignoring undeclared FD income can lead to scrutiny, higher penalties, and potential prosecution by the Income Tax Department.

## What this means in plain terms

Many of us invest in Fixed Deposits (FDs) for their assured returns, considering them a safe haven for our savings. While FDs offer predictable growth, it's crucial to remember that the interest earned on them is fully taxable as "Income from Other Sources" under the Income Tax Act. A common oversight among taxpayers is forgetting to declare this interest income when filing their annual income tax returns.

This omission, whether intentional or accidental, can lead to issues with the Income Tax Department. The department has access to information about your FD interest earnings through various sources like Form 26AS and the Annual Information Statement (AIS), which banks report. If there's a mismatch between what you've declared and what the department knows, it can trigger a notice. Thankfully, the tax laws provide a mechanism, called the Updated Return (ITR-U), to correct such errors and declare any missed income, albeit with an additional tax liability.

## Understanding FD Interest Taxation and TDS

Fixed deposit interest is considered taxable income in India. It falls under the head "Income from Other Sources" for individuals and HUFs. The interest earned is added to your total income and taxed as per your applicable income tax slab rates. This means if you are in the 30% tax bracket, your FD interest will also be taxed at 30%.

Banks are mandated to deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on FD interest if it exceeds a certain threshold in a financial year. For most individuals, this threshold is ₹40,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens). If your interest income crosses this limit, the bank will deduct 10% TDS (or 20% if PAN is not furnished) before crediting the interest to your account. Even if TDS is deducted, it does not mean the income is fully taxed; it is merely an advance tax payment. You still need to declare the entire FD interest income in your ITR and pay any additional tax if your slab rate is higher than the TDS rate. If your total income is below the taxable limit, you can claim a refund of the TDS deducted by filing your ITR.

To ensure accuracy, always cross-verify your FD interest income with your bank statements, Form 26AS, and the Annual Information Statement (AIS). Form 26AS provides a consolidated statement of tax deducted at source and tax collected at source. The AIS, introduced recently, offers a more comprehensive view of all financial transactions, including interest income from FDs, savings accounts, and other sources. Regularly checking these documents helps identify any discrepancies and ensures you declare all your income correctly.

### Key Aspects of FD Interest Taxation:
*   **Accrual Basis:** FD interest is usually taxed on an accrual basis, meaning it's taxable in the year it's earned, even if not yet received or reinvested.
*   **Form 15G/15H:** If your total income is below the taxable limit and you don't want the bank to deduct TDS, you can submit Form 15G (for non-senior citizens) or Form 15H (for senior citizens) to the bank. This declares that your estimated total income for the year is below the basic exemption limit.

## Rectifying Mistakes with an Updated Return (ITR-U)

If you've realised you missed declaring FD interest income in your original ITR, the Income Tax Act provides a window to correct this through an "Updated Return" (ITR-U). This facility was introduced to promote voluntary compliance and reduce litigation. You can file an ITR-U within two years from the end of the relevant assessment year. For example, for the financial year 2022-23 (Assessment Year 2023-24), you can file an ITR-U up to 31st March 2026.

Filing an ITR-U allows you to declare any income that was previously missed, including FD interest, and pay the additional tax due. However, this comes with an additional tax penalty on the tax payable. The penalty structure is as follows:
*   **Within 12 months** from the end of the relevant assessment year: An additional tax of 25% of the aggregate tax and interest due.
*   **Between 12 and 24 months** from the end of the relevant assessment year: An additional tax of 50% of the aggregate tax and interest due.

It is important to note that an ITR-U cannot be filed to reduce your total income, claim a refund, or increase a refund already claimed. It is primarily for declaring additional income and paying the corresponding tax. The process involves selecting the option for filing an updated return on the income tax e-filing portal and providing the details of the additional income.

## Consequences of Ignoring Undeclared FD Income

Ignoring undeclared FD income can lead to significant issues with the Income Tax Department. The department has robust data analytics capabilities and access to various financial transaction records, including those reported by banks. If a mismatch is detected between your declared income and the information available to the department (e.g., in your Form 26AS or AIS), you could receive a notice.

The consequences of not rectifying undeclared income can include:
*   **Scrutiny Notice:** The Income Tax Department may issue a notice under Section 143(2) or Section 148, initiating a scrutiny assessment.
*   **Higher Penalties:** If the undeclared income is discovered by the department, the penalties can be much higher than those for voluntarily filing an ITR-U. Penalties can range from 50% to 200% of the tax payable on the under-reported income, depending on whether the under-reporting is considered a misreporting of income.
*   **Interest on Unpaid Tax:** You will be liable to pay interest under Section 234A, 234B, and 234C for delays in filing the return and payment of advance tax.
*   **Prosecution:** In severe cases of significant tax evasion, the Income Tax Department can initiate prosecution proceedings, which may lead to imprisonment.

Therefore, it is always advisable to proactively rectify any errors or omissions in your tax returns by filing an ITR-U rather than waiting for the department to detect the discrepancy. This demonstrates good faith and can significantly reduce the financial and legal repercussions.

## A real example

Let's consider **Meena, 45, a salaried professional in Gurugram, earning ₹22 lakhs CTC**. For the Financial Year 2022-23 (Assessment Year 2023-24), Meena filed her ITR on time. She had a Fixed Deposit that matured during the year, earning ₹85,000 in interest. However, she completely forgot to declare this interest income in her original ITR. No TDS was deducted as she had submitted Form 15G.

Now, it's January 2026, and Meena realises her oversight while reviewing old bank statements. She decides to file an Updated Return (ITR-U) for AY 2023-24.

**Original Situation:**
*   Declared Taxable Income: ₹18,00,000
*   Tax paid (after deductions): ₹3,51,000 (approx., assuming standard deductions and slab rates)

**Undeclared Income:**
*   FD Interest: ₹85,000

**Calculating Additional Tax and Penalty for ITR-U:**
Meena's total income will now be ₹18,00,000 + ₹85,000 = ₹18,85,000.
Assuming Meena is in the 30% tax bracket (plus 4% cess), the additional tax on ₹85,000 would be:
*   Tax: ₹85,000 * 30% = ₹25,500
*   Cess: ₹25,500 * 4% = ₹1,020
*   Total Additional Tax: ₹26,520

Since Meena is filing the ITR-U in January 2026, which is between 12 and 24 months from the end of AY 2023-24 (which ended March 31, 2024), she will incur an additional tax penalty of 50% on the aggregate tax and interest due.

*   Additional tax penalty: 50% of ₹26,520 = ₹13,260

She will also be liable for interest under Section 234A, 234B, and 234C for the delay in payment of this tax. Let's estimate this interest to be around ₹4,000 for simplicity in this example (actual calculation would be precise based on dates).

**Total Amount Meena needs to pay with ITR-U:**
*   Additional Tax: ₹26,520
*   Additional Tax Penalty (50%): ₹13,260
*   Interest (estimated): ₹4,000
*   **Total Payable: ₹26,520 + ₹13,260 + ₹4,000 = ₹43,780**

By filing an ITR-U, Meena rectifies her mistake and pays ₹43,780 to the Income Tax Department, avoiding potential scrutiny and much higher penalties that could have resulted if the department had discovered the undeclared income first.

## What to do this week

1.  **Gather Bank Statements:** Collect all bank statements for the previous financial year(s) you suspect might have undeclared FD interest. Identify any FD interest credits.
2.  **Check Form 26AS/AIS:** Log in to the Income Tax e-filing portal (https://incometax.gov.in) and download your Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement (AIS) for the relevant assessment years. Verify if all FD interest income reported by banks matches your records.
3.  **Calculate Missed Income:** Quantify the exact amount of FD interest income you missed declaring for each relevant financial year.
4.  **Consult a Professional:** If the amount is significant or you are unsure about the ITR-U process, consider consulting a Certified Financial Planner or a tax advisor to guide you through filing the Updated Return.
5.  **Initiate ITR-U Filing:** If you confirm undeclared income, prepare to file an ITR-U on the income tax e-filing portal. Ensure you have all necessary documents and calculate the additional tax and penalty accurately.

## FAQ

### What is an Updated Return (ITR-U)?
An ITR-U is a facility provided by the Income Tax Department allowing taxpayers to correct errors or declare additional income missed in their original or revised ITR within two years from the end of the relevant assessment year.

### How long do I have to file an ITR-U?
You can file an ITR-U within two years from the end of the relevant assessment year. For example, for income earned in FY 2022-23 (AY 2023-24), you can file an ITR-U until March 31, 2026.

### What is the penalty for filing an ITR-U?
The penalty depends on when you file. If filed within 12 months from the end of the assessment year, it's 25% of the aggregate tax and interest due. If filed between 12 and 24 months, it's 50%.

### Can I file an ITR-U to claim a refund?
No, an ITR-U cannot be filed to reduce your total income, claim a refund, or increase a refund already claimed. It is specifically for declaring additional income and paying the corresponding tax.

### What if TDS was already deducted on my FD interest?
Even if TDS was deducted by the bank, you still need to declare the full FD interest income in your ITR. TDS is an advance tax, and your final tax liability depends on your income slab. You can claim credit for the TDS paid.

### What happens if I don't rectify undeclared FD income?
If the Income Tax Department discovers undeclared income, you could face scrutiny, higher penalties (up to 200% of the tax payable), interest on unpaid tax, and potentially prosecution in severe cases.
