# Market Wrap — 03 August 2026

**TL;DR**
*   Indian benchmark indices surged today, with Nifty 50 gaining 1.60% and Bank Nifty advancing 1.72%, both closing at their day's highs.
*   The primary catalyst for the rally was a significant drop in crude oil prices, driven by hopes of easing US-Iran tensions.
*   Urban Company Ltd. emerged as the top Nifty 500 gainer, soaring by 12.98%.
*   Zee Entertainment Enterprises Ltd. was the biggest Nifty 500 loser, plummeting 14.33%.
*   Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) were strong net buyers with ₹2,260 crore, while Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) also turned net buyers with ₹277 crore.
*   F&O build-up data is unavailable for today, as it requires the previous session's Open Interest for analysis.

## How the market behaved today
Indian equities witnessed a robust rally today, with both the Nifty 50 and Bank Nifty closing significantly higher, marking their day's peak. The Nifty 50 ended at 24774.30, up 1.60%, while the Bank Nifty climbed 1.72% to close at 58247.95. The broader market also participated in the rally, with Nifty Midcap 100 gaining 1.21% and Nifty Smallcap 100 rising 1.29%. The day saw a strong upward trend, culminating in a notable surge towards the closing bell.

| Nifty Midcap 100 | 63679.30 | +1.21% | 63681.80 | 63107.55 |

India VIX, the volatility gauge, closed at 11.93, up 1.45%. This slight uptick in the VIX suggests that while the market rallied, there was a marginal increase in expected near-term volatility.

## What moved the market — the factors
The Indian market's strong performance today was primarily driven by positive global cues, specifically the sharp decline in crude oil prices. Hopes of easing tensions between the US and Iran led to a significant drop in both Brent and WTI crude, which in turn alleviated inflation concerns globally. This positive sentiment translated into a rally across Asian markets and saw US stock futures rise, setting a constructive tone for Indian equities.

Domestically, strong buying support from Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) of ₹2,260 crore, coupled with Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) turning net buyers at ₹277 crore, provided a solid foundation for the market's ascent. Sector-wise, Information Technology (IT) stocks led the charge, benefiting from the improved global outlook and reduced inflation worries. Banking and FMCG sectors also contributed significantly to the upward momentum. A notable event was a nearly 200-point surge in the Nifty in the final two minutes of trading, attributed to the introduction of the Closing Auction Session for F&O stocks, which replaced the previous VWAP-based closing price mechanism.

## Sectors — winners and losers
Today's trading session saw broad-based buying, with several sectors ending in the green.

### Top 3 Gainer Sectors:
*   **IT (+3.28%)**: The sector rallied strongly, buoyed by easing global inflation concerns due to falling crude oil prices and positive sentiment from US markets.
*   **BANK (+1.72%)**: Banking stocks showed robust performance, reflecting overall market optimism and DII buying interest.
*   **FMCG (+1.72%)**: Consumer goods companies gained, likely benefiting from reduced input cost pressures due to lower oil prices and broad market strength.

### Top 3 Loser Sectors:
*   **MEDIA (-3.09%)**: The sector was significantly dragged down by a sharp decline in Zee Entertainment Enterprises Ltd.
*   **PHARMA (+0.48%)**: Despite being listed as a 'loser' in the top 3, the Pharma sector still managed a modest gain, indicating selective buying.
*   **ENERGY (+0.54%)**: Similar to Pharma, the Energy sector also posted a slight gain, suggesting that while it was among the bottom performers, it still participated in the broader market's positive trend.

## News that moved stocks
*   **Easing US-Iran Tensions and Falling Oil Prices Boost Sentiment**: Hopes of a peace deal between the US and Iran led to a sharp decline in crude oil prices, with Brent Crude falling over 7% and WTI Crude over 6%. This development significantly eased global inflation worries and boosted investor confidence across markets, including India. The positive sentiment contributed to the broad market rally and particularly benefited sectors sensitive to crude oil prices.
*   **Nifty's Late Surge Due to Closing Auction Session**: The Nifty 50 witnessed an unusual nearly 200-point jump in the final two minutes of trading. This surge was attributed to India's introduction of the Closing Auction Session for F&O stocks, which replaced the previous Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP)-based closing price with an auction-driven equilibrium price.
*   **DLF Q1 Results**: DLF Ltd. reported a 4% rise in profit to ₹794 crore for the June quarter, despite a 46% decline in revenue. This mixed result indicates profit growth despite revenue challenges.
*   **RBI's Intervention to Stabilize Rupee**: The Reserve Bank of India sold USD 14.9 billion between January and May to curb excessive volatility in the rupee. Despite this, the Indian rupee ended flat today, pulling back from an early surge due to demand for dollars from importers and state banks.
*   **Nexus Select Trust Reports Income Increase**: Nexus Select Trust announced an 11% increase in net operating income to ₹510 crore, along with a distribution of ₹370 crore to its unit holders. This news highlights growth in the digital real estate sector.

## Event breakdown — what happened today and how it hit the market
Today's market action was significantly influenced by two key developments. Globally, the prospect of easing US-Iran tensions led to a sharp decline in crude oil prices. This was a major positive catalyst, as lower oil prices typically reduce inflation concerns and improve economic outlooks, especially for oil-importing nations like India. This global optimism translated into strong buying across Indian sectors, particularly IT and Banking, which are sensitive to global sentiment and economic stability.

Domestically, the introduction of the Closing Auction Session for F&O stocks played a crucial role in the Nifty's dramatic surge of nearly 200 points in the final two minutes of trading. This new mechanism, designed to determine closing prices through an auction, created significant volatility and a strong upward move at the very end of the session. This technical factor, combined with the positive global backdrop and consistent DII buying, propelled the indices to their day's highs.

## Setup for tomorrow
Looking ahead to tomorrow, global cues remain positive. US stock futures are rising on hopes of a Mideast deal and falling oil prices, which should provide a constructive start for Asian markets. Brent crude has seen a substantial drop of over 7%, and the US 10-year yield has also fallen, indicating reduced inflation fears and a potentially more accommodative global environment. Investors will be watching for any further developments on the US-Iran front and crude oil prices. Domestically, the momentum from today's strong close, particularly the impact of the new Closing Auction Session, will be keenly observed. Sectors like IT and Banking, which led today's rally, could see continued interest, while oil-sensitive sectors may also benefit. Key levels for Nifty 50 around 24800 and Bank Nifty around 58300 will be important to watch for continuation of the upward trend.
