How it works

Verify your residential community with a society code, your employer with a work email, and your commercial building through the facility team. Only verified members can see you.

2

Set your route, departure window and travel days, as a driver offering seats or a rider looking for one. Matching scores every member of your circles against your schedule.

Why circles beat open carpooling

Open carpool apps ask you to trust a stranger's selfie. CommuteCircle starts from groups where accountability already exists: your RWA knows the driver, HR knows the rider, the building knows both.

That trust unlocks the two things open marketplaces never cracked in India: women actually pooling, and employers actually paying.

The business case

Business model

CommuteCircle is a trust-first commute marketplace with B2B distribution. Consumers get near-free commuting; employers and building operators pay for the outcomes: parking relief, retention and reportable emissions cuts. The full document lives in docs/business-model.md in this repository; this page is the working summary.

The problem

Metro commuters in India lose 1.5 to 3 hours and ₹6,000 to ₹15,000 a month to commuting, while most private cars enter tech corridors carrying one person. Employers quietly subsidise the mess through parking floors that cost ₹3,000 to 8,000 per slot per month and through attrition on long-commute hires.

Open carpool marketplaces stalled on two blockers: trust, especially for women, who are roughly half of tech park headcount but a small minority of open-app poolers, and regulatory grey zones around private cars taking paid rides. Matching strangers was never the hard part; making pooling feel safe and staying clearly non-commercial was.

Who pays, and for what

1 · Ride convenience fees

₹7–9 per matched seat-trip, paid by the rider on top of the cost share. The cost share itself is capped at fuel plus tolls and flows driver-to-rider, keeping pooling non-commercial; the platform monetises the matching, not the mileage.

2 · B2B SaaS: employers & tech parks

₹35–40 per covered employee per month for corporates (branded circles, verified onboarding, Scope 3 commute reports, guaranteed-ride-home pool), and annual licences of ₹2–3 lakh per tech park for building-level circles and parking-demand dashboards.

3 · Premium subscriptions

₹129–149 per month for priority matching, women-only pool priority, more guaranteed-ride-home cover and multi-circle visibility. Modelled at 4–7% of monthly active commuters.

4 · Partnerships & data products

Per-ride micro-insurance with an insurer partner, fuel and EV charging tie-ups, ESG commute reporting for corporates, and hyperlocal offers from businesses along dense corridors. Small in year 1, material by year 4.

Go-to-market: win one corridor at a time

Carpooling fails when demand is spread thin, so the playbook is density first. The sequence for each 5–7 km micro-market (the model's Year 1 is two of these in Bengaluru):

  1. Sign 10–15 large residential communities through RWAs; society code onboarding at the gate, launch-week pool fairs.
  2. Land two anchor employers in the destination cluster as paid pilots; HR pushes verified onboarding to every long-commute employee.
  3. Switch on women-only pools and guaranteed ride home from day one; they are the adoption unlock, not add-ons.
  4. Convert the tech park operator: building circles, priority parking for pools, licence revenue.
  5. Replicate the corridor. Every new circle raises match rates for existing members, so retention compounds with density.

Regulation and risk, addressed head-on

Cost-sharing, not commerce. Private-car pooling sits in a grey zone under the Motor Vehicles Act and some states have moved against monetised private rides. The product enforces the safe side of the line: cost recovery is capped at fuel plus tolls, drivers can never profit, and the platform's revenue is a tech convenience fee and B2B SaaS, never a cut of a fare.

Insurance. Private policies can wobble on shared rides, so a per-ride group micro-cover rides on every matched trip (costed in the P&L).

Safety incidents. Verified circles, women-only pools, live trip sharing, an SOS flow and a 24×7 response desk (costed under trust & safety).

Data. Commute patterns are sensitive; DPDP-compliant consent, circle-scoped visibility, and no sale of individual-level data.

KPIs the board watches

  • Matched-commute rate: share of posted commutes matched within 48 hours (target 60%+ in dense corridors)
  • Weekly active pools and rides per active commuter (target 14–16 matched rides a month)
  • Circle density: verified members per community and per building
  • B2B: covered employees, logo retention, PEPM expansion
  • Blended CAC per activated commuter vs contribution (target payback under 6 months)
  • Safety: incidents per 100K rides, response time, women-only pool share
